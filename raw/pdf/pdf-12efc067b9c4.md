Water fasting, ketosis and why detox causes
physical symptoms
More than any other kind of diet or cleanse, water fasting enables the body to access
deep levels of self-healing. This takes place in primarily two ways. First, by fully
excluding all calories from your diet, you allow your digestive system to switch off.
This means that your body can redirect the surplus energy normally consumed
through digestion towards cleansing and healing. Second, by excluding all calories
from your diet, you soon end up burning the energy from fat in a process called
ketosis. As the fuel provided by ketosis (so-called ketone bodies) is drawn out from fat
cells, toxins are also released. This happens because your body normally isolates and
stores toxins in fat cells, where they are less likely to interact with and cause damage
to other, more vulnerable cells in the body. As a result of being locked away, toxins
gradually accumulate over the course of your life until your fat cells eventually run
out of ‘storage space’. At this point, toxins begin to circulate freely through the body,
thereby increasing the risk of serious illness and disease. It’s only through the ketosis
of regular water fasting that you have the chance to eventually cleanse yourself from
these toxins.

Symptoms due to addiction and withdrawal

withdrawal symptoms. Here, again, reaching for a pill does nothing to address the

physical addiction.

Using natural techniques to alleviate

Even if the contents of a modern medicine cabinet are to be avoided when fasting,
I’m not suggesting that you should have to put up, shut up, and suffer while fasting.
Not at all! Instead, you can apply a variety of effective, natural techniques to the detox
symptoms associated with fasting. As the following sections describe, these take
place both before the fast, preventatively, as well as during the fast itself, if and when
any symptoms arise.
Preventative techniques: planning your
fasting environment
A little planning before the fast can go a long way to reducing symptoms during the
fast itself. As mentioned above, emotional factors invariably underlie physical
symptoms. This applies not only to how you relate to specific addictions, but also to
your general emotional state while fasting. Therefore, the more you can lower your
stress levels before and during fasting, the smoother the entire experience will be.

In this respect, perhaps the single most important factor is the environment in
which you’re fasting. In order to reduce the possibility of any unexpected last-minute
problems or changes – all of which are likely to increase stress – you should take care
in planning out your fasting environment well before the beginning of the fast. The
most important issues to consider are:
Rest:
Plan on resting and, if necessary, taking time off work during the fast.
No-one needs the stress of their job if it can be avoided. Also, working
through your fast expends energy which could otherwise be used for cleansing and
healing.
Social interaction:
Try to avoid an environment in which you’re forced into a lot of social
interaction. While fasting, your natural inner pace will slow down, and
this can create friction with the natural pace of those who are continuing their lives
as usual. The naturally high energy levels of children can be especially difficult to
bear – even if they’re your own children and you love them!
Beyond this, social interaction of any kind will almost certainly force you into
discussions about your fast. Given the fear most people feel towards water fasting,
you’ll end up having to bear the weight of their anxiety – if not their full resistance –
when instead you should be free to focus on the processes taking place in your body.

Quiet:
Try to find a quiet environment in which to fast, such as somewhere in
the countryside. For more sensitive people, the hustle and bustle of city

Hot packs and compresses:
Hot packs and compresses can be applied to specific points of the body
to relieve localised aches and pains. Heat therapy like this works well
regardless of the cause of the discomfort, which can range from (1) acid build-up in
the leg muscles to (2) sore, overworked liver and kidneys to (3) the inability of the
lymphatic system to keep up with cleansing (which can lead to an aching back or
pelvis). Used prudently, heat can also help to relieve headaches.
Technique. (1) You can use a dedicated hot pack: one containing cherry stones, gel
or any other material. These are most easily heated dry in a microwave oven, which
has the advantage that the hot pack neither drips nor feels clammy as it cools down.
(2) If you don’t own a dedicated hot pack it’s easy to improvise one. Take a hand
towel or perhaps a couple of face cloths, and fold them up so that they’ll cover the
relevant area on your body. The thicker the folds, the better your compress will retain
the heat. Then boil some water in a kettle and pour over the towel, so that it soaks
through. Carefully squeeze out the excess water and apply the towel. Covering the
compress with a dry towel helps to insulate the heat as well as to prevent dripping.
Mindfulness meditation:
Mindfulness meditation is useful against any detox symptom. Our
physical perception of discomfort is often influenced and exacerbated by
an emotional reaction against the discomfort, something like: I don’t like this feeling or:
I’m afraid the discomfort is going to get worse. Therefore, the more you can stay grounded
in the present moment and simply experience the symptom for what it is – rather
than what it is plus emotions of dislike or fear – the more bearable any symptom
becomes.
