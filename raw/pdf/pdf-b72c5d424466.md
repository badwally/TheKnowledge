Reddit Question Of The Day
"It’s taken years but I’m finally profitable, problem is I can’t really use the profits
for anything I want to get.
I make anywhere from 3k to 15k a month(profitable over the last 8 months). I
still work full time as an apprentice electrician and I enjoy the work just not the
commute or start time, but as long as I can make my entries not that big of a
deal.
My big problem is I want to move out and get a house but non of this income
can be used to improve my DTI for a mortgage due to the risk profile associated
with trading. Am I just stuck waiting until I can buy a house outright?
Also taxes, how do I compute my net with these kinds of profits? Is it taxed as
short or long term gains?
Thanks"
This was a question I found fascinating because so many gurus teach about
how to make money in trading but I’d argue that knowing what to do once you
actually make money is insanely important and doesn’t get talked about AT ALL.
I’m going to answer this in a more general manner that’s really applicable to
everyone.
First I want you to realize that trading is strictly a business. You have days
where you make a profit, days where you lose, and you might even have losing
months. What matters is what's left over at the end of the quarter and the end of
the year.
The longer the time horizon you look at, the more likely you are to succeed.
If you take one thing from this email it should be this, trading is an infinity
game, not a finite game. Let me explain…
Simon Sinek's concept of the "Infinite Game" is a framework he discusses in his
book "The Infinite Game". The concept is based on the distinction between two

types of games: finite and infinite.
In a finite game, like football or chess, the players are known, the rules are
fixed, and the endpoint is clear. The objective is to win. In an infinite game, like
business or politics, the players come and go, the rules are changeable, and
there is no defined endpoint. There are no winners or losers in an infinite game;
there is only ahead and behind.
The concept of the "Infinite Game" as applied to trading involves shifting
perspective from short-term wins and losses to long-term strategy and
resilience. In trading, a finite game approach might focus on individual trades or
short-term performance metrics, where traders aim to "win" by making a profit
on each transaction and beating the market or their competition in a given time
frame.
However, when applying Simon Sinek's infinite game mindset to trading, the
focus shifts to sustainability and adaptability over the long term. An "infinite
trader" would not obsess over the outcome of each trade but would be more
concerned with developing a robust trading strategy that can withstand market
volatility and evolve over time. Success is not measured by outperforming
others in the short term but by maintaining a resilient portfolio and achieving
personal financial goals over many years.
Infinite traders understand that the market is a complex, ever-changing system
with a multitude of participants, and there is no true "end" to the game. They are
less likely to chase trends or react emotionally to short-term market movements.
Instead, they prioritize:
Learning and Adaptability: Continuously learning from the market and
adjusting strategies as conditions change.
Risk Management: Not every trade will be profitable, but managing risk
effectively ensures that losses can be absorbed, and the trader can continue
participating in the market.
Long-Term Goals: Focusing on long-term financial objectives rather than the
excitement of short-term gains.
Emotional Discipline: Maintaining a level head and not allowing wins or losses

to dictate one's self-worth as a trader.
Strategic Vision: Having a clear understanding of why they are trading and
what they hope to achieve, beyond simply making money.
By playing the infinite game in trading, individuals can potentially avoid the
burnout and emotional rollercoaster associated with trying to win in a finite
sense. If you do this professionally to make a living, understanding this is
absolutely crucial.
Great, now that you understand that trading is a business, and it’s an infinity
game, you’re armed with the correct mindsets to move forward successfully.
Next, you need to have a strong fundamental mindset for personal finance. I
strongly suggest reading The Psychology of Money by Morgan Housel.
Some of my personal beliefs are this…
Pay Yourself Every Month: For example, at the end of the month, whatever
your profits were, pay yourself 50% of them.
Save 6 - 12 months of Expenses: Most finance gurus say to have 3 to 6
months' worth of living expenses saved in an emergency savings account. Now
keep in mind, that you do one of the riskiest jobs in the world (financially
speaking). So I’d say set aside 6 to 12 months worth.
80/20 Rule: 20% of the things you spend money on make up 80% of your
monthly expenses. Focus on the big stuff and ensure you’re not overspending
on those. Example: House, Car, Food, Insurance, etc.
