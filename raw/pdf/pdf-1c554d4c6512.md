You incorrectly assume that there was a “motion to have the $25,000 assessment brought forward as a
recommendation to the Town Hall”. There was no such motion. There were discussions with the authors
of the draft reports followed by memos as the work progressed.
As explained in our memo, the process is for the engineer to first to estimate the cash requirements
over the time period of the study, which involves professional judgement. We asked some questions
and provided clarifications of some of the information and history of the building, but the estimated
timing and cost of the work is from the engineers. They then prepare options for how that requirement
can be funded. There are any number of ways that can be done to comply with the Condominium Act
requirement that the fund must never become negative at any point in the study period (and that this
does not depend on a future vote for a special assessment). The board members advised the engineers
that we felt that the owners would prefer consistent fees, without year-to-year variations, but this was
not a formal resolution of the Board.
We also asked them to consider options with initial assessments with a view to keeping annual capital
contributions (and thus monthly condo fees) lower, and they responded with the supplemental cash
flows for an assessment of $20,000. They verbally told us that an assessment of about $25,000 would
permit the fund to be “immediately funded”, with no step-up change in contributions and only increases
of about CPI after that, but this was not presented as a calculation.
The minutes of the Board meetings are all posted on the Summer Gardens website.
