Dolby Audio in Applications Strategy
Executive Summary
Spring 2017
Key Takeaways:
• FY17 revenue on track for $1.9M.
a. Based on iQiyi agreement, there is a 3-year commitment of ~$2.8 M per year
from one account, with revenue scaling up from ~$1.3M in FY17. DAS value to
this account is a function of both improvement to the quality of experience and a
heavy emphasis on the Dolby brand.
b. iQiyi revenue was adversely impacted by iQiyi’s decision to turn the Dolby fuction
“OFF”.
• FY18 revenue will range form $2.8 to $4.3 depending on:
a. Closing Tencent and quickly implementing and scaling adoption of the solution
amongst their users.
b. Closing PPTV.
c. Closing Comcast.
d. Transitioning BT from royalty-free DAS3.0 to a royalty-bearing implementation of
DAS 4.0 with AC4 support for live Dolby Atmos.
• Overall 2021 opportunity for SVOD DAS is $45M/year based on estimates of 383M global
SVOD subscribers.
• DAS provides an entry-point for AC4 into the multiscreen market.
a. Services that instantiate an AC4 library to generate an overall improvement in
the quality of their mobile audio experience will be able to amortize the
investment in that library to HW-enabled endpoints that come online in the living
room and among ad hoc mobile OEMs (e.g. LG AC4 Smart TVs).
• As a software proposition, DAS provides the opportunity to measure and augment our
value to service providers, as well as prototype and test new avenues for value creation.
This includes:
a. Develop of content-segment specific rendering presets (e.g. “Sport Sound”,
“Music Sound”, etc) that would augment media intelligence.
b. integration of Dolby Audio for VR as a monetized incremental feature of the SDK.
c. Speculative developments like media analytics, content delivery optimization,
and behavioral analysis.
• The ideal path to market is through channel sales using system integrator and platform
partners like Ericsson and Ooyala.
Dolby Audio for Applications (DAS) presents the opportunity to grow revenue in the OTT
segment through licensing of Dolby rendering and playback technologies directly or indirectly
to service providers. FY17 Revenue for the program today is forecast for ~$1.9M across three
customers by EOY, with revenue disproportionately associated with one major Chinese OTT

service, iQiyi. Following the execution of iQiyi’s amended agreement, there will be a 3-year
committed revenue stream of ~$2.8M/year.
The opportunity is much larger. Digital TV Research forecasts OTT SVOD subscriptions to exceed
383 million subscriptions globally in 2021 and the total addressable market for SVOD to grow
from $11.13 BN in 2015 to a global total of $25.71BN in 2021. SVOD will therefore constitute
40% of the overall forecast OTT market value of $64.78BN. Assuming a market rate of
$0.01/subscriber/month, the 2021 opportunity for DAS nears $45M per year for SVOD
subscriptions alone. To generate significant returns in this space, Dolby must dedicate broader
engineering and product resources, implement a channel-sales model with partners more
accustomed to transactional service revenue, and augment core value in rendering of audio
and/or video streams in Dolby formats with broader codec support and more expansive utility
associated with data and analytics.
DAS facilitates the introduction of AC4 into the broader content marketplace. Based on the
core value of improved bit efficiency and expanded functionality, AC4 meets market
requirements for audio compression that responds to significant improvements in video
compression, encoding, and adaptive bit-rate delivery. Given OTT service provider appetite for
as-close-to-unified content prep and delivery workflows, DAS offers sufficient scale for AC4
adoption (in advance of broad OS or OEM penetration).
As a software-driven proposition, DAS also enables Dolby to augment our value proposition
to broadcasters, programmers, and content distributors as they respond to systemic shifts in
consumer expectations of unbundled video consumption models with an aggressive investment
in multiscreen content delivery. DAS offers specific utility to these services, enabling a
consistent improvement in quality of experience (QoE) across all mobile endpoints. Unlike
Dolby’s OEM licensing model, which in the absence of ubiquitous OEM adoption or full
penetration across the Android and iOS operating systems results in a fragmented addressable
feature set for services (and therefore requires services to incur unique workflow changes on a
per-device, per-service basis), DAS allows services to enhance QoE across all mobile endpoints
with a simple integration into the application stack.
DAS integration also immediately enables the rendering of a Dolby Atmos experience within
the application. Going forward, the product will also incorporate the capability to render virtual
reality experiences using the unique features associated with Dolby Audio for VR Applications
(DAVRA). As a software model, DAS also provides Dolby with a platform to extend our concept
of “experiences that transcend expectations” into a broader avenue of value creation; this may
include tangential segments such as video analytics, telemetry, content optimization, or
addressable advertising.
Coupled with the Dolby brand, DAS offers immediate and specific value to MVPD (i.e. “Pay
TV” and “TV Everywhere”), subscription VOD, transactional VOD, electronic-sell-thru, and
pay-per-view services. As connected devices such as Android STBs reduce cost through broad

use of components from the mobile supply chain, DAS may also offer broader utility beyond
smartphone and tablet devices.
Delivered to market using a channel model, DAS will also support efforts to grow the base of
Dolby Audio content and service utilization through attach to “TV-As-A-Service” offerings
from system integrators such as Cisco, IBM, and Ericsson as well as via integration into the
playback offerings from online video platform services such as Adobe Primetime, Ooyala, Pixel,
Brightcove, Neulion, etc. In concert with our Live Atmos content creation services, DAS also
augments the value Dolby can deliver to Sports and Live Events.
DAS also potentially serves as a loss-leader on DAX licensing. OEMs license based on services
delivering content to a device. Services enable a new codec based on the availability of
rendering devices. This so-called “chicken-egg” problem can be resolved, potentially, through
use of DAS. In this case, OEM(1) would license DAX based on a commitment from OPERATOR(A)
to address the OEM(1) devices at launch. If OEM(1) device footprint is too small to warrant
creation and delivery of a new audio library by OPERATOR(A), Dolby could offer the operator
use of DAS to justify the development and implementation of new codec support (i.e. AC4).
They would be able to deliver to ALL mobile endpoints, with OEM(1) supported in HW and the
remainder of mobile endpoints via DAS. As additional OEMs are engaged and licensed, they will
immediately benefit from the already-created Operator library. From an operator perspective,
there is a gradual shift from DAS to DAX-based decoding as new OEMs implement licensed AC4
support.
