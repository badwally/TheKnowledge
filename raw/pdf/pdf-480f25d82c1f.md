ANITA FINEBERG LL.B, CIPP/C
BARRISTER & SOLICITOR
77 Gothic Avenue
Toronto, Ontario
M6P 2V8
May 12, 2025
Hello,
I am the current co-owner of Summer Gardens Unit 1403, along with my sister-in-law
Sybil Shore Fineberg. We are the co-beneficiaries of the estate of my late mother --
Betty Fineberg - that includes the condo. We have recently become aware of the
proposed Special Assessment of $20,000.00 or $10,000.00, discussed at the
previous Town Hall meeting on April 15, 2025 and the upcoming meeting for
further discussion on May 14, 2025, as well as the related Reserve Fund Documents
posted on the owners tab of the condo website.
We understand that the Board has received questions and comments regarding this
proposed Special Assessment and have also had the opportunity to review some of
those communications. Having read the relevant materials we have a number of
comments:
How did the Board decide that owners might prefer capital contribution to larger
monthly fees?
There has been no decision of the Board. From interactions with owners we knew that
many were concerned that high monthly fees would suppress prices. In our discussions
with the engineers we asked them to consider scenarios that included a special
assessment to show how that might reduce monthly contriburtions to reserve fund over
time. These were provided.
No decision was made by the Board – there was no resolution. The Board minutes are
published and include all decisions of the Board.
• Given that that the engineers who did the Reserve Fund Study did not
recommend a Special Assessment, on what basis did the Board conclude that
owners might prefer the above? Are there no minutes from Board meetings that
document this decision and the one in bullet two below? (e.g., who proposed
the motion, who seconded it and the discussion that occurred prior to the
motion being proposed and voted on)
The engineers did not recommend any particular funding plan – their role is to certify
that the funding plan that is adopted complies with the requirements of the Act. Any of
the proposals under discussion will qualify and can be certified by the engineer.

• After the Board requested and the engineering firm did prepare options
3 and 4 to include a Special Assessment, why did the Board not explicitly
include the original Options 1 and 2 on the Ballot? It is not clear on the Ballot that
Option 1 from the Reserve Study is what is meant in the third bullet:
o If neither special assessment received two-thirds approval,
Reserve Fund Study funding plan will be implemented -- this option
should be expressly described so that owners clearly understand their
voting choices and can therefore make an informed decision.
Condominium law in Nova Scotia is different from that in Ontario. The decision for
owners is whether or not to have an assessment. If the assessment option is not
approved, the no-assessment contribution schedule will take effect without any action
by the owners. In any case, a very detailed explanation of all the reserve fund
contribution schedules, and later a second explanation including forecasts of total
monthly fees including the contributions was provided.
This is a critical point because this option balances the interests of current
condo owners and future owners so that the operating and capital costs are shared
between current owners and those who benefit in the future. Moreover, Option 1 reflects
a change in monthly condominium fee increases that are very common, and very
importantly, does not decrease the market value of sales of condominium units that are
subject to large Special Assessments, as is the case proposed here.
There is no inappropriate sharing between present and future owners. The
Condominium Act requires that the Reserve Fund collect in advance for future capital
costs.
Increases of monthly fee of this magnitude – 80.8% of the capital contribution portion;
20.2% of total fee - are not common here. The owners can decide if this is acceptable or
if they would prefer to pay a capital contribution that will reduce it.
The Board minutes are a necessary component of transparency and accountability to
unit owners to allow them to make an informed decision, as on the present matter.
In addition each Option requires a 66 2/3 majority independent vote.
Only the assessment options require two-thirds support
Details of Option 1
Another critical piece of information to allow unit owners to make an informed decision,
is to clearly provide them with the proposed increases in monthly condominium fees.
They must clearly understand what the year over year increases would be over the ten-
year period to compare dollars with dollars.

We provided this – forecasts of future operating costs, plus the calculated contributions
were provided. The estimate of future costs was based on a simple assumption of CPI
increases of 2.5% - not precise, but it is common across all the options so it does not
affect the comparisons.
Furthermore, in my opinion it is not appropriate and it does not engender unit owners’
trust and confidence in the Board’s decision-making, as captured in the April 15th
memo, to say Owners had a choice to “Pay me now or pay me (more) later.” I am a
condominium owner myself and it is a big difference to pay a lump sum of $20,000.00
(in this case by the end of December 2025), as opposed to smaller increases in monthly
fees. This is especially true for the number of seniors on fixed incomes who live in
Summer Gardens.
The statement that more will be paid without an assessment is correct. Owners would
pay about $840,000 less over the 12 years until the north podium project is assumed to
be completed under the option with a $20K assessment (including the assessment)
than under the no-assessment model.
Conduct of the Town Hall Meetings with Owners
• Given that there was no agenda for the April 15th meeting, nor one proposed for
the meeting on May 14th, unit owners are confused and uncertain with respect to
the process that will occur to take the Special Assessment matter to obtain unit
owners’ approval.
The purpose of the two Town Halls is to provide information to owners, allow
them to ask questions, and provide an opportunity to express their views to other
owners. There is no agenda other than that. No decisions will be made; no
resolutions will be adopted.
• This is especially problematic as the Ballot provides an email address to send it to,
but does not note when and how the “official owners’ approval” will take place.
Some owners may have already sent in their completed ballots which, for the
many reasons we and other owners have expressed, may well not be valid and
create more confusion and uncertainty among unit owners.
You may not be familiar with the process in Nova Scotia. The approval requires
support of the necessary supermajority of owners – not just those who attend a
meeting. This is done by collection of “ballots” from all owners until either two-
thirds approvals or one-third refusals are received. If neither is received within
about a month (there is no legal limit, though) the proposal will be abandoned.
We must know the outcome before we set fees to go into effect on July 1.
• According, the description of a “ballot” is clearly a misnomer. Unit owners must
approve special assessments and whether the term “consent” or “approve “as is

the language in the Condominium Act is used, there is no doubt that it needs to
be informed.
• If, as noted in the purpose of the Communications to Owners set out in advance of
the April 15th Town Hall and the upcoming May 14th Town Hall, is to provide unit
owners with an explanation of the financial options available for the financing of
the reserve fund, then it is more than reasonable to not provide any “consent” or
“approval” document to owners until all questions/concerns related to the options
have been addressed.
Agreement with Comments Made and Questions Posed by Other Units Owners
• As mentioned, we have reviewed a number of communications sent to both the Board
and unit owners expressing their concerns about the Special Assessment:
• We are in agreement with the concerns and opinions expressed in all of these
communications.
Based on my experience as a condominium owner (the Ontario and Nova Scotia
Condominium legislation is very similar) I am quite frankly concerned and surprised as
to how the Board has managed the Special Assessment process at Summer Gardens.
While they are generally similar, you should be aware that there are many differences
between the Condominium Act of Ontario and that of Nova Scotia. In Ontario the
assessment is made by the Board; and that is presumably why the Board of your
Ontario condo engaged in extensive communication as they made that decision. I note
that you did not indicate that there was any vote by owners.
Further, the process you describe was for a specific project, not for a Reserve Fund
Study.
In Nova Scotia the Board does not make the decision - a special assessment can be
made only with consent of owners of two-thirds of the common interest. This process is
commonly called a “vote” and you are correct that calling the consent document a
“ballot” is not technically correct. The Board of Summer Gardens has made no decision,
but has put the question forward to ask the owners whether they wish to have an
assessment – whether they consent to an assessment. We have placed all the
engineers’ study information before the owners, and have prepared explanatory memos
and charts. We think they are accurate and fairly present the options, and none of the
commentators has identified any errors.
Our Condominium Corporation just underwent a special assessment for the building
envelope project. The Corporation consists of two high-rise towers both built in 1977:
one has eight stories and 105 units, the other 18 stories and 225 units. Unit owners
were kept apprised of all steps in the project:

• from the retention of the engineering firm to conduct the reserve fund study;
• the iterative discussions between the Board and the engineers vis a vis costing,
options etc.;
• the basis of the Board’s decision to accept the recommendation of the engineers’
report; and
• how the Board decided to implement the special assessment by deciding on owner
payment of two different installments six months apart based on monthly
condominium fees.
There are three very important points based on how this process worked:
• all unit owners received detailed Board minutes of every meeting, including
motions proposed and seconded and by whom, as well as all matters discussed
and decisions made;
As noted above, the Board has made no decision – the decision will be made by the
owners. Any assessment will be made only with support of two-thirds of the ownership.
• the "shovels were already in the ground" when the Special Assessment
was levied for the building envelope project -the most recent Board minutes
addressing the special assessment included an update of the status of the
project, as have all Board minutes on this matter; and
As noted, your situation was very different from ours. Apparently your reserve fund was
not sufficient to cover the building envelope project when it became necessary. We are
not in that situation - we do not need the money for immediate work; the study assumes
that it wll be needed in ten years, but it might be sooner. The Condominium Act requires
us to “save up” for anticipated future costs, so our reserve fund contributions will always
have to be made before the work is to be done.The only question is the rate of saving.
Some owners prefer a capital contribution followed by smaller monthly contributions –
others prefer higher monthly contributions with no assessment. If an assessment is not
supported by two-thirds, there will be no assessment and the higher monthly fees will
apply.
• as a result, we did not have to wait 10 years to see how our Special Assessment
investment in our homes was being spent
