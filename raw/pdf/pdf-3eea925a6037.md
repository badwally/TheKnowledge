Intended Use
Flow cytometry is optimally used to immunophenotypically characterize abnormal populations when they are detected. Negative flow cytometry results do not
exclude lymphoma or neoplasia. Possible false negative flow cytometry results may occur in, but are not limited to, the following: neoplastic cells in Hodgkin
lymphoma are not typically adequately represented by routine clinical flow cytometry; neoplastic cells may be lost or inadequately represented due to
degeneration, sample processing, sampling artifact, or patchy involvement; plasma cells are typically underrepresented by flow cytometry; immature cells/blasts
may be underrepresented due to hemodilution; myeloproliferative disorders and low grade myelodysplasia may not have immunophenotypic abnormalities or
increased blasts. Correlation with all available clinical, laboratory, and morphologic data is always necessary to assess for the possibility of false negative flow
cytometry results and to establish a diagnosis.
Each marker in this analysis was used to assess for potential antigenic abnormalities or to evaluate detected abnormalities.
