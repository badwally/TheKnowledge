Core Content Month 5
Defining Institutional Swing Points
Institutional swing points can be identified by two forms: stop runs and failure
swings.
Stop runs happen when the market makes a higher high, then fails and breaks
down, causing a rejection at the highs.
The most significant price pattern to learn conceptually is the stop run.
Failure swings happen when the market makes a swing low with a higher candle to
the left and right of it.
Institutional trading creates two types of swing points.
Stop runs can be anticipated when price is hovering just below a key institutional
reference point.
When traders understand order blocks, they can avoid getting caught by the market.
Breaker pattern: a swing point that offers outstanding entry points, but it can be
scary due to the fear of reversal.
Knowing the institutional reference points (order blocks, breakers, mitigation blocks,
liquidity voids, and fair value gaps) helps anticipate these patterns unfolding.
Once the pattern breaks down, it becomes a trigger to sell or buy.
The bearish swing point with a bearish breaker is an example of a trigger point, and
when the high is broken, that becomes the trigger point.
The stop run happens when the pattern is already stopped out, knocking out the
traders, and it is an advantage to sellers.
The dropping off lulls traders into believing they are short, and it gives confidence to
sell.
The pattern is the highest and most probable condition to be a short seller in the
marketplace because it has a built-in advantage.

Institutional order flow on higher time frames helps in looking at the effects of these
patterns.
There are two conceptual ways to view the marketplace in terms of swing points:
the breaker swing point and the failure swing.
The breaker swing point is a false break above an old high rejection that quickly
moves away, and it's the best pattern in market structure to anticipate institutional
evidences.
The failure swing is when there is a resistance level, the market trades through it,
comes back and retests it, but it can't make another pass through.
We can't know with great conviction if we're going to get the breaker setup, so while
we're waiting for it to occur, we could be anticipating the failure swing.
We aim to get breaker swing points, but if we don't get them, we can still trade off
the failure swing pattern.
Market participants are trapped in positions based on net long and net short orders.
If those trapped are short, the price will be repriced higher to force them to buy back
at a higher price so they can scale off their position at a net profit.
If those trapped are long, smart money will sell, and they will want to collapse their
trade by selling at a lower price, avoiding selling at the point where smart money
can buy it back.
There are two institutional swing point theories: breaker and failure swing.
Breaker is the optimal trade entry pattern, as it is the deepest discount buy and the
most premium sell. It is scary but encourages confidence building.
In a demo account, traders can practice stepping in front of the marketplace to see
what happens to the price.
Breaker and failure swing are the only two conditions in the marketplace.
There are only two ways to sell: sell at a high or sell on a retracement back to a
break in market structure.
There are only two ways to buy: buy at a low or buy on a return or retracement back
to a market structure break.
