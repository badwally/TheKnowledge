Memo to Vaughn Keeble:
Comments on the Reserve Fund Study, Draft 2:
1. Number of units At Page ii and Page 1 you note that the building has 123 suites.
This is correct, but it must be understood that the building has 123 equivalent
suites. There are actually 118 “doors”- separately occupied units - but four of
these are double units and two are ~1.5 units (three units made into two).
Summer Gardens is unusual in that all equivalent units pay the same condo fee
and have the same common interest. To derive each unit’s fee, divide the total
cost by 123, and then double that fee for the four double units and multiply by
the appropriate factor (not exactly 1.5) for the “three-into-two” units. In our
mind, the average per unit is 1/123 of the total, although five units pay more.
2. Average cost per unit In each of the cash flow tables you include a calculation of
the average annual reserve fund contribution per unit. Beginning in 2025, you
note that the current annual contribution is $320,000, which is presented as an
average cost per unit of $3,160.80. This appears to assume that there are 103 units
(??) The calculation based on 123 equivalent units is $2,601.63, which is paid at
$216.80 in the monthly fees.
We're not sure how that calculation came about, but it is continued through each
future year, overstating the average annual contribution. However, it does not
change the analysis - the percentage increases shown are accurate.
Our calculations show that Flow 1 requires an increase of $147.45 per equivalent
unit per month in year 1, followed by increases of about $10 per month in
subsequent years. Flow 2 would mean an increase of $411.06 per equivalent unit
per year ($34.25 per month) in 2026 gradually rising to $739.15 per year ($61.60
per month) in 2030.
3. Special Assessment: Each figure and table includes revenue of a special assessment
of $246,000 in 2025. In fact, that levy was made and paid in June 2024 and was
included in the 2024 year-end Reserve Fund balance on the audited statements.
Did you take the opening balance for 2025 from those statements? If so, the
assessment may have been double counted. On the other hand, it does not
appear that the energy-related capital projects now being installed by PMC are
included in the 2025 year – the report has RF expenses of $505,380 and these
contacts alone exceed that amount. Can you confirm that the assessment revenue
and project expenses are properly accounted for in the 2025 year? If not, there
will be a significant impact.
4. Timing of biggest project We accept your listing of the capital work to be done and
your estimates of the cost. We note, however, that the biggest item - the
renovation of the membrane on the north podium - has been moved out by nine
years from 2027- 2028 to 2036-203 seven. This has a profound effect on the cash
flow.

The discussion of this is tentative. In section 1.4 you say:
“We have budgeted for the eventual rewaterproofing of all garage roof
slab areas. The timing and phasing of these projects will be based on the
long term waterproofing performance and tolerance to leakage this should
be further considered as part of the recommended garage evaluations and
future updates to this study.”
What we understand from this is that you consider it a reasonable engineering
assumption to place these projects that far forward and to base the reserve fund
plan on that assumption. However, we must recognize that with further
investigation or further developments – “performance or tolerance to leaks” -
this project may have to be advanced. If so, a special assessment will be needed.
That, of course, is true of every other item in the plan, but this one is so large and
the assumption so consequential for the cash requirements that should be made
clear. We will discuss it clearly in our meeting with owners
5. Windows: Page A 11 notes “Several sliding units have been upgraded with new
operable awning type sashes with IGUs.” This is incorrect. The two units that
you saw in the lobby were samples provided by potential suppliers. They were
not liked by our owners and none of them have been installed. The current plan
is to renovate the four-sash single-glazed units with replacement of the fibrous
weatherstripping. We believe that this is the amount that you have included in
your 2026 year.
6. Cash Flow Options You have presented two options to maintain the fund’s
solvency through the study period – one with a 72.6% increase in Year 1 (2026)
followed by modest increases (2.5%) each subsequent year and, alternatively,
with consistent 15.8% increases through the study period.
Our sense is that our owners would prefer year-to-year stability; they would not
want large increases year to year, even if they are consistent. While we have not
yet consulted with them, our sense is that they will tend to prefer Flow 1, and
they might even prefer a scenario where there is an up-front levy and lower, levelized,
annual or monthly payments over the life of the plan. This is in part because of the
widely-held feeling that large monthly condo fees will be a negative factor when
a unit is to be sold. I think we expressed this in our meeting with you. Can we
discuss whether and how a third such flow could be developed to present to our
owners?
7. It is probably not important, but it would be more correct to show your legal
name - Sense Engineering (Atlantic) Limited - on the cover sheet. (It now says
that the report was prepared by “168 Hobsons Lake Drive, Suite 300”.)
Thank you for this detailed and well-explained report. We believe it will give our
owners confidence in our building and that it is being well managed.
