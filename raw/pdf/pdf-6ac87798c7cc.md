Recap and Plan: last week ES opened with a violent gap down, which saw continuation to the bottom of the 4850 LVN
discussed last week. Then, the tariff reprieve news started. I traded it well through a mix of automated and discretionary
actions, ultimately getting a bit lucky on a couple of exits, and of course missing a few others. After the first hint of a 90
day pause faded, price action continued to bounce between 4900 and 5250, until the real news came in of the 90 day
pause on Wednesday. Then, reality seemed to set in that 145% (or whatever we’re at now) on Chinese imports still kind
of stings. On Friday consumer sentiment plunged, but ES squeezed out a modest gain on the week, closing at the
midpoint of the prior week’s range at ~5400, when the majority of the tariff-driven selling began. That puts ES in a rather
critical junction. This week, I’ll be using 5400 as my pivot, following my usual plan of using the weekly futures close.
Narrative: tariffs, deals, reprieve, rumors, deals fading, rate cuts, inflation, uncertainty, etc. Earnings are starting too,
which might enter the mix. I’m not even going to bother to split these into bullish/bearish categories.
Zooming Out: On the year to date chart, ES looks like shit. On the one year chart, ES looks like shit. This move makes
last August, which sent the VIX to record levels, seem rather ordinary. On both, the lows put in last week look to be
excessive, and with news of tariff reprieve on computers/semi-conductors from China, I expect to see ES retesting higher
prices in the near term. Failure to do so - in particular failure to get above the 20 DMA / 5600 bear shelf, is something that
I will interpret bearishly. Chop around the 5400 LVN could be expected, as well as another look below 5300 (or farther),
especially if the above narratives see bearish developments into next week.

Cyclical Analysis: ES has put in an above median move to the upside for ≥1.3% cycles, and has just barely initiated a
new ≥5% up cycle. This looks bullish to me, but with a likely retest of some lower prices first. R:R favors the bears by
~2-1 in the short term, with expected moves of 37/77 pts up/down. R:R favors the bulls by ~20:1 in the long term, with
expected moves of ~450 and ~20 pts on either side. The latter is so small because it incorporates the very low (5%) of
cycles that only put in 5% to the upside. It’s not to say that >20 pts to to the downside should not be expected in this
volatile market. Events with 5% probability can and do happen, without so much as even being considered outliers.
