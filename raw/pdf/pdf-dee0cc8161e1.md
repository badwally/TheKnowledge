STOCK TANKS
A DIY Cold Plunge typically starts with a stock tank. I use and prefer the Rubbermaid 100 gallon
plastic tank. I’m 5’10” 190 lb. and it is a comfortable size. It also comes in a 150 gallon version. Not sure
which one is best? Go to a Tractor Supply (or wherever they sell them) and climb in one. I’ve done it
many times.
The reason for my recommendation on these is that the galvanized steel tanks vary in size by up to 4.5”
and have many seams/seals that are prone to leaking. The Rubbermaids are universal and have little
potential for leaking. They also play well with ozone sanitation if that is the route you choose to go.
Note: I have used both the Rubbermaid & Galvanized Steel Tanks.
RUBBERMAID 100 GAL. RUBBERMAID 150 GAL.
CHILLERS
I own and have used both a Penguin 1/2 HP HE and an Active Aqua 1/4 HP Chiller. My preference is
the Penguin. It obviously has more cooling power, and you can get an Active Aqua in 1/2 HP... but the
Penguin takes up way less space overall and you can use standard SCH 40 PVC fittings to outfit your
cold plunge.

OZONE GENERATOR
Water sanitation was a big question mark early on in my DIY Cold Plunge journey. I wanted to avoid the
use of harsh chemicals as much as possible, but I had no idea where to start. Then, one of my friends
ordered a “Plunge” which allowed me to get up close and personal with the exact parts they used -
which happened to be this exact ozone generator.
The Ambohr Spa-124 has been fantastic for me. It comes with a US plug and all of the misc. parts
needed to hook it up to your filtration system (see the next page of this PDF). As I write this, I am over 70
days with no water changes and it is still crystal clear.
Note: I sell this ozone generator on PlungeBuddy.com and THEDIYCOLDPLUNGE.com... and they sell

IN-LINE WHOLE HOUSE FILTER
There are several options when it comes to choosing the filter for your cold plunge build. I recommend the
GE Whole House Filter pictured in Example 1 below. It has a built in shutoff and bypass valve which makes
controlling the water flow easy. With it, you will be able to change the filter without shutting off your pump
or draining your plunge.
I also prefer a filter with a clear housing to see how much debris is in the filter at any given time. If those
factors aren’t important to your build, there are many other options available. Make sure the one you choose
is a 10” water filter and has threaded 3/4” inlet and outlet holes (standard in most). Examples 2, 3 & 4 link out
to other options. There are many more available with a quick search on Amazon, Google, Home Depot, etc.
Note: You will need to build a sanitation system to use these filters. I offer a complete DIY Step-by-Step
guide on how to do just that and pair this filter with ozone sanitation.

MICRON SEDIMENT FILTER
Part of keeping water clean in your cold plunge is starting with clean water. I recommend the filter
below for the initial fill. It attaches to your hose and will ensure you start with clean water. Make sure
you run the hose for a few seconds before filling your tub, there will be sediment that needs to clear
this filter before you can use it.
Note: One of these filters should last you a long time. At this rate, I will only need to replace the water
in my plunge about 4 times per year. These filters are meant to last much longer than that.
