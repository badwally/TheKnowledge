Summer Gardens
2025 Reserve Fund Study – Financial Options
Thank you to all those who attended the Town Hall meeting, either in person or
through the Zoom. As we promised at that meeting, this memo provides further detail
and explanation of the financing options. The attached tables show the forecast monthly
condo fees with and without assessments.
The Board must select among the funding options provided by the engineer, any special
assessment must be approved by a vote of owners, and you will be asked to vote by
ballot whether or not we will have an assessment.
Forecasting operating condo fees – assumption about operating costs
Our condominium fees cover both our share of operating expenses and our contribution
to the Reserve Fund in accordance with the Reserve Fund Study. Our total annual
contribution to the Reserve Fund is $320,000, which is $216.80 per unit1 per month.
Operating Expense $648.20
Reserve Fund 216.80
Total monthly condominium fee $865.00
The operating portion will likely continue to grow with inflation, but because the new
study assumes that some items that we now include in operations will be paid from
Reserve Fund, there may be little change in operating cost for 2026.
Assumption: The calculations below assume that operating expenses for 2026 will
be the same as 2025. After that, operating expense will increase by 2.5% per year.
This assumption applies to all options, so it should not affect the comparisons.
Options for the Reserve Fund portion of condo fees
The engineers who did our reserve fund study presented two financial options that
would comply with the requirement that the fund stay positive over the full life of the
study – one that they call “immediately funded” and one that is “gradually funded.
• Option 1: The “immediately funded” plan raises contributions the first year by
80.8%, to the level that will keep the fund without deficit with increases in
subsequent years only to keep pace with inflation (2.5%, in their assumption).
• Option 2: The “gradual” plan phases in the increase over several years at 17%
per year, giving a smaller increase in the first year but larger dollar increases in
each subsequent year.
Because the Board felt that our owners might prefer capital contribution to larger
monthly fees, we asked the engineers to prepare additional scenarios in which there is
an immediate special assessment to “flatten the curve” of monthly fees. They provided
calculations of the fees under both the “immediately funded” and “gradually funded”
options with an assessment of $20,000.

• Option 3 (or 1SA2): “Immediately funded”, with initial assessment of $20,000 –
17.8 % increase first year; 2.5% (on lower base) in subsequent years.
• Option 4 (or 2SA): “Gradually funded”, with an initial assessment of $20,000 –
increases of 6% per year.
Further, at the meeting we were asked about scenarios with different levels of initial
assessment. The calculations below assume that the Reserve Fund contribution with an
assessment of $10,000 would be midway between the amounts under the $20,000
assessment and no assessment options. We do not have access to the spreadsheet used by the
engineers to do the calculations and cannot assure you that this is mathematically accurate, but
it is useful for illustrative purposes.
• Option 5: “Immediately funded” with an initial assessment of $10,000 (midpoint)
• Option 6: “Gradually funded”, with an initial assessment of $10,000 (midpoint)
Note that the Fund balance at the end of the five-year term is quite different under the
different options. This affects the required fees in the next Study period3. The higher
that balance, the lower the fees in the next five-year term under the new Study.
In the attached tables, the beginning operating expense is the 2025 operating budget,
inflated by 2.5% in each subsequent year. The “Total Capital/year” is the total annual
contribution to Reserve Fund as calculated by the engineers under Options 1, 2, 3, and
4, and the midpoint between them for Options 5 and 6. For each Option the table shows
the “Capital/unit/month” - the monthly per-unit amount; and the “Condo fee/month”
– the sum of the monthly Reserve Fund amount and the forecast monthly operating
expense (shown at the top of the column).
The first page of the table shows the first five years. The second page assumes the new
study in five years continues in the same way. The last two columns, for 2036 and 2037,
show the years when the North Podium project is assumed to take place, and each
Option has about the same balance after those large expense years.
We can’t calculate the balance of the account under Option 5 or 6, but the differences
between Option 1 and Option 3, or between Option 2 and Option 4 are significant.
For greater detail about the expenses and financial plans you can review the full draft
Reserve Fund Study, on the Summer Gardens website. It is in the Owners’ section
under Reserve Fund Study.
