Memo to owners:
Summer Gardens
Reserve Fund Study
We will hold a Town Hall meeting on Tuesday, April 15 at 7:00 pm to discuss the new
Reserve Fund Study and our options to fund it.
This is not a formal meeting of owners and no business will be done. However, this is
an important subject for all of us and we urge you to attend if possible.
Reserve Fund Studies and Contributions to the Reserve Fund
The Condominium Act requires us to have a Reserve Fund to pay for capital expenses -
repairs or renewals, and not just maintenance - of the common elements. Owners must
contribute to the fund in accordance with a Reserve Fund Study [“Study”] done by a
qualified engineer. The Study estimates the amounts that will be required in each year,
looking forward at least 20 years, and calculates the necessary contributions to pay for
them so that the fund always has enough money to cover the forecasted expenses. The
condominium corporation must collect – and the owners must pay – the contributions
set out in the Study. In effect, we are legally required to save for expenses before they
arise. The Study must be re-done every five years.
We are now contributing in accordance with our last study, done in 2021, and we are
well-funded in accordance with that study. However, because our building is at an age
where many systems must be renewed, and because there have been significant
changes in construction costs in recent years, we are redoing our reserve fund study one
year early. We have hired Sense Engineering; a firm that has considerable experience is
preparing reserve fund studies for condominiums.
With this memo you will receive a copy of the main part of their Study report. The full
report, with the detailed appendices, is posted on the Summer Gardens website. In this
memo we provide a very high-level summary of the results and the options before us.
The Reserve Fund Study involves two steps:
1. Estimate the cash requirement in each year for capital repairs, looking forward
twenty years. There is little flexibility here, although there is much engineering
judgment as to the timing and cost of the items of work. The costs can only be
estimates, but the engineers have experience with construction costs and cost
inflation and have included contingency allowances. Ultimately, the engineers
have to certify that, in their professional opinion, the projected contributions are
reasonable to cover all necessary capital work in the study period.
2. Set out a plan of contributions to the Fund to ensure that it never shows a
negative balance. The Study must show that, after considering those
contributions, interest earned, and capital expenses drawn out, the Fund is
always solvent. There is some flexibility in how this can be done, with different
payment patterns, ranging from an initial special assessment from owners to a
pattern of increases in annual contributions (and therefore in monthly
condominium fees), or by some combination of assessments and fees.

Projection of future costs.
Our engineers have provided a detailed review of all parts of our building with the
description of any visible deterioration and a discussion of when renovation or
replacement will be needed for each. The Executive Summary of the Study sets out the
main items of expense in each of the five years of the study1.
For 2026 it foresees these major items (in addition to ongoing items and studies):
• Repairs to balconies - we have identified deterioration in many concrete balconies,
including some where the re-bar has been exposed by erosion.
• Renovation of the slider windows
• Replacement of the main fire alarm panel
For 2027 it includes:
• The beginning of a major restoration of our building envelope – we have ongoing
problems with water entry.
• Replacement of our main fire pump and the fire suppression system in the basement.
• Replacement of the ventilation fans and the gas detection system in the basement.
There are similar lists in the following years.
Note that the report does not include the renovation of the North Podium membrane in
the years of the Study. The engineers determined that this can reasonably be deferred
for up to ten years. They note, however, that this will involve additional maintenance
expense, and ongoing inspections may show that it will have to be moved forward. The
funding plans provide different balances at the end of the five year Study period, as we
then move into the next Study period, including the North Podium project.
The Funding Plan
To evaluate the alternatives, remember that our contributions to the Reserve Fund are
included in our monthly condo fees. For 2025 our total contribution is $320,000, or $217
per unit2 per month, with other costs representing about $638 per month. Under the
proposed funding plans, this amount will increase, but at very different rates.
The engineers have provided three basic analyses. The first one - “status quo”- shows
the existing contributions with 2.5% increases each year: it goes significantly negative in
five years, indicating the need for an assessment that year and very high contributions
thereafter – this plan is not acceptable, and is included only as a comparison baseline.
1 The years refer to our fiscal years; “2026” means our fiscal year from July 1 2025 to June 30 2026.
2 All monthly amounts in this memo are for a standard unit. Five units are double or 1.5 units.

They then provide two potential alternatives:
• Immediately Adequately Funded – this plan calls for an increase of 81% in the first
year, with annual increases of 2.5% after that – beginning at $392 in the first year
and rising to $433 in the fifth.
• Gradual Funding Increase – this plan calls for annual increases of 17% each year,
beginning at $254 in the first year and rising to $475 by the fifth year. While this
meets the requirement of staying positive, it leaves a much lower balance going
into the next Reserve Fund Study, and the likelihood of large increases then.
Each of these alternatives probably results in monthly fees greater than $1,000 –
“Immediately” in year 1, “Gradual” in about year three.
At our request, the engineers prepared two additional options incorporating a special
assessment of $20,000 per unit in Year 1 – one based on the “immediately” model and
one based on the “gradual” model.
• “Immediately” requires a first-year increase of 17.8% and annual increases of
2.5% after that. ($255 in 2026, rising to $282 by 2030)
• “Gradual” requires annual increases of 15% in each year. ($249 in 2025 rising to
$436 by 2030)
Without doing a full run, they estimate that an assessment of about $25,000 per unit
would permit us to continue with increases of only 2.5% per year through the study
period. (Same as “status quo” scenario - $222 in 2026 rising to $245 by 2030.)
Each of these plans produces a different balance at the end of the five years, which will
affect our contributions in the next study. We have the choice: “Pay me now or pay me
(more) later”.
If there is to be a special assessment, it will have to be approved an 80% vote of owners.
If there is interest in this option the Board will conduct a vote. This would probably
provide that the amount is payable on June 30, 2025, with interest at 8% after Dec 31,
2025 (or some similar formula).
If an assessment is not approved by the owners, we will be required to implement one
of the other contribution schedules with the higher monthly fees. At the meeting we
will answer any questions from owners and invite comments to the Board on the
preferred approach.
As always, Board members are glad to discuss this with any owner.
