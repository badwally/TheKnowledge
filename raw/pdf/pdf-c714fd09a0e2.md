(1) Introduction and overview
(1.1) Introduction
Congratulations! You’ve decided to do something that ninety-nine
percent of the world’s population has never experienced: a three-day
water fast. Although humankind has been fasting for literally thousands of years,
fasting has all but died out over the last 150 years. This is partly because, for the first
time in our history, modern consumer society has been able to supply an
inexhaustible supply of food, 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, and partly because
traditional healing modalities have been supplanted by a modern, drug-based health
system.
Quite literally, we’ve forgotten how to fast.
In recent years, though, the world has begun to recognise that, despite its
benefits, modern consumer society is slowly destroying our planet. In the same way,
we’re also beginning to recognise that, despite its advantages, our modern, drug-
based health system lacks answers to the most important medical issues of our time:
from cancer to the common cold. In fact, taken to excess, drugs can end up impacting
on our inner bodily environment with no less devastation than the way that consumer
society impacts on the environment around us.

(1.2) What exactly is a three-day water fast?
In this fasting program, a three-day water fast refers to the voluntary
abstinence from all food and calorific drink for three days. This means
drinking only water and, under certain circumstances, zero-calorie herbal teas (see
3.5). In terms of the duration of the fast, it means fasting from between 2.5 to 3.5 days,
or in other words 60-84 hours (see 3.2).
(1.3) Ketosis and the goals of a three-day water fast
In contemplating the idea of something which ninety-nine percent of the
world’s population has never experienced, you probably already have
your own personal goals for practising a three-day water fast. Whatever these may
be, a very definite biological goal underlies everything else, and it’s the reason that
the three-day water fast exists as a concept in the first place.
The primary goal of the three-day water fast is to experience ketosis: your fat-
burning metabolism which comes to life after fasting for more than about 24-36
hours.
Unless you otherwise follow a strict low-carb diet, your everyday metabolism
normally draws on the energy of carbohydrates, through both (1) freshly eaten

(1.4) Benefits of a three-day water fast
(1.4a) Cleansing, detox, healing
The first benefits of a three-day water fast begin to kick in already a few
hours after you stop eating. When actively working, your digestive system normally
consumes about 20-30 percent of your entire energy. Yes, a quarter to a third of all the calories
you ever burn goes towards nothing more than keeping your digestion going. That’s a huge
amount of energy, caused both by the chemical synthesis of complex digestive
enzymes as well as by the physical processes taking place inside your stomach and
intestines. In contrast, a fast offers your digestive system a complete break, thereby
freeing up this huge amount of otherwise indisposed energy. Your body wastes no
time, turning it to good use in order to power a basic degree of cleansing and detox,
helping your body to expel the daily toxins you inhale and ingest.
The energy freed up by not eating is the first kind of healing which takes place
during a fast.
You experience a limited degree of this in everyday life while sleeping each
night, but such healing lasts only until you ‘break’ your nightly ‘fast’ with ‘break-fast’
the following morning. So-called ‘intermittent fasting’ (I.F.) can help to extend the
healing for a few more hours, but a three-day water fast obviously provides much
more time for the cleansing and detox to go deeper. It also gives a significant amount
of time for your entire digestive system to rest and recuperate from its usually
relentless work schedule.

The second kind of healing takes place through ketosis itself.
While eating a normal diet, your body simply doesn’t have the energy and

with and damaging the cells of your body, as well as from (2) interfering with the

doesn’t usually reach full strength until at least the third day of fasting (see 1.3). But
it’s equally true that ketosis and the healing caused by ketosis begin already on the
second day of any three-day water fast.

(1.4c) A stronger immune system
One of the clearest benefits of fasting is the effect it has on the immune
system. Studies have repeatedly shown that, when exposed to pathogens during or
after a fast, the probability of becoming ill is significantly reduced. This applies to
anything from the common cold to the flu, and even to deadly disease such as
tuberculosis and bubonic plague.
Specifically, a three-day water fast is often enough to ward off the bacteria and
viruses which cause the flu and other common maladies.
In general, the more adapted you are physically to fasting and the more you
feel at home with it emotionally, the more effective fasting is in combating illness.
When I myself first started fasting, for instance, the success of water fasts against the
flu was rather hit and miss. Over the years, though, it has become increasingly
effective, to the point that nowadays a 24-48 hour fast almost invariably restores me
to full health, so long as I start fasting the moment I begin to feel a virus stirring inside
me.

(2) Who should fast and who should not
(2.1) Is it safe for me to fast?
The short answer to this question is almost certainly a very emphatic yes!
Humans have been fasting for many thousands of years. More generally,
thanks to our ketosis-based metabolism, we are actually designed to survive and even
thrive during periods of famine. Given the fact that periods of scarcity have always
recurred through history, this means that we’ve been fasting ever since homo sapiens
appeared on the African savannah several hundreds of thousands of years ago – and,
before that, for literally millions of years through our more distant hominid ancestors.
If fasting were dangerous, evolution would certainly have weeded out those of
us who were incapable of doing so.

(2.2) Who should not fast?
(2.2a) Physical reasons
When approached sensibly, fasting is not only safe but also highly
beneficial to health for well over ninety-nine percent of the world’s population.
Nevertheless, there are a few important caveats.
First, those who carry an abnormally high toxic load must approach water
fasting with caution. Such a build-up of toxins is almost always due to the effect
of either (1) medical drugs prescribed for certain serious illnesses or (2) certain
recreational drugs.
Otherwise, using a water fast to cleanse the gradual, lifelong build-up of
everyday toxins is almost certainly nothing to worry about, even if you live in the
most polluted city and eat the unhealthiest diet! Quite literally, you have a greater
chance of being struck by lightning than being damaged through fasting.
If you’ve been exposed to or are currently taking high concentrations of certain
toxic drugs (see 2.2b), the main danger concerns the sheer cleansing power of the
water fast. In a nutshell, the healing metabolism of ketosis can unleash such a rapid
release of toxins into the blood stream that the body cannot cope. However, a three-
day water fast may still be possible, given the fact that you’ll be reaching into only
the initial stages of ketosis. If you’re currently taking toxic medication, it may also be
possible to first lower your dosage or switch meds before the fast.

(2.2c) Emotional and psychological reasons
As listed above (see 2.2b), the drugs prescribed for mental health issues
can be highly toxic, thereby causing potentially serious complications through the
deep cleansing of a water fast. Above and beyond this, the emotional and psychological
issues for which such drugs are prescribed in the first place themselves make fasting a double-
edged sword.
Although water fasting clearly induces physical benefits, it can unfortunately
also cause an emotional backlash in anyone who undertakes a fast for which they are
not prepared psychologically. For those with mental health issues, this is even more
likely to be the case, and can result in a further deepening of emotional and
psychological wounds. Those with eating disorders are particularly vulnerable. Although
water fasting can help to overcome the addictive behaviour which causes anorexia
and bulimia, it is all too easy for anyone suffering from these illnesses to abuse fasting
in the same way that they abuse food: as an expression of either self-hatred or
misguided self-control. This almost invariably leads either to the inability to complete
the fast (leading to further self-hatred) or to binge eating following the fast (a
complete lack of self-control).
In order to ensure success, it is essential for anyone suffering from eating
disorders to undertake their fast under the supervision of a fasting guide or
someone who fully understands the relationship between fasting and eating
disorders.

(2.3) Should I consult a medical doctor about my
planned fast?
If you don’t suffer from any medical issues, there’s no need to consult a
medical doctor about a three-day water fast. Fasting is as natural as
eating, and has nothing to do with the drug-based realm of Western medicine.
However, if you already know that you have a health problem, always discuss your fast with
a doctor beforehand! Similarly, if you are considering a water fast in order to attempt to heal
a medical condition, always discuss this first with a doctor! It’s better to be safe than sorry!
It’s true that many, if not most, medical doctors never receive the slightest
training about fasting. Consequently, they usually know little or nothing about
fasting. However, the purpose of discussing your fast with an M.D. doesn’t have to be about
gaining their blessing. Rather, discussing your case empowers you with knowledge
regarding the possible physiological effects of a zero-calorie lifestyle on your medical
condition, as well as possible side-effects caused by any drugs you may have been
taking. This way you can make an informed decision yourself.
If you do feel the need to discuss your fast with a doctor, then it would also be
worthwhile balancing his or her medical opinion with that of someone more
knowledgeable about fasting. This might include someone with a more holistic
background, such as a nutritionist, naturopath or fasting guide.

body is truly asking for, rather than what your mind thinks is best for you, or what
your ego is trying to dictate that you should do. This means that, for most healthy
people, anywhere from about once a year to once a month is enough. On rare
occasions, though, I’ve worked with people who have felt a bodily calling to fast as
frequently as three days every week, in order to address serious medical issues.
Whatever the general timeframe, I’d definitely recommend avoiding an overly
regular – and therefore arbitrary – fasting timetable. Holding rigidly to a fixed
frequency hampers your sensitivity towards tuning into the actual needs of your
body. Beyond this, I’d also urge you to keep one additional factor in mind.
On a purely physical level, you need to experience the healing metabolism of
ketosis at least once a year in order to grant your body easy access it.
In terms of ‘fine-tuning’ or ‘deepening’ your fast, I’d always recommend
eventually aiming for a ‘water-only’ water fast (see 3.4a), if you supplemented this
fast with lemon slices, herbal or green teas – or anything else for that matter. If you
found your current experience of a three-day water fast to be hard going, there’s no
need to push yourself prematurely in this direction. In the long term, though, a water-
only water fast is certainly the most powerful both physically and emotionally, since
it forces you to confront issues of addiction towards food and eating in the most
intense manner possible. If you’re also interested in the spiritual aspects of fasting,
then a water-only water fast likewise cuts straight to the heart of the matter, in terms
of experiencing different levels of being.
