What is Options Trading?
Options can seem intimidating at first, but don't worry, I'm here to break it down for you in a way
that's easy to understand. Think of it like this: options trading is similar to buying shares of a stock.
In fact, they are connected to stocks. You purchase option contracts on stock at a certain price,
and if the value increases, you can sell them for a profit. However, with options, you can also make
money if the stock price goes down. That's one of the key differences between options and
normal shares.
Options also allow you to control a large amount of stock for a smaller investment, which can lead
to much larger profits compared to buying the stock outright. They allow you to turn a small
amount of money into a large amount of money very fast, but you can also lose it just as fast if
you don’t know what you’re doing. Options are more risky than trading normal shares, butyou can
only lose what you put in.
So let's take it one step at a time and build a solidfoundation for your options trading journey.
That’s the simplest explanation of what options trading is. Now there are 5 parts to options that
you should know. I’ll give the technical definition, and a super easy to understand definition in
bold. These will all start to make more sense when we jump into Webull and start actually trading.
1. Underlying stock: The stock that the options are tied to. For example, Tesla, Apple,
Amazon, S&P 500.(it’s just what stock we are trading)
2. Option type: There are two types of options: call options and put options. A call option
goes up in value if the stock price goes up. A put option goes up in value if the stock price
goes down. Vice versa.
(buy calls if you think the stock will go up, buy puts if you think the stock will go
down)
3. Strike price: The predetermined price at which the holder has the right to buy or sell the
underlying stock.(super simple, well get more intothat later on)
4. Expiration date: The date on which the option contract expires and the holder must
exercise their right to buy or sell the underlying stock.(again super simple, well get more
into that later on)

5. Premium: The fee that the holder pays to purchase the option contract. The premium is
determined by supply and demand in the options market and is based on a variety of
factors, such as the price of the underlying stock, the length of time until the expiration
date, and the level of volatility in the market.(justa fancy word for the price of the
option)
That’s really the basics of options if I had to explain it to a 5 year old. Again, this is just the extreme
basics, we’ll slowly get more advanced. Most of the information about options you find online
doesn’t pertain to what we’re doing here.We are solelytrading the options premiums, we have no
intent to “exercise” the options.This is why fromfirst glance options can be so confusing.

Support and Resistance/Supply and Demand Zones
Support and resistance refer to certain price levels that tend to act as barriers, preventing the
price of a stock from moving in a certain direction.
Support refers to a price level where there is significant buying on a stock, causing the price to
rise. When the price of a stock reaches a support level, it is expected to bounce back up due to
the increased demand.
On the other hand, resistance refers to a price level where there is significant selling of a stock,
causing the price to fall. When the price of a stock reaches a resistance level, it is expected to fall
back down due to the increased supply.
Support and resistance levels can be identified by analyzing historical price data and looking for
price points where the stock has had difficulty breaking through. These levels can be used by
traders as a way to make buying or selling decisions, as well as to set stop-loss orders to limit
potential losses.

I highly recommend you watch some youtube videos to further get a grasp & a visual

Optional - Trendlines
Trendlines are graphical tools used in technical analysis to identify the direction of a trend and to
forecast future price movements. A trendline is a straight line drawn on a chart that connects two
or more price points. The slope of the trendline indicates the direction of the trend, with a steep
slope indicating a strong trend and a shallow slope indicating a weak trend.
There are two types of trendlines: uptrend lines and downtrend lines. An uptrend line is drawn by
connecting the lows of a stock's price action, and it indicates that the stock is in an uptrend. A
downtrend line is drawn by connecting the highs of a stock's price action, and it indicates that the
stock is in a downtrend.
Trendlines are used by traders to identify potential entry and exit points in the market, as well as
to set stop-loss orders. They can also be used to identify trend strength, as a trend that is able to
sustain a steep trendline is considered to be stronger than a trend with a shallow trendline.
It is important to note that trend lines are not always reliable, and they can be broken if the price
of a stock moves significantly above or below the trendline. It is therefore important for traders to
use trendlines in conjunction with other technical indicators and tools to confirm the strength and
direction of a trend.

Risk Management - Knowing When to Sell - Defense

Risk management is arguably the most important aspect of day trading, as it helps traders to
minimize their potential losses and protect their capital. Here are a few strategies that traders
should use to manage risk in day trading:
POSITION SIZING
1. Position sizing refers to the number of shares or contracts that a trader buys at a certain
time. By properly sizing their positions, traders can limit their risk relative to their account
size.
When playing cards, you don’t go all in on every hand right? Same here. You should
NEVER put all your money into a single trade. A trade can always go against you and lead
to huge losses, especially if you don’t follow your stop loss. The common position sizing is
5-20% of your total account. For example, if you have a $1,000 account, you should only
be entering each trade with $50-$200. Whatever your risk tolerance is. For people with
smaller accounts, they tend to size bigger, which is ok, but it shouldneverexceed 20% in
my opinion. If you enter a trade with $200 on a $1,000 account, and it hits your stop loss.
You only lost 4% of your total account.The smalleryou size, the more times you can allow
yourself to be wrong.My personal size per trade is10% of my total account. I am
comfortable with this sizing because I can comfortably lose a trade without it hurting my
entire account too much.
WHEN THE TRADE GOES AGAINST YOU
Use Stop Losses: A stop-loss is the point where a trade is going against you and you
decide to sell your position no matter what. By having a stop-loss, traders can limit their
potential losses if the market moves against them. (I do this manually, but you can make
automatic stop loss orders so when it hits your stop loss it automatically sells.)
The common stop loss is -20%. It is IMPORTANT that you stick to it and sell once it
reaches your stop loss. This picture shows the importance of a stop loss. The ability of you

sticking to it will determine how successful you will be as a trader long term. Accepting
when you’re wrong and sticking to this is one of the hardest things to do in trading.
Defense wins championships.
WHEN THE TRADE GOES YOUR WAY
Use a trailing stop: A trailing stop is a type of stop-loss order that you adjust when the
trade is moving in your favor. This can help traders to lock in profits while still protecting
against potential losses.
The WORST feeling is letting a green trade go red. There was a time in my trading career
where I was up 300% on a trade, I didn’t take any profits, and I let it go all the way down to
-50% before I sold. I LET A 300% TRADE GO TO -50%. That’s absurd and should never
happen. To stop this from happening, you can set trailing stops. For example, if you are up
20% on a trade, you can move your stop loss up to breakeven (0%). Or if you’re up 100%
you can move your stop loss up to 80%. This ensures if a trade goes your way, you will
ALWAYS make money. Essentially, your stop loss is just following the trade on a tight string
to lock in profits.

Risk management is arguably the most difficult thing about trading. The people that walk into

decisions that may not align with their overall trading goals or risk management strategies.
As such, managing and controlling one's trading psychology is an important aspect of successful

My Personal Strategy
I like to think my strategy is really simple. Because it really is. It’s nothing advanced, but I have the
execution down to a science because I’ve done it so many times. I have an edge with my intuition
that I cannot put into words from experience.
I MAINLY trade 0DTE SPY every single day. These are the riskiest options you can get in the
entire stock market. So I don't necessarily recommend this to beginners. But, through lots of
failure, trial & error & persistence, I have found my way to navigate the stormy seas.
I enter a trade with 5-10% of my portfolio, depending on how confident I am on the trade.
The main thing I look for are key levels, support and resistances. When the market opens I
immediately have the premarket high level and the premarket low level on watch. Whichever way
the stock is pulling towards I enter a position towards shortly after the market opens, I call those
magnet levels. They almost always get tested. I then either play calls on the rejection, or the
breakthrough of that level.
I also have the previous day’s high and low levels on watch too. Then I just wait until it tests
another support/resistance, or VWAP, and based on the candles/volume around those areas I
either play calls on the rejection, or the breakthrough of the level again.
I’m mainly trading on the 1 and 5 minute chart because I’m trading 0DTE and they move
extremely fast, but I’m constantly scrolling through the 15 minute, and the hourly at the same time
to analyze what those charts are looking like too. It is important to map out the major support and
resistance levels on the larger timeframes, You can’t see them unless your chart is at LEAST on
the 15 minute chart.
