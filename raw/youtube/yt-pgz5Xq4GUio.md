[23] Forensic investigations of the event will

[49] Today’s video is on the collapse of the

[73] The 12-story oceanfront tower was constructed

[78] Miami suburb of Surfside.
[80] The building sat atop an underground parking

[85] area that residents called the pool deck.
[88] The building had 136 condominium units. Unlike

[93] Champlain Towers South was collectively maintained

[100] with board members.
[101] In the early hours of June 24th, we know that
there was a failure of the pool deck adjacent
[106] to the building.
[108] Tourists at a nearby hotel were swimming when

[111] They walked over to the Champlain Towers to

[157] high winds to the area.

[181] Of course, the most critical question of the
collapse is why it happened.
[185] Unfortunately it’s a difficult one to answer.
[188] The Town of Surfside put all of their records
and correspondence about the building online
[192] in the interest of public transparency.
[194] It was in the process of being recertified,
a requirement in Miami-Dade County for all
[199] buildings when they reach 40 years in age,
and every 10 years afterwards.
[203] That process starts with a detailed inspection
by a structural engineer.
[207] For Champlain Towers South, the inspection

[211] The findings of that structural inspection

[216] about the cause of the building’s collapse.
[219] Among the items of concern identified during
the inspection, one of the most important
[223] was the pool deck.
[225] The large concrete slab adjacent to the building
also served as a ceiling to the underground
[229] parking garage.
[230] The inspection report noted major structural

[236] result of poor drainage and failed waterproofing.
[239] The issue was that rainwater on the pool deck

[243] concrete slab, and then it had nowhere to
go.
[246] So, instead of flowing along a properly sloped
slab to drains, it simply pooled above the
[252] slab like a bathtub.
[254] Unlike a bathtub, this system wasn’t watertight.
[257] Runoff was leaking into the concrete below
through joints, cracks, and into the pores
[262] of the slab itself.
[264] This wasn’t a surprise.
[265] It was a well-known problem at the condominium,

[270] in various locations along the ceiling of

[274] from cars and walkways.
[276] But the water wasn’t just a nuisance to

[278] It was also slowly deteriorating the reinforced

[291] Reinforced concrete is an extraordinarily
versatile building material because it is
[296] strong, durable, relatively inexpensive, and
can be cast into just about any shape.
[302] For better or worse, it is one of the most
ubiquitous building materials of the modern
[306] world.
[307] But like all building materials, it has its
weaknesses and is subject to deterioration
[312] over time.
[313] One of the most prevalent of those weaknesses
is the corrosion of steel reinforcement.
[318] Embedded steel is usually safeguarded against
corrosion by the impermeable covering of concrete
[325] and its alkalinity, which creates a protective
oxide layer around the steel.
[330] However, over time, water flowing through
concrete can leach certain constituents out,
[336] making the concrete more porous and less alkaline.
[340] That makes the steel more subject to corrosion.
[342] This is especially true in coastal areas where
salt laden air from the sea can carry chloride
[348] ions toward inland structures.
[351] When these chloride ions saturate the concrete,
they accelerate the degradation of the protective
[357] oxide coating around the steel.
[359] Corrosion doesn’t just weaken steel, it
also causes it to expand in volume, creating
[364] pressure within a reinforced concrete structure.
[368] Eventually the corrosion can reach a point
where the pressure is too much.
[371] The surrounding concrete breaks away, leading
to cracks, spalls (which are small areas of
[377] flaked off concrete), or delamination, where
parts of the concrete along mats of reinforcing
[383] steel are completely separated.
[385] Once the steel is no longer surrounded and
protected by concrete, the corrosion can progress
[390] much more quickly and may eventually lead
to a structural failure.
[394] The engineering and construction industries
have made huge improvements in design and
[399] construction of concrete structures in the
past 30 years thanks in large part to the
[403] Federal Highway Administration and the International
Concrete Repair Institute.
[409] However, Champlain Towers South was designed
and built before modern building codes included
[414] best practices for concrete structures in
harsh coastal environments.
[419] The engineer who inspected the tower pointed

[423] language, stating that “failure to replace
the waterproofing in the near future will
[428] cause the extent of the concrete deterioration
to expand exponentially.”
[433] Keeping water, especially salty water, away
from reinforced concrete is vital.
[438] If inadequate waterproofing turns out to be
the cause of the failure, it won’t have
[443] been the first time.

[449] two people.
[450] The cause of the collapse was corrosion of
