# AI vs SaaS: Peak Pessimism

### 10 Potential Rebounds

The ‘Saaspocalypse’ narrative peaked in early April. A Middle East flareup had pushed Brent to ~$113, the Nasdaq slipped into formal correction territory (*down >10% from October highs*), and the IGV software ETF fell to a 52-week low of ~$75.

The recovery since the lows has been swift. Aided in part by the better than expected earnings in the latest quarter. Since the early April bottom:

**IGV is up ~24% off the lows****IGV logged its best week since October 2001**in mid-April as the panic broke**Snowflake and ServiceNow have led the bounce**, up ~46% and ~26% off their lows respectively, with Barron’s recently calling them the names pulling software ‘out of the abyss.’**Q1 2026 prints have given the rally a fundamental backbone**, not just a sentiment lift

The single most important takeaway from this earnings cycle is that **Software companies are monetizing AI. They are not being eaten by it. **SaaS will and is adapting to the new reality.

The data points are hard to argue with:

**Microsoft:**AI business run rate is now $37B (*up 123% YoY*)**ServiceNow:**Now Assist (*its AI product*) customers with Annual Contract Value (*ACV*) >$1M grew more than 130% YoY. Management is guiding >$1B in Now Assist ACV for 2026**Snowflake:**Closed FY26 with 9,100+ accounts on AI features, up from 7,300 just one quarter earlier. AI was a decisive factor in roughly half of the new bookings in H2 FY26.**Salesforce:**Agentforce ARR hit $800M just 15 months after launch (*up 169% YoY*), with 2.4 billion Agentic Work Units delivered and 29,000 deals closed.**Cloudflare:**Strongest quarter in years (*revenue +34% and $1M+ deals up 73%*), explicitly tied to a surge in agentic AI traffic.**Toast:**ARR of $2.2B (*up 26% YoY*), Adjusted EBITDA grew to $179M from $133M a year ago, full-year EBITDA guidance raised to $790 to $810M, and a fresh $500M buyback authorization.

The ‘agents will replace workflow software’ thesis is, in the field, playing out opposite of what the bears had predicted. Agents need workflow orchestration, audit trails, governance, and tool-calling rails. That’s exactly what Software platforms sell.

#### But here’s the catch. Not every cheap software stock is a buy.

What separates the survivors from the value traps in any major technology shift isn’t the multiple. It’s the intangibles. Brand. Customer trust. Regulatory standing. Embedded workflows. Network density. Proprietary data. Decades of installed base. None of this shows up on a P/E screen, and none of it can be conjured out of thin air by an AI startup. Competition in the industry will increase, but AI benefits the incumbents in this competition.

David Teece made this point back in 1986: innovators rarely capture the profits. The owners of the complementary assets do.

**EMI**invented the CT scanner and lost to GE, which had the hospital sales and service network**RC Cola**was first to market with both canned and diet cola, then lost to Coke and Pepsi on distribution and brand**IBM**was late to the PC and won anyway, because it controlled the ecosystem around it

No 2 transitions are the same. Still, the above lens is helpful while analyzing SaaS. For Constellation, FICO, DoorDash, and ServiceNow, the code was never the moat. The workflows, the regulatory plumbing, the network density, and the decades of customer trust were. AI lowers software development costs for everyone. It cannot replicate any of the other moats.

One sober reminder. Roughly 78% of the US market cap is now exposed to AI disruption in some form, and a P/E screen will not distinguish survivors from casualties. The focus below is on names where the intangible moat does the heavy lifting, not on the valuation/multiple.

The selloff was indiscriminate. There can be an opportunity here. Let’s dig in.
