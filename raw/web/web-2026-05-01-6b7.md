## Abstract

Wind turbine major components failures represent a disproportionate share of wind farm operation and maintenance costs. Robust statistical inference on their reliability is however challenging, as large-scale datasets aggregate turbines exposed to heterogeneous environmental conditions, creating confounding between intrinsic design variability and wind-driven effects. This study proposes a Bayesian hierarchical Accelerated Failure Time model to disentangle these contributions. Applied to a dataset of over 8,000 gearboxes and 64,000 cumulative operating years, the framework explicitly captures manufacturer-specific baseline reliability while estimating the influence of environmental covariates. Results show that intrinsic design differences dominate the failure signal, while capacity factor emerges as the most robust environmental driver of lifetime reduction, consistent with fatigue-based degradation mechanisms. The proposed framework provides a rigorous basis for fleet-scale reliability assessment, maintenance planning, and long-term asset management in wind energy systems.
