## Abstract

The Knowledge Graph Question Answering (KGQA) system aims to transform natural language questions into structured query syntax, typically in SPARQL, to retrieve answers from a knowledge base. The accuracy of this query generation process is a key determinant of overall system performance. Traditional approaches, such as direct text-to-SPARQL translation, often suffer from entity translation errors and require extensive training data and computational resources. Alternatively, multi-label classification methods generate query templates and populate them through entity matching, but these approaches often involve complex architectures and fail to capture dependencies among labels effectively. This paper presents a novel method that integrates the strengths of multi-label classification and text-to-text sequence transformation. Specifically, the proposed approach reformulates the SPARQL generation task as a multi-label text-to-text problem, where each label corresponds to an RDF triple pattern in SPARQL. These labels are treated as target sequences, significantly simplifying the output space and reducing sequence complexity. The design confines the label combinations to only 20, with sequence lengths ranging from one to three, thereby mitigating entity translation errors and enhancing generation accuracy. Unlike prior methods that rely on large-scale pre-trained models such as BART or GPT, the proposed framework employs Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)-based, ConvS2S, and Transformer encoder–decoder architectures. Among these, the ConvS2S model demonstrates the best performance in handling multi-label sequences. Experimental results on QALD-9 and LC-QuAD1.0 datasets show that the proposed method achieves substantial improvements: a 17% gain in Subset Accuracy and a 2.1% improvement in F1-measure over existing multi-label and text-to-text models. Moreover, in end-to-end evaluations, it attains F1-measures of 55.3% on QALD-9 and 74.6% on LC-QuAD1.0, outperforming several state-of-the-art KGQA systems. Notably, these results are achieved without the reliance on large pre-trained models, highlighting the method’s efficiency and applicability in resource-constrained environments.

## Introduction

In recent years, Natural Language Processing (NLP) has been applied to Knowledge Graph Question Answering (KGQA) systems \[1\], \[2\], enabling them to understand user-input questions and provide appropriate answers. The architecture of such systems, as shown in Fig. 1, consists of four main steps: Preprocessing, Entity Mapping, SPARQL Generation, and Evaluation.

The preprocessing step processes the input question by performing tokenization, Part-of-Speech (POS) tagging, and Named Entity Recognition (NER) to extract critical information effectively. Next, in the Entity Mapping step, essential terms of the question are matched with entities in the knowledge graph. After entity mapping, the SPARQL Generation step constructs the SPARQL query syntax for the question based on the extracted information and its understanding of the input question. Finally, in the Evaluation step, the generated SPARQL query \[3\] is used to retrieve answers from a knowledge base, such as Wikidata \[4\] or DBpedia \[5\], which are then returned to the user.

SPARQL query syntax comprises RDF (Resource Description Framework) triples \[6\], which describe relationships between entities. Each RDF triple consists of a subject, a predicate, and an object, representing the core structure for modeling the connections in the knowledge graph. The subject in an RDF triple represents the described entity or resource, typically identified by a URI (Uniform Resource Identifier) or a blank node (an anonymous resource). The object is the value or entity associated with the subject via the predicate. It can be another resource (identified by a URI or blank node) or a literal value, such as a string, number, or date. The predicate defines the relationship between the subject and the object, serving as a property or attribute of the subject. Like the subject, a URI also represents the predicate to specify the property.

In a KGQA system, the accuracy of query syntax generation directly impacts the correctness of the returned results. Currently, there are three primary approaches to generating SPARQL query syntax: methods based on knowledge graph structure \[1\], \[7\], sequence transformation \[8\], \[9\], and template-based methods \[10\], \[11\]. Methods based on knowledge graph structure: These methods involve searching for relevant subgraphs (subgraph matching) within the knowledge graph by utilizing all entities identified during the entity-matching step. The goal is to retrieve all potential RDF triples. However, this approach can be computationally expensive due to the complexity of subgraph searching.

Sequence transformation methods treat SPARQL as the target sequence, and natural language questions are directly converted into SPARQL query syntax using machine translation models. Although effective, this method demands significant amounts of training data and computational resources. With advancements in generative AI technologies, large models such as T5 (Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer) \[12\] and GPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer) \[13\] have introduced new perspectives for SPARQL generation, offering simplified and more effective approaches. Traditional sequence transformation methods for converting natural language questions into SPARQL queries (commonly referred to as “text-to-SPARQL”) treat SPARQL syntax as the target sequence. However, traditional text-to-SPARQL methods typically treat the complete SPARQL query as the target sequence. While this enables the transformation between natural language and structured query language, it requires a substantial amount of training data and computational resources. More importantly, previous research \[14\] has pointed out that such methods are prone to named entity translation errors, which can significantly affect overall performance.

Template-based methods \[15\], \[16\] involve creating query templates through dataset analysis. These methods rely on predefined query templates, into which entities predicted through classification are inserted to generate SPARQL queries. Identified entities are integrated into these predefined templates during the entity-matching step to generate the query syntax. Label Powerset (LP) treats label combinations as single classes, capturing inter-label relationships, but when the number of labels is large, the number of combinations can explode, degrading performance. Although this approach can be effective, it requires a separate classification model for each label, making the system architecture relatively complex \[16\]. In addition, commonly used multi-label strategies each have their drawbacks. For instance, Binary Relevance (BR) decomposes the multi-label problem into multiple binary classification tasks; while simple, it ignores correlations between labels. Classifier Chains (CC) incorporate label dependencies, but errors can propagate along the chain, leading to unstable predictions. Ensemble BR improves classification performance but comes with high training costs, which becomes especially burdensome when dealing with a large number of labels.

Chen et al. \[15\] proposed utilizing multi-label learning methods for SPARQL generation. In their approach, RDF triple patterns within SPARQL are treated as templates, each representing a label. As a result, a SPARQL query can be interpreted as a combination of one or more labels. Based on this concept, they trained a multi-label model to predict labels for a given input question. The predicted labels are then used to assemble matched entities into corresponding RDF triples, enabling the generation of SPARQL query syntax. However, although this multi-label classification approach showed promising results in generating SPARQL queries, it necessitates a separate classification model for each label, which increases the complexity of the machine learning framework \[16\].

To harness their advantages, this paper proposes a hybrid method that combines the second approach (sequence transformation) with the third approach (template-based methods) to transform the multi-label classification problem into a text-to-text task for improving the performance of the KGQA system. Specifically, the outputs of multi-label classification are treated as multi-label sequences. Addressing text-to-text problems, commonly used encoder–decoder architectures include Recurrent Neural Network (RNN)-based models \[17\], \[18\], ConvS2S \[19\], \[20\], and Transformer \[21\], \[22\], each excelling in capturing dependencies, local features, and global features, respectively. To achieve optimal model performance, this paper explored BERT (Bidirectional Encoder Representations form Transformers) \[23\] and GloVe (Global Vectors for Word Representation) \[24\] for the embedding layer in the encoder. Both pre-trained models possess strong language processing capabilities. For the embedding layer in the decoder, Word2Vec \[25\] was employed further to train word vectors for all possible label sequences.

In the proposed approach, the input question is treated as the source sequence, while the corresponding multi-label sequence serves as the target sequence. Each label within the multi-label sequence is constructed based on RDF triples derived from SPARQL queries. Prior studies have demonstrated that employing shorter target sequences in sequence transformation frameworks can substantially enhance translation performance \[17\], \[21\]. To reduce the number of RDF triples in the SPARQL query, the multi-label sequences designed in this study have lengths ranging from one to three, resulting in only 20 possible combinations. This constrained label space helps to reduce translation errors effectively. For instance, given the input query “Who founded Apple?”, an erroneous translation might be “dbr:Apple\_(fruit) dbo:foundedBy ?ans”, which incorrectly identifies Apple as the fruit. The correct translation should be “dbr:Apple\_Inc. dbo:foundedBy ?ans”, where Apple is correctly interpreted as the technology company. This example illustrates the importance of precise entity disambiguation in improving translation accuracy.

To effectively evaluate and train the KGQA system, two commonly used datasets, QALD (Question Answering over Linked Data) \[26\] and Lc-QuAD (Large-scale Complex Question Answering Dataset) \[27\], were employed. These datasets include natural language questions, corresponding SPARQL query syntax, and gold standard answers, providing researchers with reliable benchmarks to assess the performance of different systems. The performances were evaluated using three key metrics: subset accuracy, BLEU-1 (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy Score – Unigram Precision), and F1-measure. Compared with other studies on text-to-text query syntax generation, the proposed method outperformed previous studies in all metrics. The results are summarized below.

- (1)
Compared to previous studies utilizing multi-label classification approaches such as Ensemble BR \[16\] and LP \[15\], the proposed method achieved higher Subset Accuracy.

- (2)
The proposed method achieved F1-scores of 80.2% on QALD-9 and 85.18% on LC-QuAD1.0, outperforming SGPT (Generative Pre-trained Transformer) \[28\] and Stage-I \[29\] by approximately 19% and 2.1%, respectively. Notably, while some studies on text-to-text tasks rely heavily on large pre-trained models such as BART (Bidirectional and Auto-Regressive Transformers), LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory) \[30\] and SGPT, which uses GPT-2 as its decoder, the proposed method achieved superior performance without relying on such models, surpassing SGPT and BART by approximately 19% and 37% in F1-score, respectively.

- (3)
The proposed End-to-End system achieved superior performance compared to other methods \[15\], \[16\], \[31\], \[32\], \[33\], \[34\], \[35\], \[36\], \[37\] listed on the KGQA leaderboard \[38\], outperforming them on both the QALD-9 and LC-QuAD1.0 datasets. Specifically, on the LC-QuAD1.0 dataset, the proposed method achieved an approximately 8% improvement in F1-score over the MHC-LSTM system \[31\]. Furthermore, the results demonstrate that despite not relying on large pre-trained models, the proposed system surpassed the F1-scores of KGQA systems by more than 10% on both datasets.


The main contributions of this study are summarized as follows:

- (1)
This study introduces a novel multi-label text-to-text framework, where RDF triples in SPARQL are formulated as labels to construct a multi-label sequence used as the target output. This formulation notably reduces the complexity of the target sequence. Furthermore, the use of shorter target sequences enhances the efficiency of sequence transformation.

- (2)
By avoiding direct entity generation, the proposed method substantially reduces translation errors and effectively alleviates issues related to named entity disambiguation.

- (3)
Without relying on large-scale pre-trained models (e.g., BART or GPT), the proposed approach still achieves competitive performance, demonstrating its strong applicability in resource-constrained environments.

## Conclusions

This study utilized a multi-label text-to-text approach to train a multi-label sequence model and applied it to a knowledge graph question-answering system. Experimental results on the QALD-9 and Lc-QuAD1.0 datasets demonstrated that all three models (RNN-based, ConvS2S, Transformer) provide stable and efficient performance. Notably, the ConvS2S model combined with BERT achieved the best performance, confirming the superiority of sequence models in handling multi-label tasks.
