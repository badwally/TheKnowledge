## When Is a Condo Audit Required?

Under the Condominium Act, 1998, every condo corporation in Ontario must appoint an auditor each fiscal year. The corporation must also produce audited financial statements. This is not optional. It is a statutory requirement regardless of size, unit count, or budget.

The audit must be completed before the annual general meeting. The board is responsible for appointing the auditor on time. If the corporation fails to do so, any unit owner can apply to the court to have one appointed.

There are limited exceptions. A corporation may pass a by-law to waive the requirement. This needs at least 80% of unit owners to vote in favour. In practice, the waiver is uncommon. Most lenders, insurers, and property managers still expect audited statements.

## CSRS 4200 (Previously Known as NTR) vs. Audits

Not every financial statement engagement is an audit. Some condo corporations that have waived the audit requirement may receive a compilation instead. This falls under Canadian Standard on Related Services (CSRS) 4200, previously called a Notice to Reader (NTR).

The difference is significant:

**Audit**— The CPA tests transactions, verifies balances, and evaluates internal controls. The auditor then issues an opinion on whether the financial statements are fairly presented. This provides the highest level of assurance.**CSRS 4200 compilation**— The CPA compiles financial statements from data provided by management. There is no verification and no testing. The practitioner does not confirm the numbers are accurate. They simply organize data into a financial statement format.


For most condo corporations in Ontario, an audit is the default and legally required engagement. A CSRS 4200 compilation does not satisfy the Act unless the corporation has passed the 80% waiver by-law.

Not sure which engagement your corporation currently receives? Review your governing documents and consult your CPA. Boards sometimes inherit a compilation from a prior manager without realizing it falls short of what the Act requires.
