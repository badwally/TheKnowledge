Brain maps (e.g. topographies like retinotopy, somatotopy) vary across individuals. This is thought to reflect computational differences, motivating personalized functional localization. However, artificial neural networks (ANNs) show that similar performance and internal representations can coexist with diverse circuit layouts. Consequently, we tested whether spatial diversity reflects computational diversity in the brain. Using task and resting-state fMRI data, we compared regional functional topographies and representational geometries-the within-individual dissimilarities among activity patterns that capture computable information.
