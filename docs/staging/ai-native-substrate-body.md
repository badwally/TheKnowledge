# AI as Substrate

## Summary

A business operating model where AI replaces traditional human labor and software logic as the *substrate* the business runs on — distinct from AI-as-feature (a bolt-on enhancement to an otherwise traditional product) and AI-as-product (the offering is itself a foundation model or its raw API). The substrate-first frame is the operative model for solo founders and tiny teams: a functional agent stack handling coding, content, support, design, and ops costs $300–$500 per month, replacing functions that historically required $80,000–$120,000 per month in human payroll [[sources/web-2026-04-23-e4c]].

## Key claims

- AI-as-Substrate companies — Surge AI, Mercor, Lovable, Decagon, Polsia — power internal workflows, execution, and service delivery with AI agents and models; the offering sold to the customer may or may not itself be AI [[sources/web-2026-04-23-e4c]].
- AI-as-Feature is the bolt-on case: Zendesk, Notion, Canva add AI to an existing core product, but the economic and organizational models remain traditional SaaS [[sources/web-2026-04-23-e4c]].
- AI-as-Product means the offering is the foundation model itself or its raw API; OpenAI, Anthropic, Mistral are the canonical referents [[sources/web-2026-04-23-e4c]].
- Substrate-first organizations are radically lean: Surge AI reached approximately $1B ARR with about 110 employees, roughly $9.1M per person; Gamma operates a player-coach model where leaders spend most of their time building rather than managing [[sources/web-2026-04-23-e4c]].
- The primary technical competency of a substrate-first operator is context engineering — architecting the information systems, retrieval pipelines, and governance rules that make off-the-shelf agents reliable across multi-step workflows [[sources/web-2026-04-23-e4c]].
- The pricing atomic unit shifts away from per-seat toward usage-based or outcome-based pricing (e.g., Decagon charging per resolution); capital efficiency becomes a strategic discipline, decoupling headcount from revenue growth [[sources/web-2026-04-23-e4c]].
- The "Pets.com dilemma" for substrate-first companies: high variable inference costs against commoditized AI output compress gross margins to roughly 50–60%, making unit economics fragile unless the company surrounds the AI core with low-compute high-value workflows (the "Canva playbook") [[sources/web-2026-04-23-e4c]].
- Boundary case — Anysphere/Cursor: substrate-first by organizational and operating DNA, but ships an AI-coding product. The taxonomy treats it as substrate-first because it is an application layer atop foundation models rather than itself a foundation-model provider [[sources/web-2026-04-23-e4c]].
