# Stream 5 — Governmental / policy (SPINE CORE — analytical working note)

> Working note, not canonical wiki. Date: 2026-06-10. Deep-research verified
> (111 agents; primary government/CIFAR/ISED + C.D. Howe). Grounded via
> filter-correct + re-ingest. This is the spine — the "why Canada" verdict.

## The verdict (adversarial, evidence-led)

**Canada's funded AI mechanisms back talent, commercialization, and — since
Budget 2024 — compute hardware. NO funded federal program builds or operates
shared/vertical DATA infrastructure that competing firms could pool through.**
The "data-sharing ambition is unmatched by funded, usable mechanisms"
(C.D. Howe's "missing pillar"). → `[[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]]`.

This validates the project's central risk: **the "why Canada" case cannot rest
on a funded regulatory/strategy tailwind for pooling.** It must rest on two
*lighter* levers (below) plus market structure.

## The evidence

- **Pan-Canadian AI Strategy Phase 2** — C$443M (Budget 2021, launched Jun 22
  2022; up to C$208M to CIFAR). Every line = talent / institutes / standards /
  commercialization / compute. **Zero data-infrastructure line item.** →
  `[[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]]`.
- **Sovereign AI Compute Strategy** — C$2B (Budget 2024): up to C$700M private
  data-centre mobilization + C$1B public supercomputing + C$300M AI Compute
  Access Fund. **Exclusively compute procurement**; Access Fund explicitly lists
  data-transfer fees as *ineligible*; first major disbursement C$240M to **Cohere**
  (a single firm's data centre, Mar 2025). → `[[sources/web-2026-06-04-76b]]`.
- **Scale AI Global Innovation Cluster** — funds AI commercialization projects,
  not a cross-firm data collaborative/trust. → `[[sources/web-2018-02-15-c35]]`.
- **AIDA dead; no binding AI statute** (Stream 4) → `[[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]]`.

## The two usable levers (what "why Canada" actually rests on)

1. **Economy-wide data-mobility right** (PIPEDA amendment, 2026; banking first
   iteration) — a *holder-directed portability* right extensible to any sector
   with a secure interoperable framework. The concrete legal hook a collective
   could ride. → `[[sources/web-2025-11-21-9a5]]` (IAPP).
2. **CAN/DGSI 100-7** (formerly CAN/CIOSC 100-7) — a Canadian **National Standard
   for governing data trusts / collaboratives / cooperatives**. A ready-made
   governance *blueprint* and credibility anchor — but **no funding, mandate, or
   operational infrastructure attached**. *[SCC page 403; verified 3-0 in
   deep-research; cite by reference: CAN/DGSI 100-7.]*

Distinct strand: **Indigenous data sovereignty / OCAP** (FNIGC) — a mature
Canadian data-governance framework about who controls pooled data; relevant
precedent for member-controlled governance. → `[[sources/web-2025-11-28-252]]`.

## US contrast

**NAIRR pilot** (NSF) — US federal posture is *research-infrastructure* (shared
compute + data for researchers), not a commercial cross-firm pooling mechanism;
broader US AI posture is deregulatory (Stream 4). → `[[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]]`.
Net: neither country funds commercial data pooling; Canada's *governance
standard + data-mobility right* are a marginally more usable scaffold than the
US's research-only NAIRR, but the gap to a *funded* mechanism is real in both.

## Synthesis read for Task 10 ("why now / why Canada / who captures")

The honest "why Canada" is **not** "the government will fund/mandate this." It is:
(a) a national **governance standard** (CAN/DGSI 100-7) that lends legitimacy;
(b) an **economy-wide data-mobility right** that gives data holders a legal lever
to direct their data into a collective; (c) **data-sovereignty framing** (incl.
Indigenous-led precedent) that resonates politically; and (d) market structure
(Streams 6–7). The compute-heavy strategy is a **distraction**, not a tailwind,
for a data play. **If the venture's pitch leans on "Canada's AI strategy backs
this," that pitch is rhetoric — the funded mechanism is not there.**
