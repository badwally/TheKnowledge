# Stream 4 — Regulatory (analytical working note)

> Working note, not canonical wiki. Date: 2026-06-10. Deep-research verified
> (114 agents; primary government/regulator sources). Grounded via
> filter-correct + re-ingest. US + Canada primary, EU reference.

## THE pivotal finding (reframes the policy spine)

**No verified regulation COMPELS or FUNDS cross-competitor data pooling.** Every
mechanism examined is a **consumer-/holder-directed access regime** — the data
subject directs disclosure to an authorized recipient, with downstream reuse
constrained. There is no "pool your data with competitors" mandate or subsidy in
US or Canadian law.

**Consequence for the thesis:** the regulatory tailwind the venture might hope
for does not exist as a *pooling* mandate. What exists is **data portability /
consumer-directed mobility** — a *different* structural template. It still helps
a condo-style collective (the data *holder* — a condo corporation, a PM — can
**direct** its data to a collective), but the design must ride
holder-directed-portability rails, not a compelled-pooling regime.

## Data-portability mandates (the usable lever)

- **Canada Consumer-Driven Banking Act** — enacted via Bill C-15 (Royal Assent
  **Mar 26 2026**) but **not yet fully in force**; Phase 1 read-access, mandates
  participation only for large banks, others opt-in; oversight moved to Bank of
  Canada. → `[[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]]`.
- **PIPEDA amendment → economy-wide right to data mobility** (same budget bill;
  originated as C-27 s.72, re-enacted separately from the dead AIDA). Banking is
  "the first iteration"; extends to any sector that builds a secure
  interoperable framework. **This is the single most important Canadian policy
  hook for the venture** — an economy-wide data-mobility right the collective
  could ride. → `[[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]]`.
- **US CFPB §1033** (Dodd-Frank open banking) — finalized but **under
  preliminary injunction since Oct 2025**, being reconsidered via ANPR; closed-
  loop ("use limited to the requested product"). Contrast: less certain than
  Canada's. *(CRS IF13117 by reference.)*
- **US health info-blocking / TEFCA** — compelled *disclosure* (21st Century
  Cures) + voluntary exchange framework; enforcement live 2026. →
  `[[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]]`, `[[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]`.
- **Sector data governance — Ag Data Transparent** — a private *transparency/
  governance standard* for farm data (closest thing to cross-firm data
  governance), not a mandate. → `[[sources/web-2026-05-05-b07]]`.
- **EU Data Act** (reference) — regulated data-access/portability obligations;
  precedent, not in-scope policy. → `[[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]]`.

## AI regulation status (the gap)

- **Canada: AIDA is DEAD** (died with Bill C-27 at prorogation Jan 6 2025;
  Minister confirmed June 2025 "off the table as drafted"). The gap is filled by
  **non-statutory** instruments: the **Sept 2023 Voluntary Code of Conduct on
  Generative AI** and **CAISI** (under ISED). → `[[sources/web-2026-06-04-a90]]`,
  `[[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]]`. **Canada has no binding AI statute as of
  mid-2026.**
- **US:** Biden EO 14110 rescinded → Trump EO 14179 (Jan 23 2025) → July 2025 AI
  Action Plan (PDF wouldn't convert; by reference); NIST AI RMF operative; Senate
  killed the 10-yr state-AI-moratorium 99-1 (Jul 2025); Dec 11 2025 EO launched a
  DOJ task force to preempt state AI laws. Net: **deregulatory federal posture,
  contested state laws.**

**Read for the spine:** neither country mandates pooling, and AI regulation is
*lighter* than the thesis might assume (Canada has no AI statute; US is
deregulatory). So the "why Canada" case cannot rest on a binding regulatory
tailwind — it must rest on the **data-mobility right + funding/strategy
programs** (Stream 5) and market structure, not on compulsion.
