  The rubric for backlog decisions

  Four dimensions, applied in order:

  1. Does it change daily operational outcomes?
  Work that the system does every day without prompting (scheduled agents, monitors, pollers) compounds permanently. Work that improves authoring ergonomics or schema
  modeling does not compound — it helps at write time only.

  2. Is the bottleneck engineering or human judgment?
  If the item requires per-domain calibration (QUAL-10), per-page LLM review (ONT-1), or cross-system coordination (AGT-7), engineering delivers an interface, not an outcome.
   Human-bottlenecked items have lower effective leverage per sprint.

  3. Is effort proportionate to the use case that actually exists right now?
  ONT-1 (~1000 concept reclassifications) and ARCH-12 (second NLM backend) are both L-effort. The concept overflow doesn't block any current workflow. The single-vendor risk
  is real but not acute. L-effort items need a concrete forcing function, not just "this is the right architecture."

  4. Are dependencies satisfied?
  Several items are technically unblocked but were listed as depending on things that shipped (K2, K4, ONT-3, ONT-4). These should be re-evaluated rather than assumed
  blocked.
