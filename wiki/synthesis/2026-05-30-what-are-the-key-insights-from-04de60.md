# Insula to ventral striatal projections mediate compulsive eating produced by intermittent access to palatable food.

**Authors:** Samantha Spierling, Giordano de Guglielmo, Dean Kirson, Alison Kreisler,

## Synthesis

The research establishing that projections from the insula to the ventral striatum drive compulsive eating provides a crucial neurobiological framework for understanding how GLP-1 medications alter behavior. Integrating this pathway with the broader clinical and preclinical evidence reveals several key insights into how GLP-1 alters the reward system and dictates dosing optimization:

**1. Modulating the Ventral Striatum's "Volume Knob"**
The ventral striatum, which encompasses the nucleus accumbens, is a central hub of the brain's reward system [1, 2]. GLP-1 receptor agonists act directly on regions like the ventral striatum to provide "neural gain control" [2, 3]. Rather than acting as a blunt "off switch," GLP-1 functions like a "volume knob," dialing down the intensity of the dopamine surges that drive compulsive cravings for highly palatable foods [3, 4].

**2. The Insula, Cue Reactivity, and Silencing "Food Noise"**
The insula works closely with the ventral striatum and the orbitofrontal cortex to process environmental food cues and evaluate internal physical sensations of satisfaction [5, 6]. By altering communication in these networks, GLP-1 medications effectively reduce the brain's reactivity to environmental triggers and silence "food noise" [2, 5]. Dampening the noise from this limbic circuitry gives the brain's prefrontal cortex the necessary mental space to pause, reflect, and exert intentional executive control over impulsive urges [7, 8].

**3. The Risk of Reward Over-Suppression (Anhedonia)**
Because the ventral striatum and wider dopamine circuits do not differentiate between the dopamine released from food, drugs, or natural healthy joys, GLP-1 medications can indiscriminately dampen all of them [9-11]. If doses are pushed too high, this aggressive suppression of the reward circuitry can clinically manifest as anhedonia—a "flat affect" characterized by emotional numbness and a loss of motivation for daily activities [9, 12, 13].

**4. Strategic Dosing Optimization**
Understanding that GLP-1 directly manipulates cellular receptors in pathways like the ventral striatum provides a clear biological rationale for strategic dosing [2] [[nlm:962f9ad6-45f8-43ed-9c17-52c2745d3574]]. To safely navigate these neurobiological changes, clinicians advocate for customizing the dose [14, 15]. This optimized approach provides a gentle, sustainable modulation of the insula and ventral striatum [16] [[sources/yt-FjyZSXPrbyk]]. It strikes the optimal biological balance: providing just enough signal to quiet the compulsive food noise while preserving receptor sensitivity and maintaining the baseline dopamine required for everyday vitality and joy [16, 17].
