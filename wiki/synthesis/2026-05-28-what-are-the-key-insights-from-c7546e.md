# What are the key insights from "The Marc Andreessen Agent with Claude" in the context of AI-native business design for solo founders and tiny teams running on AI substrate? The source describes: How to scrape his entire public catalog, build the system prompt and ask the questions that actually matter

## Synthesis

**Synthesize an Elite Board of Advisors on Demand:** Solo founders and tiny teams often lack direct access to top-tier investors and strategic advisors. By scraping an expert's entire public catalog—such as Marc Andreessen’s 20 years of public writing on startups, markets, and technology—a solo founder can essentially put world-class expertise on "speed dial" [1] [[nlm:57b7bb8d-6ed0-4ec2-84fe-de1b56495388]]. This enables tiny teams to ask high-stakes questions about whether to raise capital, rebuild for AI, or assess big tech competitor threats, and receive highly informed guidance [1] [[nlm:57b7bb8d-6ed0-4ec2-84fe-de1b56495388]].

**Ground the Agent in Actual Data, Not Generic Personas:** To make this workflow valuable, founders must avoid relying on a standard chatbot simply guessing what an expert sounds like [2] [[nlm:57b7bb8d-6ed0-4ec2-84fe-de1b56495388]]. Instead, they must implement rigorous context engineering by scraping the specific sources into a single folder, loading it into a tool like Claude Code, and building a strict system prompt [2, 3].

**Force Citations to Ensure Authentic Judgment:** A crucial part of this system is that the agent must be prompted to cite the specific source file before it provides an answer [2] [[nlm:57b7bb8d-6ed0-4ec2-84fe-de1b56495388]]. By forcing the agent to ground its outputs in the expert's actual catalog, founders ensure that the advice they receive is based on documented frameworks and historical thinking rather than AI hallucinations [2] [[nlm:57b7bb8d-6ed0-4ec2-84fe-de1b56495388]].
