## Synthesis

**The US is expected to narrowly avoid a recession**, with Goldman Sachs assigning only a 35% probability of a US recession over the next 12 months, which is significantly below the consensus median forecast of 65% [1, 2]. Although financial conditions are tightening, real disposable personal income is expected to rebound, providing a boost that outweighs the financial drag [3] [[sources/pdf-girquark-2022-global-economics-analyst]].

**Core PCE inflation is projected to decline from 5% to approximately 3% by late 2023, accompanied by only a ½ percentage point rise in the unemployment rate** [1, 4]. The analysts argue that **"this cycle is different"** because post-pandemic labor market overheating has manifested in unprecedented job openings rather than excessive employment, making it much less painful to unwind [4, 5]. Additional disinflationary impacts are anticipated from the normalization of supply chains and rental housing markets [5, 6].

**The Federal Reserve is expected to hike rates by another 125 basis points to a peak of 5%–5.25%** to ensure growth remains below potential as real incomes recover [1, 7]. Importantly for market expectations, **Goldman Sachs does not expect the Fed to cut rates in 2023**, forecasting the first gentle 25bp cut to arrive only in the second quarter of 2024 [1, 8].

**The Euro area and the UK are likely already in a recession** due to the severe real income hit from surging energy bills [9, 10]. However, **this downturn is expected to be mild** [9] [[sources/pdf-girquark-2022-global-economics-analyst]]. Europe is benefiting from post-pandemic improvements, such as easing supply bottlenecks in chip and automotive production, and has managed to cut Russian gas imports without crushing economic activity [9, 11, 12]. Consequently, the European Central Bank (ECB) is expected to hike rates through May to a peak of 3%, while the Bank of England is projected to reach a terminal rate of 4.5% [9, 13].

**China will likely experience a bumpy reopening**, with slow growth in the first half of 2023 as Covid caution persists, followed by a sharp acceleration in the second half [9, 14]. However, China's long-term growth trend is expected to slide due to structural headwinds, including a contracting property sector, US chip export restrictions, and demographic challenges [9, 15-17].

**Early-hiking Emerging Markets (EM) have shown notable resilience**, suggesting that the current high-inflation period does not necessitate a deep global recession [18, 19]. While core inflation is moderating in countries like Brazil—where rate cuts are anticipated by Q2 2023—Central and Eastern European (CEE) economies face a much more difficult position due to their exposure to the European energy crisis, which will likely force their central banks to continue tightening [20, 21].
