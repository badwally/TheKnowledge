# Ozempic, GLP-1 Drugs &amp; The Truth About Weight Loss | Former FDA Chief Explains

## Synthesis

**The Brain's Reward Circuits and Cue-Induced "Wanting"**
Former FDA Chief Dr. David Kessler explains that obesity is deeply rooted in neural reward circuits that are hijacked by highly processed, energy-dense foods [1, 2]. He clarifies that dopamine is not merely a "pleasure" molecule, but functions as a "learning" and "gating" molecule [3] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. Dopamine locks in specific environmental or emotional cues—such as feeling a pressing unease at 10:00 PM—which triggers a powerful, cue-induced compulsion or "wanting" to self-medicate with food [3-5]. 

**Counterbalancing Reward Through Aversive Signaling**
To combat these deeply ingrained addictive compulsions, GLP-1 medications slow down the emptying of food from the stomach, which sends signals to the hindbrain's aversive circuits, specifically the brain's vomiting center [6] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. Pharmaceutical companies have figured out how to titrate the dosing of these drugs to push a patient right to the **"edge of nausea"** [7] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. This creates an aversive physiological signal that is strong enough to counterbalance and tamp down the hyperactive reward circuits that drive overeating [7-9].

**The Risks of "Microdosing" and Vanity Use**
When discussing dosing optimization, Dr. Kessler expresses significant concern over the modern trend of individuals "microdosing" or manipulating doses simply to lose a few cosmetic pounds [10, 11]. He emphasizes that these are powerful drugs designed to treat visceral adiposity and severe metabolic dysfunction, rather than for vanity weight loss [12, 13]. Because the medication is so effective at suppressing the drive to eat, patients often consume fewer than 1,000 calories a day [14] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. Dr. Kessler warns that this severe restriction essentially mimics **"forced anorexia"** and requires careful clinical supervision to avoid severe malnourishment or gastrointestinal distress [14, 15].

**Managing Relapse and Long-Term Optimization**
Real-world data shows that patients typically only remain on GLP-1 medications for 8 to 9 months [9, 16]. Crucially, the drug merely suppresses the urge to eat while active; **the underlying addictive reward circuits remain completely intact once the medication is stopped** [17] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. Therefore, to optimize long-term success, patients must use the temporary window provided by the drug to actively change their relationship with food and implement sustainable nutritional habits [17, 18]. Because a vast majority of overweight individuals are dealing with insulin resistance, Dr. Kessler suggests optimizing the diet by reducing rapidly absorbable carbohydrates, as they add "fuel to the fire" of hyperinsulinemia [19, 20]. If patients rely solely on the drug without changing these patterns, they will inevitably relapse and regain the weight once the aversive signaling of the medication is removed [16, 17].
