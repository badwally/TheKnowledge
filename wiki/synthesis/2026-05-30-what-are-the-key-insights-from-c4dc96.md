# Ozempic, GLP-1 Drugs &amp; The Truth About Weight Loss | Former FDA Chief Explains

## Synthesis

**The Brain's Reward Circuits and Cue-Induced "Wanting"**
Former FDA Chief Dr. David Kessler explains that obesity and overeating are heavily driven by neural reward circuits that are triggered and hijacked by highly palatable, energy-dense foods [1, 2]. He clarifies that dopamine is not merely a "pleasure" molecule, but acts as a "learning" and "gating" molecule that locks in specific environmental or emotional cues [3, 4]. For instance, a cue like feeling a pressing unease at 10:00 PM can trigger a powerful, cue-induced "wanting" or compulsion to self-medicate with food [3-5]. 

**Counterbalancing Reward Through Aversive Signaling**
To combat these deeply ingrained addictive compulsions, GLP-1 medications slow the emptying of food from the stomach, which sends signals to the hindbrain's aversive circuits—specifically the area postrema (the brain's vomiting center) and the nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS) [6] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. Pharmaceutical companies have managed to titrate the dosing of these drugs to push a patient right to the **"edge of nausea"** [6, 7]. This creates an aversive physiological signal that is strong enough to counterbalance and tamp down the hyperactive reward circuits that normally drive overeating [7, 8].

**The Risks of "Microdosing" and Vanity Use**
When discussing dosing optimization, Dr. Kessler warns against the modern trend of individuals **"microdosing"** or tampering with dosages simply to lose a few cosmetic pounds [9, 10]. He emphasizes that these are powerful drugs with potential adverse effects—such as pancreatitis, gastrointestinal distress, and malnourishment—that should be strictly reserved for treating visceral adiposity and improving metabolic health [11, 12]. Because the medication is so effective at suppressing the appetite, patients often consume fewer than 1,000 calories a day [13] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. Dr. Kessler equates this severe restriction to **"forced anorexia"** and warns it can lead to severe malnourishment and dehydration if not clinically supervised by a care team [13, 14].

**Managing Relapse and Long-Term Optimization**
Real-world data indicates that patients typically remain on GLP-1 medications for an average of 8 to 9 months [8, 15]. Crucially, the drug merely suppresses the urge to eat while active; **the underlying addictive reward circuits remain completely intact, leading to weight regain once the medication is stopped** [16] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. To optimize long-term success, patients must use the temporary window provided by the drug to actively retrain their relationship with food and implement sustainable lifestyle and nutritional habits [16, 17]. Because many overweight individuals suffer from insulin resistance and hyperinsulinemia, Dr. Kessler recommends optimizing the diet by reducing **rapidly absorbable carbohydrates**, as they add "fuel to the fire" and increase visceral fat [18, 19]. Without making these foundational behavioral and nutritional changes, patients will inevitably relapse once the aversive signaling of the medication is removed [16, 20].
