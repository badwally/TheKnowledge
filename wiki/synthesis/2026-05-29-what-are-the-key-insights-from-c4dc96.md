# Ozempic, GLP-1 Drugs &amp; The Truth About Weight Loss | Former FDA Chief Explains

**Channel:** Live Well Be Well with Sarah Ann Macklin  
**Duration:** PT56M22S  
**Views:** 5358  
**Published:** 2025-

## Synthesis

**The Brain's Reward Circuits and Cue-Induced "Wanting"**
Dr. David Kessler explains that obesity is heavily driven by neural reward circuits that have been hijacked by highly processed, energy-dense foods [1] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]], [2] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. He clarifies that dopamine functions less as a "pleasure" molecule and more as a "learning" and "wanting" molecule [3] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. Dopamine acts as an attentional gate that locks in specific environmental or emotional cues—such as feeling stressed or uneasy at 10:00 PM—creating a powerful, cue-induced compulsion to self-medicate with food [4] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]], [5] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]], [3] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. 

**Overpowering Reward Through Aversive Signaling**
To combat these deeply ingrained addictive compulsions, GLP-1 medications slow down the emptying of food from the stomach, which in turn sends signals to the hindbrain—specifically the area postrema (the brain's vomiting center) and the nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS) [6] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. Dr. Kessler notes that pharmaceutical companies have deliberately optimized the dosing of these drugs to push a patient right to the **"edge of nausea"** [7] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. This creates an aversive physiological signal strong enough to counterbalance and tamp down the hyperactive reward circuits that drive overeating [8] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]].

**The Risks of "Microdosing" and Vanity Use**
When discussing dosing optimization, Dr. Kessler expresses significant concern over the modern trend of individuals "microdosing" or manipulating doses simply to lose a few cosmetic pounds [9] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]], [10] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. He emphasizes that these are powerful drugs designed to treat visceral adiposity and severe metabolic dysfunction, not for vanity weight loss [10] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]], [11] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. Because the medication is so effective at suppressing the drive to eat, patients often consume fewer than 1,000 calories a day [12] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. Dr. Kessler warns that this severe restriction essentially mimics **"forced anorexia"** and requires careful clinical supervision to avoid triggering eating disorders, severe malnourishment, or gastrointestinal distress [12] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]], [13] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]], [14] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]].

**Managing Relapse and Long-Term Optimization**
Real-world data shows that patients typically only remain on GLP-1 medications for 8 to 9 months [15] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. Crucially, the drug merely suppresses the urge to eat while active; **the underlying addictive reward circuits remain completely intact once the medication is stopped** [16] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. Therefore, to optimize long-term success, patients must use the temporary window provided by the drug to actively change their relationship with food and implement sustainable nutritional tools [16] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]], [17] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. Because the vast majority of overweight individuals are dealing with insulin resistance, Dr. Kessler suggests optimizing the diet by reducing rapidly absorbable carbohydrates, which add "fuel to the fire" of hyperinsulinemia [18] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]], [19] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. If patients rely solely on the drug without changing these patterns, they will inevitably relapse and regain the weight once the aversive signaling of the medication is removed [20] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]], [16] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]].
