# What does Florida's Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) mandate require for condominium associations, what elements must be inspected, and how does it change reserve funding obligations for Florida condos

## Synthesis

Florida’s Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) mandate requires residential condominium and cooperative associations with buildings **three or more habitable stories in height** to complete a specialized reserve study focused on structural safety [1-3]. These studies must be conducted at least **every 10 years** [4, 5]. The initial compliance deadline for associations existing before July 1, 2022, is December 31, 2025, though this can be extended to December 31, 2026, if completed simultaneously with a mandatory milestone inspection [6, 7]. The visual inspection portion of the SIRS must be performed by a Florida-licensed professional engineer, architect, or a certified reserve specialist/professional reserve analyst [5, 8]. 

**Elements That Must Be Inspected**
A SIRS specifically assesses eight critical structural components of the building to diagnose deficiencies and prevent hazards [4, 9, 10]. The mandated elements include:
1. **Roof systems** [11, 12]
2. **Load-bearing walls** and primary structural systems [11, 12]
3. **Fireproofing and fire protection systems** [11, 12]
4. **Plumbing systems** [11, 12]
5. **Electrical systems** [11, 12]
6. **Waterproofing and exterior painting** [11, 12]
7. **Windows and exterior doors** [11, 12]
8. **Any other item exceeding $25,000** in deferred maintenance or replacement costs whose failure would negatively affect the structural integrity of the above systems [11, 12]. (This threshold was raised from $10,000 in 2025 and requires annual adjustments based on the Consumer Price Index) [9, 12, 13].

**Changes to Reserve Funding Obligations**
The SIRS mandate fundamentally shifts how Florida condominiums manage their finances by enforcing strict funding requirements and removing prior loopholes:
* **No Waiving or Reducing Reserves:** For budgets adopted on or after December 31, 2024, unit owners are **strictly prohibited from voting to waive or reduce reserve contributions** for the eight mandatory SIRS components [14-17]. 
* **Baseline Funding Requirement:** Associations must follow a baseline funding plan that ensures the reserve cash balance for these structural components stays above zero throughout the entire funding period [9, 15].
* **Strict Separation of Funds:** SIRS reserve funds must be **tracked separately** from general operating funds and non-SIRS reserves (such as landscaping, paving, or amenity replacement). Additionally, any interest earned on SIRS reserve funds must remain exclusively within the SIRS account and cannot be commingled [15, 18].
* **Limited Flexibility for Repairs:** If a recent structural milestone inspection identifies urgent repairs, associations are allowed to temporarily pause or reduce SIRS reserve contributions for up to two consecutive budget years to prioritize funding those immediate repairs, provided the membership approves [6, 18].

Upon completing a SIRS, officers and directors must sign an affidavit acknowledging its receipt, distribute a copy or notice of the report to all unit owners, summarize the findings in the annual budget, and electronically report their compliance status to the Florida Division of Condominiums, Timeshares and Mobile Homes (DBPR) within 45 days [19, 20]. Failure to comply can result in DBPR regulatory action, personal liability for board members, and severe complications with insurance renewals or mortgage lending [21, 22].
