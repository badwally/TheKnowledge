# What are the four reserve funding goals defined in the National Reserve Study Standards — Full Funding, Threshold Funding, Baseline Funding, and Statutory Funding — and how do they compare in homeowner cost and risk

## Synthesis

**Full Funding (Fully Funded Model)** aims to keep the reserve balance at or near 100% funded, meaning the cash in the account is equal to the estimated value of the accumulated wear of all major components [[~/code/knowledge/wiki/sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines.pdf]]. Software platforms implement this by ensuring the funding plan stays at or above the fully funded mark each year [[~/code/knowledge/wiki/sources/Reserve Study Software for Reserve Specialists | PropFusion]]. 
*   **Cost & Risk:** This is the most conservative and stable financial model, offering the lowest risk of surprise special assessments for homeowners [[~/code/knowledge/wiki/sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines.pdf]]. However, it carries the highest regular homeowner cost, as it requires steady, robust contributions to build the fund and eliminate any reserve deficits [[~/code/knowledge/wiki/sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines.pdf]].

**Threshold Funding** sets a goal of keeping the reserve cash balance above a specific, predetermined financial floor or threshold throughout the planning period [[~/code/knowledge/wiki/sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines.pdf]] [[~/code/knowledge/wiki/sources/Reserve Study Software for Reserve Specialists | PropFusion]]. 
*   **Cost & Risk:** This model serves as a middle ground for cost and risk. Depending on the association's specific mix of common area components and the chosen threshold floor, this model can be more or less conservative than full funding, allowing boards to balance regular homeowner dues against the risk of reserve depletion [[~/code/knowledge/wiki/sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines.pdf]].

**Baseline Funding** sets a minimum goal of keeping the reserve cash balance at or above $0 at the end of each projected year [[~/code/knowledge/wiki/sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines.pdf]]. The objective is strictly to keep the balance positive using minimum contributions [[~/code/knowledge/wiki/sources/Reserve Study Software for Reserve Specialists | PropFusion]].
*   **Cost & Risk:** While this approach minimizes the regular monthly cost to homeowners, it introduces significant financial risk. By operating just above a zero balance, the association has no financial cushion for unexpected component failures, sudden inflation, or cost overruns, which highly increases the likelihood of emergency special assessments to cover cash shortfalls. 

**Statutory Funding** refers to the mandatory reserve funding levels dictated by specific state or provincial legislation, overriding discretionary board choices. *(Note: While the provided corpus explicitly details Full, Threshold, and Baseline as the three standard modeling choices, it outlines statutory funding through specific jurisdictional examples)* [[~/code/knowledge/wiki/sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines.pdf]] [[~/code/knowledge/wiki/sources/Reserve Study Software for Reserve Specialists | PropFusion]]. For example, under Florida's Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) laws, qualifying associations are legally mandated to maintain a baseline funding plan that keeps the cash balance above zero for eight critical structural components, and unit owners are strictly prohibited from voting to waive or reduce these contributions [[~/code/knowledge/wiki/sources/Florida Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) 2026 | PropFusion]].
*   **Cost & Risk:** The cost to homeowners is dictated entirely by legal compliance and engineering requirements rather than association preference. The risk shifts from strictly financial to legal and market-based: failure to meet statutory funding requirements exposes board members to personal liability and regulatory action, and can render the building uninsurable and ineligible for conventional mortgage financing (non-warrantable), severely depressing property values [[~/code/knowledge/wiki/sources/Florida Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) 2026 | PropFusion]].
