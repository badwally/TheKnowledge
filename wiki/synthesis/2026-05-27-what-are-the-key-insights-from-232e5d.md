# What are the key insights from "meet the new economy, same as the old economy" in the context of AI-native business design for solo founders and tiny teams running on AI substrate? The source describes: moats and their discontents

## Synthesis

Dan Davies’ analysis of the economics of AI in “meet the new economy, same as the old economy” pulls back the curtain on why tech giants are pouring billions into AI. For a solo founder or tiny team designing a business on an AI substrate, this source reveals the underlying economic dynamics of your core suppliers and highlights where you must avoid competing. 

Here are the key insights translated into business design for the AI-native solo founder:

**1. Exploit "Subsidized Intelligence"**
The most critical realization for a solo founder is that **the AI models powering your business are effectively being subsidized by tech monopolies** [1] [[sources/web-2026-05-22-e40]]. Hyperscalers are using the vast, high-margin profits from their existing legacy businesses to fund their AI services, keeping the technology artificially cheap to lock in user loyalty [1] [[sources/web-2026-05-22-e40]]. For a tiny team, this is an incredible advantage: your core labor substrate (AI compute and inference) is being offered at a massive "consumer surplus" [1, 2]. You should aggressively exploit this subsidized intelligence to run your operations at a fraction of what human labor would cost.

**2. Avoid the "Dollar Auction" at the Infrastructure Layer**
Hyperscalers are making massive, staggering capital investments into AI that would not be justifiable as stand-alone businesses [3, 4]. They are doing this purely as a defensive measure to protect their existing monopoly moats (like Google Search) from being disrupted by new entrants [3, 4]. Davies describes this as a "negative sum game" between monopolists and a "dollar auction" where giants light capital on fire out of fear of obsolescence [1, 5]. As a solo founder, you must recognize this dynamic and **never attempt to compete at the model or infrastructure layer**. You cannot outspend terrified monopolies. 

**3. Prepare for the Death of Search-Based Distribution**
As Google and other incumbents migrate to "intelligent search boxes" that provide semantic answers directly in the interface, they are actively working to keep users inside their own walled gardens [2, 6]. Because frontier LLMs turn traditional keyword searches into commodities, relying on traditional SEO to acquire users is increasingly dangerous [7] [[sources/web-2026-05-22-e40]]. Solo founders must engineer new distribution channels and build products that do not rely on outbound clicks from major search engines to survive.

**4. General-Purpose Chat is a Commodity; Niches are Your Moat**
Because tech giants are forced to offer highly capable, natural-language interfaces for free or very cheap to protect their turf, building a generic "chat" wrapper is a guaranteed failure [1, 4]. The baseline of what is available for free to consumers will constantly rise. Therefore, a tiny team's defensibility must come from **building deep, domain-specific workflows and specialized agentic actions** that go far beyond the generalized capabilities a hyperscaler is willing to offer in a standard search interface.
