# What does 'percent funded' mean for a condo reserve fund, how is it calculated under the CAI/APRA national reserve study standards, and how do statutory minimums (Florida full-funding mandate, BC's 10% CRF rule, Ontario's 'adequate fund' standard) and lender overlays (Fannie Mae's reserve-allocation requirement) interact with it

## Synthesis

**Percent funded**, also referred to in industry reports as the "progressive reserve," is the ratio of a condominium's actual (or projected) reserve fund balance to its fully accrued (100% funded) liability balance, expressed as a percentage [1] [[nlm:97fc8200-55c5-433c-853e-b5861dd8ace0]], [2] [[nlm:97fc8200-55c5-433c-853e-b5861dd8ace0]]. For example, if a building's fully accrued reserve target is $100,000 but the actual cash in the reserve fund is $76,000, the fund is 76% funded [2] [[nlm:97fc8200-55c5-433c-853e-b5861dd8ace0]]. 

While the provided sources do not explicitly detail the mathematical formula under the specific "CAI/APRA" acronyms, they outline the established industry benchmarks for evaluating this calculation: [[nlm:97fc8200-55c5-433c-853e-b5861dd8ace0]]
*   **Poor Level (0% to 30% funded):** Often called the "Unfunded or Special Levy Model," communities at this level do not have enough cash to cover expected renewal costs, making sudden special assessments highly common [3] [[nlm:97fc8200-55c5-433c-853e-b5861dd8ace0]], [4] [[nlm:97fc8200-55c5-433c-853e-b5861dd8ace0]].
*   **Fair Level (31% to 69% funded):** The reserve is considered to be in a mid-range, transitional state [4] [[nlm:97fc8200-55c5-433c-853e-b5861dd8ace0]].
*   **Good/Strong Level (70% or higher funded):** The reserve is financially secure, and cash flow problems or special levies are rare [4] [[nlm:97fc8200-55c5-433c-853e-b5861dd8ace0]].

Statutory minimums and lender overlays interact with a building's percent-funded status in distinctly different ways: [[nlm:97fc8200-55c5-433c-853e-b5861dd8ace0]]

**1. Statutory Minimums** [[nlm:97fc8200-55c5-433c-853e-b5861dd8ace0]]
*   **Florida's Full-Funding Mandate (SIRS):** Florida now requires a Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) for eight critical building elements (such as roofs, structural systems, and plumbing) [5] [[sources/web-2026-01-01-361]], [6] [[sources/web-2026-01-01-361]]. As noted in our previous conversation, Florida law eliminates the ability for owners to vote to waive or partially fund these reserves. Consequently, associations are legally forced to maintain a 100% funded trajectory for these specific structural components to ensure life safety.
*   **British Columbia’s 10% CRF Rule:** Under the Strata Property Act, if a strata's Contingency Reserve Fund (CRF) drops below 25% of its operating budget, the corporation is legally required to contribute a minimum of 10% of its annual operating funds to the CRF [7] [[nlm:e6b905ae-3ca5-4d88-a153-087607f2172e]], [8] [[nlm:97fc8200-55c5-433c-853e-b5861dd8ace0]], [9] [[nlm:97fc8200-55c5-433c-853e-b5861dd8ace0]]. However, achieving this legal minimum is **generally inadequate for long-term stability** [10] [[nlm:fb879099-d205-411a-9fb5-6b5448312cec]], [11] [[nlm:fb879099-d205-411a-9fb5-6b5448312cec]]. Merely following the 10% rule keeps the "percent funded" status dangerously low and typically results in guaranteed special levies when major components fail [11] [[nlm:fb879099-d205-411a-9fb5-6b5448312cec]]. 
*   **Ontario’s 'Adequate Fund' Standard:** Ontario requires a Reserve Fund Study (RFS) every three years [12] [[nlm:8714238d-5825-4b73-9a11-72896269f684]], [13] [[nlm:3db1e3ed-443d-4562-8150-bd2102d2c89c]]. Within 120 days of receiving the study, the condo board must propose and implement a funding plan to ensure the reserve fund is "adequate" by the following fiscal year [14] [[nlm:3db1e3ed-443d-4562-8150-bd2102d2c89c]]. Because "adequate" is defined by adherence to the professional RFS recommendations rather than a flat percentage, it legally obligates boards to proactively target a healthy percent-funded status [12] [[nlm:8714238d-5825-4b73-9a11-72896269f684]], [15] [[nlm:8714238d-5825-4b73-9a11-72896269f684]].

**2. Lender Overlays (Fannie Mae)** [[nlm:97fc8200-55c5-433c-853e-b5861dd8ace0]]
Fannie Mae's stringent new overlays effectively override local statutory minimums by dictating whether buyers can secure conventional mortgages. Fannie Mae does not merely look at the "percent funded" ratio; it enforces strict contribution rules to ensure financial viability: [[nlm:97fc8200-55c5-433c-853e-b5861dd8ace0]]
*   **Without an Updated Reserve Study:** Effective January 4, 2027, if an association lacks a recent professional reserve study, Fannie Mae requires a minimum of **15% of the annual budget** to be allocated to reserves (an increase from the previous 10% baseline) [16] [[nlm:f50a3ed0-5f16-4edb-9626-819a772c5af3]], [17] [[nlm:4f978af7-c49d-4a0f-976d-21530dff9f5c]].
*   **With an Updated Reserve Study:** Effective August 3, 2026, Fannie Mae will no longer accept baseline funding models [18] [[nlm:f50a3ed0-5f16-4edb-9626-819a772c5af3]], [19] [[nlm:f1361ace-4a31-4a55-b359-a82d417f296d]]. Instead, lenders must verify that the association's budget reflects the **highest recommended allocation** outlined in their updated reserve study [20] [[nlm:f50a3ed0-5f16-4edb-9626-819a772c5af3]], [19] [[nlm:f1361ace-4a31-4a55-b359-a82d417f296d]]. 

If an association relies on a low statutory minimum (like BC's 10% rule) and fails to adopt the highest recommended funding level, Fannie Mae will designate the building as "ineligible" or "unwarrantable" [21] [[nlm:f50a3ed0-5f16-4edb-9626-819a772c5af3]], [22] [[nlm:f50a3ed0-5f16-4edb-9626-819a772c5af3]]. This freezes access to conventional financing, shrinks the buyer pool, and can immediately depress unit market values by 5% to 30% [23] [[nlm:f50a3ed0-5f16-4edb-9626-819a772c5af3]].
