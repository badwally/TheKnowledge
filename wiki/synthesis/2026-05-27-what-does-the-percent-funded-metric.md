# What does the Percent Funded metric measure in HOA reserve studies, what thresholds indicate healthy versus at-risk reserves, and what are California's requirements when a board borrows from reserve funds

## Synthesis

The **Percent Funded** metric in an HOA reserve study measures the ratio of the association's actual accumulated cash reserves against the estimated value of the accumulated wear and tear of all major components [1-3]. Essentially, it compares the money currently in the bank to the "desired balance" needed to keep pace with the deterioration of the building's infrastructure [3, 4].

Regarding the thresholds for healthy versus at-risk reserves:
*   **Healthy:** The ultimate goal of a "Fully Funded" model is to keep the reserve balance at or near **100% funded** [5, 6].
*   **At-Risk / Poorly Funded:** A reserve balance that falls **below 80%** is generally considered "poorly funded" [7] [[nlm:aeb3b642-7122-4bc9-9ae7-e53237134ea2]]. 
*   **Critical:** If the reserve balance drops **under 50%**, the association is sitting in "critical" territory and is at high risk for relying on sudden special assessments to cover cash shortfalls [7] [[nlm:aeb3b642-7122-4bc9-9ae7-e53237134ea2]]. 

When a board in California needs to temporarily borrow or transfer money from the reserve fund to the general operating fund to cover short-term cash flow needs, it must comply with strict requirements under California Civil Code Section 1365.5 [8, 9]. The requirements include:

*   **Advance Notice:** The board must issue a notice of meeting indicating its intent to consider the transfer. This notice must explicitly state the reasons the transfer is needed, the options for repayment, and whether a special assessment may be considered [9] [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
*   **Written Findings in Minutes:** If the board authorizes the transfer, it must issue a written finding recorded in the meeting minutes that explains why the transfer was necessary and details when and how the money will be repaid to the reserve fund [9] [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
*   **Strict Repayment Timeline:** The borrowed funds must be restored to the reserve account within **one year** of the initial transfer [9] [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]]. The board can temporarily delay this repayment, but only by giving the same level of notice as the initial transfer and making a documented finding that a delay is in the best interest of the community [9] [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
*   **Special Assessments:** The board is legally required to exercise prudent fiscal management and, if necessary, must levy a special assessment to recover the borrowed funds within the legal time limits [9] [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]]. 
*   **Litigation Requirements:** If the reserve funds are borrowed specifically to pay for litigation, the board must notify the members in the very next available mailing and must make a full accounting of those litigation expenses available for inspection at least on a quarterly basis [10] [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
