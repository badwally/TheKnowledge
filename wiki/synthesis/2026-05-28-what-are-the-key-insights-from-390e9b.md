# Do Ozempic or Mounjaro Cause Depression? (Obesity Doctor Explains)

**Channel:** Dr. Spencer Nadolsky

**Views:** 2778  
**Published:** 2025-11-24T22:30:13Z

## Synthesis

**Direct Action on the Dopamine Reward System**
Dr. Spencer Nadolsky confirms that GLP-1 medications exert very powerful effects on the brain's dopamine and reward systems [1] [[sources/yt-naN3oOo5FB0]]. While this is highly beneficial for curbing addictive behaviors like excessive drinking, gambling, or nail-biting, he notes that it is entirely possible for a patient's brain to receive *too much* of this reward-dampening effect [1, 2].

**Anhedonia vs. Depression (The "Zepbound Blahs")**
Dr. Nadolsky clarifies that despite online fears of GLP-1s causing clinical depression or suicidal ideation, the reality of the side effect is usually different [1, 3, 4]. Instead, patients—particularly those on higher doses like 10 to 15 mg of tirzepatide—often experience what he calls the "Zepbound blahs" [2] [[sources/yt-naN3oOo5FB0]]. He clinically defines this as **anhedonia** or a "flat affect" [2] [[sources/yt-naN3oOo5FB0]]. It is not necessarily sadness, but rather a profound lack of emotion and a complete loss of motivation to engage in previously enjoyed hobbies, such as exercising or cooking [2, 5]. 

**Dosing Optimization as the Primary Solution**
To combat this generalized reward dampening, Dr. Nadolsky's first line of action is **dosing optimization through dose de-escalation** [4, 5]. If a patient is experiencing anhedonia on a 15 mg dose, he frequently lowers them to 10 mg [5] [[sources/yt-naN3oOo5FB0]]. He points out a fascinating clinical observation: because the difference in weight loss efficacy between these high doses is relatively minimal, patients rarely regain weight when stepping down [5] [[sources/yt-naN3oOo5FB0]]. In fact, they sometimes *lose* more weight on the lower dose because their dopamine levels recover just enough to restore their natural motivation to exercise and move [5] [[sources/yt-naN3oOo5FB0]].

**Pharmacological Adjuncts for Dopamine Recovery**
In cases where adjusting the dose does not fully resolve the anhedonia, Dr. Nadolsky utilizes an additional optimization strategy: prescribing the medication **bupropion** [5] [[sources/yt-naN3oOo5FB0]]. Because bupropion acts as a dopamine and norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor, it actively leaves more dopamine available in the brain's reward circuits [5] [[sources/yt-naN3oOo5FB0]]. This targeted boost can successfully help patients regain their passion and drive while remaining on their GLP-1 therapy [4, 5].
