# Ozempic, GLP-1 Drugs &amp; The Truth About Weight Loss | Former FDA Chief Explains

**Channel:** Live Well Be Well with Sarah Ann Macklin  
**Duration:** PT56M22S  
**Views:** 5358  
**Published:** 2025-

## Synthesis

**The Battle Between Reward and Aversive Circuits**
Former FDA Chief Dr. David Kessler explains that obesity is fundamentally driven by neural reward circuits that have been hijacked by hyper-palatable, ultra-processed, energy-dense foods [1, 2]. He clarifies that **dopamine is not simply a pleasure molecule, but a "wanting" and learning molecule that locks in environmental and emotional cues** (such as a specific time of night or feelings of unease), creating a powerful, cue-induced compulsion to eat [3, 4]. 

To break this addiction, GLP-1 medications target the brain's aversive circuits—specifically in the hindbrain via the area postrema (the brain's vomiting center) and the nucleus tractus solitarius (NTS) [5] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. By intentionally optimizing and titrating the medication's dose to push a patient right to the **"edge of nausea," the drugs successfully tamp down and overpower the hyperactive reward and addiction circuits**, acting similarly to how food poisoning creates a strong, lasting aversion to a specific meal [5-7].

**The Dangers of "Microdosing" and Vanity Weight Loss**
The host expresses deep concern over the growing trend of individuals **"microdosing" or manipulating doses just to lose the last few pounds for cosmetic reasons**, rather than to treat actual obesity [8] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. Dr. Kessler stresses that these powerful medications were explicitly designed to treat visceral adiposity and severe metabolic dysfunction, not for vanity weight loss among people without metabolic issues [8, 9].

Taking these drugs without proper medical supervision or to achieve cosmetic thinness carries severe risks, including **profound malnourishment and the potential to trigger eating disorders** [10, 11]. Because the medication is so powerful at suppressing the drive to eat, patients often consume fewer than 1,000 calories a day, a drastic reduction that Dr. Kessler warns essentially mimics "forced anorexia" [10] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]].

**Chronic Treatment and Long-Term Behavioral Change**
While pharmaceutical companies have optimized the dosing of these drugs to sustain that critical "edge of nausea," real-world data shows that patients typically only stay on the medication for 8 to 9 months [5, 12]. **Because the drug simply acts to put the food addiction on ice, the addictive reward circuits remain completely intact once the medication is stopped**, leading to almost inevitable weight regain [13] [[nlm:1f3fde93-f23a-42fb-993d-9df14b706863]]. 

Therefore, to optimize long-term success, patients must use the temporary window of silenced cravings to actively change their relationship with food and implement sustainable nutritional and behavioral tools before they ever decide to come off the medication [13, 14].
