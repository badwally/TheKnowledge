# The effect of semaglutide 2.4 mg once weekly on energy intake, appetite, control of eating, and gastric emptying in adults with obesity.

**Authors:** Martin Friedrichsen, Astrid Breitschaft, Sayeh Taday

## Synthesis

While the full text of the specific legacy import by Friedrichsen et al. is not present in the sources, the extensive literature provided details exactly how semaglutide (the active ingredient in Wegovy and Ozempic) impacts energy intake, gastric emptying, and the reward system, which directly informs clinical dosing optimization strategies.

**Prolonged Gastric Emptying and Continuous Satiety**
Naturally occurring GLP-1 is degraded by DPP-4 enzymes in the body in approximately two minutes, making it ineffective for long-term appetite suppression [1] [[nlm:dae8f5db-b213-4ce8-98e5-e4e028c72d5b]]. Semaglutide is chemically engineered to resist this degradation, extending its half-life to a full seven days [2] [[nlm:dae8f5db-b213-4ce8-98e5-e4e028c72d5b]]. This sustained presence delays gastric emptying, keeping food in the stomach longer to send continuous feelings of fullness to the brain, thereby physically restricting the capacity for excess energy intake [3] [[sources/yt-8OihoI4tdPw]].

**Direct Neural Control and Meal Termination**
Semaglutide suppresses appetite almost entirely by acting directly on the central nervous system [4] [[nlm:dae8f5db-b213-4ce8-98e5-e4e028c72d5b]]. Because semaglutide is a large peptide, it bypasses the standard blood-brain barrier by accessing the brain through circumventricular organs, such as the area postrema, where the barrier is naturally weakened [5] [[nlm:dae8f5db-b213-4ce8-98e5-e4e028c72d5b]]. Once in the brain, it targets distributed neural pathways—including the lateral parabrachial nucleus, which is directly involved in controlling meal termination [6] [[sources/pubmed-32213703]].

**Altering the Reward System via GABA Modulation**
Semaglutide deeply infiltrates the brain's reward circuitry to actively suppress the compulsion to consume highly palatable foods and addictive substances [7] [[sources/pubmed-37192005]]. **Research demonstrates that semaglutide increases spontaneous inhibitory postsynaptic currents in the central amygdala and the infralimbic cortex, indicating an enhanced release of the inhibitory neurotransmitter GABA** [7-9]. This inhibitory signaling serves as a "behavioral brake," dampening the anticipatory dopamine response that drives intense cravings and the "wanting" of rewards . 

**Implications for Clinical Dosing Optimization**
The profound neurobiological impact of semaglutide makes personalized dosing optimization essential to maximize therapeutic benefits and minimize adverse effects:
*   **Managing Nausea via Tachyphylaxis:** Because semaglutide activates the area postrema (the brain's vomiting center), patients frequently experience transient nausea [10] [[nlm:dae8f5db-b213-4ce8-98e5-e4e028c72d5b]]. Dosing optimization relies on a biological process called tachyphylaxis, where the receptors adapt and downregulate over time [11] [[nlm:dae8f5db-b213-4ce8-98e5-e4e028c72d5b]]. **By starting at a sub-therapeutic dose and escalating slowly month-by-month, patients can build tolerance and safely reach the maximum therapeutic concentrations required for significant weight loss** [2, 11].
*   **Mitigating Severe Muscle Loss:** Semaglutide so powerfully shuts down energy intake that rapid weight loss frequently ensues [2] [[nlm:dae8f5db-b213-4ce8-98e5-e4e028c72d5b]]. Without proactive lifestyle adjustments, **between 25% and 33% of the total weight lost can be valuable lean muscle mass** [12] [[nlm:dae8f5db-b213-4ce8-98e5-e4e028c72d5b]]. To optimize long-term health outcomes and prevent muscle wasting, clinical dosing must be rigorously paired with adequate protein intake and resistance training [12, 13].
