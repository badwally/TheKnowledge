# What are the key insights from "Façade Inspection & Safety Program (FISP) Filing Instructions" in the context of Condominium and HOA management with an emphasis on integrated long-horizon proactive planning? The source describes: # Façade Inspection & Safety Program (FISP) Filing Instructions

Owners of buildings higher than six (6) stories **must** have exterior walls and appurtenances inspected every five (5) years and a technical façade report **must** be electronically filed with the Department through DOB NOW: *Safety*.

## Synthesis

The Façade Inspection & Safety Program (FISP) Filing Instructions enforce a rigid regulatory framework that forces condominium and HOA boards to integrate exterior building envelope maintenance directly into their long-horizon capital planning. By mandating recurring evaluations and strict technical reporting, the rules effectively eliminate the ability of a board to defer exterior maintenance.

Key insights into how these filing instructions shape proactive capital management include:

**1. The 5-Year Cyclical Capital Baseline**
The instructions mandate that owners of buildings higher than six stories must have their exterior walls and appurtenances inspected every five years by a Qualified Exterior Wall Inspector (QEWI) [1] [[sources/web-2025-01-01-742]]. For long-horizon planning, this legally establishes a non-negotiable, 5-year recurring inspection cycle that must be permanently integrated into the association's reserve fund forecasting. 

**2. Forced Board Accountability and Digital Continuity**
A major challenge in long-term HOA planning is the loss of institutional memory when volunteer boards or property managers turn over. The FISP guidelines address this by requiring building owners to register their own accounts in the Department's eFiling system [2] [[sources/web-2025-01-01-742]]. The owner must explicitly log in to give official consent to the QEWI’s technical reports [2] [[sources/web-2025-01-01-742]]. By routing all documentation through the centralized *DOB NOW: Safety* portal, the building's structural history and compliance data are preserved permanently, ensuring new board members always have access to the building's historical baseline.

**3. Strict Enforcement of Repair Timeframes**
A critical insight for capital planning is how the Department of Buildings scrutinizes the contents of the technical reports. The instructions warn that reports will be rejected if they lack precise timeframes for recommended repairs, clear and current color photographs, sketches of conditions, or documentation of scaffold drop locations [3] [[sources/web-2025-01-01-742]]. For an HOA, this means that if a component is flagged as requiring a repair program, the board cannot simply file the paperwork and indefinitely defer the work to save money. The QEWI’s repair timeframe becomes a strict operational deadline that must be immediately funded and scheduled within the reserve plan.

**4. The "Amended Report" and Mandatory Violation Resolution**
If a building's facade requires repair, the board's long-horizon plan must ensure sufficient liquidity to act quickly. To clear safety flags, the board and their QEWI must file an Amended Report that explicitly details, item-by-item, how the unsafe conditions were corrected [4] [[sources/web-2025-01-01-742]]. Furthermore, the technical review dictates that all open violations and complaints regarding the facade must be fully corrected, work permits must be provided, and the stability of appurtenances must be proven before the Department will accept the report [4] [[sources/web-2025-01-01-742]]. This closes any loopholes for deferred maintenance, forcing associations to proactively maintain their building envelopes to avoid rejected reports and subsequent penalties.
