# Condo Reserve-Study Data Collective — Comprehensive Cross-Domain Case

## Synthesis

**Verdict:** the condo reserve-study market is the strongest available application of the pooled-data mechanism, because reserve-study condition data behaves as a *non-rivalrous* signal like the fraud/AML utilities that are the only durable precedent [[sources/web-2026-04-03-04f]] rather than the competitive substitute that defeats most collectives [[sources/arxiv-2305.16052]] — and a statutory compliance wave supplies demand the general thesis lacks [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]]. This is the applied, cross-domain extension of the foundation synthesis [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].

### 1. The pooled-proprietary-data mechanism and its precedents

A data collective lets otherwise-competing or complementary stakeholders commit proprietary data to a shared model all members use to compete. The mechanism is real but the incentive is fragile: the only durable commercial pattern is the fraud/AML utility, where rivals pool a signal none of them competes on — Early Warning Services (bank-owned) [[sources/web-2026-04-03-04f]] and Cifas (competitor fraud consortium) [[sources/web-2026-05-21-5db]]. Direct substitutes competing in one market have weak-to-negative naive incentives to pool, because contributed data also improves rivals' models [[sources/arxiv-2305.16052]]; the MELLODDY pharma consortium overcame this only because each contributor exclusively owned its task-specific model components and the underlying assay data was structurally complementary [[sources/web-2025-08-18-fa4]].

The transferable lesson for condo: **pool the non-rivalrous signal — asset-condition and component-failure data — not the competitive signal (clients, bids, fees).** The realistic legal form is a fiduciary data cooperative or trust pooling statutory data rights rather than data-as-property [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].

### 2. The condo application — contributors, market, statutory tailwinds, incumbent gap

Three stakeholder types hold complementary slices of ground truth: **engineering firms** hold component condition assessments and replacement schedules; **property managers** hold maintenance logs, work orders, and actual replacement expenditures; **condo corporations / HOA boards** hold reserve-fund financial records and special-assessment outcomes — the consequence layer that validates any forecast [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]]. None competes on degradation rates, so the pooled signal is non-rivalrous by construction.

The incumbent software gap is the wedge: the dominant CINC + SmartProperty "Living Reserve Study" integration pipes live financial actuals but runs a linear scheduled-replacement model with flat inflation and no stochastic risk bands [[sources/web-2023-05-02-683]] [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]]. The leading Canadian AI-native entrant, OctoAI/Eli Report, frames forecasting as deterministic "simple math" and acknowledges failure modes (inflation deviation, regime-switching contributions, disaster acceleration) it does not model probabilistically [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]] [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]]; ReservePath, the only cloud entrant with AI generation, is waitlist-only [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]]. Meanwhile a wave of non-deferrable mandates creates a compliance-forcing tailwind — Florida SB 4-D structural reserve studies and the post-2024 anti-pooling rule [[sources/web-2026-03-09-423]], Washington WUCIOA three-plan disclosure [[sources/web-2026-01-01-361]], and Fannie Mae's tightened reserve-adequacy / 15%-default warrantability rule governing ~75% of US condo mortgages [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]]. A multi-jurisdiction collective producing calibrated probabilistic forecasts has a tailwind pure-software competitors without pooled ground truth cannot replicate.

### 3. Viable business models built on top

Three stacked revenue layers run on the same pooled corpus: (1) a transactional reserve-study production service (~50–100 studies/yr at $8–15K in a bootstrapped Year 1) whose output quality exceeds deterministic incumbents because it is calibrated on pooled data [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]]; (2) a subscription tail converting the one-time study into recurring dashboards and re-forecasting ($99–299/mo per corporation), which is also the data flywheel that keeps improving the engine; (3) a data-services tier selling aggregate benchmarks and failure-rate distributions to insurers, reinsurers, and lenders who need actuarially grounded reserve-adequacy signals [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]] [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].

The insurer/lender channel is the durable one: Fannie Mae governs ~75% of US condo mortgages and tightened standards post-Surfside, making a pooled benchmark directly monetizable as a lender-risk input [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]]. Eli Report's freemium shape (free extraction tier, paid advanced + expert review) signals that baseline extraction is commoditizing while probabilistic forecasting preserves margin [[sources/web-2024-10-07-033]] — and the collective's pooled-failure-data engine is exactly the defensible paid tier.

### 4. The agentic-AI architecture and the why-now thesis

The privacy-preserving substrate that makes pooling safe is mature and production-ready: confidential clean rooms / TEEs [[sources/web-2026-06-03-4ff]], cross-silo federated learning [[sources/arxiv-2206.07284]], and differential privacy [[sources/web-2025-08-21-f21]] let members contribute without exposing raw records. The venture is architected agentic-first — software-led, services-delivered, no hardware in 24 months [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]]. The why-now demand thesis is that agents need trustworthy, domain-grounded, current data to *act* (not just summarize), so a governed pooled corpus becomes agent-ready ground truth.

This is the brief's sharpest tension, stated plainly as a bet: across the foundation's precedent, technical, and industrial inquiries, **no verified commercial precedent surfaced for an AI agent acting on a pooled cross-firm data substrate** — the closest coopetition cases were complementary-data or single-firm, not agentic-on-pool [[sources/web-2025-08-18-fa4]]. The agentic layer is therefore the strongest demand argument and the thinnest evidentiary position simultaneously; it is first-mover greenfield, not a documented trend.

### 5. The policy, legal, and market-structure opening — Canada-first vs US

No US or Canadian regulation compels or funds cross-competitor pooling; Canada's funded AI money is compute, not data infrastructure [[sources/web-2026-06-04-76b]], a gap the C.D. Howe Institute calls the "missing pillar" of data supply chains [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]]. "Why Canada" therefore rests not on subsidy but on three lighter levers: an economy-wide data-mobility right that lets data holders *direct* their data into a collective [[sources/web-2025-11-21-9a5]], a data-sovereignty tradition including Indigenous-led OCAP governance [[sources/web-2025-11-28-252]], and the CAN/DGSI 100-7 national standard for governing data trusts. The US contrast is research-only infrastructure (NAIRR) and otherwise deregulatory [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].

On legal envelope: there is no property right in data, so the pool's value rights are contractual [[sources/web-2019-01-23-bbd]]; the 2023 US DOJ withdrawal of information-sharing safety zones moved antitrust to case-by-case review, while Canada's two-track regime is comparatively clearer — a genuine legal-certainty edge for the Canada-first sequencing [[sources/web-2023-02-14-d0c]] [[sources/web-2024-06-27-57a]]. The condo statutory wave [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]] supplies demand-side urgency the general data-collective thesis lacks.

### 6. Who captures the market and the exit path

The capturer is not the holder of the most data — pooled scale is frequently not a durable moat [[sources/web-2019-05-09-487]]. It is whoever first assembles the member network around the non-rivalrous signal, operates trustworthy neutral fiduciary governance [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]], and integrates into members' workflow. The chief execution risk is cold-start / SME-onboarding — even the well-funded Catena-X data space stalled at the SME tier and needed public funding [[sources/web-2026-06-08-406]]. The exit path runs through the incumbent CAM/reserve-software consolidators who already pipe financial actuals but lack a probabilistic, pooled-data engine — CINC/SmartProperty being the proximate integrator and acquirer-shaped counterparty [[sources/web-2023-05-02-683]] [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].

### 7. Key risks and uncertainty ledger

The load-bearing risks: the **substitutes trap** — if scope drifts to pooling competitive data (clients, bids), the incentive collapses per the coopetition economics [[sources/arxiv-2305.16052]]; the **unproven agentic-demand bet** — zero commercial precedent for agent-on-pool [[sources/web-2025-08-18-fa4]]; **no policy tailwind as compulsion** — the Canadian levers are governance scaffolding, not funding [[sources/web-2026-06-04-76b]]; and **cold-start fragility** [[sources/web-2026-06-08-406]]. Domain-specific additions: licensed-engineer (P.Eng.) sign-off and downstream-model liability for a forecast that proves wrong.

Open questions (unresolved by the current corpus): Does agentic demand for pooled domain data materialize commercially? Is the non-rivalrous condition-data framing sufficient to get competing engineering firms to contribute at scale? What is the in-force scope of Canada's data-mobility right? Will the statutory tailwind convert to paid pooled-data demand before incumbents add a probabilistic layer?
