## Synthesis

**1. The Severe Volatility of Linear Deterministic Funding**
The October 16, 2024 Draft 1 report starkly illustrates the catastrophic financial shocks caused by traditional deterministic math. To keep the reserve fund solvent against upcoming multi-million-dollar expenditures (such as a $2.11 million spend projected for exactly 2027), the engineer's "Immediately Adequately Funded" scenario mandates a massive **155.4% fee increase** in a single year, jumping from a $320,000 annual contribution in 2025 to $817,339 in 2026 [1-3]. Even the alternative "Gradual Funding Increase" plan is highly punishing, forcing three consecutive years of **60.1% year-over-year increases** [4] [[nlm:814902dd-e64e-4881-90ab-57fd1e00045b]]. **AI-native probabilistic platforms solve this "jagged curve" problem** by utilizing Monte Carlo simulations and stochastic financial modeling to intelligently smooth contribution schedules over time, preventing boards from having to levy extreme, reactive assessments [5, 6].

**2. Brittle Flat Financial Assumptions**
Draft 1 calculates its 10- and 25-year cash flow projections using rigidly flat macroeconomic assumptions: a **2.5% inflation rate and a 2.0% interest rate** [7, 8]. This static approach represents a major vulnerability because it fails to capture real-world economic volatility. For example, recent market realities have seen construction costs escalate at 6–9% while deterministic linear plans blindly assumed a flat 2–4% [9] [[nlm:4e003e20-1a95-412b-8965-b2bccc9fa338]]. Modern AI-native engines replace these rigid inputs with dynamic models that stress-test funding against varying economic climates and inflation cycles, ensuring the long-term forecast isn't eroded by sudden cost spikes [9] [[nlm:4e003e20-1a95-412b-8965-b2bccc9fa338]].

**3. The Flaw of Uncalibrated Single-Point Estimates**
The engineering report forecasts exact, single-point dollar amounts and dates for massive component failures. For example, it predicts the North Podium waterproofing replacement will cost exactly $1,355,306 in 2027 and $1,389,189 in 2028 [10, 11]. Because physical infrastructure actually deteriorates on a probability curve rather than failing on a guaranteed fixed date, AI-native platforms abandon these deterministic single-line forecasts [12] [[nlm:03d894af-7f4b-48a6-aaa2-f6aeb9efaac1]]. Instead, they use Weibull-based reliability modeling to output **calibrated uncertainty bands (P10/P50/P90)** [6, 13]. This provides boards with a realistic statistical probability of failure timing and cost rather than forcing them to hoard cash for a single, guessed year [12, 14].
