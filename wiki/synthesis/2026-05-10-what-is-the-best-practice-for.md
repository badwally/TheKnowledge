# WHAT IS THE BEST PRACTICE FOR PREDICTIVE MAINTENANCE FOR CONDO BUILDINGS WITH >100 UNITS IN CANADA

## Synthesis

The best practice for predictive maintenance (PdM) in Canadian condominiums with over 100 units requires abandoning static, calendar-based maintenance in favor of a continuous, data-driven approach that integrates live building telemetry with stochastic financial forecasting. 

To implement a defensible and highly effective predictive maintenance program, large condo corporations should adopt the following framework:

**1. Target the "Big Six" Capital Components**
For a 100+ unit building, predictive efforts should not be wasted on low-variance items like paint or lobby finishes. Instead, prioritize the six high-variance components that dominate reserve fund expenditures and trigger massive special assessments: **roof systems, the building envelope, plumbing risers, the HVAC central plant, elevators, and the parking deck/podium structure** [1, 2].

**2. Deploy Non-Invasive IoT and Sensor Fusion**
Instead of relying on the mandated 3-to-5-year visual inspections dictated by provincial Reserve Fund Studies (such as BC's Depreciation Reports or Ontario's RFS), buildings must transition to continuous monitoring [3-6]. 
*   **Leverage LoRaWAN and Universal Gateways:** To overcome the technical fragmentation of older buildings, use universal gateways (like BACnet or Modbus) and wireless LoRaWAN sensors that can penetrate concrete and steel without requiring expensive new cable runs [7-11]. [[sources/web-2025-08-01-8c2]]
*   **Focus on Shared Infrastructure:** Rather than installing sensors in individual units—which requires high friction and 100 individual owner approvals—install vibration and temperature sensors on shared chillers and boilers, door cycle counters on elevators, and flow/pressure meters on central plumbing risers [12-14].

**3. Implement Machine Learning for Fault Detection and Diagnostics (FDD)**
Do not rely on simple rule-based alarms. Best practices dictate using supervised machine learning algorithms trained on high-resolution telemetry to identify complex, hidden anomalies before catastrophic failure occurs [15-18].
*   **Utilize XGBoost:** Research on high-resolution building datasets demonstrates that **Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost)** is the most effective model for building systems, achieving up to **95% accuracy and 94% recall** in detecting critical faults [19-22]. 
*   **Detect Systemic Inefficiencies:** These models can cross-reference multiple data points (e.g., correlating CO2 or computer-vision occupancy data with HVAC damper positions) to predict exact failure probabilities and flag massive energy-wasting faults like `Damper_Open_No_Occupancy` [23-25].

**4. Mitigate False Positives via Digital Twins and Seasonal Adjustments**
A major risk in predictive maintenance is generating false alarms due to sudden weather shifts or holiday occupancy variations. To prevent this, integrate **seasonal adjustment mechanisms** and adaptive learning algorithms that recalibrate the system's sensitivity based on the time of year [26, 27]. Furthermore, test these predictive algorithms within a **Digital Twin**—a virtual replica of the physical building—to simulate environmental stresses and validate the models before applying them to live building operations [28] [[nlm:92443269-6cd6-4634-8219-6a844f48b32f]].

**5. Ingest CMMS Work-Order History**
Historical repair data is the best leading indicator of component degradation [29] [[nlm:6fb7af6b-708c-421a-adfe-2e1af9a41997]]. A best-in-class program automatically extracts recurring maintenance logs from Computerized Maintenance Management Systems (CMMS) [30, 31]. This historical truth acts as a covariate, allowing the software to adjust a component's "effective age" and shift its baseline lifespan using the **Weibull distribution** to accurately model its "wear-out" phase [32, 33].

**6. Connect Predictive Outputs to Stochastic Financial Smoothing**
The ultimate goal of predictive maintenance in a condo setting is protecting the owners' financial equity. The operational insights generated by the PdM platform must feed directly into the condominium's capital reserve model [34] [[nlm:f1361ace-4a31-4a55-b359-a82d417f296d]]. By combining these highly accurate failure probabilities with **Monte Carlo simulations and Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) optimization**, boards can proactively "smooth" or ramp up owner contributions years in advance [6, 35-37]. This fully eliminates the "reactive crisis" model of governance and prevents the catastrophic special assessments that render buildings uninsurable or unsellable under strict new lender guidelines [34, 38, 39].
