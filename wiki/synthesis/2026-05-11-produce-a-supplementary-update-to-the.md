**Supplementary Update: BC Housing Maintenance Matters Integration**

### (a) Roofing Inspection Cadence and Service-Life Intervals
Roofing systems must be inspected twice a year: in the spring to address any winter damage, and in the fall to prepare for the upcoming winter snow and rain [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-02]]. Furthermore, unscheduled inspections should occur following extreme weather events—such as high winds, extreme rain, or hail—as well as after any construction has taken place on the roof [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-02]]. Depending on the roof design, construction, material type, and environmental exposure, roofing service-life expectancies generally range from 10 years to over 30 years [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-02]]. 

### (b) Deck and Balcony Membrane Replacement Intervals
Decks and balconies require professional inspections at least every two years for both new and existing buildings [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-06]]. For new buildings, inspections should also take place before the end of year one and year two to detect potential problems stemming from structural movement or wood shrinkage [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-06]]. Deck surfaces, drains, and gutters should be cleaned at least twice a year to remove leaves and debris, preventing clogging and water build-up [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-06]]. Membrane replacement intervals vary by material type [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-06]]:
*   **Liquid-Applied Urethane:** Usually has an expected service life of 10 years, though this timeframe can be extended with regular maintenance, lower traffic, and less exposure to the elements [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-06]].
*   **Sheet-Applied Vinyl (PVC):** Typically lasts 10 to 15 years, heavily dependent on the degree of sun exposure, wetting, and general wear and tear [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-06]].

### (c) Building Envelope Maintenance Program Structure
Effective building envelope maintenance operates on a "Plan-Do-Check-Adjust" cyclical pattern, which allows strata corporations to refine their plans as they learn more about their building's specific renewal needs, ultimately resulting in an efficient and reliable program over time [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]]. A successful program requires clear role definitions [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]]:
*   **Program Administrator:** Serves as the primary liaison for the maintenance team, coordinates the activities of contractors and consultants, arranges access, tracks progress, and provides budgets and written annual reports to the strata corporation [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
*   **Homeowners:** Responsible for providing access to their homes with reasonable notice so personnel can execute tasks without undue interference, and are expected to promptly notify the council or administrator of any identified problems [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
*   **Consultants/Specialists:** Retained when comprehensive planning is needed, technical drawings and specifications are required, the plan needs to be updated, or the required maintenance spans more than one trade contract [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
