## Synthesis

**1. The Brittleness of Flat Macroeconomic Escalators**
The 10-year projected expenditures spreadsheet prominently hardcodes a static **"Inflation Rate (%) = 2.5%"** as the foundational assumption for its entire analysis timeframe [1] [[nlm:754250f7-4187-4551-b74b-0e18c4178d13]]. This rigid structure forces analysts to apply a single, unyielding escalator across a decade of complex capital projects, ignoring real-world economic volatility. An AI-native approach fundamentally abandons these flat percentages, deploying instead regime-switching cost-escalation models that explicitly simulate construction inflation, labor-supply shocks, and material-cost cycles to continually stress-test the funding plan against shifting economic climates [2] [[nlm:03d894af-7f4b-48a6-aaa2-f6aeb9efaac1]].

**2. The Illusion of Single-Point, Column-Based Failure Dates**
The spreadsheet forces continuous structural deterioration into rigid, exact years—for example, plotting a massive **$1,290,000 North Podium waterproofing job precisely into 2036 and 2037** [3] [[nlm:754250f7-4187-4551-b74b-0e18c4178d13]], and locking a **$250,000 balcony repair allowance onto a strict 10-year cycle** starting in 2026 [1] [[nlm:754250f7-4187-4551-b74b-0e18c4178d13]]. Because deterministic models force a single point estimate for component failure into a specific spreadsheet column, boards are trapped into binary financial planning against arbitrary fixed dates [4] [[nlm:03d894af-7f4b-48a6-aaa2-f6aeb9efaac1]]. AI-native engines replace these rigid point estimates with continuous statistical probability distributions, utilizing Weibull and lognormal survival models to generate calibrated uncertainty bands (P10/P50/P90) that reflect the true statistical risk of failure over the horizon [2, 4, 5].

**3. Generic Age-Based "Cycles" vs. Ground-Truth Deterioration**
The spreadsheet dictates rigid lifespans for every building element under its "Cycle" column, assigning a static **25-year cycle for phased exterior sealant replacement** and a **45-year cycle for operable sliding window assemblies** [6] [[nlm:754250f7-4187-4551-b74b-0e18c4178d13]]. This generic, age-based methodology fundamentally ignores a building's real-time operational wear and tear. An AI-native platform solves this by directly integrating with a building's live Computerized Maintenance Management System (CMMS) work-order data [2] [[nlm:03d894af-7f4b-48a6-aaa2-f6aeb9efaac1]]. Instead of blindly relying on an industry-average "cycle" column, the AI actively updates component survival curves using ground-truth maintenance logs, shifting from static guesses to continuous, building-specific condition monitoring [2, 7].

**4. Budgeting for Built-In Document Obsolescence**
At the bottom of the expenditure projections under "Consulting Services," there is a recurring explicit line item budgeting **$6,500 for a "Reserve Fund Study Update" on a strict 5-year cycle** (e.g., 2030, 2035, 2040) [1, 8]. This line item exposes the core limitation of the traditional engineering model: the condominium is explicitly paying for a static snapshot that immediately begins degrading in accuracy the moment it is finalized. An AI-native approach transforms capital planning from a static compliance document into a living software subscription [7] [[nlm:4e003e20-1a95-412b-8965-b2bccc9fa338]]. By continuously ingesting live operational data, vendor costs, and shifting economic variables, the forecast remains a perfectly synchronized, real-time asset, mathematically updating the posterior data as new evidence lands and breaking free from the 5-year statutory blind spot [2, 7].
