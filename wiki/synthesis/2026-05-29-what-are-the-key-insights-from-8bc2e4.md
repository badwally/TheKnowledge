# What are the key insights from "Choosing a depreciation report provider for a strata" in the context of AI-native probabilistic reserve studies for condominium associations under regulated reserve-study mandates? The source describes: Some suggestions for choosing a depreciation report provider.

## Synthesis

**1. The Regulatory "Human-in-the-Loop" Mandate**
British Columbia’s regulations explicitly mandate that depreciation reports can only be legally provided by individuals registered in one of six designated professional groups, such as professional engineers, architects, applied science technologists, certified reserve planners, accredited appraisers, or professional quantity surveyors [1-3]. This regulatory boundary dictates that **AI-native platforms must operate as tech-enabled services rather than fully autonomous software** [1] [[nlm:6b6d27e2-1f39-4fc6-99d9-d280802b3d98]]. While an AI engine executes the complex probabilistic forecasting, a licensed human analyst from one of these designated professions must remain in the loop to review the output and provide the statutory sign-off required by the province [1-3].

**2. The Provincial Push for Electronic Access and "Living" Documents**
When advising strata boards on how to evaluate a provider, the province explicitly suggests asking candidates: "Are materials electronically available for easy access and updating?" [4] [[nlm:6b6d27e2-1f39-4fc6-99d9-d280802b3d98]]. This question highlights a major recognized weakness in the traditional consulting model, which typically delivers static PDF reports that immediately begin to age. **Modern AI-native platforms natively answer this provincial mandate by replacing static documents with interactive digital dashboards**, which dramatically reduces the friction and cost of the mandatory five-year update cycle [4, 5].

**3. The Demand for Highly Customized Cash Flow Models and Dynamic Scenario Adjustments**
The guidelines encourage strata boards to evaluate providers based on the degree to which they will customize the report, specifically highlighting the need for customized "phasing of major expenditures" and "cash flow models which reflect the strata corporation's circumstances" [4] [[nlm:6b6d27e2-1f39-4fc6-99d9-d280802b3d98]]. Furthermore, the province advises boards to ask: "What are the post-presentation services? Can requests for changes in replacement scheduling be made following the initial presentation? If so, is there a fee involved?" [4] [[nlm:6b6d27e2-1f39-4fc6-99d9-d280802b3d98]]. While legacy deterministic consultants often charge extra fees and require slow, manual recalculations for "what-if" scenarios, **AI-native platforms natively deploy stochastic financial optimization** [4] [[nlm:6b6d27e2-1f39-4fc6-99d9-d280802b3d98]]. This allows boards to dynamically adjust schedules and instantly visualize customized funding outcomes without waiting or paying for manual engineering revisions [4] [[nlm:6b6d27e2-1f39-4fc6-99d9-d280802b3d98]].

**4. Algorithmic Scaling for Varying Building Complexities**
The source notes that the knowledge and expertise required to prepare a report for a simple six-plex is considerably different from what is needed for a complex high-rise condo tower with its own power-generating plant, sections, and an underground parkade [6] [[nlm:6b6d27e2-1f39-4fc6-99d9-d280802b3d98]]. While traditional engineering firms often need to manually assemble a specialized "team of people" to bridge this gap, **an AI-native probabilistic engine systematically solves it by utilizing data models keyed to specific structure-type cohorts** [6, 7]. This ensures the component life expectancies and funding models automatically scale to match the precise structural complexity of the building being analyzed, from townhomes to massive residential towers [6, 7].
