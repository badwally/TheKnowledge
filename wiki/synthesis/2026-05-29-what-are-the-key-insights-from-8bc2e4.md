# What are the key insights from "Choosing a depreciation report provider for a strata" in the context of AI-native probabilistic reserve studies for condominium associations under regulated reserve-study mandates? The source describes: Some suggestions for choosing a depreciation report provider.

## Synthesis

**1. The Regulatory "Human-in-the-Loop" Mandate**
In British Columbia, the government strictly dictates that depreciation reports can only be legally provided by individuals registered in one of six designated professional groups, such as a Professional Engineer, Architect, Applied Science Technologist, Certified Reserve Planner, Accredited Appraiser, or Professional Quantity Surveyor [1-4]. **This regulatory boundary validates the strategic necessity for AI-native platforms to operate as tech-enabled services rather than fully autonomous software.** While an AI engine can execute complex probabilistic forecasts and optimize funding, a licensed human analyst must remain in the loop to review the output, ensure legal compliance, and provide the statutory sign-off required by the province [3, 4]. 

**2. The Provincial Push for Electronic Access and "Living" Documents**
When advising strata boards on how to evaluate a provider, the province explicitly suggests asking candidates: "Are materials electronically available for easy access and updating?" [5] [[nlm:6b6d27e2-1f39-4fc6-99d9-d280802b3d98]]. **This exposes a major recognized weakness in the traditional consulting model, which typically delivers static PDF reports that immediately begin to age.** Modern AI-native platforms directly answer this provincial mandate by replacing static documents with interactive digital dashboards that seamlessly integrate with live operating data, drastically reducing the friction and cost of the mandatory five-year update cycle [5, 6].

**3. The Demand for Highly Customized Cash Flow Models and Dynamic Scenario Adjustments**
The guidelines encourage strata boards to evaluate providers based on "To what degree is the provider going to customize the report," specifically highlighting the need for "phasing of major expenditures" and "cash flow models which reflect the strata corporation's circumstances" [5] [[nlm:6b6d27e2-1f39-4fc6-99d9-d280802b3d98]]. Furthermore, the province advises boards to ask: "What are the post-presentation services? Can requests for changes in replacement scheduling be made following the initial presentation? If so, is there a fee involved?" [5] [[nlm:6b6d27e2-1f39-4fc6-99d9-d280802b3d98]]. **While legacy deterministic consultants often charge extra fees and require slow, manual recalculations for "what-if" scenarios, AI-native platforms natively deploy stochastic financial optimization and interactive sliders.** This allows boards to dynamically adjust schedules and instantly visualize customized funding outcomes without manual engineering revisions.

**4. Algorithmic Scaling for Varying Building Complexities**
The source notes that the knowledge and expertise required to prepare a report for a simple six-plex is considerably different from what is needed for a complex high-rise condo tower with its own power-generating plant, sections, and an underground parkade [7] [[nlm:6b6d27e2-1f39-4fc6-99d9-d280802b3d98]]. While traditional engineering firms often need to manually assemble a specialized team to bridge this gap [8] [[nlm:6b6d27e2-1f39-4fc6-99d9-d280802b3d98]], **an AI-native probabilistic engine systematically solves it by utilizing Bayesian hierarchical priors keyed to specific structure-type cohorts.** This ensures the data models and component life expectancies automatically scale to match the precise structural complexity of the building being analyzed.
