# What are the key insights from "PRESS RELEASES" in the context of Condominium and HOA management with an emphasis on integrated long-horizon proactive planning? The source describes: **January 7, 2026**

**Florida DBPR Highlights 2025 Accomplishments that Protect Floridians and Support a Growing Economy**

*Consumer protection, enforcement, modernization, and more than $730 million supporting key statewide priorities.*

**TALLAHASSEE, Fla. – **The Florida Department of Business

## Synthesis

**1. The Digitization of Compliance and Institutional Memory**
A major hurdle in long-horizon planning is the loss of records and data when volunteer boards turn over. The DBPR addressed this by actively enforcing the HB 913 (2025) requirement that condominium associations create and maintain centralized online accounts [1] [[nlm:923bd700-002b-439e-b27f-908b3c32bc47]]. By migrating key condominium reporting to these online accounts, the state has simplified compliance and significantly improved data accuracy [2] [[nlm:923bd700-002b-439e-b27f-908b3c32bc47]]. By late 2025, over 16,104 associations had successfully created these mandatory accounts, ensuring a permanent digital repository for required structural and financial reporting [1] [[nlm:923bd700-002b-439e-b27f-908b3c32bc47]].

**2. Operationalizing the Building Safety Program**
The state has transitioned from simply passing safety laws to building the administrative apparatus required to enforce them. The Florida Building Commission officially approved the Building Safety Program, establishing the minimum technical requirements for mandatory milestone inspections directly within the Florida Building Code [3] [[nlm:923bd700-002b-439e-b27f-908b3c32bc47]]. The DBPR characterizes this program as an important safeguard for aging structures, ensuring that long-horizon physical condition assessments become a permanent, regulated fixture in building management rather than an optional exercise [3] [[nlm:923bd700-002b-439e-b27f-908b3c32bc47]].

**3. A Massive Scale-Up in Board Education**
Because recent statutes require volunteer boards to execute highly complex financial and engineering plans, the DBPR recognized a severe knowledge gap and undertook a massive educational campaign. To help constituents understand the strict new requirements under HB 1021 (2024) and HB 913 (2025), the Division of Condominiums, Timeshares and Mobile Homes (CTMH) hosted or participated in 92 educational sessions, reaching over 41,000 attendees across the state [1, 4]. Furthermore, the DBPR expanded access to its free Board Member Certification course in both English and Spanish, ensuring boards have the necessary training to fulfill their fiduciary duties and execute multi-decade capital planning [4] [[nlm:923bd700-002b-439e-b27f-908b3c32bc47]].

**4. Financial Protection Through Dispute Resolution and Vendor Enforcement**
Executing a long-term capital plan requires protecting the association's reserve funds from legal drains and bad actors. The DBPR heavily utilized its Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) program to resolve conflicts early, completing 167 successful mediations that recovered over $1.5 million while avoiding costly litigation for communities [5] [[nlm:923bd700-002b-439e-b27f-908b3c32bc47]]. Additionally, the state conducted coordinated sweeps to crack down on unlicensed contractors [6] [[nlm:923bd700-002b-439e-b27f-908b3c32bc47]]. This enforcement is critical for proactive planning, as it protects HOAs from wasting their hard-earned reserve funds on substandard, illegal, or non-compliant repair work [6] [[nlm:923bd700-002b-439e-b27f-908b3c32bc47]].
