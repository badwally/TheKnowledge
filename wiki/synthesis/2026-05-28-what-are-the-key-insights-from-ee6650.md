## Synthesis

**1. The Severe Volatility of Deterministic Funding Schedules**
The Draft 2 report acutely demonstrates the extreme financial shocks caused by linear deterministic math. To satisfy statutory requirements and avoid a deficit, the engineer's "Immediately Adequately Funded" scenario mandates a devastating 72.6% spike in annual contributions in a single year, forcing the requirement to jump from $320,000 in 2025 to $552,390 in 2026 [1-4]. The alternative "Gradual Funding Increase" option is similarly punishing, forcing 15.8% year-over-year increases for five consecutive years to catch up to the funding requirements [5-7]. **AI-native probabilistic platforms solve this "jagged curve" problem by utilizing stochastic financial optimization algorithms**, which mathematically smooth the contribution schedule across thousands of Monte Carlo simulations to balance statutory compliance with long-term fee stability.

**2. Brittle Flat Macroeconomic Assumptions**
The study calculates its entire 25-year financial projection using rigidly flat assumptions: specifically, a 2.5% inflation rate and a 2.0% interest rate [3, 6, 8, 9]. This deterministic approach represents a major vulnerability, as it completely fails to capture real-world macroeconomic volatility, such as sudden construction-cost escalations or labor supply shocks. **Modern AI-native platforms abandon these static 2.5% escalators**, instead deploying regime-switching cost-escalation models that dynamically stress-test the funding plan against shifting economic climates.

**3. Uncalibrated Single-Point Estimates**
The report relies on uncalibrated, single-point estimates, predicting exact dollar amounts and fixed replacement years for major components—such as forecasting the main and penthouse roof replacements for exactly 2030 at precisely $452,563 [10, 11]. Physical infrastructure actually deteriorates on a probability distribution influenced by usage and environmental stress, not on a guaranteed fixed date. **An AI-native platform replaces these rigid guesses with calibrated uncertainty bands (P10/P50/P90)**, using reliability modeling to provide boards with a continuous, realistic probability distribution of infrastructure failure over a 30-year horizon.

**4. The 5-Year Static "Snapshot" Limitation**
The document explicitly notes that its methodology is designed to satisfy the regulatory requirement to update the study "every five years, with site inspections" [12] [[nlm:4b881bbc-063d-4404-9f24-0f703cabc1c8]]. This highlights the inherent limitation of the traditional consulting model: condominium boards pay for a static PDF snapshot that immediately begins degrading in accuracy between inspection cycles. **An AI-native approach transforms this rigid 5-year cycle into a continuous-monitoring software platform**, automatically ingesting live operating budgets, maintenance records, and work orders to ensure the capital forecast remains a continuously updated, real-time reality.
