# Per Bylund

## Summary

Austrian economist based in Tulsa, Oklahoma; author of "How to Think about the Economy: A Primer" (2022, Mises Institute), a deliberately short book aimed at economic literacy by making Austrian economic theory accessible to the general public [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].

## Key facts

- Author of "How to Think about the Economy: A Primer" (2022), published by the Mises Institute under Creative Commons Attribution NonCommercial 4.0 International License, ISBN 978-1-61016-755-0 [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- Based in Tulsa, Oklahoma as of the book's writing (July 2022) [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- Dedicates the book "To Carl Menger, Ludwig von Mises, and the other giants upon whose shoulders I have the great honor of standing" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- Describes the book's aim as "economic literacy" and states it was "intentionally kept very short to be inviting rather than intimidating, as economics books typically are" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- Argues that the economy is "people acting and interacting" and "little or nothing else," and that what we typically think of as the economy — resources, machines, businesses, jobs — "are all means to ends" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- Argues that the economy has immutable "laws" and that economics over the past three centuries has been about "identifying, learning, and understanding those laws" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- Structures the book in three parts: Economics (Chapters 1–3), Market (Chapters 4–7), and Intervention (Chapters 8–9), plus a conclusion titled "Action and Interaction" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- Benefited from feedback on draft manuscripts from Porter Burkett, Susanne Bylund, Richard Gajan, David Gordon, Jonathan Newman, and Mikael Nordin [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
