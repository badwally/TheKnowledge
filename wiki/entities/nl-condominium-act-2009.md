# Newfoundland & Labrador Condominium Act, 2009

## Summary

The Newfoundland & Labrador Condominium Act, 2009 is the Province of Newfoundland & Labrador's primary condominium statute and the authorizing legislation under which the Newfoundland & Labrador Condominium Regulations (NLR 80/11) were promulgated by Order in Council O.C. 2011-233 (filed September 13, 2011) under the authority of section 88 of the Act [[sources/web-2011-08-18-1e7]]. The Act establishes the reserve-fund-study renewal cadence (subsection 49(5)) that NLR 80/11 operationalizes through prescribed content, method, conflict-of-interest, and qualified-preparer requirements [[sources/web-2011-08-18-1e7]]. NL's Act sits alongside the New Brunswick Condominium Property Act (c16.05, effective 2010-01-01) and the Nova Scotia Condominium Act (amendments proclaimed May 23, 2023) in the Atlantic-Canada statutory cluster framing the condo-capital-infra Canada-first GTM sequence under ADR-0004 [[sources/web-2017-07-26-1f3]] [[sources/web-2018-03-31-c63]] [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].

## Key facts

- Newfoundland & Labrador's primary condominium statute, cited in NLR 80/11 as the source of the regulation-making authority used by Order in Council O.C. 2011-233 [[sources/web-2011-08-18-1e7]].
- Section 88 of the Act is the regulation-making authority under which NLR 80/11 was promulgated [[sources/web-2011-08-18-1e7]].
- NLR 80/11 was filed September 13, 2011 and comes into force on the date the Newfoundland & Labrador Condominium Act, 2009 is proclaimed [[sources/web-2011-08-18-1e7]].
- Subsection 49(5) of the Act sets the schedule by which the reserve-fund status certificate prepared under NLR 80/11 section 4(7) must identify when a new reserve fund study is required to be completed — making subsection 49(5) the statutory renewal-cadence anchor for the Newfoundland & Labrador reserve-fund-study regime [[sources/web-2011-08-18-1e7]].
- The parallel Atlantic-Canada statute, the New Brunswick Condominium Property Act (c16.05), took effect on January 1, 2010 — roughly two years before NLR 80/11's 2011 promulgation — and introduced mandatory reserve funds, mandatory reserve fund studies for corporations with more than 10 units, a 30-year forward forecast horizon, a 10-year primary study cadence with 3-year updates, and a 10-day cooling-off period for buyers [[sources/web-2017-07-26-1f3]] [[sources/web-2018-03-31-c63]].
- The NB regime is additionally two-tier: condominium corporations with fewer than 11 units must maintain a reserve fund equal to 100 percent of the annual operating budget of the corporation (or a greater percentage if specified in the corporation's bylaws) in lieu of obtaining a reserve fund study, while corporations with 11 or more units must obtain a full reserve fund study — a unit-count-keyed two-tier structure not surfaced as part of the NL reserve-fund-study regime in either the NL Condominium Act, 2009 or NLR 80/11's prescribed-contents framework [[sources/web-2018-03-31-c63]].
- The CMHC NB provincial fact sheet additionally documents that NB reserve funds are tapped to pay for substantial alterations and improvements to common elements approved by 60%-vote of common-element ownership — a reserve-scope feature not present in NL's NLR 80/11 financial-analysis framing and a NB-specific divergence within the Atlantic-Canada cluster [[sources/web-2018-03-31-c63]].
- CMHC's NB provincial fact sheet additionally documents an NB conversion-condominium disclosure asymmetry — developers of conversion condominiums must provide buyers the most current building inspection report if the building has 10 units or less and the most current reserve fund study if the building has 11 units or more — a unit-count-keyed disclosure asymmetry without direct analog in NL's NLR 80/11 disclosure regime, where the reserve-fund status certificate requirement under section 4(7) applies uniformly across reserve-fund-study-eligible corporations [[sources/web-2018-03-31-c63]] [[sources/web-2011-08-18-1e7]].
- The third statutory pillar in the Atlantic-Canada cluster, the Nova Scotia Condominium Act, was amended by proclamation on May 23, 2023 (amendments introduced March 25, 2023) — the amendments require condominium corporations to undertake more frequent reserve-fund studies to help corporations plan for future capital repairs, alongside developer-to-elected-board transition rules, modernized voting methods including email balloting, and consumer-protection provisions (rental-agreement disclosure to the board; clear bare-land-condominium advertising) — making NS the cluster's cadence-tightening outlier alongside NB's stable 10-year-with-3-year-updates regime and NL's subsection 49(5)-anchored cadence under NLR 80/11 [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].
