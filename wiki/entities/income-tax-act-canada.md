# Income Tax Act (Canada)

## Summary

The *Income Tax Act* (Canada) is the federal statute that governs Canadian income taxation. Several provisions of the Act are directly load-bearing for the tax treatment of Canadian condominium corporations: paragraph 149(1)(*l*) (the non-profit organization exemption from Part I tax), paragraph 150(1)(*a*) (the annual-filing obligation that applies to all corporations regardless of taxable status), paragraph 20(1)(*a*) (capital cost allowance), paragraph 20(1)(*aa*) (landscaping deduction), and paragraphs 20(1)(*qq*) and 20(1)(*rr*) (disability-related modifications) [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]]. The Act is supported by the *Income Tax Regulations*, which include Class 1 of Schedule II (the CCA class for condominium-unit buildings acquired after 1987) and subsections 1100(11), 1100(14), 1101(1ac) and 1101(1ae) (rental-property CCA restrictions and the separate-class rule) [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].

## Key facts

- Paragraph 149(1)(*l*) provides the non-profit organization exemption under which most residential condominium corporations qualify and are exempt from Part I tax [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Paragraph 150(1)(*a*) requires all corporations, including condominium corporations, to file an income tax return each year, even if exempt from Part I tax [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Paragraph 20(1)(*a*) authorizes capital cost allowance deductions [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Paragraph 20(1)(*aa*) authorizes deduction of landscaping costs [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Paragraphs 20(1)(*qq*) and 20(1)(*rr*) permit current-year deduction of certain capital expenditures for disability-related devices or building modifications listed in sections 8800 and 8801 of the Regulations [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Class 1 of Schedule II of the *Income Tax Regulations* is the default capital cost allowance class for condominium-unit building portions acquired after 1987; pre-1988 acquisitions may have qualified for Class 3, Class 6, Class 31, or Class 32 [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Subsection 1100(11) of the Regulations restricts the CCA that may be claimed on rental properties, preventing creation or increase of a net rental loss [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Subsection 1100(14) defines 'rental property' for the purposes of the subsection 1100(11) restriction [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Subsection 1101(1ac) requires the establishment of a separate CCA class for each rental property with a capital cost of at least $50,000; if a taxpayer owns two or more units or lots in the same building with aggregate capital cost ≥ $50,000, all such units in the same building are considered a single rental property in a separate prescribed class [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Amended by S.C. 1994, c. 7 Sch. VIII (1993, c. 24), reflected in the changes to the Act referenced in the IT-304R2 explanation of changes [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
