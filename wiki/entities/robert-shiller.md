# Robert Shiller

## Summary

Economist whose 2019 book Narrative Economics — building on earlier 1984 work — argues that economists must look beyond typical economic indicators to incorporate the contagion of stories that affect individual and collective economic behavior; Bhargava, Lou, Ozik, Sadka, and Whitmore (2022) build their entire empirical framework on Shiller's call for a serious quantitative analysis of narratives [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].

## Key facts

- Argues that to improve our understanding of economy and financial markets, economists must look beyond typical economic indicators to incorporate stories that affect individual and collective economic behavior [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
- Quoted directly in the paper's epigraph: "We need to incorporate the contagion of narratives into economic theory. Otherwise, we remain blind to a very real, very palpable, very important mechanism for economic change, as well as a crucial element for economic forecasting" [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
- His 2019 book Narrative Economics is presented as the recent driver of traction for incorporating narratives into economic models, expanding on previous work spanning decades including Shiller (1984) [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
- The field of study Shiller championed has called "for a more serious quantitative analysis of narratives" — the call Bhargava et al. position their paper as answering [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
- Shiller (2019) uses the frequency at which words are used in books (Google Ngram) and news sources (ProQuest) to construct simple narrative measures; Bhargava et al. position their NLP approach over 150,000-source media reservoirs as a quantitative successor [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
- Characterizes narratives as "deeply human phenomena," framing the technical challenge that quantifying them entails reducing complex multi-dimensional narratives to numbers fed into statistical modeling [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
