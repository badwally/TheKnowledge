# Lyndon B. Johnson

## Summary

36th President of the United States; per Kai Volatility's Q2 2022 commentary, his May 1964 "Great Society" agenda combined with concurrent Vietnam War fiscal spending laid the groundwork for the initial surge of 1965-1980 inflation by shifting the aggregate demand curve up faster than the U.S. economy's ability to supply, and the resulting price acceleration is the political-driver evidence Kai Volatility uses to argue the modern Fed is misreading 2022 inflation as psychological [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].

## Key facts

- Laid out his agenda for a "Great Society" — an ambitious series of policy initiatives with goals of ending poverty, reducing crime, and abolishing inequality — in May 1964 [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Per Kai Volatility, until recently the Great Society program was the largest social reform plan in U.S. history and rivaled in fiscal spending only by FDR's New Deal of the 1930s [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- The "dual fiscal spending on both war fronts" — the war on poverty and the war in Vietnam — gave consumer demand a "big shot in the arm" while government spending simultaneously accelerated, with demand exceeding the economy's supply capacity and "everything" beginning to cost significantly more [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Came to power in an electoral landslide and served Kennedy's last two years and one full term of his own; was legally allowed to pursue a second full term but "shockingly opted not to" as approval ratings deteriorated under inflation and the Vietnam War [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- By the end of 1968, the inflation rate climbed over 5% and could no longer be ignored; both the business community and consumer groups railed against the Democratic Party and Republicans printed "Great Society, Funny Money" protest signs and won the presidency in a landslide [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Per Kai Volatility, LBJ pushed the Federal Reserve "front and center" with a mandate to rein in prices — making the central bank responsible for an inflation problem the political branch had created [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
