# Jones & Tonetti (2020), "Nonrivalry and the Economics of Data"

## Summary

"Nonrivalry and the Economics of Data" is a peer-reviewed theoretical paper by Charles I. Jones and Christopher Tonetti, published in the American Economic Review in September 2020 [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]]. The paper formalizes the welfare economics of nonrival data and argues that, in a model that incorporates privacy preferences, allocating data property rights to consumers produces allocations close to the social optimum [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].

## Key facts

- Published in American Economic Review, volume 110, issue 9, pages 2819–2858, September 2020 [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
- DOI: 10.1257/aer.20191330 [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
- Authors: Charles I. Jones and Christopher Tonetti [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
- Central thesis: data is nonrival, which produces increasing returns and implies social gains to broad use across firms even in the presence of privacy considerations [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
- Policy result: assigning data property rights to consumers (rather than to firms that collect data) yields equilibrium allocations close to socially optimal, because consumers trade off privacy against the gains from selling data broadly [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
- Diagnoses a creative-destruction motive for firm-side data hoarding: firms restrict use of the data they hold to deter rivals, producing an inefficient allocation relative to the broad-use benchmark [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
- JEL classification: C80, D11, D21, D83, E22, K11, O34 — spanning data-collection methodology, consumer theory, firm theory, information economics, intangible capital, property law, and intellectual property [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
- Theoretical-foundation paper: does not analyze any named data collective, governance structure, or regulatory context [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
