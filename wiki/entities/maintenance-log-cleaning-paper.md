# Cleaning Maintenance Logs with LLM Agents for Improved Predictive Maintenance

## Summary

A 2026 PHM Society conference paper proposing the use of large language model (LLM)-based agents to automate the cleaning of equipment maintenance logs, with the goal of improving the quality of datasets used to train survival analysis and machine learning models for Predictive Maintenance (PdM).

## Key facts

- The paper was published on 13 January 2026 in the PHM Society proceedings (papers.phmsociety.org) and is authored by Valeriu Ionut Dimidov, Faisal Hawlader, Sasan Jafarnejad, and Raphael Frank [[sources/web-2026-01-13-6bf]].
- The paper argues that maintenance logs serve as the backbone of data-driven Predictive Maintenance (PdM) systems by providing the information used to create and label datasets for training survival analysis and machine learning models [[sources/web-2026-01-13-6bf]].
- It identifies a structural data-quality problem: because personnel manually enter information into maintenance logs and tracking systems allow varying levels of flexibility, service records often contain errors [[sources/web-2026-01-13-6bf]].
- The current state of practice is described as manual cleaning of equipment maintenance records by experts such as data scientists or reliability engineers, which the authors characterise as time-consuming and unable to entirely eliminate noise [[sources/web-2026-01-13-6bf]].
- The paper proposes using LLM-based agents to automate this cleaning task, provides an implementation, defines metrics to assess agent performance, and compares the performance of several LLMs on the task [[sources/web-2026-01-13-6bf]].
- The authors' empirical results indicate that LLM-based agents are a promising solution for improving the quality of PdM datasets and, in turn, producing predictive maintenance models that are more reliable and useful [[sources/web-2026-01-13-6bf]].
- The paper is released under a Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 Unported License [[sources/web-2026-01-13-6bf]].
