# Florida Condominium Act (Chapter 718)

## Summary

The Florida Condominium Act, codified at Chapter 718 of the Florida Statutes, is the comprehensive state statutory framework governing the creation, governance, operation, and dissolution of condominiums in Florida. Section 718.111 ("The Association") is the central operational-governance section, and it cross-references numerous other sections — including §718.112 (bylaws and meeting procedures), §718.114 (acquisition of leaseholds), §718.116 (assessments and liens), §718.302 (developer transition), §718.501 (regulatory authority), and §718.110 (declaration amendments) — that collectively define the legal architecture for Florida condominium associations [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]]. §718.112 ("Bylaws") is the companion governance-procedure section that enumerates required bylaw provisions covering board composition, officer roles, owner-inquiry handling, quorum/voting/proxies, board meeting notice and procedure, and unit-owner meetings including board elections and term limits [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].

## Key facts

- Codified at Chapter 718 of the Florida Statutes [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- §718.111 governs the corporate organization and operational powers of condominium associations [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- §718.110(1)(a) governs declaration amendments, including the process for amending consolidated-condominium operations for pre-1977 declarations [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- §718.112 enumerates required bylaw provisions covering administration, owner inquiries, quorum and voting, board meetings, and unit-owner meetings; any required provision absent from a bylaw is deemed included [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- §718.112(2)(a) sets the default board of administration at five members (three members for not-for-profit corporations with five or fewer units) and specifies the default officer roster of president, secretary, and treasurer [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- §718.112(2)(a) requires the board to respond in writing within 30 days to certified-mail inquiries from unit owners of residential condominiums, with longer timelines (60 days) when a legal opinion is requested; failure to respond precludes recovery of attorney fees in subsequent litigation arising from the inquiry [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- §718.112(2)(b) prohibits general proxies for unit-owner votes in residential condominiums, requires limited proxies for specified votes (reserve waivers/reductions under §718.112(2)(f)2, financial reporting waivers under §718.111(13), declaration amendments under §718.110, and bylaw/articles amendments), and prohibits proxy use in board-member elections except as provided in paragraph (d) [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- §718.112(2)(b) caps proxy validity at 90 days from the first meeting for which it was given and makes proxies revocable at any time [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- §718.112(2)(c) requires the board of administration of a residential condominium association of more than 10 units to meet at least once each quarter, with member question time on the agenda at least four times each year; meetings at which a quorum is present must be open to all unit owners [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- §718.112(2)(c) requires posted board-meeting notice with all agenda items at least 48 continuous hours before the meeting, and 14-day mailed notice (plus affidavit evidence of compliance) for meetings considering a nonemergency special assessment or amendments to rules regarding unit use [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- §718.112(2)(c) requires that notice of any board meeting at which regular or special assessments will be considered specifically state that assessments will be considered and provide the estimated cost and description of the purposes [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- §718.112(2)(d) requires annual unit-owner meetings within 45 miles of the condominium property absent contrary bylaws (timeshare condominiums excepted), and requires that vacancies caused by expiration of a director's term be filled by election by secret ballot when contested [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- §718.112(2)(d) imposes an 8-consecutive-year board-member term limit (residential condominiums only; only service on or after July 1, 2018 counts), which may be exceeded only by a 2/3 supermajority of votes cast in the election or when there are not enough eligible candidates [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- §718.112(2)(d) makes a unit owner delinquent in any assessment ineligible to be a candidate for the board, and bars convicted felons from board service unless their civil rights have been restored for at least 5 years [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- §718.112(2)(d)2 governs vacancy-fill procedures when an officer or director is removed following a covered criminal charge [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- §718.112(2)(o) limits the board's power to convey common elements to condemning authorities [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- §718.114 governs the approval process for the association's acquisition of leaseholds [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- §718.116 establishes the association's lien authority for unpaid assessments, which extends to expenses incurred under the abandoned-unit-access provision of §718.111(5)(b) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- §718.302 imposes limitations on consolidated common-expense assessments for pre-1977-declaration consolidated condominium operations [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- §718.501(1)(e) provides for civil penalties enforced against officers, directors, or managers who knowingly solicit, offer to accept, or accept kickbacks [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Chapter 718 operates in conjunction with Part I of Chapter 607 (for-profit corporation law) and Chapter 617 (not-for-profit corporation law) for associations' general corporate powers, per §718.111(2) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- §617.0830 provides the duty-of-care standard imported by §718.111(1)(d) for association officers, directors, and agents [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- §617.0834 provides the monetary-damages liability standard imported by §718.111(1)(d) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Chapter 718 is one of four Florida statutory frameworks (alongside Chapters 719, 720, and 721) under which a multi-community group-insurance program may be established under §718.111(11)(a)2 [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
