# Pan-Canadian Artificial Intelligence Strategy (PCAIS, 2017)

## Summary

The Pan-Canadian Artificial Intelligence Strategy (PCAIS) is Canada's federal AI policy framework, launched in 2017 in partnership with CIFAR to build a Canadian AI talent pipeline and ecosystem, including the establishment of centres of research, innovation and training at the national AI institutes (Amii, Mila, Vector Institute) [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]] [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]]. A second phase was launched on June 22, 2022, backed by more than C$443 million committed in Budget 2021, to bridge research capacity with commercialization and adoption [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]].

## Key facts

- Phase 1 was launched in 2017 in partnership with CIFAR to build a strong Canadian talent pipeline and ecosystem, including centres of research, innovation and training at the national AI institutes [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]] [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]].
- Phase 2 was launched on June 22, 2022 in Toronto by the Honourable François-Philippe Champagne, Minister of Innovation, Science and Industry, with more than C$443M committed in Budget 2021 [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]].
- Phase 2 funding allocation: C$60M for the national AI institutes (Amii, Mila, Vector Institute) to translate AI research into commercial applications and grow business AI adoption; C$125M for Canada's Global Innovation Clusters for AI commercialization; C$8.6M for the Standards Council of Canada to advance AI standards and a conformity assessment program; C$160M for CIFAR to attract, retain and develop academic talent and maintain centres at the national AI institutes; C$48M for CIFAR to renew advanced research, training and knowledge mobilization programs; C$40M for dedicated computing capacity for AI researchers across Canada [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]].
- Phase 2 implementation partners are CIFAR, the national AI institutes, the Standards Council of Canada, and Canada's Global Innovation Clusters [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]].
- Since 2017, more than 100 researchers have been recruited as Canada CIFAR Artificial Intelligence Chairs, roughly half of them international, and the national AI institutes had collectively trained over 1,500 graduate students and post-doctoral fellows by the Phase 2 announcement [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]].
- At the Phase 2 launch, Canada was reported to rank fifth on the Stanford Global AI Vibrancy Index and third among G7 nations [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]].
- The Phase 2 announcement frames the proposed Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA) as the companion governance instrument for responsible design, development, deployment and operation of high-impact AI systems [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]].
