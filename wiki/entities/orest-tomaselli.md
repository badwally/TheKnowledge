# Orest Tomaselli

## Summary

Orest Tomaselli is the president and CEO of Strategic Inspections, a reserve study preparation firm active in the NYC condo/co-op market. He is quoted in Habitat Magazine's April 2026 Bricks & Bucks coverage of the Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac March 2026 GSE policy update as the principal industry expert on the 15% reserve-funding floor, the retirement of Limited Review, the cascade of GSE-ineligibility designations into jumbo and commercial lending, and the rollback of the roof full-replacement-cost insurance requirement [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].

## Key facts

- President and CEO of Strategic Inspections, a reserve study preparation firm [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Cited in NYC condo/co-op market coverage as the industry expert characterizing the cost range for a reserve study at $7,500 to $9,500 [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Framed the 15% reserve-funding floor as adding "an additional 5% to be added to operating costs" on top of inflationary cost pressure facing boards as of January 2027 [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Characterized the pre-March-2026 limited-review regime as having allowed "many condominium communities [to skirt] through without reserving at all for future repairs" [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Stated that under the post-March-2026 full-review regime, "for properties that had not been compliant with the rules on reserves or critical repairs or special assessments there is nowhere to hide" [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Noted that a reserve study can require a contribution far higher than 15%, making the actual reserve-funding requirement a community-by-community determination [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Stated that when a building is flagged as ineligible for government-backed financing, other lenders often follow suit; characterized the designation as "an almost automatic reason not to lend in any capacity in the community," producing impact felt equally from affordable units to luxury condos [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Characterized the rollback of the requirement to insure roofs at full replacement cost as "spectacular" relief, particularly for buildings previously denied residential mortgage financing because insurance policies could not be aligned with the prior guidelines [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
