# 21st Century Cures Act

## Summary

The 21st Century Cures Act ("Cures Act") is a 2016 US federal statute that, among other provisions, made sharing electronic health information the expected norm in healthcare and authorized the Secretary of Health and Human Services (HHS) to identify "reasonable and necessary activities that do not constitute information blocking" [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]]. The statute's information-blocking regime is codified at Section 3022(a) of the Public Health Service Act and implemented at 45 CFR Part 171 [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].

## Key facts

- Enacted in 2016 as a US federal statute [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- Established sharing of electronic health information (EHI) as the default norm across three classes of regulated actors: healthcare providers, health IT developers of certified health IT, and health information exchanges / health information networks (HIEs/HINs) [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- Section 3022(a) of the Public Health Service Act, as added by the Cures Act, defines "information blocking" and identifies the actor classes to whom the prohibition applies [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- 45 CFR Part 171 implements the information-blocking definition and the enumerated exceptions [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- Authorized HHS to identify activities that do not constitute information blocking via the exceptions regime [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- Section 4004 of the Cures Act authorizes HHS to establish "disincentives" for healthcare providers who knowingly and unreasonably interfere with EHI access, exchange, or use except as required by law or covered by a regulatory exception [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- Operates as a compelled-disclosure regulatory regime in the EHI domain: covered actors must permit access, exchange, or use of EHI on lawful request, and refusal is presumptively unlawful absent an exception [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
