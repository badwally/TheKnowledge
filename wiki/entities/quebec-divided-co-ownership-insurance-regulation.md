# Regulation to establish various measures in matters of divided co-ownership insurance (CCQ, r. 4.1)

## Summary

CCQ, r. 4.1 — the Regulation to establish various measures in matters of divided co-ownership insurance — is one of 19 named regulations made under the Civil Code of Québec (chapter CCQ-1991), and is the only one of those 19 regulations explicitly directed at the divided co-ownership (condominium-equivalent) domain [[sources/web-2022-09-02-033]]. "Divided co-ownership" (copropriété divise) is the Québec civil-law construct corresponding to condominium ownership in common-law jurisdictions; CCQ, r. 4.1 sits in the Code-and-regulation hierarchy as the dedicated insurance instrument for those properties [[sources/web-2022-09-02-033]]. The substantive operative provisions of the regulation are not contained in the source of record — the captured CCQ excerpt confirms only the regulation's title, chapter designation, and structural position under the CCQ-1991 [[sources/web-2022-09-02-033]]. The regulation operates alongside the broader Bill 16 framework (enhanced by Bill 31, implementing regulation published July 30, 2025 and in force August 14, 2025), which was incorporated into the Civil Code of Québec itself and extended the Code's substantive treatment of divided co-ownership beyond insurance to include mandatory maintenance logbooks, contingency fund studies, buyer certificates, and trustee-deposit rules for new project deposits [[sources/web-2025-07-30-00d]].

## Key facts

- Chapter designation: CCQ, r. 4.1 — one of 19 regulations made under the Civil Code of Québec (chapter CCQ-1991) [[sources/web-2022-09-02-033]].
- Full title: "Regulation to establish various measures in matters of divided co-ownership insurance" [[sources/web-2022-09-02-033]].
- Confirmed by the captured CCQ table of contents (Updated to 1 November 2022) as the sole condo-domain regulation under the Code; all other 18 named regulations under the CCQ-1991 address non-condo subject matter (bodily-injury damages discounting, civil marriage/union, civil status, land registration, register of personal and movable real rights, liability of carriers by water, etc.) [[sources/web-2022-09-02-033]].
- Listed in the table of contents between CCQ, r. 4 (Regulation respecting change of name and of other particulars of civil status) and CCQ, r. 5 (Regulation respecting the publication of a notice of tardy declaration of filiation) [[sources/web-2022-09-02-033]].
- The substantive operative provisions of the regulation are not contained in the source of record; only the regulation's title, chapter designation, and existence under the CCQ-1991 are confirmed by the 2022-vintage source [[sources/web-2022-09-02-033]].
- The Bill 16 framework — enhanced by Bill 31 and operationalized by the regulation published in the *Gazette officielle du Québec* on July 30, 2025 (in force August 14, 2025) — was incorporated into the Civil Code of Québec itself rather than enacted as a sister regulation under the Code, extending the Code's substantive divided-co-ownership treatment beyond CCQ, r. 4.1's insurance scope to include mandatory maintenance logbooks, contingency fund studies, buyer certificates, and trustee-deposit rules [[sources/web-2025-07-30-00d]].
