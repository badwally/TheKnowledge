# Quebec Bill 16 (Divided Co-Ownership Framework)

## Summary

Quebec Bill 16 is the provincial legislation that establishes a new regulatory framework governing the management of divided co-ownership (copropriété divise) buildings in Québec. The framework was subsequently enhanced by Bill 31 and incorporated into the Civil Code of Québec [[sources/web-2025-07-30-00d]]. Its stated aims are to strengthen financial predictability, improve syndicate governance, and ensure the long-term sustainability of Quebec's condominium real estate stock [[sources/web-2025-07-30-00d]]. The implementing regulation was published in the *Gazette officielle du Québec* on July 30, 2025 and came into force on August 14, 2025 [[sources/web-2025-07-30-00d]].

## Key facts

- Bill 16 established a new framework governing the management of divided co-ownership buildings in Québec [[sources/web-2025-07-30-00d]].
- The framework was subsequently enhanced by Bill 31 [[sources/web-2025-07-30-00d]].
- The framework was incorporated into the Civil Code of Québec [[sources/web-2025-07-30-00d]].
- Stated legislative aims: strengthen financial predictability, improve syndicate governance, ensure long-term sustainability of the real estate stock [[sources/web-2025-07-30-00d]].
- The implementing regulation was published in the *Gazette officielle du Québec* on July 30, 2025 [[sources/web-2025-07-30-00d]].
- The regulatory framework came into force on August 14, 2025 [[sources/web-2025-07-30-00d]].
- Establishes four principal categories of new obligation: a mandatory standardized maintenance logbook, a periodic contingency fund study, a buyer certificate, and trustee-deposit rules for new project deposits [[sources/web-2025-07-30-00d]].
- Bill 31's amendments adjusted the maintenance-logbook review cycle to ten years (rather than five) for buildings with a maximum of 8 private units or 3 above-ground storeys, and for horizontal co-ownerships subject to legal-qualification confirmation by a notary or lawyer [[sources/web-2025-07-30-00d]].
- The framework's stated rationale is to promote more proactive, transparent, and professional management of divided co-ownerships, addressing long-standing concerns expressed by stakeholders in the sector [[sources/web-2025-07-30-00d]].
