# Paul F. Desmond

## Summary

President of Lowry's Reports Inc.; author of the 2002 Charles H. Dow Award paper "Identifying Bear Market Bottoms and New Bull Markets," which defines 90% Downside Days and 90% Upside Days as intensity-based measures of panic selling and buying, documents their occurrence across 69 years of NYSE data (1933–2002), and demonstrates that the combination pattern — multiple 90% Downside Days followed by a 90% Upside Day — has reliably identified major market bottoms far earlier than fundamental, economic, or conventional technical indicators [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].

## Key facts

- Affiliated with Lowry's Reports Inc. at the time of the paper's presentation on February 26, 2002 [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- The paper received the Charles H. Dow Award, as indicated by the manuscript title "Dow Award Paper" [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- Reviewed the daily history of both price changes and volume of trading for every stock traded on the New York Stock Exchange over a period of 69 years, from 1933 to the date of publication [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- Decomposed volume into Upside (buyers) Volume and Downside (sellers) Volume, and compiled full and fractional dollars of price change into Points Gained and Points Lost — the four components that have been an integral part of the Lowry Analysis since 1938 [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- Defined a 90% Downside Day as one in which Downside Volume equals 90.0% or more of total volume AND Points Lost equals 90.0% or more of total points — measuring panic selling intensity rather than just activity [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- Defined a 90% Upside Day as the mirror signal in which Points Gained and Upside Volume each reach 90.0% or more of their respective totals — measuring the return of aggressive demand [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- Documented that almost all significant market declines in the 69-year record contained at least one, and usually more than one, 90% Downside Day [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- Documented the historical occurrence of the reversal pattern at the 1962, 1970, 1973–74, 1980, 1987, 1990, and 1998 market bottoms, with a detailed appendix covering all 90% Days since January 1, 1960 [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- Codified eight interpretive rules for 90% Days, including that a single isolated 90% Downside Day lacks long-term trend implications but warns that more could follow, and that back-to-back 80% Upside Days can substitute for a single 90% Upside Day in signaling a reversal [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- Analyzed the 2000–2002 bear market in the paper's conclusion, finding that as of February 2002 there had been seven 90% Downside Days but no confirming 90% Upside Day, leaving the reversal pattern incomplete [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
