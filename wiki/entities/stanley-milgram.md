# Stanley Milgram

## Summary

Psychology professor who, after Corporal Hitler's rise demonstrated how far authority could lead ordinary people into mass destruction, designed an experiment showing that a man posing as a professor could trick ordinary people into administering what they believed were massive, torturous electric shocks to innocent fellow citizens; Munger argues the experiment's extreme result was driven by at least six psychological tendencies acting in confluence and that this should have been obvious from a checklist approach, yet it took over a thousand published papers for the psychology professoriate to reach roughly ninety percent of that understanding [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].

## Key facts

- Designed his experiment after World War II to determine "exactly how far authority figures could lead ordinary people into gross misbehavior" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- In the experiment, a man posing as an authority figure (a professor governing a respectable experiment) was able to trick a great many ordinary people into giving what they believed were massive electric shocks inflicting heavy torture on innocent citizens [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Munger argues the experiment demonstrated not only Authority-Misinfluence Tendency but also Social-Proof Tendency (inactive bystanders whose silence communicated the behavior was acceptable), plus at least four other tendencies acting in confluence [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Munger uses Milgram's experiment as a case study for the Lollapalooza Tendency — the tendency to get extreme consequences from confluences of psychological tendencies — arguing that "almost any intelligent person" with his checklist would have immediately diagnosed the six-tendency confluence, whereas over a thousand psychology papers were published before the professoriate got the experiment only about ninety percent understood [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- The experiment is also cited by Munger as evidence of "extreme ignorance in the psychology professoriate right after World War II" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
