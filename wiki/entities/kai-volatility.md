# Kai Volatility

## Summary

Volatility-focused market research firm publishing under the domain www.KaiVolatility.com; author of the Q2 2022 market commentary "Worshiping False Prophets: Why Powell's 'Volcker Moment' is Misguided and Destined to Fail," which argues the Powell Fed is following the wrong playbook at the wrong time because the 1965-1980 inflation was structurally driven by political forces — LBJ's Great Society, Vietnam War fiscal spending, and 40 subsequent years of monetary-policy-driven inequality and populism — rather than by the "inflationary psychology" the modern Fed treats as inflation's primary cause [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].

## Key facts

- Authored the Q2 2022 22-page market commentary "Worshiping False Prophets: Why Powell's 'Volcker Moment' is Misguided and Destined to Fail," published under the domain www.KaiVolatility.com [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Central thesis: not all inflationary moments are created equal, and a "cookie-cutter" Volcker playbook ignores the structural driver of the inflation, the structural realities of the current economy, the economic cost of a Fed-generated slowdown, and the political feedback loop in response to that slowdown [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Argues the prevailing "inflationary psychology" theory of secular inflation — that wage-price spirals can be short-circuited by aggressive Fed action before expectations entrench — fails to contemplate that "1965 was not the same time as 1980" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Frames the COVID fiscal response as "nearly $9 Trillion and counting," dwarfing LBJ's Great Society in absolute size and rivaled in adjusted size only by FDR's New Deal of the 1930s [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Treats Donald Trump and Bernie Sanders as "simply populist vehicles created by the Federal Reserve & forty years of unencumbered monetary policy," with the structural transition of the political Right to the populist Left under Trump as the catalyst that made the COVID-era fiscal tidal wave inevitable [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Argues that after 40 years of monetary policy dominance and secularly declining interest rates from 1981-2021, "the world likely faces its first secular inflationary period in two generations" — driven primarily by populism rather than psychology [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
