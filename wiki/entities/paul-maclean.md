# Paul MacLean

## Summary

Neuroscientist who in the 1960s proposed the triune-brain model — that the human brain has evolved with three areas of function (the reptile brain, the limbic brain, and the neocortex) — invoked by Tom Hougaard as the neurological frame for why trading psychology is dominated by subconscious survival reflexes rather than conscious logic [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].

## Key facts

- In the 1960s, MacLean proposed that the human brain has evolved with three areas of function: the reptile brain, the limbic brain, and the neocortex [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
- Hougaard uses MacLean's model to argue that the neocortex (logic, the part of the mind the trader can hear) is not really in charge — the reptile brain is — and that this asymmetry is the structural reason traders cannot "think their way" out of losing behaviour [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
