# Competitor Collaboration Guidelines (Competition Bureau of Canada, 2021)

## Summary

The Competitor Collaboration Guidelines (CCG) are the Competition Bureau of Canada's enforcement guidelines describing how the Bureau assesses collaborations between competitors under the Competition Act [[sources/web-2024-06-27-57a]]. The 2021 final CCG was preceded by a draft published July 29, 2020 for public consultation between July 29, 2020 and September 28, 2020, which updated the prior guidelines published December 23, 2009 [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].

## Key facts

- Issued by the Competition Bureau of Canada as enforcement guidelines, not a legal document and not a binding statement of how the Commissioner or Director of Public Prosecutions will exercise discretion in a particular matter [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Describe the Bureau's approach to applying sections 45 (criminal conspiracy) and 90.1 (civil agreements) of the Competition Act to collaborations between competitors [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Supersede all previous statements of the Commissioner of Competition or other Bureau officials regarding the administration and enforcement of sections 45 and 90.1 [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Updated with the benefit of nearly ten years' experience following the 2009 amendments to the Competition Act, which created the modern criminal-cartel enforcement regime [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Organized into five sections: (1) analytical framework, (2) the section 45 criminal prohibition, (3) the section 90.1 civil agreements provision, (4) hypothetical illustrative examples, (5) text of relevant provisions of the Act [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- The draft includes twelve worked hypothetical examples: price fixing, market allocation between potential competitors, output restriction, dual-distribution, franchises, joint selling, information sharing, joint research and development, buying groups, output exchange, product exchange or swap, and trade association guidelines [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Example 7 (Information sharing) is the worked case most directly relevant to data-collective and pooled-data arrangements among competitors [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Final interpretation of the law remains the responsibility of the Competition Tribunal and the courts; the Bureau states it may revise the Guidelines in light of experience and tribunal/court decisions [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Firms can request a binding written opinion from the Commissioner on the applicability of provisions of the Act to future conduct under section 124.1 of the Act [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
