# Trusted Exchange Framework and Common Agreement (TEFCA)

## Summary

TEFCA is the US nationwide framework for electronic health information exchange among providers, patients, public health agencies, and payers, created by the Department of Health & Human Services Office of the National Coordinator for Health Information Technology (ONC) to remove barriers to sharing health records electronically. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]

## Key facts

- TEFCA was created by the US Department of Health & Human Services Office of the National Coordinator for Health Information Technology (ONC) to remove barriers to sharing health records electronically among healthcare providers, patients, public health agencies, and payers. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- TEFCA establishes a "universal floor for interoperability," creating a path for health data to be shared beyond proprietary boundaries. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- TEFCA was formally announced in 2022; in December 2023 it surpassed a major milestone when the first Qualified Health Information Networks (QHINs) were designated, and within days health data began flowing among TEFCA QHINs. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- TEFCA comprises three foundational documents: the Common Agreement (legal contract signed by all QHINs), the Trusted Exchange Framework (TEF, defining the underlying principles, standards, and specifications), and the QHIN Technical Framework (QTF, covering patient identity resolution, authentication, and performance measurement for QHIN-to-QHIN exchange). [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- The Common Agreement was first published in January 2022 and updated in November 2023. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- TEFCA is designed to support exchange for any purpose, but each request for data must indicate the purpose (an "Exchange Purpose"); ONC and the Recognized Coordinating Entity (RCE) expand the permissible purposes over time in response to market needs raised by the QHIN Governing Council. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- TEFCA imposes a common set of data privacy and security requirements on participating health information networks and health IT developers regardless of whether they are HIPAA-covered entities. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- ONC sets TEFCA policy and guides network governance, while the Recognized Coordinating Entity develops and maintains the Common Agreement and administers the QHIN designation and monitoring process. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- The Sequoia Project serves as the Recognized Coordinating Entity under a 5-year contract with ONC awarded in August 2023. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- Stated benefits of TEFCA include increased access to patient data across care settings, decreased provider costs by eliminating the need to join multiple networks or build one-off point-to-point connections, and strengthened privacy and security through common requirements applied uniformly across participating networks. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
