# Illinois Condominium Act §605/9 and CICAA §160/1-45 (Reserves)

## Summary

Illinois governs association reserve funding under two parallel statutes: the Common Interest Community Association Act (765 ILCS 160/1-45) for non-condominium common interest communities, and the Condominium Property Act (765 ILCS 605/9) for condominium associations. Both require the board to adopt budgets providing reasonable reserves for capital expenditures and deferred maintenance, with a 2/3-vote waiver mechanism available to condominium associations whose instruments do not impose a reserve requirement [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].

## Key facts

- The Common Interest Community Association Act requires the board to give each owner a copy of the proposed annual budget, which must provide for reasonable reserves for capital expenditures and deferred maintenance for repair or replacement of the common elements, per 765 ILCS 160/1-45 [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- The Condominium Act requires the board of managers to adopt a budget that provides for reasonable reserves for capital expenditures and deferred maintenance for repair or replacement of the common elements [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- In determining the appropriate reserve amount, the condominium board shall take into consideration any independent professional reserve study which the association may obtain [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- An association without a reserve requirement in its condominium instruments may elect to waive in whole or in part the reserve requirement by a vote of 2/3 of the total votes of the association, per 765 ILCS 605/9 [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Disclosure statements must include a statement of the status and amount of any reserve or replacement fund and any other fund specifically designated for association projects [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Reserve studies performed by the association must be included in the association's records, per 765 ILCS 160/1-30 and 1-90 [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Illinois is a funding-mandate-without-study-mandate jurisdiction: it appears on the funding-required list but not on the study-required list [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
