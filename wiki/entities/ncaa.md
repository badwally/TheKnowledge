# National Collegiate Athletic Association

## Summary

Governing body of intercollegiate athletics in the United States whose rules strictly limit student-athlete compensation, creating substantial economic rents at Division 1 Football Subdivision (FBS) schools; per Garthwaite, Keener, Notowidigdo, and Ozminkowski (2020), FBS schools earned $8.5 billion in revenue by 2016, with 58 percent coming from football and men's basketball, yet less than 7 percent of that revenue was paid to athletes — compared to approximately 50 percent in professional leagues — leaving the surplus as institutional rents redistributed to other sports, facilities, coaches, and administrative personnel [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].

## Key facts

- Division 1 Football Subdivision (FBS) is the most competitive division of intercollegiate athletics, formerly described as "Division 1-A," comprising 130 teams organized in 10 athletic conferences [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- FBS schools earned $4.4 billion in revenue in 2006; over the next decade those revenues grew to $8.5 billion [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- 58 percent of total athletic department revenue comes directly from only two sports: men's football and men's basketball [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- All other sports directly account for about 15 percent of total revenue [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- The remaining 27 percent comes from other sources such as the sale of media rights, with most of the value stemming from the ability to broadcast football and men's basketball programs [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Athlete compensation is strictly limited to academic scholarships that cover the cost of attendance and a modest stipend for living expenses [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Less than 7 percent of football and men's basketball revenue is paid to athletes through scholarships and stipends [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Even 7 percent likely overstates compensation since scholarship values are based on list price of tuition, not the net price paid by the average student — full-time students at public and private non-profit universities on average have a net tuition and fees price that is 40–45 percent of list price [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Under their respective collective bargaining agreements, professional football and men's basketball players in the US receive approximately 50 percent of the revenue generated by their athletic activities as salary [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Football and men's basketball are commonly referred to as "revenue sports," with all other sports termed "non-revenue sports" — though the distinction reflects large differences in average revenue levels and revenue-to-expense ratios, not zero revenue from non-revenue sports [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Teams in the FBS are members of conferences that serve as the primary organizing vehicle for schedules, rules, refereeing, and other features related to athletic competition; conferences also negotiate post-season championship participation, media rights, and other economically meaningful financial issues [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
