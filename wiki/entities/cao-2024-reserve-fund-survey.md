# CAO 2024 Report on Reserve Fund Survey Findings

## Summary

The "Report on Reserve Fund Survey Findings" is a September 2024 publication of the Condominium Authority of Ontario (CAO) presenting the results of two surveys issued in April 2023 — one to condo corporations (724 responses, approximately 6% of Ontario's 12,000+ corporations) and one to condo owners (5,986 responses) — to investigate the health of reserve funds under the Ontario Condominium Act, 1998 [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]]. It is the authoritative Ontario regulator-published primary-source market frame for the Year-2 ON expansion arm of the condo-capital-infra Canada-first GTM, providing quantitative longitudinal data on reserve-fund inflation assumptions, contribution levels, special assessments, and loan use [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]]. The empirical survey is the quantitative companion to the CAO's practitioner-reference "Best Practices Guide: Ensuring Healthy Reserve Funds" (last updated December 2023; `[[entities/cao-best-practices-guide]]`), which anchors the same statutory regime (Condo Act §§93(4), 94(1), 115(2); O. Reg. 48/01 §§29(2), 29(3)) on the prescriptive side and operationalizes the Class 1/2/3 cadence schema, the $500 component-inventory threshold, and the 30-year minimum financial-analysis horizon that the survey then measures in operating practice [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]].

## Key facts

### Publication metadata

- Title: "Report on Reserve Fund Survey Findings"; publisher: Condominium Authority of Ontario (CAO); publication date: September 2024 [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- The publication explicitly characterizes itself as preliminary data and potential insights, with results not meant to be conclusive and representing only an initial step in CAO's ongoing research on reserve funds [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- The disclaimer documents that survey limitations include sample size and representation among condo corporations, abnormally high inflation rates at the time of the survey, and the complex nature of reporting reserve-fund data [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- 35 advisory-panel and pilot-group participants were consulted in survey development, of which 33 agreed to be named publicly in the acknowledgements; an additional advisory-panel group reviewed preliminary findings [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].

### Methodology

- Two surveys were developed in consultation with two advisory panels and two pilot groups consisting of reserve fund specialists, lawyers, condo managers, board directors, and condo owners [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- The CAO provided a unique survey link to each of the 12,000+ condo corporations in its database to avoid duplicate responses; the corporation survey was open for five weeks [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- The owner survey was emailed directly to condo community members who either subscribed to the CAO's mailing list or had previously engaged with CAO services, and was also available via a link on the CAO website open to the public [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- 724 corporation responses were received (approximately 6% response rate against the 12,000+ Ontario corporation universe); 5,986 owner responses were received, of which 5,212 (87%) were current or former directors, 769 (13%) had never served on a board, and 5 (<0.1%) did not specify [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].

### Inflation-assumption findings (corporation survey)

- Reserve fund study providers are required to predict forward-looking inflation over at least the next 30 years and use an estimated inflation rate to adjust predicted repair costs in their analysis [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- For studies completed in 2021, 51% of corporations used assumed forward-looking inflation rates between 1% and 2%, and 30% used rates in the next-higher band [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- Forward-looking inflation assumptions are higher in studies completed in 2022 and 2023 than they were for studies completed in 2021 — direct empirical evidence that reserve-study inflation-rate assumptions drift in response to macroeconomic regimes rather than holding fixed [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- Based on consultation with reserve fund specialists, the data provided further insights into the complexity of various factors affecting reserve fund planning, including inflation rates and funding strategies [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].

### Contribution-adequacy findings (corporation survey)

- Nearly two-thirds of respondent corporations received recommendations to increase reserve fund contributions by more than 3% in the most recent reserve fund study [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- 79% of respondent corporations reported that budgeted contributions would meet or exceed the recommended reserve fund contributions for their current fiscal year at the time of the survey; further research is required on the remaining 21% to confirm either compliance with contribution requirements or errors in reporting [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- Most respondent corporations were found to be following their reserve fund study recommendations with respect to annual contributions [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- Two-thirds of respondent standard condo corporations contributed more than 30% of their total budget to the reserve fund in 2023 [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- 63% of respondent standard condo corporations contributed more than $2,000 per voting unit to the reserve fund in 2023 [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].

### Funding-strategy findings (corporation survey)

- Special assessments and loans to adequately fund the reserve fund and steep increases to common expenses fees appear to be on the rise [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- Rising construction costs over the last three years may be triggering more special assessments and loans as corporations may be finding repair project costs are higher than originally expected [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].

### Owner-familiarity findings (owner survey)

- Over 90% of non-director condo owner respondents reported being very, somewhat, or slightly familiar with the reserve fund, compared to 100% of owners who were current or former directors [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- More than two-thirds of non-director condo owner respondents reported being very, somewhat, or slightly familiar with key reserve fund notices, compared to over 90% of owners who were current or former directors [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- Less than half of non-director condo owner respondents reported being very, somewhat, or slightly familiar with the CAO Guide on Condo Reserve Funds, compared to 70% of owners who were current or former directors [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- The "CAO Guide on Condo Reserve Funds" referenced in the survey owner-familiarity question is the CAO Best Practices Guide (`[[entities/cao-best-practices-guide]]`); the under-50% non-director-owner familiarity finding is therefore a direct empirical measurement of practitioner-reference document reach to non-board condo owners — a regulator-curated transparency-gap data point [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]] [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]].

### CAO publication ecosystem (practitioner-reference complement)

- The CAO's "Best Practices Guide: Ensuring Healthy Reserve Funds" (last updated December 2023) is the practitioner-reference companion to this empirical survey, defining the Ontario Class 1/2/3 reserve-fund-study cadence schema (Class 1 within one year of declaration registration; alternating Class 2 with site inspection and Class 3 without on at least a three-year cycle), the $500 component-inventory threshold, the 10%-of-operating-budget pre-RFS contribution floor, the at-least-30-year financial-analysis horizon, and the qualified-preparer category set — the prescriptive how-to surface whose operating practice the 724-corporation survey then measures empirically [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]].
- The CAO Best Practices Guide's explicit acknowledgment that reserve fund studies are "only ever able to provide an estimate of future costs and may be an under-estimation of repair and replacement costs as unexpected expenses may arise" is the regulator-curated interpretive lens through which the survey's 21% recommended-vs-budgeted gap and the rising use of special assessments and loans should be read — Ontario's primary regulator and the empirical survey share a deterministic point-estimate methodology baseline that concedes its own under-estimation risk [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]] [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].

### Implications for Year-2 Ontario expansion (condo-capital-infra)

- The 21% gap between recommended and budgeted contributions and the rising use of special assessments and loans surface a structural funding-adequacy shortfall an AI-native reserve-study firm can position against in the Year-2 ON expansion arm of the Canada-first GTM under ADR-0004 [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- The under-50% non-director owner familiarity with the CAO Guide identifies a transparency gap — board members understand reserve funds materially better than owners do, creating room for an owner-facing reporting layer in the Year-2 ON product [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
- The 2021-vs-2022/2023 shift in forward-looking inflation rates (51% of 2021 studies in the 1-2% band; materially higher in 2022 and 2023) is empirical evidence that single-point inflation-rate assumptions are policy variables rather than physical constants — directly supporting the regime-switching cost-escalation framing in the condo-capital-infra engine design [[sources/pdf-condominium-authority-2024-report-on-resrve]].
