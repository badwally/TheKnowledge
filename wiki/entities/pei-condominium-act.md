# Prince Edward Island Condominium Act

## Summary

The Prince Edward Island Condominium Act is the province's primary condominium statute and the only Canadian provincial regime in the wiki's comparator set that imposes NO mandatory reserve fund requirement, NO reserve-fund-study cadence, and NO statutory rules around special assessments — making PEI the structural outlier in the Canadian provincial regulatory spectrum that spans from BC SPA's five-year depreciation reports, ON Condo Act 1998's mandatory reserve fund, NB CPA c16.05's 10-year/3-year regime, NL NLR 80/11's prescriptive contents framework, NS Condominium Act's May-2023 "more frequent" cadence-tightening, AB and MB regimes, and QC Civil Code Bill 16 contingency-fund-study regime down to PEI's discretionary framework [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]]. Outside ADR-0004's NS → Atlantic Canada → ON → US Y2 GTM sequence, PEI is not addressable as a regulatory-driven market for the condo-capital-infra engine because no statutory demand engine for reserve studies exists [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]]. CMHC's PEI fact sheet directs PEI condominium owners to the Canadian Condominium Institute (CCI) Nova Scotia Chapter for representation — there is no PEI-specific CCI chapter referenced in the fact sheet, confirming PEI's structural dependence on Nova Scotia's CCI representation infrastructure [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

## Key facts

### Governing legislation

- Primary condominium statute of Prince Edward Island; cited in the CMHC PEI provincial fact sheet as the Condominium Act under which developers, condominium corporations, owners, and units operate [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Reserve fund and reserve study (the load-bearing null findings)

- Provincial legislation/regulations do NOT require that all condominiums in Prince Edward Island have a reserve fund [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- The developer of a new condominium is NOT obligated to put aside reserves as soon as the condominium is registered [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Provincial legislation/regulations have NO rules regarding special assessments [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- The CMHC PEI fact sheet does not describe a reserve-fund-study requirement, cadence, or qualified-preparer set — the absence of a reserve-fund regime makes a reserve-fund-study regime structurally impossible under the Act [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Borrowing and lien rights

- The Condominium Act specifies that condominiums may make bylaws authorizing the borrowing of money [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- A condominium corporation can place a lien on an individual unit if an owner doesn't pay his or her contributions to the common expenses [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- A lien expires in three months unless the condominium corporation registers a notice of lien at the Registry Office within that time [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- A lien may be enforced the same way as a mortgage, but does NOT take priority over mortgages or other debts registered against the property [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Governance

- The board of directors must have at least three members, elected by members of the corporation [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- A corporation must hold an annual meeting not more than three months after the registration of the declaration and description, and subsequently not more than fifteen months after the holding of the last preceding annual meeting [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- The board must call and hold a meeting if it receives a written request from members who together own at least 25 percent of the common elements; if the board does not act within 30 days, any requesting member may call the meeting, which must be held within 60 days of receiving the request [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Members must receive at least 10 days' written notice of every meeting, specifying place, date, hour, and nature [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Provincial legislation does NOT require condominiums to impose any bylaws or rules [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- A condominium corporation may make its own bylaws if voted on by members who own 66 2/3rds percent of the common elements (or a greater percentage if specified in the declaration) [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Changing governing documents

- Changing the condominium's declaration and description requires 100 percent consent from all owners and anyone else who has a mortgage or other debt registered against the units and common interests [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Changing the condominium's bylaws requires a vote of members who own 66 2/3rds percent of the common elements, or a greater percentage if specified in the declaration [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- All changes to the declaration, description, and bylaws must be registered with the Registrar of Deeds to be effective [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Substantial changes to common elements

- Substantial changes to common elements and assets (additions, alterations, improvements, renovations) must be approved by members who own at least 80 percent of the common elements [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Owners who disagree with substantial changes can ultimately request the corporation to purchase their units and common interests; the corporation may be obligated to do so, depending on what is in its declaration [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Less substantial changes may be voted on by a majority of members [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Developer obligations on sale

- Developers must take all reasonable steps to sell without delay the property's residential units other than any units subject to a prior lease [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Developers must take all reasonable steps to deliver to the purchaser without delay a registrable deed or transfer of the unit [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Developers must hold in trust all money (other than rent or occupancy charges) collected from purchasers on behalf of the condominium corporation until purchasers take ownership [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Developers must deliver to purchasers copies of: the declaration; parts of the description that apply to the unit and the exclusive use of the common elements; a statement of the intended recreational or other amenities and any conditions that apply to them; any bylaws or rules governing the use of common elements; any agreement for the management of the property or insurance trust agreement; and a budget statement prepared by the developer if the sale is entered into within the year immediately following registration of the declaration and description [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Provincial legislation/regulations do NOT stipulate what happens if a developer has inaccurately estimated the operational costs of a condominium — a developer-budget-forecast-defensibility gap that contrasts with Quebec's Bill 16 (2019, c.28) Civil Code amendment imposing developer liability for underestimated amounts in the budget forecast [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Warranty and consumer protection

- Provincial legislation/regulations do NOT require that developers of new condominiums provide a new home warranty to buyers; some developers offer their own home warranty or provide one through a third-party home warranty program [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- With the consent of the current owner, the purchaser of a re-sale condominium can request that the corporation provide a certificate that indicates whether or not the current owner has defaulted on his or her condo fees; the corporation must provide the certificate within seven days of receiving a request for it [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Dispute resolution

- There is nothing in Prince Edward Island's condominium legislation that addresses a process for handling disputes or complaints; owners with unresolved disputes can take the matters to court, or seek alternative conflict resolution services [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Rental of units

- Owners have a basic legislated right to rent out their units under the Rental of Residential Property Act; if there are any restrictions on renting, they must be stated in the condominium's declaration [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Owner payment obligations

- An owner cannot stop paying condominium fees if he/she is unhappy with the condominium's board of directors and/or property management [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Taxes and additional costs

- There is no provincial sales tax on new or re-sale condominiums in PEI [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Buyers pay Goods and Services Tax (GST) on new units but not on previously owned units, with re-sale GST exceptions for units acquired by sellers for primarily business purposes (other than long-term rental income), units for which sellers have claimed input tax credits, or substantially renovated units [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Real property transfer tax: 1 percent of the property's purchase price or assessed value, whichever is greater [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Registration fee for deeds of mortgage: $77.25 to $453.65 depending on the principal sum of the mortgage [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Registration fee for deeds of conveyance: $77.25 to $463.65 depending on the property value [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Condominium registration

- A new condominium corporation is registered by the developer submitting a declaration and description of the property to the Registrar of Deeds [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- The declaration describes how the condominium is organized and operated, including the proportion in which owners are to contribute to the common expenses [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- The description includes information such as a survey plan of the boundaries of each unit and the common elements [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- Once these documents are registered, a title is issued for each unit and the condominium corporation is created as a legal entity that owns all of the common spaces and facilities [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Industry resource (CCI Nova Scotia Chapter)

- The CMHC PEI fact sheet directs PEI condominium owners and corporations to the Canadian Condominium Institute (CCI) Nova Scotia Chapter (www.ccinovascotia.ca) as the cited industry resource — the same CCI chapter that reported the proclamation of NS Condominium Act amendments in 2023 — confirming the absence of a PEI-specific CCI chapter and PEI's structural dependence on Nova Scotia's CCI representation infrastructure [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Cross-jurisdictional comparator framing

- PEI is the only Canadian province in the wiki's comparator set with no mandatory reserve-fund requirement — placing it structurally below NS Condominium Act (mandatory reserve-fund studies under the May-2023 cadence-tightening amendments), NB CPA c16.05 (mandatory reserve-fund studies for >10-unit corporations, plus 100%-of-operating-budget reserve fund for ≤10-unit corporations), NL Condominium Act 2009 + NLR 80/11 (mandatory reserve-fund studies on subsection 49(5) cadence), BC SPA (mandatory depreciation reports on 5-year cycle for 5+-strata-lot corporations), ON Condo Act 1998 (mandatory reserve fund + regular studies), AB Condominium Property Act (mandatory), MB Condominium Act (mandatory 5-year studies), and QC Civil Code Bill 16 (mandatory contingency-fund-study regime) [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- PEI's discretionary regime is structurally similar to the "less prescriptive" Atlantic-Canada / North framing referenced in the condo-capital-infra engagement synthesis but represents the most permissive end of that spectrum — closer to a pre-statutory baseline than to any in-scope jurisdiction in ADR-0004's Canada-first GTM sequence (NS → Atlantic Canada → ON → US Y2) [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Implication for condo-capital-infra GTM

- PEI is not addressable as a regulatory-driven market for the condo-capital-infra reserve-study engine because there is no statutory demand engine — no mandatory reserve-fund requirement, no reserve-fund-study cadence, and no special-assessment rules to push corporations into engaging reserve-study firms [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
- The PEI discretionary baseline is the "null comparator" against which the NS Condominium Act's May-2023 "more frequent reserve-fund studies" amendment can be benchmarked — surfacing PEI as the closest pre-statutory analog and reinforcing the policy lift of the NS 2023 amendments within the Atlantic-Canada cluster [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
