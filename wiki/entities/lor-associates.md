# LOR Associates

## Summary

Investment firm founded by Leland, O'Brien, and Rubinstein that created "portfolio insurance" in the 1980s — a dynamic hedging technique that replicated the payoff of a purchased index put for institutional clients; Ambrus Capital invokes it as the historical analog to modern dealer gamma hedging [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]], and Andrew Constan's firsthand account from the Brady Commission confirms that two major institutional sellers using the LOR technique dominated the selling on Black Monday (October 19, 1987), with the strategy's covering of short futures positions on the way up likely partly causal for the 39% YTD rally that preceded the crash [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].

## Key facts

- Created by Leland, O'Brien, and Rubinstein [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Created the portfolio insurance technique at a time when index options were only six years old (since 1981) and lacked institutional-scale liquidity [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- The technique replicated the payoff of a purchased index put for institutional clients — "exactly the same as a dealer who has sold a put to a client would use to dynamically hedge their exposure" — requiring only a liquid hedging market and moderate realized volatility [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Portfolio insurance strategies start with a short futures position; as the market rallies, the portfolio insurer covers the short — a dynamic that was likely partly causal for the 39% YTD rally in the SPX leading up to the August 1987 peak [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Two major institutional sellers using the LOR technique dominated the selling on October 19, 1987 (Black Monday) — "it's very clear from their activities on 10/19 that they caused the extreme move that day" [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Sold a product labeled "portfolio insurance" whose mechanism was to systematically sell assets when the market fell below a certain level, in order to exit positions quickly and prevent larger losses [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- When the product was adopted en masse, it created a suction of liquidity by producing a profile of sellers that grossly outweighed buyers at the pre-set exit prices [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- Generally acknowledged as one of the reasons for the 1987 stock-market crash, in which the stock market dropped more than 20% in one day [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- Ambrus Capital invokes the LOR episode to argue that today's price-insensitive gamma hedging is mechanically very similar to portfolio insurance in pulling liquidity when prices go down [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- Per Constan, today's parallel: long-only investors lever and delever with futures, hedge via purchasing puts whose liquidity has massively improved, but this leaves dealers with "Portfolio Insurance" dynamic hedging to do in a downdraft [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
