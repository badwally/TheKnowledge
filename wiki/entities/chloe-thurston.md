# Chloe N. Thurston

## Summary

Assistant Professor of Political Science at Northwestern University and IPR Associate; co-author of the June 2018 IPR working paper WP-18-16 "The Limits of Policy Feedback as a Party-Building Tool," which sketches in cursory fashion the limits of policy feedback as a party-building tool and makes three arguments against treating policy success as tantamount to political success [[sources/pdf-5e6d63eac790]].

## Key facts

- Listed on the paper as Assistant Professor of Political Science at Northwestern University and an IPR Associate [[sources/pdf-5e6d63eac790]].
- Co-author with Daniel J. Galvin of "The Limits of Policy Feedback as a Party-Building Tool" (Version: June 20, 2018; IPR Working Paper WP-18-16) [[sources/pdf-5e6d63eac790]].
- Authors are listed alphabetically and are noted to have contributed equally to the article [[sources/pdf-5e6d63eac790]].
- Cited in the paper for prior work on submerged policies — Thurston (2015) and Thurston (Forthcoming) — qualifying the claim that visibility/traceability are necessary for constituency formation [[sources/pdf-5e6d63eac790]].
- Acknowledges Phil Rocco, Andrew Kelly, Matthew Lacombe, and Mara Suttmann-Lea for comments on an earlier version of the paper [[sources/pdf-5e6d63eac790]].
