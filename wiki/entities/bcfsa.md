# BC Financial Services Authority

## Summary

The BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA) is the British Columbia provincial regulator responsible for, among other duties, supervising real estate licensees and real estate developers under provincial legislation including the Real Estate Development Marketing Act (REDMA), and the regulator-curated interpretive channel through which BC publishes advisories notifying licensees and developers of regulatory amendments — including amendments to the Strata Property Regulation under the Strata Property Act that affect depreciation reports [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]]. BCFSA operates two distinct regulator-curated interpretive channels: numbered Advisories (used to notify licensees and developers of Strata Property Regulation amendments, e.g., Advisory 24-014 on the July 1, 2024 depreciation-report amendments) and a blog ("bcfsa.ca → About Us → News → Blog") used to announce qualified-provider-list expansions affecting strata corporations, e.g., the January 26, 2026 blog post "More Professionals Can Provide Strata Depreciation and Electrical Planning Reports" announcing the October 27, 2025 expansion of the qualified-provider lists for both depreciation reports and electrical planning reports [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]] [[sources/docx-ea451e0d4901]]. BCFSA is the British Columbia provincial-regulator counterpart to the California Department of Real Estate (DRE; publisher of the 2010 Reserve Study Guidelines for the predecessor Davis-Stirling § 1365 / § 1365.5 regime), the Florida DBPR Division of Florida Condominiums, Timeshares and Mobile Homes (FL SIRS reporting), and the Condominium Authority of Ontario (CAO; publisher of the December 2023 Best Practices Guide and the September 2024 Reserve Fund Survey Findings) — making it the BC node in the cross-jurisdictional regulator-curated interpretive-guidance set framing reserve-study practice across the project's Year-1 NS, Year-2 ON/BC/FL footprint under ADR-0004 [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].

## Key facts

- BC provincial regulatory authority issuing formal Advisories to real estate licensees and real estate developers; identified in BCFSA Advisory 24-014 as 'BC Financial Services Authority (BCFSA)' [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- Publishes numbered Advisories with date-stamped distribution lists — Advisory 24-014 (May 21, 2024) was distributed to Real Estate Developers and Real Estate Professionals, replacing the earlier Advisory 24-012 (May 6, 2024) on the same subject [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- Operates a reissuance-and-clarification pattern in which superseding advisories are published within short windows (Advisory 24-012 reissued as Advisory 24-014 fifteen days later) specifically to clarify disclosure requirements for real estate developers — a regulator-curated guidance-iteration channel that produces a primary-source record of interpretive refinement over time [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- Receives REDMA disclosure statement filings from real estate developers; per Advisory 24-014, if a developer has already filed a disclosure statement with BCFSA but does not expect to complete the development property until on or after July 1, 2027, the developer may be required to file a disclosure statement amendment if the Strata Property Regulation changes would result in a misrepresentation in the disclosure statement — establishing BCFSA as the receiving authority for REDMA disclosure-statement filings and amendments [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- The May 21, 2024 Advisory 24-014 surfaces the four substantive amendments to the Strata Property Regulation effective July 1, 2024 in the BCFSA-curated interpretive framing: (1) remove indefinite deferral of depreciation reports by annual 3/4 vote; (2) lengthen the cadence from every three years to every five years; (3) restrict preparation to qualified professionals; (4) require developers to provide new strata corporations (established on or after July 1, 2027 with five or more strata lots) with funding for depreciation reports (minimum $5,000, plus $200 per strata lot, up to maximum $30,000; paid into the strata contingency reserve fund no later than the date of the strata corporation's first annual general meeting) [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- Cites Order of the Lieutenant Governor in Council 204 - 2024 as the formal instrument carrying the transitional provisions specifying dates by which various existing or new strata corporations must obtain depreciation reports — establishing BCFSA Advisory 24-014 as the regulator-curated interpretive surface and OIC 204-2024 as the binding statutory-instrument source [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- Frames depreciation reports in the BCFSA-curated interpretive layer as 'comprehensive assessments of the condition of strata buildings and infrastructure, aiding in long-term financial planning and maintenance strategies for strata corporations' [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- BCFSA's regulator-curated interpretive guidance on depreciation reports is the BC parallel to the California Department of Real Estate's 2010 Reserve Study Guidelines for the predecessor § 1365 / § 1365.5 regime, the Florida DBPR Division's SIRS Reporting framework under HB 913, and the Condominium Authority of Ontario's December 2023 Best Practices Guide — four state/provincial-regulator-curated interpretive channels across the project's in-scope reserve-study jurisdictions [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].

### Blog channel (bcfsa.ca/about-us/news/blog) — qualified-provider-list expansions

- BCFSA operates a blog channel at bcfsa.ca → About Us → News → Blog as a second regulator-curated interpretive surface alongside its numbered-Advisory channel, used specifically to announce qualified-provider-list expansions affecting strata corporations — confirming that BCFSA's regulator-curated guidance architecture uses Advisories for Strata Property Regulation amendments with REDMA disclosure-statement implications and blog posts for qualified-provider-list expansions [[sources/docx-ea451e0d4901]] [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- BCFSA published the blog post "More Professionals Can Provide Strata Depreciation and Electrical Planning Reports" on January 26, 2026, announcing the October 27, 2025 expansion of the qualified-provider lists for both strata depreciation reports (adding licensed professional engineers, architectural technologists, and certified technicians) and electrical planning reports (adding licensed professional engineers and certified technicians) — a 91-day publication lag between the October 27, 2025 regulatory effective date and the January 26, 2026 blog-post announcement, surfacing a longer interpretive-publication interval than the BCFSA Advisory channel's two-week revision pattern (Advisory 24-012 → 24-014, May 6 to May 21, 2024) [[sources/docx-ea451e0d4901]] [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- The January 26, 2026 blog post directs readers to two Province of British Columbia operational landing pages: the strata electrical planning report page (gov.bc.ca → housing-tenancy → strata-housing → operating-a-strata → the-environment → electrical-planning-report) and the choosing-a-depreciation-report-provider page (gov.bc.ca → housing-tenancy → strata-housing → operating-a-strata → repairs-and-maintenance → depreciation-reports → choosing-a-depreciation-report-provider) — establishing the Province-of-BC landing-page surfaces as the downstream operational interpretive layer flowing from BCFSA's regulator-side announcement [[sources/docx-ea451e0d4901]].
- BCFSA's blog channel covers both the strata depreciation report regime (under SPA Strata Property Regulation) and the electrical planning report (EPR) regime as two parallel BCFSA-supervised strata-corporation-facing report regimes — confirming that BCFSA's regulatory perimeter extends beyond depreciation reports alone to encompass the EPR qualified-provider-restriction regime as an additional supervised report-level mandate [[sources/docx-ea451e0d4901]].

### Implication for condo-capital-infra GTM

- BCFSA Advisory 24-014 confirms BCFSA as the operative BC regulator-curated interpretive surface for SPA depreciation-report compliance — making BCFSA the BC-channel counterparty for any vendor-or-product positioning targeting the BC depreciation-report market under ADR-0004's Year-2 Vancouver wave, parallel to how the CAO Best Practices Guide and CAO 2024 Reserve Fund Survey position the CAO for the Year-2 Toronto wave [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- The BCFSA-administered REDMA disclosure-statement-amendment trigger (filing required if Strata Property Regulation changes would result in a misrepresentation in a previously-filed disclosure statement) is the load-bearing enforcement mechanism propagating the SPA developer-funding obligation into pre-sale compliance, surfacing BCFSA as the BC regulator with direct enforcement reach into developer-side disclosure compliance for properties completing on or after July 1, 2027 [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- BCFSA's January 26, 2026 blog announcement of the October 27, 2025 qualified-provider-list expansion is the load-bearing primary-source surface for any customer-facing BC regulatory citation chain on professional eligibility — replacing or supplementing secondary-source vendor- and practitioner-curated summaries (e.g., Condo Control's February 2025 summary) that may not have anticipated or accurately captured the October 2025 expansion timing [[sources/docx-ea451e0d4901]].
