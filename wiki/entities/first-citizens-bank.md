# First Citizens Bank

## Summary

First Citizens Bank operates a dedicated Community Association Banking Services group that offers a structured HOA lending program built around three named loan products — quick-term loans, traditional term loans, and revolving lines of credit — and a published underwriting standard requiring a minimum of 25 units, an effective collection policy, and up-to-date financials [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]]. The Community Association Banking Services group is led by Director Jeff Barnett, who is the named expert in First Citizens's April 14, 2026 board-facing financing guide on firstcitizens.com [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]]. First Citizens positions itself to HOA boards as a bank with deep HOA-segment industry knowledge whose bankers will attend HOA meetings to explain loan terms and answer resident questions, and whose banking specialists will work closely with HOA accountants and attorneys on financial-operations best practices around HOA financing [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].

## Key facts

### HOA banking practice

- Operates a Community Association Banking Services group dedicated to HOA lending, deposits, and cash management [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- The Community Association Banking Services group is led by Director Jeff Barnett [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Markets HOA bankers as available to attend HOA board meetings to explain loan terms and answer resident questions [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Markets HOA banking specialists as working closely with accountants and attorneys to ensure understanding of typical payment methods and operational aspects of an HOA financing program [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Frames its value proposition to HOA boards in terms of capital plus deep industry knowledge guiding HOAs and management companies through the entire financing process, from loan selection to homeowner communication [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].

### HOA loan products

- Offers three named HOA lending product types: quick-term loans, traditional term loans, and revolving lines of credit [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Quick-term loans: up to 10-year terms, no minimum loan amounts, 3- to 5-day turnaround times when limited documentation is required [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Traditional term loans: 5- to 15-year terms, fixed rates, no maximum loan limits [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Revolving lines of credit: useful for short-term cash flow shortfalls at the community, with limits on total dollar amounts and a requirement that balances be repaid annually [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].

### Underwriting standard

- Requires a minimum of 25 units for HOA loan applications [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Requires an effective collection policy at the applicant association [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Requires up-to-date financials at the applicant association [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Application review covers the HOA's income statement, balance sheet, and aging report [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Depending on the association's governing documents and state laws, a board vote or an amended HOA declaration may also be required as a condition of the loan [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].

### Published guidance

- Published a board-facing HOA financing guide titled "How to pay for an upcoming HOA project" on firstcitizens.com, dated April 14, 2026 [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- The guide positions financing as enabling associations to complete all phases of a project at once while spreading repayment over time, avoiding the pitfalls of phased construction [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- The guide instructs HOA boards to weigh financial strength as well as experience when evaluating lenders, characterizing banks with long histories serving HOAs and strong balance sheets as better positioned to fund projects on favorable terms and provide dependable, ongoing support [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- The guide includes embedded video segments featuring Jeff Barnett on three topics: (i) financing as an alternative to phased construction, (ii) what banks look for when assessing a community's financial readiness, and (iii) how early conversations with a financing partner can make a difference [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
