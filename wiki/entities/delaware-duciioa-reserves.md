# Delaware Uniform Common Interest Ownership Act §81 (Reserves)

## Summary

The Delaware Uniform Common Interest Ownership Act (DUCIOA), codified in Title 25, Chapter 81, requires that condominium declarations create and maintain a fully funded repair and replacement reserve based on a current reserve study, in addition to any contingency reserve. Delaware is one of the few US states that imposes both a study requirement and a hard funding requirement [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].

## Key facts

- Condominiums must contain within their declaration provisions mandating that the association create and maintain — in addition to any contingency reserve — a fully funded repair and replacement reserve based on a current reserve study, per §81-205(14) [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Minimum contributions to reserves vary based on either the reserve study or a statutory formula based on the number of common area components, per §81-315 [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Condominium disclosure statements must include the current balance in reserves and the most recent reserve study, per §81-409 [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Condominiums and cooperatives must contain a line item in their annual budget funding any replacement and repair reserve, per §81-324 [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Delaware imposes reserve obligations on developers (declarants) as well, making it one of five states with developer reserve-study requirements and one of six with developer reserve-funding requirements [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
