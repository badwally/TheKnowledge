# Governing the Commons: The Evolution of Institutions for Collective Action

## Summary

*Governing the Commons: The Evolution of Institutions for Collective Action* is Elinor Ostrom's 1990 book that introduced her eight design principles for successfully managed common-pool resources and established the empirical foundation for the modern study of commons governance [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].

## Key facts

- Published in 1990 by Elinor Ostrom [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Argued against the dominant interpretation of the commons exemplified by Garrett Hardin's "Tragedy of the Commons," on the grounds that real commoning institutions had regulated CPR access successfully for centuries [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Reviewed commoning practices including irrigation communities in the Philippines, village commons in Japan, and the common pasture of Torbel, Switzerland, to identify the eight design principles [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Brought the Swiss village of Torbel — managed in common since the 15th century — to international attention as a paradigm of long-lived common-property regimes [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
