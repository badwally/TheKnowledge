# Nanex

## Summary

Market data research company that started the "Fed Robbery" controversy with its paper "Einstein and The Great Fed Robbery," asserting that markets in Chicago and New York reacted almost simultaneously to the September 18, 2013 FOMC "no taper" announcement — a result incompatible with the time needed to move information from Washington DC at the speed of light — and concluding "There are 2 possibilities, and both aren't good news for Wall Street," alleging foul play [[sources/pdf-fa83c61dfa2d]].

## Key facts

- Described as a "market data research company" [[sources/pdf-fa83c61dfa2d]].
- Published "Einstein and The Great Fed Robbery," the study that initiated the controversy over the September 18, 2013 FOMC news release [[sources/pdf-fa83c61dfa2d]].
- Asserted that markets in Chicago and New York reacted almost simultaneously to the Fed announcement, which is incompatible with the time needed to move information from Washington DC to either city at the speed of light [[sources/pdf-fa83c61dfa2d]].
- Concluded there were two possibilities — both implying foul play — alleging that the simultaneous reaction could not be explained by legitimate release mechanisms [[sources/pdf-fa83c61dfa2d]].
- The analysis was criticized by Virtu Financial, which pointed out inaccuracies in Nanex's method [[sources/pdf-fa83c61dfa2d]].
- Nanex stated that the embargo release mechanism was not in conformance with the rules then in effect [[sources/pdf-fa83c61dfa2d]].
- The Quincy Data paper "The Fed Robbery Revisited" provides an alternative explanation: the data is consistent with an embargoed release simultaneous at both the CME (Aurora) and Nasdaq (Carteret) colocation centers, not a lockup release from DC [[sources/pdf-fa83c61dfa2d]].
