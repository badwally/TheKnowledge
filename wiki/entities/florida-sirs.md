# Florida Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS)

## Summary

Florida's Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) regime is the post-Surfside reserve-study mandate that, per the condo-capital-infra engagement synthesis, forces every condo of three or more habitable stories into a mandatory reserve study every ten years across eight components, with no waivers permitted [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]]. The synthesis attributes the regime to Florida SB-4D and HB 913 (2025) and identifies SIRS as the demand engine that has reset the Florida reserve-study software market and created the 5-7 year demand window the engagement is built around [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]]. CS/CS/HB 913 (2025) — the Florida House bill sponsored by Rep. V. Lopez with companion CS/CS/SB 1742 (Bradley) — is the operative 2025 statutory update that materially refines SIRS by codifying conflict-of-interest restrictions, permitting reserve-account pooling, raising the non-specified-items threshold, requiring DBPR rulemaking on useful-life criteria and a standard SIRS form, indexing thresholds to CPI, and introducing alternative funding mechanisms including line-of-credit, secured-line-of-credit majority approval, and natural-emergency contribution pauses [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]]. The operative reporting artifact through which associations file SIRS information with the state is the DBPR SIRS Reporting Form (Revised 9/2024), authorized under ss. 718.112(2)(g)9 & 10, F.S. (condominiums) and 719.106(1)(k)9 & 10, F.S. (cooperatives), with a 12/31/24 postmark deadline for the initial reporting window [[sources/pdf-b922a12f47f0]].

## Key facts

### Synthesis-level framing (engagement synthesis)

- SIRS applies to every Florida condo of three or more habitable stories and must be repeated every ten years [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- The regime requires full funding of the structural reserves; owner waivers and partial-funding votes are no longer permitted, except for the HB 913 (2025) carve-outs — line-of-credit votes in lieu of annual funding, secured-line-of-credit majority approval, and reserve-waiver votes contingent on condominium termination (see § Funding-flexibility carveouts below) [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]] [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- A separate 25-year milestone structural inspection runs alongside the 10-year reserve study; the two are run by separate firms today and joined manually, which the synthesis flags as the gap at the intersection of structural inspection and reserve modeling [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- SIRS, together with following bills in California, New Jersey, and Virginia, is the regulatory engine the engagement explicitly relies on for its market-window thesis [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- The synthesis attributes the SIRS regime to Florida SB-4D and HB 913 (2025) [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].

### Post-Surfside cross-jurisdictional spillover

- The Champlain Towers South collapsed on June 24, 2021 in Surfside, Florida — the precipitating event that drove the post-Surfside legislative response across multiple US states [[sources/web-2023-05-02-683]].
- Gov. Ron DeSantis signed Florida's post-Surfside reserve legislation into law in June 2022; the legislation requires condominiums and cooperatives within the state to impose substantial new structural safety and reserve requirements, including a mandatory reserves study to be completed every ten years [[sources/web-2023-05-02-683]].
- As of May 2023, similar legislation has passed or is expected to pass in Colorado, Hawaii, Georgia, and Maryland — four additional state-level regulatory-spillover jurisdictions beyond the California / New Jersey / Virginia set captured in the engagement synthesis, broadening the post-Surfside regulatory-window the engagement's market-thesis relies on [[sources/web-2023-05-02-683]].
- The May 2023 CINC Systems × SmartProperty Living Reserve Study® partnership announcement frames the cross-jurisdictional spillover as the explicit motivation for the integration — "states across the country are responding to the tragedy in Surfside, Florida, and imposing new reserve requirements" per CINC CEO Ryan Davis — confirming that the FL-SIRS-anchored regulatory wave is already shaping CAM software roadmaps beyond Florida itself [[sources/web-2023-05-02-683]].

### Statutory anchoring (reporting-form authority)

- The SIRS reporting regime is anchored in ss. 718.112(2)(g)9 & 10, F.S. (for condominium associations) and ss. 719.106(1)(k)9 & 10, F.S. (for cooperative associations); these statutory subsections are the cited authority on the DBPR SIRS Reporting Form (Revised 9/2024) [[sources/pdf-b922a12f47f0]].
- The same subsections require that the completed SIRS be available for inspection and copying by written request [[sources/pdf-b922a12f47f0]].
- The form unifies the condominium and cooperative SIRS regimes operationally — a single project-type checkbox (Condominium / Cooperative) routes both populations through the same data-collection instrument [[sources/pdf-b922a12f47f0]].

### Statutory scope and applicability (HB 913, 2025)

- SIRS is required for condominium buildings that are three habitable stories or higher in height [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- Four-family dwelling units are exempt from the SIRS requirements, similar to such units' exemption from the milestone inspection requirements [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The threshold for non-specified items that must be included in the SIRS is raised from $10,000 to $25,000 [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The base amount of an expense or replacement cost for a SIRS-related item must be modified annually based on the Consumer Price Index, and DBPR must post on its website by February 1 of each year the revised expense or replacement cost — an automatic indexation mechanism that propagates inflationary updates into the statutory threshold without further legislative action [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Conflict of interest (HB 913)

- It is a conflict of interest for any person who performs a SIRS or a milestone inspection to provide services for the repair or replacement of the condominium property that was the subject of such milestone inspection or SIRS [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- It is a conflict of interest for any person who performs a SIRS or a milestone inspection to have a financial interest in the entity or person providing the repair or replacement services [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Reserve-account governance (HB 913)

- A vote of the association's members is not required to create the association's reserve accounts in accordance with the most recent SIRS [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- An association's reserve accounts for two or more required components may be pooled [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The reserve funding indicated in the proposed annual budget must be sufficient to ensure that available funds meet or exceed projected expenses for all components in the reserve pool, based on the most recent SIRS [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- An officer or director of an association must sign an affidavit acknowledging receipt of the completed SIRS [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Funding-flexibility carveouts under HB 913 — refinements to the synthesis-level "no waivers" framing

- The board of directors of an association may levy special assessments and obtain loans to perform necessary maintenance, as required by the milestone inspection report and SIRS report, without the prior approval of the condominium association's membership [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The members of a unit-owner-controlled association may approve, by majority vote, a secured line of credit to meet the reserve funding schedule recommended by the SIRS report [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- Unit owners may vote to use a line of credit in lieu of fully funding reserves annually, provided the unit owners receive clear notice that reserves will not be funded annually, that the association will use credit instead, and the amount of projected future assessments; any line of credit secured by an association must be disclosed to subsequent purchasers [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- A board of an association may pause contributions to its reserves, without obtaining the prior approval of a majority of its members, if a local building official determines that a condominium building is uninhabitable due to a natural emergency (hurricane, storm, flood, severe wave action, drought, or earthquake — s. 252.34(8), F.S.) [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- Members of an association may vote to waive the maintenance of reserves recommended by the most recent SIRS if the association votes to terminate the condominium [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Alternative funding method (HB 913, Section 3)

- The definition of "funding method" under the Florida Condominium Act is amended to mean a method approved by the Division for funding the capital expenditures and deferred maintenance obligations for any multicondominium association — removing the prior restriction limiting the alternative funding method to multicondominium associations operating at least 25 condominiums [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### DBPR rulemaking on SIRS (HB 913, Section 7)

- By October 1, 2025, DBPR must initiate rulemaking to establish criteria for determining the estimated useful life of the building components that are subject to SIRS [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- DBPR must initiate rulemaking, in coordination with the Florida Building Commission, to create the form to be used for SIRS [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The current operative reporting instrument — the DBPR SIRS Reporting Form (Revised 9/2024) — is the pre-rulemaking baseline that the HB 913 coordinated DBPR / Florida Building Commission standardized form is mandated to supersede [[sources/pdf-b922a12f47f0]] [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Local-government confirmation and enforcement coupling (HB 913, Sections 1-2)

- Each local government that has authorized and permitted construction of a building three habitable stories or more in height subject to the condominium form of ownership must confirm with each condominium association whether the SIRS and milestone inspection have been completed and filed with the Division, or whether the condominium has failed to complete and file the requisite SIRS or milestone inspection report [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- Each local government must report to the Division by December 31, 2025 its findings on whether each condominium association within the local government's jurisdiction has completed or failed to complete the SIRS or milestone inspection [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Reporting form artifact (DBPR, Revised 9/2024)

- The DBPR SIRS Reporting Form (Revised 9/2024) is the operational paper-submission instrument by which condominium and cooperative associations report SIRS information to the state, mailed to Division of Florida Condominiums, Timeshares and Mobile Homes, Attention: Structural Integrity Reserve Study Reporting, 2601 Blair Stone Road, Tallahassee, Florida 32399-1030 [[sources/pdf-b922a12f47f0]].
- Associations with multiple projects or buildings must complete a separate form for each one; all individual forms may be submitted together and must be postmarked by 12/31/24 for the initial reporting window [[sources/pdf-b922a12f47f0]].
- A companion "Additional Building Address Form" must accompany the main form for each additional address when a project includes buildings at multiple addresses [[sources/pdf-b922a12f47f0]].
- The form codifies four preparer-credential categories: Architect, Engineer, Reserve Specialist, Qualified Person [[sources/pdf-b922a12f47f0]].
- Per-building data collected on the form: contact identification (name, phone, email); project identification (project name, project license number, association name, association license number); project type (Condominium or Cooperative); project address (street, line 2, city, state, ZIP, county); year of building construction based on certificate of occupancy; total units per building; total floors per building; name of individual and name of company that conducted the SIRS; total cost of the SIRS excluding assessment cost; whether an assessment resulted from the SIRS and if so the total assessment cost plus per-unit breakdown; SIRS completion date; and method of distribution to unit owners (Sent by Mail / Electronic Transmission via E-mail / Sent by Personal Delivery / Facsimile Transmission) [[sources/pdf-b922a12f47f0]].
- The form includes a certification block requiring printed name and signature certifying that the information provided is true, accurate, and complete to the best of the signer's knowledge [[sources/pdf-b922a12f47f0]].

### Bill identification

- CS/CS/HB 913 (2025), titled Condominium Associations, was sponsored by Rep. V. Lopez with companion CS/CS/SB 1742 (Bradley); the bill cleared Housing 18-0 and Agriculture & Tourism Commerce 23-0 in committee [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
