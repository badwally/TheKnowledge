# Daniel J. Galvin

## Summary

Associate Professor of Political Science at Northwestern University and IPR Fellow; co-author of the June 2018 IPR working paper WP-18-16 "The Limits of Policy Feedback as a Party-Building Tool," which argues that policies do not always generate their own political supports, that even when they do they rarely cement partisan loyalties, and that policy-building and party-building — though symbiotic — do fundamentally different things [[sources/pdf-5e6d63eac790]].

## Key facts

- Listed on the paper as Associate Professor of Political Science at Northwestern University and an IPR Fellow [[sources/pdf-5e6d63eac790]].
- Co-author with Chloe N. Thurston of "The Limits of Policy Feedback as a Party-Building Tool" (Version: June 20, 2018; IPR Working Paper WP-18-16; marked DRAFT, "Please do not quote or distribute without permission") [[sources/pdf-5e6d63eac790]].
- Authors are listed alphabetically; the paper states both contributed equally to the article [[sources/pdf-5e6d63eac790]].
- Acknowledges Phil Rocco, Andrew Kelly, Matthew Lacombe, and Mara Suttmann-Lea for comments on an earlier version of the paper [[sources/pdf-5e6d63eac790]].
- Frames Obama's presidency as a test case in which a policy-centered approach "did not do much to help Hillary Clinton in the 2016 election or down-ballot Democrats during his two terms," pointing to losses of 63 House seats, 11 Senate seats, 10 governorships, 27 state legislative chambers, and almost 1,000 state legislative seats over that period [[sources/pdf-5e6d63eac790]].
