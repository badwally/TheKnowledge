# Goldstein et al. 2022 (ECoG / Autoregressive LM Alignment)

## Summary

Goldstein et al. (2022) used electrocorticography (ECoG) — an invasive monitoring technique that records electrical activity directly from the exposed surface of the brain — on epilepsy patients to establish that the brain and autoregressive language models share specific computational mechanisms, not merely correlated outputs [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].

## Key facts

- Used ECoG on epilepsy patients to record electrical activity directly from the exposed cortical surface during language processing [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- Established that the brain and autoregressive language models share specific computational mechanisms — a stronger alignment claim than population-level activity correlation [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- Cited by Hammond as primary empirical support for the claim that language models work as well as they do because they emulate the brain's language centers, lending credence to the "simulator theory" of LLMs [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- Forms the empirical anchor of a three-study arc (Goldstein 2022 → 2024 LLM/FBN study → TopoLM 2025) that progressively tightens the case for brain-like architecture and organization in LLMs [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
