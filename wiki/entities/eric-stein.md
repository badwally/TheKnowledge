# Eric Stein

## Summary

Chief Investment Officer, Fixed Income; author of the Global Income chapter "Fed 'Pivot Trade' Likely to Drive 2023 Fixed Income Markets" in the December 12, 2022 Morgan Stanley "2023 Investment Outlook," in which he argues that even though the Fed pivot clearly hasn't happened yet, the pivot trade is starting to get priced in by a forward-looking market and will be the major factor for fixed income in 2023 — with high-quality spread products (agency MBS and securitized), U.S. duration, and emerging-markets debt as the three most attractive areas [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].

## Key facts

- Listed title in the outlook: Chief Investment Officer, Fixed Income; carries the CFA designation [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Characterizes 2022 fixed-income markets as across-the-board selloffs, sparked by both duration exposure (as inflation surprised to the upside and the Fed began raising rates) and credit-spread widening (as investors anticipated a slowing economy) [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Reads the late-November 2022 yield curve as continuing to invert — front end selling off, back end rallying — and notes that as December began, some spreads started to come in as credit markets began to price in a Fed "pivot" [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Argues that while the Fed pivot clearly hasn't happened yet, the market is a forward-looking mechanism and investors now expect rate increases to come at a slower pace than most of the second half of 2022, making the pivot trade the major factor for 2023 fixed-income opportunities [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- First attractive area — high-quality spread products: particularly agency mortgage-backed securities (MBS) and some securitized products, where spreads have stayed wide and have not come in as much as high yield and investment grade issues; expects investors to still want extra yield over Treasurys, drawing flows [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Second attractive area — U.S. duration: starting to look attractive as the curve inverts; sees value in U.S. Treasurys when the 10-year is in the range of 3.60% to 3.80% (down from the recent peak of 4.20%); execution options include direct U.S. Treasury issues or duration-heavy strategies like core plus or municipal bonds [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Third attractive area — emerging-markets debt: attractive for investors comfortable with a riskier asset class, particularly issues denominated in local currency; the U.S. dollar has started to weaken but is still at very strong historical levels, and EM corporate debt typically does well in a weak-dollar environment [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Argues the most important variable looking forward is what inflation will be over the medium term, and expects inflation will continue to come down in combination with a significant slowdown of the U.S. economy [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
