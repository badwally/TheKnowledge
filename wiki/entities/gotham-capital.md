# Gotham Capital

## Summary

Value-investing firm founded and run by Joel Greenblatt; per his Columbia Special Situation Investing class, the firm averaged 40% per year for 20 years (so that $1,000 at inception would be $836,683), stayed small by returning outside capital, and concentrated each portfolio in just 5 to 8 securities [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].

## Key facts

- Averaged 40% per year for 20 years, with $1,000 invested at inception growing to $836,683 [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Stayed small deliberately and returned outside capital so it could invest in as many situations as possible without size constraints [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Runs concentrated portfolios — 5 to 8 securities — on the premise that knowing your companies very well is more safe than diversifying [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- With three-to-five-year holding periods and only 4 to 6 securities, only needs one or two new ideas a year — which Greenblatt cites as the reason he has time to teach his Columbia class [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Brian Gains was an analyst at Gotham and a guest speaker in Greenblatt's Columbia class [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
