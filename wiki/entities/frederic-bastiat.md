# Frédéric Bastiat

## Summary

French nineteenth-century economist cited by Per Bylund for capturing the essence of economic order in the question "How does Paris get fed?" — illustrating that a large city's food supply emerges without any central plan directing farmers on what to sow, where to sell, or at what prices [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].

## Key facts

- Identified as a "French nineteenth-century economist" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- Posed the question "How does Paris get fed?" to illustrate that Parisians do not produce food but still have abundant access to it, despite the absence of any central plan for what types and quantities of food are offered and when [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- Bylund uses Bastiat's question to demonstrate that the economy is "a decentralized and distributed system where all people — farmers and city folk alike — make their own plans and decisions" rather than carry out orders from some central command [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- Bylund's Chapter 9, "Regulatory Intervention," extends Bastiat's classic seen-versus-unseen framework by adding a third category — "The Unrealized" — effects of intervention that never come into existence because the intervention prevents them [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
