# Karl Friston

## Summary

Karl Friston is the computational neuroscientist whose body of work extends hierarchical predictive coding from a model of perception into a unified account that also encompasses action, learning, and the control of cortical plasticity [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]]. Clark's 2013 BBS target article treats Friston's formulation (Friston 2005; Friston 2010; Friston & Stephan 2007; Friston et al. 2009; Brown et al. 2011) as the main focus for its treatment of "hierarchical prediction machine" approaches to brain function [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].

## Key facts

- Friston's framework combines top-down probabilistic generative models with the predictive-coding strategy of transmitting only residual prediction errors upward through a cortical hierarchy [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].
- Friston's extensions are credited with carrying predictive coding from perception into action, offering an attractive, unifying perspective on the brain's capacities for learning, inference, and plasticity [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].
- The reciprocally connected bidirectional cortical structure that predictive coding requires is argued to map onto known facts about cortical wiring (Friston 2005; Lee & Mumford 2003) [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].
