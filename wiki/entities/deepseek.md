# DeepSeek

## Summary

China-based AI lab whose open-weight R1 model, released January 2025, signaled that Chinese labs could approach frontier performance despite U.S. export controls on advanced semiconductors; Time Magazine labeled R1 one of the "Best Inventions of 2025" [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].

## Key facts

- Released DeepSeek-R1 in January 2025; the release signaled that Chinese labs could approach frontier performance despite U.S. export controls on advanced semiconductors [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
- DeepSeek-R1 was named one of Time Magazine's "Best Inventions of 2025" [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
- Achieved gold-medal performance on questions from the 2025 International Math Olympiad (IMO), alongside OpenAI and Google DeepMind [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
- Released DeepSeek-V3.2-Speciale in December 2025, the first open model capable of IMO gold-level scores as of the paper's publication [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
- Did not invent the mixture-of-experts (MoE) architecture but, with R1, popularized the use of large-scale MoE architectures in open frontier models [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
- With R1, helped boost the popularity of chain-of-thought reasoning, in which models reason through intermediate steps before producing a final answer [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
- A 2025 Hoover Institution study cited in the Stanford Emerging Technology Review 2026 found that more than half of China's leading AI researchers behind DeepSeek's breakthrough large language model were educated and trained entirely in China [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- Innovation Endeavors' June 2025 "State of Foundation Models" report identifies DeepSeek-VL as released in March 2024 with an estimated training cost of less than $10M, contrasted against GPT-4's $100M+ cost approximately one year earlier as evidence of frontier-model depreciation and open-source convergence [[sources/pdf-5bec4feeb233]].
- Innovation Endeavors (June 2025) lists DeepSeek v2 and v3 among notable mixture-of-experts models, alongside Mixtral and (rumored) GPT-4 modeled as 8×220B [[sources/pdf-5bec4feeb233]].
- Innovation Endeavors (June 2025) attributes to a DeepSeek paper on Generalist Reward Modeling the framing that generalist reward models are "the holy grail" of reward-model research but are difficult to build [[sources/pdf-5bec4feeb233]].
