# Tobias Moskowitz

## Summary

Finance professor at the University of Chicago Booth School of Business and NBER; lead author of the 2011 working paper "Time Series Momentum," which documents significant positive return predictability from a security's own past returns across 58 liquid futures and forward contracts spanning equity indices, currencies, commodities, and sovereign bonds over more than 25 years [[sources/pdf-lasse-pedersen-2011-title-how-to]].

## Key facts

- Affiliated with the University of Chicago Booth School of Business and NBER on the paper [[sources/pdf-lasse-pedersen-2011-title-how-to]].
- Lead author of "Time Series Momentum" (Working Paper No. 79, Chicago Booth Paper No. 12-21, September 2011; SSRN abstract id 2089463), co-authored with Yao Hua Ooi and Lasse H. Pedersen [[sources/pdf-lasse-pedersen-2011-title-how-to]].
- Thanks the Initiative on Global Markets at the University of Chicago Booth School of Business and CRSP for financial support for the research [[sources/pdf-lasse-pedersen-2011-title-how-to]].
- The paper documents that the past 12-month excess return of each of 58 liquid instruments is a positive predictor of its future return, with the effect persisting for about a year and then partially reversing over longer horizons [[sources/pdf-lasse-pedersen-2011-title-how-to]].
- Finds that a diversified portfolio of time series momentum strategies across all asset classes yields a Sharpe ratio greater than one on an annual basis — roughly 2.5 times the market Sharpe ratio — with little correlation to passive benchmarks or standard asset pricing factors [[sources/pdf-lasse-pedersen-2011-title-how-to]].
- Co-authored earlier cross-sectional momentum and industry momentum research: Moskowitz and Grinblatt (1999) on industry momentum, and Asness, Moskowitz, and Pedersen (2010) on cross-sectional momentum and value strategies across asset classes [[sources/pdf-lasse-pedersen-2011-title-how-to]].
