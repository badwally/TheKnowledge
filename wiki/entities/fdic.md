# Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC)

## Summary

The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation (FDIC) is an independent agency of the United States government that protects depositors' funds in savings associations and banks by insuring deposits up to a specified statutory limit per depositor, per bank, per ownership category [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]]. FDIC insurance is backed by the U.S. government and was established in 1933; since then, no bank or savings association depositor has lost any FDIC-insured funds [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]]. The FDIC is the US-side analog to Canada's CDIC, BC's CUDIC, Alberta's CUDGC, and Ontario's FSRA-administered Deposit Insurance Reserve Fund — but with a higher per-depositor coverage ceiling ($250,000) and a narrower ownership-category structure under which homeowners associations are classified [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].

## Key facts

- Independent agency of the United States government [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Protects depositors' funds in savings associations and banks by insuring a certain amount per depositor [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Established in 1933 [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- FDIC insurance is backed by the U.S. government [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Since 1933, no bank or savings association depositor has lost any FDIC-insured funds [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Standard insured limit: $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- If a bank or savings association fails, depositors can claim their funds up to the FDIC's coverage limit [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].

### Covered deposit products

- Savings accounts [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Checking accounts [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Time deposits, including certificates of deposit (CDs) [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Money market deposit accounts (MMDAs) [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Negotiable Order of Withdrawal (NOW) accounts [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Money orders, cashier's checks, and other official bank-issued items [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].

### Non-covered financial products

- Mutual funds [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Bond investments [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Stock investments [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Annuities [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Life insurance policies [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Cryptocurrency assets [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Municipal securities [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Safe deposit boxes and their contents [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- U.S. Treasury notes, bills, and bonds [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].

### HOA classification

- Homeowners associations — whether incorporated or unincorporated — typically fall under the Corporation/Partnership/Unincorporated Association Accounts ownership category [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Under that category, an HOA has a $250,000 reserve-funds FDIC limit per Taxpayer Identification Number [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- All deposit accounts owned by the same association at the same bank — including the bank's branches — are aggregated and treated as one for FDIC-limit purposes [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- HOAs cannot use multiple deposit account types at the same bank to circumvent the FDIC limit [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- If an HOA exceeds the FDIC limit at a single bank, that excess may be noted in the independent auditor's report [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
