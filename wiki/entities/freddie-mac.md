# Freddie Mac

## Summary

Freddie Mac is one of the two US government-sponsored enterprises (GSEs) whose project standards and property insurance requirements determine whether condominium unit loans qualify for the conventional secondary mortgage market. On March 18, 2026, Freddie Mac issued — jointly with Fannie Mae — a significant update to project standards and property insurance requirements designed to simplify certain processes, respond to insurance market challenges, and strengthen the financial health of condominium communities; Freddie Mac's parallel guidance is Bulletin 2026-C [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]]. The March 2026 update is characterized by industry commentators as the most significant round of condominium lending standard updates in several years [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]]. Industry coverage in the NYC condo/co-op market has emphasized that the 15% reserve-funding floor and the retirement of Limited Review will drive substantial demand for reserve-study services and cascade ineligibility designations into adjacent jumbo and commercial lending markets [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]]. Community-association legal commentary groups Freddie Mac alongside Fannie Mae and Ginnie Mae as semi-governmental agencies that establish minimum standards for mortgage lending in order to stabilize the housing market and make homeownership affordable in the U.S. [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]].

## Key facts

- Full legal name: Federal Home Loan Mortgage Corporation [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Is a government-sponsored enterprise that purchases mortgages from lenders after those loans are made; the eligibility standards Freddie Mac imposes effectively govern most conventional residential mortgages in the United States [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Characterized by community-association legal commentary as one of three semi-governmental agencies — alongside Fannie Mae and Ginnie Mae — that establish minimum standards for mortgage lending in order to stabilize the housing market and make homeownership affordable in the U.S. [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]].
- Evaluates the entire condominium project, not just the borrower; if an association's finances, reserves, or insurance fail Freddie Mac's standards, buyers in that community cannot obtain affordable conventional financing [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Issued Bulletin 2026-C on March 18, 2026 in coordination with Fannie Mae's Lender Letter LL-2026-03, updating project standards and property insurance requirements for condominium loans [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]] [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Stated rationale for the joint update: simplify certain processes, respond to insurance market challenges, and strengthen the financial health of condominium communities [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Freddie Mac project-review criteria are explicitly characterized by CAI Advocacy as not always aligned with actual financial stability and performance of condominium communities [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Began implementing new condominium underwriting standards in late 2021, alongside Fannie Mae, in response to the June 2021 partial collapse of Champlain Towers South in Surfside, Florida [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Retired the limited review process for loans with application dates on or after August 3, 2026 (limited review historically represented roughly 40% of all project reviews) [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]]; NYC industry coverage characterizes the limited review process as having been used by "at least 40% of loans in the U.S." [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- The Limited Review retirement applies specifically to established condominium projects with more than 10 units; every loan in those communities must now go through Full Review [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Industry commentary characterizes the pre-March-2026 limited-review regime as having allowed "many condominium communities [to skirt] through without reserving at all for future repairs," per Strategic Inspections president and CEO Orest Tomaselli [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- For properties that had not been compliant with the prior rules on reserves, critical repairs, or special assessments, "there is nowhere to hide" under the post-March-2026 full-review regime, per Tomaselli [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Expanded eligibility for a waiver of project review to include projects with up to 10 units [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- The previous project-review-waiver threshold was 4 units; the March 2026 update raised it to 10 units [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- For projects in the five-to-ten-unit range, the community cannot be part of a master association or larger development in order to qualify for the project-review waiver [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Eliminated the long-standing 50% investor concentration limit [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]] [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Retired Florida-specific review requirements for new, attached condominium projects [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Increased the minimum reserve funding requirement from 10% to 15% of the annual budget, effective January 4, 2027 [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]] [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]]; NYC industry coverage frames the change as adding "an additional 5% to be added to operating costs" on top of inflationary cost pressure facing boards [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- The 15% budget allocation may be waived if the association has a reserve study conducted or updated within the last three years and is following the highest recommended level of funding; baseline funding is not allowed as a substitute [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]] [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]]; NYC market coverage characterizes the alternative window as "a reserve study completed within the past 36 months" [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Reserve studies in the NYC condo/co-op market cost between $7,500 and $9,500, per Strategic Inspections president and CEO Orest Tomaselli [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Industry commentators note that a reserve study can require a contribution far higher than 15%, making the actual reserve-funding requirement a community-by-community determination, per Tomaselli [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Buildings will "eventually run out of that reserve study benefit," per Moritt Hock & Hamroff partner David Fitzhenry, meaning the reserve-study alternative is a time-limited workaround for buildings that do not choose to hold the full 15% [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Without holding the 15% minimum in capital reserves, boards will increasingly find loans are not approved for their building, per Fitzhenry [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Fitzhenry has characterized the impact of the 15% requirement as "another sticker shock" for boards while acknowledging the policy is "well intentioned" [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- The baseline funding method (which essentially targets a near-zero reserve balance) is no longer permitted under any circumstances [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- When a condominium building is flagged as ineligible for Freddie Mac financing, other lenders — including jumbo and commercial — often follow suit, per Tomaselli; the ineligibility designation has been characterized as "an almost automatic reason not to lend in any capacity in the community," producing impact felt equally from affordable units to luxury condos [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Eliminated strict replacement cost documentation requirements and removed the requirement to insure roofs at full replacement cost; roofs may now be covered on an actual cash value basis [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]] [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]]; industry commentary characterizes the roof-insurance rollback as "spectacular" relief, particularly for buildings previously denied residential mortgage financing because insurance policies could not be aligned with the prior guidelines, per Tomaselli [[sources/web-2026-04-21-260]].
- Removed the inflation guard requirement for condominium association property insurance [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]] [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Imposed a new maximum deductible cap of $50,000 per unit on master property insurance policies, effective July 1, 2026 [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]] [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Introduced new loan-servicer obligations: annual verification of insurance coverage, monitoring for reductions in coverage, and a new annual borrower reminder to maintain insurance, fully effective by January 1, 2027 [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Maintains a published ineligible project list that condominium communities are encouraged to consult to confirm eligibility status [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Maintains the Freddie Mac Condo Project Advisor at sf.freddiemac.com/tools-learning/condo-project-advisor/overview as the project-review tool used by lenders [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
