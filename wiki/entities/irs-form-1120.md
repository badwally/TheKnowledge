# IRS Form 1120 (U.S. Corporation Income Tax Return)

## Summary

IRS Form 1120 is the regular U.S. corporation income tax return form. A homeowners association may elect to file as a regular corporation using Form 1120 instead of using the HOA-specific Form 1120-H, and this election decision can change annually [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]]. Form 1120 is much more difficult to prepare than Form 1120-H, and can potentially cause membership net income to be taxable, but it offers a much more favorable tax rate of 15% on the first $50,000 of taxable income [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]]. Reserve interest income is taxable under Form 1120, the same as under Form 1120-H [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].

## Key facts

- The regular U.S. corporation income tax return form [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Available as an annual filing election for homeowners associations as an alternative to Form 1120-H [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Much more difficult to prepare than Form 1120-H [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Can potentially cause membership net income to be taxable, in contrast with Form 1120-H where membership income is generally non-taxable [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Taxes the first $50,000 of taxable income at a 15% rate, more favorable than the Form 1120-H 30% rate on non-exempt function income [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Reserve interest income is taxable on Form 1120 [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Form 1120 requires the association to involve a CPA during tax season for proper handling of capital vs. non-capital reserve project categorization [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- The decision to file Form 1120 carries inherent risks that the board must weigh, including more restrictive accounting procedures and what appears to be additional IRS audit risk [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- More restrictive Form 1120 accounting procedures include segregation of operating and reserve cash, adoption and adherence to a budget that agrees with a reserve study, and adequate accounting for various reserve items by capital and non-capital categories [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- The board must carefully weigh whether the association qualifies to file Form 1120 and whether the tax advantages outweigh the audit risk [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
