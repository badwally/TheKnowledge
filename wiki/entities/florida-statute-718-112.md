# Florida Statute §718.112 (Bylaws)

## Summary

Florida Statute §718.112 is the section of the Florida Condominium Act (Chapter 718) that governs the bylaws of Florida condominium associations. Subsection (2) ("Required Provisions") enumerates provisions that bylaws shall contain — and that are deemed included if absent — covering association administration and officer/board composition, unit-owner written inquiries, quorum and voting requirements (including limited vs. general proxies), board of administration meeting procedures and notice, and unit-owner meetings (including board elections, term limits, and candidate eligibility) [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].

## Key facts

### Administration (§718.112(2)(a))

- In the absence of bylaw provisions, the board of administration is composed of five members, unless the condominium has five or fewer units, in which case the board of a not-for-profit corporation shall consist of not fewer than three members [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Default officer roster (absent contrary bylaws) is a president, a secretary, and a treasurer, who perform the duties of such officers customarily performed by officers of corporations [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Unless prohibited by bylaws, the board may appoint other officers and grant them the duties it deems appropriate [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Officers serve without compensation and at the pleasure of the board unless the bylaws provide otherwise; board members serve without compensation unless the bylaws provide otherwise [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- When a unit owner of a residential condominium files a written inquiry by certified mail with the board, the board shall respond in writing within 30 days after receipt, either substantively, by notifying the inquirer a legal opinion has been requested, or by notifying the inquirer that advice has been requested from the division [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- If the board requests advice from the division, the board shall within 10 days of receiving that advice provide a written substantive response to the inquirer; if a legal opinion is requested, the board shall within 60 days after receipt of the inquiry provide a written substantive response [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Failure to provide a substantive response precludes the association from recovering attorney fees and costs in any subsequent litigation, administrative proceeding, or arbitration arising out of the inquiry [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- The association may by rule limit responses to one written inquiry per unit in any given 30-day period; additional inquiries are responded to in subsequent 30-day periods [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].

### Quorum, voting, and proxies (§718.112(2)(b))

- Unless a lower number is provided in the bylaws, a quorum is a majority of the voting interests; absent contrary provisions, decisions are made by a majority of the voting interests represented at a meeting at which a quorum is present [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Unit owners in a residential condominium may not vote by general proxy but may vote by limited proxies substantially conforming to a limited proxy form adopted by the division [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- A voting interest or consent right allocated to a unit owned by the association may not be exercised or considered for any purpose, whether for a quorum, an election, or otherwise [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Limited proxies must be used for votes to waive or reduce reserves under §718.112(2)(f)2, to waive financial reporting requirements of §718.111(13), to amend the declaration under §718.110, and to amend the articles of incorporation or bylaws [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Except in limited circumstances under paragraph (d), a proxy may not be used in the election of board members in a residential condominium [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- A proxy is effective only for the specific meeting for which originally given and any lawfully adjourned meetings; a proxy is not valid longer than 90 days after the first meeting for which it was given and is revocable at any time [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- A board or committee member's participation by telephone, real-time videoconferencing, or similar real-time electronic or video communication counts toward a quorum, and such member may vote as if physically present, provided a speaker is used so the member's conversation can be heard by attendees [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].

### Board of administration meetings (§718.112(2)(c))

- In a residential condominium association of more than 10 units, the board of administration shall meet at least once each quarter; at least four times each year the meeting agenda must include an opportunity for members to ask questions of the board [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Meetings of the board of administration at which a quorum is present are open to all unit owners; unit owners may tape record or videotape the meetings, subject to division rules [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Members of the board may use e-mail as a means of communication but may not cast a vote on an association matter via e-mail [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Adequate notice of all board meetings, specifically identifying all agenda items, must be posted conspicuously on the condominium property at least 48 continuous hours before the meeting, except in an emergency [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- If 20 percent of the voting interests petition the board to address an item of business, the board within 60 days of receipt must place the item on the agenda at its next regular or a special board meeting [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- An item not included on the notice may be taken up on an emergency basis by a vote of at least a majority plus one of the board members; such emergency action must be noticed and ratified at the next regular board meeting [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Written notice of a meeting at which a nonemergency special assessment or an amendment to rules regarding unit use will be considered must be mailed, delivered, or electronically transmitted to unit owners and posted conspicuously on the condominium property at least 14 days before the meeting; compliance with the 14-day notice requirement must be evidenced by an affidavit filed with the official records [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Notice of any meeting at which regular or special assessments will be considered must specifically state that assessments will be considered and provide the estimated cost and description of the purposes for the assessments [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- If an agenda item relates to the approval of a contract for goods or services, a copy of the contract must be provided with the notice and made available for inspection and copying upon written request, or made available on the association's website or downloadable mobile application [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- The open-meeting requirement does not apply to meetings between the board (or a committee) and the association's attorney with respect to proposed or pending litigation when held for the purpose of seeking or rendering legal advice, or to board meetings held for the purpose of discussing personnel matters [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].

### Unit owner meetings, board elections, and term limits (§718.112(2)(d))

- An annual meeting of the unit owners must be held at the location in the bylaws; if the bylaws are silent, the meeting must be held within 45 miles of the condominium property (the 45-mile rule does not apply to timeshare condominiums) [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Unless the bylaws provide otherwise, a vacancy on the board caused by expiration of a director's term must be filled by electing a new board member, and the election must be by secret ballot; an election is not required if the number of vacancies equals or exceeds the number of candidates [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- A board member may not serve more than 8 consecutive years unless approved by an affirmative vote of unit owners representing two-thirds of all votes cast in the election or unless there are not enough eligible candidates to fill the vacancies at the time of the vacancy [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Only board service that occurs on or after July 1, 2018 may be used when calculating a board member's term limit [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- In a residential condominium association of more than 10 units (or any residential condominium association that does not include timeshare units or interests), co-owners of a unit may not serve as members of the board of directors at the same time unless they own more than one unit or there are not enough eligible candidates to fill the vacancies [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- A person who is delinquent in the payment of any assessment due to the association is not eligible to be a candidate for board membership and may not be listed on the ballot; the due date is that specifically identified in the declaration, bylaws, or articles, or otherwise the first day of the assessment period [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- A person who has been convicted of any felony in Florida, in a United States District or Territorial Court, or of any offense in another jurisdiction which would be considered a felony if committed in Florida, is not eligible for board membership unless the felon's civil rights have been restored for at least 5 years as of the date the person seeks election; the validity of an action by the board is not affected if a board member is later determined ineligible due to a felony conviction [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- The board-term-limit provisions of subparagraph (d)2 do not limit the term of a member of the board of a nonresidential or timeshare condominium [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
