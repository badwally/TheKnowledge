# Scott Redler

## Summary

Professional trader and Chief Strategic Officer of T3 Live and T3 Trading Group; author of "The Ultimate Guide to Moving Averages," a 23-page eBook in which he argues that exponential moving averages — specifically the 8, 21, 50, and 200 day EMAs — sit at the top of his analytic stack above headlines, economic data, earnings, and other indicators, and operationalizes the 8 & 21 day EMAs as the primary road map for short-term market exposure [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].

## Key facts

- Has been a professional trader since 1999 [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Co-founded T3 Companies, LLC in 2007 after eight years of successful trading and serves as Chief Strategic Officer of both T3 Live and T3 Trading Group [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Became a recurring guest on CNBC at the onset of the financial crisis, repeatedly warning investors of the danger ahead [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Regularly appears on CNBC, Bloomberg Television, and Fox Business, and his technical analysis work has been cited by the Wall Street Journal, Reuters, MarketWatch, and Investor's Business Daily, among others [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Has trained traders around the world and has appeared in documentaries by the BBC and China's CCTV-2 [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Operates Redler All-Access, a service through which he helps traders, money managers, and individual investors view markets through his eyes [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Argues moving averages help him determine how aggressive to be with his portfolio, which stocks to be long or short, the strength of the current market trend, and what news matters and what doesn't [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Rates moving averages above headlines, economic data, earnings, and "just about any indicator you can think of" [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- States that if he were a beginning trader looking to build net worth, moving averages would be his #1 focus [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Uses 8 EMA (very short-term), 21 EMA (short-term), 50 EMA (intermediate), and 200 EMA (long-term), choosing exponential MAs because they are more sensitive to recent action and give a slightly better read on the near-term trend [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Argues that the exact moving averages used (e.g., 8 vs 10 day, 21 vs 20 day) matter less than how well the trader uses them to manage risk; he sticks with 8 and 21 because his brain is trained to judge action on those time frames [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Whether on CNBC, Twitter, or T3 Live's Virtual Trading Floor, his commentary centers on the 8 & 21 day EMAs because, in his framing, they are the most accurate short-term road map he has found [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Cites Beyond Meat (BYND) early 2020 as a case study where the stock had two prior failures at the 8 & 21 day, then resolved above them with a $22 move in 4 days on the McDonald's partnership expansion [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Cites Tesla (TSLA) November–Cybertruck rally as a case where the stock held the 8 & 21 day EMAs through the Cybertruck unveiling backlash and rallied — "one of the biggest hits in Redler All-Access history" [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Cites SPY's June 30, 2016 post-Brexit reclaim of the 8 & 21 day EMAs as a textbook "statement of strength" he expects to be teaching ten years from now [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Operates two primary market postures: a Portfolio Approach (4–12 long positions, plenty of tech, no short hedge unless extended) when SPY trends above the 8/21/50/200 EMAs, and a Tactical Approach (take profits, look for shorts, hedge with SPY short) when SPY breaks the 8 & 21 day [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- If a stock, index, or ETF loses the 8 & 21 day EMAs, his next reference is the 50 day EMA, which he treats as the intermediate trend [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Uses the 200 day EMA as the long-term trend reference, with SNAP cited as a 50 day case study and MJ and GLD cited as 200 day case studies [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
