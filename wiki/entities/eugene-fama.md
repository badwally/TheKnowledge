# Eugene Fama

## Summary

Finance professor at the University of Chicago; central figure in the Efficient Market Hypothesis (EMH) and Cliff Asness's classroom mentor — whose finance class Asness recalls for the moment Fama tells students "markets are almost assuredly not perfectly efficient," arguing that perfect efficiency is "an extreme and unrealistic hypothesis" [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].

## Key facts

- Taught a two-class Finance series at the University of Chicago that Cliff Asness attended three years in a row (once as a student, twice as a T.A.) [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Per Asness, in the first few weeks after introducing the basic idea of EMH Fama would shock the class by announcing that "markets are almost assuredly not perfectly efficient," eliciting a mild gasp [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Per Asness, Fama's argument is that perfect efficiency is "an extreme and unrealistic hypothesis," with the Grossman-Stiglitz (1980) paradox as the formal proof of this impossibility [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Co-authored Fama and French (2005), which Asness cites as formalizing the notion that investor tastes for things other than risk and return will affect prices and expected returns going forward, except in the highly unlikely case where they exactly offset [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Asness describes Fama as "nothing if not intellectually honest about such things" [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
