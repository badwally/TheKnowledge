# Internal Revenue Code §528 (Certain homeowners associations)

## Summary

Internal Revenue Code §528 is the federal statutory authority under which qualifying homeowners associations may elect special tax treatment — excluding exempt function income from gross income in exchange for a flat tax rate on non-exempt taxable income [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]]. The election is made annually by filing Form 1120-H, and the statute, together with its implementing regulations at Treas. Reg. §§1.528-1 through 1.528-10, defines the three permissible association types, the qualifying-association threshold tests (60% gross income / 90% expenditure / no private benefit), the definition of exempt function income, allowed deductions, and the regulatory grace mechanism for late elections (Treas. Reg. §301.9100-2) [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]]. The §528 regime is jurisdiction-agnostic at the federal level and operates uniformly across all US states, sitting alongside Chapter 718 (Florida), 765 ILCS 605/9 (Illinois), NJ S2760/S3992, and other state condominium statutes that govern state-level reserve-funding and disclosure obligations.

## Key facts

### Qualifying-association requirements

- To qualify as a homeowners association under §528, at least 60% of the association's gross income for the tax year must consist of exempt function income [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- At least 90% of the association's expenses for the tax year must consist of expenses to acquire, build, manage, maintain, and care for its property, and, in the case of a timeshare association, for activities provided to or on behalf of members of the timeshare association [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- No private shareholder or individual can profit from the association's net earnings except by acquiring, building, managing, maintaining, or caring for association property, or by a rebate of excess membership dues, fees, or assessments [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- The association must file Form 1120-H to elect under §528 to be treated as a homeowners association [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].

### Definitions imported from §528

- Three §528 association types are recognized: a condominium management association; a residential real estate management association; and a timeshare association (other than a condominium management association) [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- A timeshare association cannot also be a condominium management association [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- Association property includes real and personal property that the association holds, that the association's members hold in common, that the association's members hold privately within the association, and that is owned by a governmental unit and used to benefit the unit's residents; see Treas. Reg. §1.528-3 [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- Timeshare association property includes property related to the timeshare project that the association or its members have rights to use, where those rights arise out of recorded easements, covenants, or other recorded instruments [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- Exempt function income consists of membership dues, fees, or assessments from owners of condominium housing units; owners of real property in the case of a residential real estate management association; or owners of timeshare rights to use or timeshare ownership interests in the case of a timeshare association — and must come from the members as owners, not as customers, of the association's services [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].

### Implementing regulations

- Treas. Reg. §1.528-3 governs the definition of association property [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- Treas. Reg. §1.528-4 governs the 'substantially all' test for condominium management associations and residential real estate management associations [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- Treas. Reg. §1.528-8(e) governs the consequence of an association losing exempt status [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- Treas. Reg. §1.528-9 governs the further enumeration of exempt and non-exempt function income [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- Treas. Reg. §1.528-10 governs the cost-allocation rule when facilities or personnel are used for both exempt and non-exempt purposes [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- Treas. Reg. §301.9100-2 provides the automatic 12-month extension for making the §528 election where the regulatory election was not timely made [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].

### Allowed and disallowed deductions

- §528 taxable income is the excess, if any, of gross income for the tax year (excluding exempt function income) over allowed deductions directly connected with producing that non-exempt gross income, including a specific $100 deduction [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- The §172 net operating loss deduction is not allowed against §528 taxable income [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- Deductions under Part VIII of subchapter B (special deductions for corporations) are not allowed against §528 taxable income [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
