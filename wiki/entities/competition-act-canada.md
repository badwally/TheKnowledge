# Competition Act (Canada)

## Summary

The Competition Act is the Canadian federal competition statute administered and enforced by the Competition Bureau [[sources/web-2024-06-27-57a]]. It pairs a criminal cartel prohibition (section 45) with a civil reviewable-matters regime (Part VIII, including section 90.1 for agreements between competitors that substantially prevent or lessen competition) [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].

## Key facts

- Section 45 — criminal conspiracy provision; reserved for naked-restraint agreements between competitors to fix prices, allocate markets, or restrict output [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Section 47 — bid-rigging offence; bid consortia made known to the person who called for the bids at or before submission/withdrawal are not assessed under section 45 [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Section 49 — provision for agreements between federal financial institutions; subsection 49(1) routes such agreements out of section 45, and subsection 49(2) creates an exemption [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Sections 75 through 84 — reviewable-matters provisions in Part VIII covering purely vertical agreements between suppliers and customers; vertical arrangements are generally assessed here rather than under section 45 [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Section 79 — abuse-of-dominance provision; can apply to agreements between competitors who are jointly dominant where the agreement has or facilitates exclusionary, predatory, or disciplinary conduct [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Section 90.1 — civil agreements provision for agreements between competitors that prevent or lessen, or are likely to prevent or lessen, competition substantially; subsection 90.1(9) exempts agreements for which the Minister of Finance has issued a financial-policy certification [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Sections 92 and following — merger provisions; acquisitions of control or significant interest are generally assessed here rather than under sections 45 or 90.1 [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Sections 11 and 15 — formal information-gathering powers the Bureau may invoke when determining the appropriate analytical track for an arrangement [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Section 124.1 — mechanism for firms to request a binding written opinion from the Commissioner on the applicability of provisions of the Act to proposed future conduct [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- The 2009 amendments to the Act created the modern criminal cartel regime (a per-se-style section 45) and explicitly removed the threat of criminal sanctions for legitimate collaborations, to avoid chilling pro-competitive alliances [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
