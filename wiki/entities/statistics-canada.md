# Statistics Canada

## Summary

Statistics Canada is the Canadian federal statistical agency whose administrative datasets Sen (2026) identifies as a "gold-standard" potential training corpus for private-sector AI/ML development and as the linchpin of his proposed data-supply-chain pilot [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].

## Key facts

- Maintains administrative datasets on individual tax records, income, employment status, and access to healthcare; Sen (2026) characterizes these as extremely difficult for private-sector firms to replicate [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
- Does not collect or release real-time data, but its near-universal Canadian population coverage gives its datasets minimal selection bias relative to private digital-platform datasets [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
- Operates the Longitudinal Administrative Databank, a 20% sample of all Canadian tax filers with annual records from 1982 onward [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
- Distributes the National Ambulatory Care Reporting System (NACRS) across Canadian health facilities [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
- Sen (2026) recommends granting carefully regulated private-sector access to some confidential Statistics Canada datasets on a pilot basis as the central recommendation of his data-supply-chain framework [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
- Sen (2026) notes the Canadian public generally trusts Statistics Canada and perceives societal benefits from sharing its information; maintaining this trust requires transparency about who has access to any shared data [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
- Sen (2026) argues Statistics Canada is well-positioned to enforce the three properties of a secure AI data supply chain: statistically representative collection, secure storage, and access under strict privacy protocols including PETs [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
