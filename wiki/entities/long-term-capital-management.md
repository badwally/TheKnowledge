# Long-Term Capital Management (LTCM)

## Summary

A $4 billion hedge fund founded by approximately a dozen former Salomon Brothers colleagues that took leveraged positions in global fixed income and equity markets to extract arbitrage profits; its near-collapse in 1998 — triggered by the Russian Ruble crisis, compounding earlier losses, and mass margin calls across Wall Street — required a Fed-engineered bailout of $3.5 billion from 14 banks and 75 basis points of rate cuts in under two months including an intermeeting surprise cut, making it the defining leverage-event deleveraging of the modern era [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].

## Key facts

- Founded by "a dozen or so ex colleagues from Salomon Brothers" [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Took leveraged positions in global fixed income and equity markets to extract arbitrage profits within and across asset markets [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- After the 1997 Asian Financial Crisis, hedge funds and proprietary desks began questioning the opportunity set; LTCM returned $2 billion to its investors to address the smaller opportunity set [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- By Q2 1998, LTCM had started giving back profits on holdings [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- The Russian Ruble crisis in mid-August 1998 exposed LTCM and other multi-strategy hedge funds to both EM risk and similar trades [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- LTCM was the "well-deserved poster child" of the 1998 deleveraging, but the exposure was broader — it was "part of a large, concentrated exposure that included banks" [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Every silo at Salomon and across most of Wall Street had some exposure to LTCM [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Constan's equity derivatives business at Salomon held 5 million vega of 4-year straddles purchased from LTCM as a hedge to other customer positions; uncertainty about whether the hedge would survive an LTCM bankruptcy forced Salomon to bid up long-term implied volatility to replace the exposure, creating a vicious circle as this required LTCM to place more collateral [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- The Fed engineered the bailout by organizing investment of $3.5 billion of capital by 14 banks [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- The Fed then cut rates by 75 basis points in less than 2 months, including a highly unusual intermeeting surprise cut late in the afternoon on October 15, 1998 [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- The bailout was designed not to keep LTCM afloat in perpetuity but to slow down the liquidation [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- When LTCM finally unwound positions months later, they had over 100 million vega to buy; the mark to market was so inflated that straddles traded well below the bid side, and from that moment implied volatility never returned to those levels for years [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Constan was sent to LTCM by Sandy Weill with colleagues to attempt to buy the book of positions; was "rebuffed nicely" by Victor Haghani, who now runs Elm Wealth (focused on optimal beta investing) [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- The SPX fell 19.2% from peak in July 1998, making a low on October 8, and recovered rapidly — just 46 days later — to retake the July high [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Per Constan, "the economy didn't miss a beat, and the tech bubble inflated further due to the actions taken during this deleveraging" [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
