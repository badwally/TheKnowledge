# Ginnie Mae

## Summary

Ginnie Mae is grouped by community-association legal commentary alongside Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac as one of three semi-governmental agencies that establish minimum standards for mortgage lending in order to stabilize the housing market and make homeownership affordable in the United States [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]]. Unlike Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac (whose project standards directly determine condominium loan warrantability), Ginnie Mae's role in the KSN explainer is structural-framing only — Gonzalez introduces all three GSEs at the top of the article to position FNMA as the operative gatekeeper for condominium mortgage eligibility, then focuses the article exclusively on Fannie Mae's March 18, 2026 LL-2026-03 reserve-funding update.

## Key facts

- Listed by community-association legal commentary as a semi-governmental agency that establishes minimum standards for mortgage lending in order to stabilize the housing market and make homeownership affordable in the U.S. [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]].
- Grouped alongside Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac in this gatekeeper framing; among the three, Fannie Mae is the agency Gonzalez characterizes as playing the critical role in condominium mortgage eligibility (~75% governance share) [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]].
- KSN's March 24, 2026 GSE-update explainer does not assign Ginnie Mae any specific condominium-project-review or reserve-funding-standard role beyond the introductory framing — the operative March 18, 2026 changes are FNMA (LL-2026-03) and the parallel Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-C; no Ginnie Mae directive of comparable scope is cited [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]].
