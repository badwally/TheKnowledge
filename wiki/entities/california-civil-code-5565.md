# California Civil Code § 5565 (Reserve Summary Required Elements)

## Summary

California Civil Code § 5565 is the section of the Davis-Stirling Common Interest Development Act that specifies the required content of the reserve summary that a common interest development association must distribute to its members. The reserve summary required by § 5565 is the disclosure artifact called for by paragraph (2) of subdivision (b) of Civil Code § 5300, and must be based on the most recent reserve review or study conducted pursuant to Civil Code § 5550 [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]]. § 5565 imposes three threshold constraints on the summary — it must be based on the most recent § 5550 review/study, it must be based only on assets held in cash or cash equivalents, and it must be printed in boldface type — and then enumerates four substantive content categories: per-component estimated replacement cost / remaining life / useful life; fiscal-year-end cash-reserve estimates (required cash, accumulated cash, and construction-defect-litigation proceeds); the percentage funded; and the current deficiency in reserve funding expressed on a per-unit basis [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].

## Key facts

### Linkage to § 5300 and § 5550

- The § 5565 reserve summary is the disclosure required by paragraph (2) of subdivision (b) of Civil Code § 5300 [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- The summary must be based on the most recent review or study conducted pursuant to Civil Code § 5550 [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].

### Threshold formatting and asset-basis constraints

- The summary must be based only on assets held in cash or cash equivalents [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- The summary must be printed in boldface type [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].

### Per-component disclosure (§ 5565(a))

- Subdivision (a) requires disclosure, for each major component, of (i) the current estimated replacement cost, (ii) the estimated remaining life, and (iii) the estimated useful life [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].

### Fiscal-year-end cash-reserve disclosure (§ 5565(b))

- Subdivision (b)(1) requires disclosure of the current estimate of the amount of cash reserves necessary to repair, replace, restore, or maintain the major components, as of the end of the fiscal year for which the study is prepared [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- Subdivision (b)(2) requires disclosure of the current amount of accumulated cash reserves actually set aside to repair, replace, restore, or maintain major components, as of the end of the fiscal year for which the study is prepared [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- Subdivision (b)(3) requires disclosure, if applicable, of the amount of funds received from any compensatory damage award or settlement for injuries to real or personal property arising out of construction or design defects, plus the expenditure or disposition of those funds, including amounts expended for the direct and indirect costs of repair of those defects [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- Subdivision (b)(3) amounts must be reported as separate line items under cash reserves, alongside the (b)(2) accumulated cash reserves figure [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- Subdivision (b)(3) provides an alternative-compliance path: an association obligated to issue a review of its financial statement pursuant to Civil Code § 5305 may include in that review a statement containing all of the information required by (b)(3), in lieu of disclosing in the reserve summary itself [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].

### Percent-funded disclosure (§ 5565(c))

- Subdivision (c) requires disclosure of the percentage that the (b)(2) amount (accumulated cash reserves) equals the (b)(1) amount (current estimate of cash reserves necessary) [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].

### Per-unit deficiency disclosure (§ 5565(d))

- Subdivision (d) requires disclosure of the current deficiency in reserve funding expressed on a per-unit basis [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- The default per-unit deficiency calculation is: subtract the (b)(2) amount from the (b)(1) amount, then divide the result by the number of separate interests within the association [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- If assessments vary by the size or type of ownership interest, the association must calculate the current deficiency in a manner that reflects that variation, rather than using the simple separate-interest divisor [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
