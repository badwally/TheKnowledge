# AQR Capital Management

## Summary

Quantitative investment firm based in Greenwich, CT; institutional home of Cliff Asness, whose paper "The Less-Efficient Market Hypothesis" argues that markets have become less informationally efficient over the past 30+ years, particularly in the relative pricing of common stocks over medium horizons [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].

## Key facts

- Headquartered in Greenwich, CT [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Cliff Asness is its managing principal [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Began trading basic value and momentum strategies in 1994 [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- The firm's strategies have evolved into far more involved descendants today, with many more factors, much better measurement of factors, and innovations such as alternative-data strategies combined with machine learning [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- AQR's "more holistic" value approach uses multiple measures of value while taking out as much industry bet as possible, in contrast to simple Fama-French price-to-book sorts [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Per Asness, AQR-style strategies typically hold stocks for about 9-12 months, making nanosecond-level information speed largely irrelevant to their performance — and lagging trades a full week from the decision to trade produces only modest deterioration in medium-term backtests [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- AQR's richer quant models include diversifying investment themes beyond value, and the firm continues to add sources of alpha that aren't "value" by either narrow or broad definition (e.g., machine learning for fundamental momentum, alternative data sources) [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
