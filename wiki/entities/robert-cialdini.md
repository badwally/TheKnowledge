# Robert Cialdini

## Summary

Psychology professor at Arizona State University and author of *Influence*, a popular-audience book on social-psychology experiments demonstrating how humans manipulate each other through intrinsic thinking flaws; identified by Charlie Munger as the catalyst for his own systematic study of psychology, for which Munger gave Cialdini a share of Berkshire Hathaway Class A stock [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].

## Key facts

- Held the rank of "super-tenured 'Regents' Professor'" at Arizona State University, achieved at a very young age [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- *Influence* devised, described, and explained "a vast group of clever experiments in which man manipulated man to his detriment, with all of this made possible by man's intrinsic thinking flaws" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Sold hundreds of thousands of copies of *Influence* — a remarkable feat for a social psychology book that did not promise to improve the reader's sex life or make him money [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Described by Munger as "a profoundly ethical man" whose book was nevertheless bought in huge numbers by salesmen wanting to become more effective in misleading customers — a perverse outcome Munger uses as an example of incentive-caused bias [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Designed an experiment demonstrating the power of reciprocal concession: after "compliance practitioners" first made a ridiculous request (supervise juvenile delinquents for two years) and were rejected, a follow-up request (one afternoon at the zoo) tripled the acceptance rate from 1-in-6 to 1-in-2 [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Used reciprocal-concession theory to explain the Watergate burglary: after an aggressive subordinate's extreme proposal involving whores and a yacht was rejected, the attorney general conceded to the smaller request of authorizing a burglary — a subconscious reciprocation that Cialdini and Munger believe contributed to a president's resignation [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Described an Authority-Misinfluence case in which a physician's written order "Two drops, twice a day, r. ear" was interpreted by a nurse as directing eardrops into the patient's anus [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
