# Michael J. Mauboussin

## Summary

Researcher at Counterpoint Global (Morgan Stanley); first-listed author of the June 4, 2024 Counterpoint Global Insights / Consilient Observer report "Stock Market Concentration: How Much Is Too Much?", which surveys U.S. stock-market concentration since 1950 and argues that the recent surge in top-10 weighting is justified by the underlying economic-profit distribution of the leading companies [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].

## Key facts

- Listed author of "Stock Market Concentration: How Much Is Too Much?" (Counterpoint Global Insights / Consilient Observer, June 4, 2024); contact email michael.mauboussin@morganstanley.com [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Co-authored the report with Dan Callahan, CFA [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Frames the report around four topics: concentration over the past three-quarters of a century, whether there is a "correct" level of concentration (cross-country and counterfactual), whether fundamental corporate performance supports the current rise, and what the development means for active equity managers [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Argues that the U.S. top-10 weighting nearly doubled from 14% to 27% over the decade ending in 2023, approaching the prior 30% peak set in 1963 [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Argues that the rate of increase in concentration over the last decade is the most rapid since 1950 — even though the absolute level has plenty of historical precedent [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Cites a counterfactual analysis discounting the top 1, 3, and 10 market caps back five and ten years at the market return, concluding that 2013 and 2018 concentration would have been materially higher had markets "efficiently" priced what later happened — i.e., concentration may have been too low, not today's too high [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Argues that economic profit — (ROIC – WACC) × Invested Capital — is the right way to test whether concentration is justified by fundamentals; in 2023 the top 10 stocks were 27% of market cap and earned 69% of the universe's economic profit [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Treats opportunity cost as the conceptual anchor for active management: the SEC requires U.S. mutual funds to report results against an "appropriate broad-based securities market index," and the S&P 500 is by far the most popular benchmark for U.S. equity funds [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Notes that a high percentage of large-cap equity funds have underperformed the S&P 500 over the past decade but argues "the facts about active management are not as dire as some headlines proclaim, and manager skill exists" [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
