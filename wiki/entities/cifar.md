# Canadian Institute for Advanced Research (CIFAR)

## Summary

The Canadian Institute for Advanced Research (CIFAR) was the lead partner asked to deliver the 2017 Pan-Canadian Artificial Intelligence Strategy (PCAIS) and continues to lead Phase 2, launched in June 2022 with C$443M in Budget 2021 funding [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]] [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]].

## Key facts

- CIFAR was the federal government's lead partner in delivering PCAIS Phase 1 from 2017 onward, working with Amii, Mila and the Vector Institute [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]] [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]].
- Elissa Strome served as Executive Director of the Pan-Canadian Artificial Intelligence Strategy at CIFAR at the time of the Phase 2 launch, describing CIFAR's role as "forging Canada's reputation as a global leader in advancing research excellence and responsible AI development" [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]].
- Under PCAIS Phase 2, CIFAR receives C$160M to attract, retain and develop academic research talent and maintain centres of research, innovation and training at Canada's national AI institutes [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]].
- CIFAR additionally receives C$48M under Phase 2 to renew and enhance its advanced research, training and knowledge mobilization programs [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]].
- Since 2017, the Canada CIFAR AI Chairs program had recruited more than 100 top researchers, roughly half of them international, by the June 2022 Phase 2 announcement [[sources/web-2022-06-22-4be]].
