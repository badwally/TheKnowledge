# Torsten Sløk

## Summary

Apollo Chief Economist (PhD); author of the December 2022 Apollo Global Management white paper "2023 Economic and Capital Markets Outlook," which argues US inflation peaked in June 2022, that a Fed-engineered soft landing in 2023 is now "real," that the Fed will reach peak rates around 5.5% in mid-2023, and that — after a disastrous 2022 for 60/40 portfolios — investors will rotate into private markets where Apollo sees a "historic entry point in private credit" [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].

## Key facts

- Listed as Apollo Chief Economist on the byline of "2023 Economic and Capital Markets Outlook" (December 2022) and credited with the PhD designation [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Argues US inflation "appears to have peaked in June 2022," with the CPI showing good reason to believe we are now in the beginning of a downward trend [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Argues the inflation downtrend doesn't mean we will get back to the Fed's 2% annual target anytime soon — "history shows it could take up to two years for us to get there," and elsewhere in the paper revises this to "two to three years — and more Fed hikes" [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Argues the decline in inflation is taking place without a sharp increase in the unemployment rate, which points to a higher probability that the Fed might engineer a much-desired soft landing — a scenario "that many thought very unlikely just a few months ago" [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- States that as of the paper's writing, Apollo sees the Fed reaching "peak rates" in mid-2023 around 5.5% [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Argues that a less aggressive Fed — or a potential Fed "pivot" in 2023 — should be bullish for asset prices (public and private) ranging from rates to credit to equities [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Argues that capital markets will likely remain vulnerable in 2023 and volatility will likely persist because capital remains scarce and expensive, and high-yield primary credit markets will likely stay virtually shut down for the time being [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Argues "selectivity in asset selection, valuations, and entry points will be paramount" in 2023 [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Argues many investors — "weary and battered after a disastrous performance of 60/40 portfolios of public equities and bonds in 2022" — are likely to turn to private markets as they adjust their holdings in 2023 [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Argues Apollo sees "a historic entry point in private credit and attractive opportunities in private equity for investors able to be providers of capital in a time of stressed and distressed markets" [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Frames the root causes of inflation as both demand-driven (Covid fiscal response: stimulus checks, higher unemployment benefits, childcare tax credits, PPP loans) and supply-driven (Covid supply-chain problems), and argues the near-identical inflation paths in the US and Europe — despite a US fiscal response twice the EU's — strongly suggests inflation has not been entirely driven by demand [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Reads goods-sector inflation as having fallen sharply in recent months while service-sector inflation continues to rise; notes services accounts for roughly 80% of GDP, which makes the services stickiness especially consequential [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Frames the central themes of 2022 — high inflation, potential economic recession, and dislocated asset prices — as expected to continue into 2023, but with a different story as the fast-and-furious tightening of monetary conditions begins to take effect across the macroeconomic spectrum [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
