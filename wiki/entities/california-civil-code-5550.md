# California Civil Code §5550–5520 (Reserve Studies)

## Summary

California Civil Code Sections 5550 through 5520 govern the reserve-study and reserve-account responsibilities of common interest development (CID) boards of directors, imposing both a quarterly review obligation and a triennial visual-inspection-based study cycle, along with a minimum scope for the study itself [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].

## Key facts

- Applies to common interest development boards of directors in California [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- On a quarterly basis, boards must review reserve accounts and compare reserves to the previous year [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- At least once every three years, boards must conduct a competent and diligent visual inspection of the property the association is obligated to repair, replace, restore, or maintain as part of a study of the reserve account requirements [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Boards must annually review the reserve study to consider and implement necessary adjustments to the analysis of reserve account requirements [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- The reserve study must, at minimum, include: identification of major components that the association is obligated to repair, replace, restore, or maintain that have a remaining useful life of less than 30 years as of the date of the study; identification of probable remaining useful life of those components; an estimate of repair/replacement/restoration/maintenance cost; an estimate of the total annual contribution necessary to defray that cost during and at the end of useful life (net of total reserve funds on the study date); and a reserve funding plan describing how the association intends to fund the identified contribution [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- California does not statutorily require reserves to be funded — only studied — making it a study-mandate-without-funding-mandate jurisdiction [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
