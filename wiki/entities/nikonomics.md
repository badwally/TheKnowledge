# Nikonomics

## Summary

Day trader and author of the free 22-page beginner's manual "Nikonomics — The Options Trading Guide to Success," framed as the chronological roadmap the author would take "if I could start all over again knowing absolutely nothing," focused on the mechanics of trading options premiums (not exercising) on retail brokerages [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].

## Key facts

- Authored "Nikonomics — The Options Trading Guide to Success," billed as a complete-beginner-to-advanced options day trading guide "in chronological order" [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- States the book is being made available free of charge but signals intent to start charging for paid educational content once the author hits a self-set milestone of making $100,000 per month from trading [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Distances himself from "the stereotypical 'guru' who sells a course when they barely know what they're talking about," and frames the book as a "gesture of goodwill" until that milestone [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Assumes a reader with elementary stock-market literacy and explicitly tells readers to use YouTube/Google for any concept the book does not explain [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Explicitly recommends Webull as the brokerage for the curriculum, citing no commissions, paper trading with options, advanced charting in the app, and a cash-account loophole that allows unlimited day trades under $25,000 [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Provides a Webull partnership link (https://a.webull.com/i/nikonomics) in the book promising up to $30,000 in free stocks after any-amount deposit [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Recommends Interactive Brokers as a fallback for non-US readers, while noting Webull is US-only [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Frames options trading specifically as trading the option premium with no intent to exercise the contract, and calls this the reason "options can be so confusing" from first glance because most online material assumes exercise [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Recommends beginners start in Webull paper trading, master the basics there, then transition to real money to feel the emotional component of risk that paper trading cannot replicate [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Recommends new traders "start trading with AT LEAST 5 day expirations" (1 week away from current day) and trade only "the 5 closest OTM strike prices" as a default beginner rule [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Suggests $SPY (the S&P 500 ETF, described as "the 500 largest companies all in one") as the default underlying for beginners [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Emphasizes that consistent application — not reading — is what produces a good trader: "I can give you the sauce, but you gotta be the one to make the pasta" [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
