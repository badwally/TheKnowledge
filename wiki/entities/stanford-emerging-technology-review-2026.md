# Stanford Emerging Technology Review 2026

## Summary

Third annual report from the Stanford Emerging Technology Review (SETR) initiative, published by Stanford University in 2026 to survey ten key emerging technologies and their policy implications for U.S. decision makers in both the public and private sectors [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].

## Key facts

- Subtitle is "A Report on Ten Key Technologies and Their Policy Implications" [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- Co-chaired by Condoleezza Rice, Jennifer Widom, and Amy Zegart, with Herbert S. Lin as Director and Editor in Chief and Martin Giles as Managing Editor [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- Produced through the first-ever collaboration among Stanford University's School of Engineering, the Hoover Institution, and Stanford's Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence (HAI) [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- Surveys ten technology fields: Artificial Intelligence; Biotechnology and Synthetic Biology; Cryptography and Computer Security; Energy Technologies; Materials Science; Neuroscience; Quantum Technologies; Robotics; Semiconductors; and Space [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- Frames the moment as a "hinge of history," arguing that the choices made today across labs and legislatures are likely to have consequences for generations [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- Frames U.S. technology policy as no longer the unique province of government, with federal and state officials struggling to keep up with technological advances and inventors and investors struggling to reconcile commercial opportunities with national interests [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- States that the SETR continuous education campaign now involves more than 100 Stanford scholars across 40 departments and research institutes, and has briefed senior leaders in Congress, the White House, the Department of Commerce, the Department of Defense, and the intelligence community [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- Argues that academia's role in U.S. innovation is essential and at risk, citing trends in federal R&D funding, China's rising basic-research investment, and the migration of AI talent from universities to industry [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
