# Brian Hurst

## Summary

Investment researcher at ClearAlpha; author of the 2023 thought-leadership paper "The Math of the Multi-Manager: Fad or Better Mousetrap?", which argues that multi-manager hedge funds outperform traditional 2/20 structures and balanced hedge fund portfolios because of two structural advantages — assembly of diverse, lowly correlated strategies and cash-efficient implementation [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].

## Key facts

- Authored "The Math of the Multi-Manager: Fad or Better Mousetrap?" (2023), published as thought leadership from ClearAlpha [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
- Argues that the inherent edge in multi-manager funds is structural and driven by portfolio construction math rather than the often-cited reasons of attracting top portfolio management talent or quickly cutting underperforming managers [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
- Identifies two vital components of multi-manager success: assembly of a significant number of diverse investment strategies with low correlations to one another, and implementation of these strategies in a cash-efficient manner [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
- References an earlier ClearAlpha whitepaper "The Evolution of Alpha" (available upon request) for additional reasons behind the multi-manager fund structure's success [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
- References the ClearAlpha whitepaper "Building a Diversified Alpha Portfolio - Why do Correlations all go to 1.0?" for in-depth discussion of what drives strategy correlations [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
- Concludes that the multi-manager fund structure has delivered more alpha than other structures and is "likely here to stay" [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
