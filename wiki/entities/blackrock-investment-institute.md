# BlackRock Investment Institute

## Summary

BlackRock's in-house research arm; publisher of the "2023 Global Outlook: A new investment playbook," a 16-page institutional report arguing that the Great Moderation — the four-decade period of largely stable activity and inflation — is over, replaced by a new regime of greater macro and market volatility driven by production constraints, and that investors need a new playbook involving more frequent portfolio changes and more granular sector/region/sub-asset-class views rather than broad exposures [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].

## Key facts

- Published the "2023 Global Outlook" with six named contributors: Philipp Hildebrand (Vice Chairman, BlackRock), Jean Boivin (Head, BII), Wei Li (Global Chief Investment Strategist, BII), Alex Brazier (Deputy Head, BII), Vivek Paul (Head of Portfolio Research, BII), and Scott Thiel (Chief Fixed Income Strategist, BII) [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- The outlook's core thesis is that a new regime of greater macro and market volatility is playing out, driven by production constraints rather than excess demand, and that central banks are on course to overtighten policy as they seek to tame inflation [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- Identifies three long-term structural trends cementing the new regime: aging populations causing continued worker shortages, persistent geopolitical tensions rewiring globalization and supply chains, and the transition to net-zero carbon emissions causing energy supply-demand mismatches [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- Organizes the 2023 tactical view around three themes: "Pricing the damage" (equity valuations don't yet reflect the recession ahead), "Rethinking bonds" (short-term government bonds and investment-grade credit attractive, long-term government bonds structurally underweight), and "Living with inflation" (inflation-linked bonds overweight on both tactical and strategic horizons) [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- Presents a stylized four-quadrant matrix mapping risk sentiment (on/off) against damage-priced-in (yes/no) to guide tactical allocation shifts, with the November 2022 positioning at "risk off, damage not priced" [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- States that the underweight on nominal long-term government bonds is the "strongest conviction in any scenario" within the matrix [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- Argues that what worked in the past won't work now — the old playbook of "buying the dip" doesn't apply because loose policy is not on the way to help support risk assets [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- Bottom line: the new regime requires more frequent portfolio changes, balancing views on risk appetite with estimates of how markets are pricing economic damage, and taking more granular sector/region/sub-asset-class views rather than broad exposures [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
