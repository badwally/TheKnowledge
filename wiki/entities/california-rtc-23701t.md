# California Revenue and Taxation Code Section 23701t

## Summary

California Revenue and Taxation Code Section 23701t is the California statutory provision under which homeowners' associations — including condominium management associations, residential real estate management associations, cooperative housing corporations, time-share associations, and mutual water companies — may obtain California state tax-exempt status [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]]. The provision is patterned after Internal Revenue Code Section 528 and Federal Treasury Regulations Section 1.528 but, unlike the federal annual-election regime, requires affirmative application via FTB Form 3500 and issuance of an Exempt Determination Letter by the California Franchise Tax Board [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]]. The substantive operational requirements — 85% residential test, 60% source-of-income test, 90% expenditure test, no private inurement, trust requirement on unused dues/assessments, and a two-unit-different-owners membership minimum — are codified at §23701t and operationalized through FTB Form 1028 [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].

## Key facts

- Codified at California Revenue and Taxation Code Section 23701t [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Patterned after Internal Revenue Code Section 528 and Federal Treasury Regulations Section 1.528 [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- California requires affirmative application and a determination letter, not annual election; the federal regime by contrast allows annual exemption election by filing IRS Form 1120H [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Available to: condominium management associations, residential real estate management associations, cooperative housing corporations, time-share associations, and mutual water companies; the property must be at least 85 percent residential [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- For each eligible property type, membership in the association must be a condition of owning a unit, home, or lot in the development (except for mutual water companies, which must provide water to residential members) [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Establishes the source-of-income test: 60% or more of gross income must come from membership dues, fees, and assessments [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- For cooperative housing corporations, payments from stockholder members to pay acquisition indebtedness or other fees are treated as fees and assessments for source-of-income test purposes [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Establishes the expenditure test: 90% or more of annual expenses must be for acquisition, construction, management, maintenance, and care of association property (or, for time-share associations, activities provided to or on behalf of members) [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Prohibits any part of net earnings from benefiting any private individual other than in the normal course of association activities [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Imposes a trust-fund requirement: amounts received as membership dues, fees, and assessments are transferred and held in trust to provide for the management, maintenance, and care of association property if they are not used for association purposes during the year [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Establishes the membership-definition test: a minimum of two units or lots must be owned by different persons [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Mutual water companies must additionally satisfy: water used for residential purposes only; members not charged based on water usage (assessment must be equal-basis or property-size/value); metered-assessment income combined with other nonexempt-function income must not exceed 40% of total receipts [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- California law does not have a counterpart to IRC §501(c)(12); a mutual ditch/irrigation company exempt federally under §501(c)(12) is not automatically exempt in California — it must qualify under §23701t [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- An HOA exempt under §23701t must file Form 100 only if taxable income exceeds $100 [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
