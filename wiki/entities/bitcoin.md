# Bitcoin

## Summary

Cryptocurrency and proof-of-work-based payment protocol; the cryptocurrency with by far the largest market capitalization among the roughly 10,000 that claim to hew to the original Nakamoto protocol, and the central case study of Taleb's "Bitcoin, Currencies, and Fragility," which argues that BTC has failed both as a currency and as a store of value [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].

## Key facts

- Is, "by far," the cryptocurrency with the largest market capitalization among the roughly 10,000 cryptocurrencies that claim to follow the original Nakamoto protocol [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Combines three pre-existing technologies — the hash function, the Merkle tree (to chain blocks of transactions tagged by the hash function), and the concept of proof of work — all of which originated in the academic literature [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Bitcoin transactions are irreversible by design, no matter the reason, by virtue of the blockchain structure [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Bitcoins are zero-sum by virtue of the numerus clausus: the total supply is limited to a fixed number of coins, with miners eventually compensated by transaction fees alone once coins are depleted [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- The distribution of bitcoin holdings follows a power law with tail index ≈ 5, "no different from the distribution of wealth in the U.S." [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Validating most transactions takes about 10 minutes, with the median transaction fee at about $20 as of June 2021, per Eswar Prasad in The New York Times as cited by Taleb [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Twelve years into its life at the time of the paper, the only price quoted in BTC observed by Taleb is the price tag of Salvadoran permanent residence (3 bitcoins); no other prices are fixed in bitcoin floating in fiat currencies in the economy [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Three-month annualized return volatility of BTC has not dropped over time, and at higher levels of capitalization "return volatility compounds"; in 2021 a swing of half a trillion dollars in BTC capitalization took place [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Bitcoin transactions are "considerably more expensive than wire services or other modes of transfers, or ones in other cryptocurrencies" and are "orders of magnitude slower than standard commercial systems used by credit card companies" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
