# CRA External Technical Interpretation 2014-0528171E5

## Summary

CRA External Technical Interpretation 2014-0528171E5, issued August 18, 2014, is a severed-letter response by Roger Filion (Manager, Non-Profit Organizations and Aboriginal Issues, Income Tax Rulings Directorate) addressing whether a Canadian condominium corporation's entry into a leasing contract for the installation of solar panels on common-element roofs would jeopardize its ability to claim the paragraph 149(1)(l) tax exemption under the *Income Tax Act* [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]]. The T.I. articulates CRA's framework for analyzing third-party-revenue arrangements in condominium corporations: incidental income from common-area rentals may be treated as income of the Corporation without affecting tax-exempt status, but non-incidental income is generally treated as income of the unit owners under an agency theory rooted in provincial law (subsection 11(2) of the Ontario *Condominium Act, 1998* cited as the operative example) [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]]. The document bears an explicit disclaimer that it may not represent the current position of the CRA [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].

## Key facts

- Document identifier: 2014-052817 [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Date of issue: August 18, 2014 [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Author: Roger Filion, Manager, Non-Profit Organizations and Aboriginal Issues, Business and Employment Division, Income Tax Rulings Directorate, Legislative Policy and Regulatory Affairs Branch [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Response to an April 2, 2014 inquiry regarding the tax consequences of a condominium corporation entering into a leasing agreement with a third party for the right to install solar panels on roofs within the complex, with output introduced to the public electrical grid [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Bears the standard CRA disclaimer that the document, although believed to be correct at the time of issue, may not represent the current position of the CRA [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Restates the four-prong test for paragraph 149(1)(l) exemption: (i) club, society or association; (ii) not a charity; (iii) organized and operated exclusively for social welfare, civic improvement, pleasure, recreation, or any other purpose except profit; and (iv) income not available for the personal benefit of a member or shareholder [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- States that a condominium corporation is not exempt from tax because it is a condominium corporation; rather, it is exempt only while it meets the 149(1)(l) requirements [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Confirms the courts' recognition that a 149(1)(l)-claiming organization can earn a profit so long as the profit is incidental and arises from activities directly connected to its not-for-profit objectives [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- States that maintaining reasonable operating reserves or bank accounts required for ordinary operations is generally an activity undertaken to meet the not-for-profit objectives of an organization, and that incidental profit arising from those reserves or accounts will not affect tax-exempt status [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Defines "incidental" income (in the leasing context) as both minor AND directly related to activities undertaken to meet the Corporation's not-for-profit objectives of managing and maintaining the condominium property and required reserves [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- States that whether the leasing income is incidental or related to the Corporation's not-for-profit objectives is a question of fact [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- States that incidental income from the rental of common areas may be treated as income of the Corporation and generally will not affect the tax-exempt status of the Corporation [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- States that income that is not incidental will usually be considered to be income of the unit owners, if this is appropriate under the relevant provincial law [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Cites subsection 11(2) of the *Condominium Act* of Ontario (S.O. 1998, c.19) as the operative provincial-law authority for the agency theory: "the owners are tenants in common of the common elements and an undivided interest in the common elements is appurtenant to each owner's unit" [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- States that under the agency theory, the Corporation may be viewed as acting as an agent for the unit owners in entering into the lease agreement; if so, the agreement generally would not jeopardize the Corporation's tax-exempt status, although the related profit must be allocated appropriately to the unit owners and included in their income for tax purposes [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- States that where the relevant provincial law indicates the income is the income of the Corporation, the Corporation may not be tax-exempt pursuant to paragraph 149(1)(l) [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Flags CRA's specific concern that lease-agreement income flowing to a freeze or reduction in members' condominium fees would likely be available for the personal benefit of members of the Corporation [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Flags CRA's secondary concern as to whether the lease agreement would be connected to the Corporation's not-for-profit objectives [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- In the underlying fact pattern, the third party would assume all costs and charges relating to installation, maintenance, ongoing servicing and repairs of the solar panels and associated equipment, plus maintenance, repair, and upkeep of the leased common-element roofs during the term of the contract [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- The Corporation would receive annual income and/or a percentage of revenue generated per Kilowatt hour produced during the term of the contract [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- The lease income would be contributed to the Corporation's reserve fund, potentially offsetting or freezing annual reserve fund increases, reducing annual reserve fund contributions, and/or reducing property owners' monthly maintenance fees [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Explicitly disclaims confirmation of the income tax treatment of a particular situation involving a specific taxpayer; states such confirmation is only available through an advance income tax ruling request submitted under Information Circular IC 70-6R5 [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Published as a severed letter on taxinterpretations.com under the URL taxinterpretations.com/cra/severed-letters/2014-0528171e5 [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
