# Nassim Nicholas Taleb

## Summary

Quantitative-finance practitioner and author affiliated with Universa Investments and the Tandon School of Engineering at New York University; author of "Bitcoin, Currencies, and Fragility," forthcoming in Quantitative Finance, which applies quantitative-finance methods and economic arguments to cryptocurrencies and argues that Bitcoin fails as currency, store of value, inflation hedge, and safe haven [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].

## Key facts

- Lists affiliations with Universa Investments and the Tandon School of Engineering at New York University on the paper, with email NNT1@nyu.edu [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Authored "Bitcoin, Currencies, and Fragility," forthcoming in Quantitative Finance, in which he argues that bitcoin "failed to satisfy the notion of 'currency without government'" and "proved to not even be a currency at all" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Argues in the same paper that bitcoin "can be neither a short nor long term store of value (its expected value is no higher than 0), cannot operate as a reliable inflation hedge, and ... does not constitute, not even remotely, a safe haven for one's investments" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- States Principle 1 ("Cumulative ruin"): if any non-dividend yielding asset has the tiniest probability of hitting an absorbing barrier causing its value to become 0, then its present value must be 0 [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Acknowledges discussions on the paper with Gur Huberman, Mark Spitznagel, Brandon Yarkin, Arthur Breitman, Trishank Karthik Kuppusamy, Jim Gatheral, Joe Norman, Zhuo Xi, David Boxenhorn, Antonis Polemitis, and Joe Shipman, among others [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
