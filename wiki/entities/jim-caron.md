# Jim Caron

## Summary

Senior Advisor on the Morgan Stanley Fixed Income Team (taking on a new role as Co-Chief Investment Officer, Co-Deputy Head and Senior Portfolio Manager of Global Balanced Risk Control in January 2023); author of the Income chapter "Expect a Year of 'Long and Variable Lags'" in the December 12, 2022 Morgan Stanley "2023 Investment Outlook," in which he argues monetary policy will continue to drive asset prices in 2023, that below trend-line growth and significant job losses could result from the inflation fight, and that short-term pain will pave the way for long-term gains for the economy [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].

## Key facts

- Listed title in the outlook: Senior Advisor, Fixed Income Team; in January 2023 takes on a new role as Co-Chief Investment Officer, Co-Deputy Head and Senior Portfolio Manager of Global Balanced Risk Control (GBaR) [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Argues monetary policy will continue to be the key driver of asset prices in 2023 [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Frames Fed action through "long and variable lags" between a monetary policy action and its impact on the economy [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- States the Fed's mission is to help lower inflation to its 2% to 2.5% target and keep it there, durably [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Identifies the inflation culprit as a tight labor market keeping wage inflation elevated, with goods inflation having fallen sharply late in 2022 but service-sector inflation remaining stubbornly high and harder to tame [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Reads the Fed's dual mandate as full employment and stable prices, with a stated plan to increase policy rates to around 5% and hold them there for an extended period, even if the economy slips into a mild recession [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Expects any 2023 recession to be mild because the jobs market will likely remain strong: "it's difficult to expect a deep recession with such a robust labor market" [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Frames the Fed's policy choice as a Cost-Benefit Analysis: cost is measured by job losses, benefit is measured in quality future growth with lower inflation [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Argues the Fed believes 2023 may have a window to tame inflation by keeping policy rates higher for longer; economic growth and asset valuations may be collateral damage but worth it, because "aggressive action now may mean eliminating the need to act more aggressively later, creating greater damage" [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Argues that with peak inflation likely behind us, high-quality bonds may benefit while riskier assets and lower-quality credit may suffer; if the Fed's plan to durably stem inflation works, asset valuations may benefit over the longer term [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Counsels investors to "be nimble and prepared to adjust investment positions as events unfold in 2023" [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
