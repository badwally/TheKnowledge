# Nova Scotia Condominium Act

## Summary

The Nova Scotia Condominium Act (R.S.N.S. 1989, c. 85) is the province's primary condominium statute; amendments most recently revised by NS Bill No. 106 (2022) and proclaimed on May 23, 2023 modernize governance and management of condominium corporations, enhance consumer protection, and — most critically for capital-renewal planning — require condominium corporations to undertake more frequent reserve-fund studies to help corporations plan for future capital repairs [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]] [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]]. The 2023 proclamation was brought forward under the responsibility of Colton LeBlanc, Minister of Service Nova Scotia and Internal Services [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]]. The Act is the load-bearing regulatory anchor for the Year-1 Nova Scotia arm of the condo-capital-infra Canada-first GTM and sits alongside the New Brunswick Condominium Property Act (c16.05) and the Newfoundland & Labrador Condominium Act, 2009 + NLR 80/11 as the Atlantic-Canada statutory cluster in the cross-jurisdictional reserve-study statutory comparator set; CMHC's PEI provincial fact sheet additionally documents that PEI — the fourth Atlantic-Canada province — has no mandatory reserve-fund regime, making NS the cadence-tightening anchor of the Atlantic-Canada cluster against PEI's discretionary baseline [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]] [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]]. The operative reserve-fund-study cadence is every five years with site inspections, confirmed by the June 2025 Summer Gardens Reserve Fund Study (Sense Engineering for Halifax Condominium Corporation No. 130) citing the Act and the Condominium Regulations under sections 33 and 46 — resolving the previously-open question between the synthesis source's 10-year framing and the May 2023 proclamation's "more frequent" wording [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]] [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]] [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].

## Key facts

### Procedural timeline (2022-2023 amendments)

- The 2023 amendments to the Nova Scotia Condominium Act were introduced on March 25 and proclaimed on May 23, 2023 [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].
- Sponsoring minister at proclamation: Colton LeBlanc, Minister of Service Nova Scotia and Internal Services [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].
- The June 11, 2025 Summer Gardens Reserve Fund Study (Sense Engineering, project no. 24hA007A) cites the operative Act as "Condominium Act R.S., c. 85 last revised in 2022 by NS Bill No. 106 and Condominium Regulations under Section 33 and 46 of the Condominium Act" — anchoring Bill 106 (2022) as the substantive revision instrument, with the May 23, 2023 proclamation acting as the in-force date for the amendment package [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
- Stated purpose, framed by Minister LeBlanc at proclamation: "By making changes to the act, the rules condominium owners and corporations need to follow will be current and relevant to their day-to-day work" — and the amendments "will also improve consumer protection by creating more transparency for current and prospective condo owners" [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].
- The proclamation was reported in CCI-Nova Scotia Chapter News on June 5, 2023 under the headline "Changes to the Condominium Act have been Proclaimed" [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].

### Governance and management changes

- The amendments ensure a smooth transition between the developer's board and the first board elected by the condominium corporation [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].
- The amendments clarify the percentage of the affirmative vote required to make decisions and set timelines for the first general meeting [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].
- The developer is prohibited from purchasing additional units on behalf of the corporation until the first elected board is in place [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].
- Corporations are permitted to modernize voting methods, such as accepting ballots by email [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].

### Reserve-fund-study cadence (5-year, primary-source confirmed)

- The May 23, 2023 amendments require more frequent reserve-fund studies to help corporations plan for future capital repairs [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].
- The operative cadence is every five years, with site inspections, to confirm that funding levels are adequate to provide for future anticipated repairs/replacements — confirmed by the June 11, 2025 Summer Gardens Reserve Fund Study (Sense Engineering for Halifax Condominium Corporation No. 130) citing the Act and the Condominium Regulations under sections 33 and 46 directly [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
- The 5-year cadence resolves the previously-open question between the synthesis source's claim of 10 years and the May 2023 proclamation's "more frequent" framing; the new primary-source citation establishes 5 years as the current statutory cadence under the Act post-Bill 106 [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]] [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]] [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].
- The 5-year cadence aligns NS directly with BC SPA's mandatory 5-year depreciation-report cycle for 5+-strata-lot corporations, putting NS at the same cadence as British Columbia rather than at the longer 10-year NB CPA c16.05 primary cadence — structurally favorable engagement-density profile for an AI-native reserve-study firm pursuing Year-1 NS revenue under ADR-0004 [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]] [[sources/web-2013-11-28-cda]] [[sources/web-2017-07-26-1f3]].
- The NS uniform 5-year cadence with site inspection is structurally distinct from the Ontario Condominium Act, 1998 + O. Reg. 48/01 tiered Class 1/2/3 reserve-fund-study schema codified in the CAO Best Practices Guide (last updated December 2023): under the ON regime, a Class 1 comprehensive on-site study is required within one year of declaration registration, followed by alternating Class 2 (with site inspection) and Class 3 (records-only) updates every three years thereafter — yielding a tiered-cadence framework that maps to a tiered-pricing product offering absent from NS's uniform-update regime [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]] [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].

### Statutory minimum physical analysis horizon (20 years)

- The Act and associated Regulations require the reserve fund study to contain a physical analysis including an inventory and assessment of each common element item that is expected to require major repair or replacement within at least 20 years of the date of the study [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
- The financial analysis must recommend a funding plan to adequately offset the anticipated reserve fund expenditures [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
- Operational practice extends beyond the 20-year statutory minimum — the Summer Gardens 2025 study projects reserve fund expenditures over a 25-year period for the financial analysis, with the physical-analysis statutory minimum acting as the floor rather than the ceiling [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
- The 20-year physical-analysis minimum is in tension with the synthesis source's claim of a 30-year minimum planning horizon for NS, which appears to conflate practice (financial analysis often runs to 25-30 years) with the statutory minimum on physical analysis (20 years) [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]] [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- The NS 20-year statutory minimum physical-analysis horizon is materially shorter than the Ontario regime's at-least-30-year minimum financial-analysis horizon codified in O. Reg. 48/01 §29(3) and surfaced in the CAO Best Practices Guide — the ON regulator additionally identifies a 45-year-or-longer timeline as a best practice, framing ON's prescribed forward forecast window as 50% longer than the NS statutory minimum and surfacing horizon length as another cross-provincial dimension on which NS and ON diverge [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]] [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].

### Operational study conventions (per Sense Engineering 2025 RFS)

- Studies typically itemize projects with anticipated costs greater than ~$5,000; the operating budget is assumed to fund minor repairs, routine maintenance activities, and replacements costing less than ~$5,000 [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
- Cost estimates are inflated and include contingencies (typically 5 to 15%) and allowances for design/project management (5 to 15%), where appropriate [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
- HST (15%) is included in cost estimates — Nova Scotia is part of the harmonized-sales-tax regime, putting capital-renewal cost estimates 15% above pre-tax estimates as a baseline convention [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
- Site visit methodology in 2024-2025 NS practice involves visual review of representative samples of exterior at grade, parking garage, roofs, common areas, service rooms, and a stratified sample of residential suites — the Summer Gardens study reviewed 12 suites across floors 11-21 [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
- Operational methodology is deterministic point-estimate: cost, useful life, remaining useful life, and inflation rate are treated as scalar inputs rather than distributions — consistent with the CAI methodology baseline and not requiring probabilistic methods [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
- NS's ~$5,000 operational itemization threshold is materially higher than Ontario's $500 component-inventory threshold codified in the CAO Best Practices Guide — a ten-fold difference in the cost cutoff for items tracked through the reserve fund vs. handled through the operating budget, surfacing NS as a coarser-grained reserve-study regime than ON at the component-inventory level [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]] [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].

### Funding plan structure (Immediately Adequately Funded option)

- The Act's required financial analysis recommends a funding plan to adequately offset the anticipated reserve fund expenditures, with the board of directors selecting among funding-plan scenarios [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
- One recognized funding-plan option is the "Immediately Adequately Funded" (IAF) plan — a scenario showing the contribution level required to cover anticipated repairs and replacements without a deficit, with subsequent annual contribution increases only needed to keep pace with inflation [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
- The June 2025 Summer Gardens Reserve Fund Study documents Halifax Condominium Corporation No. 130's Board of Directors electing to follow the Immediately Adequately Funded Plan, with first-year contribution rising from $320,000 (2025) to $578,619 (2026) — an 80.8% one-time increase — followed by 2.5% annual increases over 2027-2030; per-unit contribution rises from $2,633 to $5,192 over the same window — a concrete operating model for the NS reserve-fund-study funding-plan structure [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].

### Consumer-protection and transparency changes

- Unit owners are required to disclose property rental agreements to their condominium board [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].
- Bare-land condominiums must be clearly advertised as condominiums — a disclosure requirement intended to prevent prospective buyers from mistaking a bare-land condominium for a non-condominium subdivision lot [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].
- Definition surfaced in the proclamation announcement: a "bare land condominium" is a type of condominium complex in which the owners each own their individual lot [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].

### Regulation source

- Details of the Regulations promulgated under the Act are available at https://novascotia.ca/just/regulations/regs/conregs.htm [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].
- The reserve-fund-study provisions sit under sections 33 and 46 of the Condominium Act as the regulatory anchors per the June 2025 Sense Engineering RFS citation [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].

### Cross-jurisdictional comparator framing

- Among the Atlantic-Canada statutory cluster, the NS Act's May 2023 cadence-tightening contrasts with New Brunswick's Condominium Property Act c16.05 (effective 2010-01-01), which sets a 10-year primary reserve-fund-study cadence with 3-year updates for corporations with more than 10 units, and with the Newfoundland & Labrador reserve-fund-study regime under the NL Condominium Act, 2009 + NLR 80/11 (NLR 80/11 filed September 13, 2011), where the renewal cadence is anchored in NL Condominium Act subsection 49(5) [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].
- The 5-year cadence confirmed by the primary-source Summer Gardens RFS places NS at twice the engagement frequency of NB's 10-year primary cycle within the Atlantic-Canada cluster — a structurally favorable engagement-density profile for an AI-native reserve-study firm pursuing Year-1 NS revenue under ADR-0004 [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]] [[sources/web-2017-07-26-1f3]].
- Prince Edward Island, the fourth Atlantic-Canada province, sits at the discretionary-baseline end of the cluster — CMHC's PEI provincial fact sheet confirms that PEI provincial legislation/regulations do not require that all condominiums in Prince Edward Island have a reserve fund, the developer of a new condominium is not obligated to put aside reserves as soon as the condominium is registered, and there are no rules regarding special assessments — making NS the cadence-tightening anchor of the Atlantic-Canada cluster against PEI's null-statutory-regime baseline, alongside NB's stable 10-year-with-3-year-updates regime and NL's subsection 49(5)-anchored cadence under NLR 80/11 [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]] [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]].
- The Ontario regime documented in the CAO Best Practices Guide (December 2023) operates on a structurally different cadence architecture from the NS uniform 5-year cycle: the ON Class 1/2/3 schema delivers one Class 1 comprehensive study at registration followed by alternating Class 2 (site inspection) and Class 3 (records-only) updates at least every three years thereafter — yielding approximately one comprehensive plus three updates per decade per corporation versus NS's two uniform site-inspection studies per decade — making ON the higher-engagement-density regime by study count while NS retains higher per-study scope uniformity [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]].
- The synthesis frames NS specifically — and Atlantic Canada plus the North more generally — as operating under "more discretionary frameworks" relative to the prescriptive regimes in Ontario, BC, and Quebec; this framing is in mild tension with the wiki's existing evidence that NB's CPA c16.05, NLR 80/11, the NS Act's May 2023 amendments, and the operational NS RFS practice (5-year cadence, $5K itemization threshold, 5-15% contingency/PM markup, HST inclusion, deterministic methodology) are each prescriptive within their respective scopes, while PEI's no-reserve-fund framework provides the only fully-discretionary Atlantic baseline [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]] [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]] [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].

### Industry representation infrastructure (CCI Nova Scotia)

- CMHC's PEI provincial fact sheet directs PEI condominium owners and corporations to the Canadian Condominium Institute (CCI) Nova Scotia Chapter (www.ccinovascotia.ca) as the cited industry resource — confirming that PEI has no PEI-specific CCI chapter and depends structurally on Nova Scotia's CCI representation infrastructure for industry advocacy and member services, reinforcing CCI-Nova Scotia's role as the Atlantic-Canada industry representation anchor [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].

### Implication for condo-capital-infra GTM

- The "more frequent reserve-fund studies" change tightens NS's reserve-study cadence to 5 years — directly increasing the addressable engagement frequency per corporation under the ADR-0004 Canada-first sequence Year-1 NS arm; the 5-year cadence aligns NS with BC SPA's mandatory 5-year cycle and roughly doubles the engagement density per corporation versus the synthesis's previously-assumed 10-year framing [[sources/web-2026-01-01-577]] [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
- The 5-year cadence, combined with the 20-year statutory physical-analysis horizon and the operational convention of 5-15% contingency and 5-15% design/PM markup plus HST 15%, defines the engagement-volume and pricing-anchor parameters for sizing the Year-1 NS revenue model under the Canada-first GTM [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
- The 80.8% one-time contribution increase documented in the 2025 Summer Gardens RFS (followed by 2.5% annual increases) is a concrete operational example of the post-Bill-106 funding-adequacy correction NS condominium boards face under the Immediately Adequately Funded plan structure — a real-world data point for the Halifax engagement (Brief-0004) demand-side narrative [[sources/pdf-3c6b4345c8c4]].
