# Ontario Condominium Act, 1998 (S.O. 1998, c. 19)

## Summary

The Ontario Condominium Act, 1998 (S.O. 1998, c. 19) is the comprehensive Ontario provincial statute governing condominium corporations, including their creation, registration, governance, financial obligations, reserve funds, owner protections, dispute resolution, and termination [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]]. The Act establishes a delegation-based regulatory architecture in which the Lieutenant Governor in Council designates a condominium authority and enters into an administrative agreement to administer specified provisions, and defines distinct corporation types (freehold, leasehold, common elements, and the further variants of phased and vacant land corporations) each carrying their own procedural regimes [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]]. The current consolidation runs from October 1, 2023 to the e-Laws currency date, with the most recent amendment being 2023, c. 9, Sched. 7, ss. 1-12 [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].

### Structural organization (per Table of Contents)

- The Act is organized into multiple Parts spanning Definitions and Interpretation (Part I); designation of the condominium authority and administrative-agreement framework; the Condominium Authority Tribunal; registration and the Registrar's role; real property and corporate structure (including types of corporations and requirements for declaration and description); the board of directors, owners' meetings, by-laws, rules, and audit; the annual budget, contribution of owners, and reserve-fund regime; amalgamation; termination; enforcement and offences; phased condominium corporations; common elements condominium corporations; vacant land condominium corporations; leasehold condominium corporations; and transitional provisions [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- Named section topics visible in the TOC include Designation of condominium authority, Administrative agreement, Policy directions, Compliance by condominium authority, Minister's authority to appoint administrator, Condominium Authority Tribunal, Registration, Types of corporations, Board of directors, Standard of care, Indemnification, Insurance, Procurement process, Disclosure by director/officer of interest, Turn-over meeting, Performance audit, By-laws, Rules, Annual budget, Contribution of owners, Lien upon default, Reserve fund, Reserve fund study, Use of reserve fund, Property insurance, Amalgamation, Termination with consent, Compliance order, Oppression remedy, Offences, and Transition provisions [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].

### Part I definitions — corporate and structural terms

- "Board" means the board of directors of a corporation [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Building" means a building included in a property [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "By-law" means a by-law of a corporation; a pending amendment (2015, c. 28, Sched. 1, s. 1(2)) would add "or a by-law made under subsection 21.1(4)" at the end of the definition on a day to be proclaimed [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Common elements" means all the property except the units [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Common expenses" means the expenses related to the performance of the objects and duties of a corporation and all expenses specified as common expenses in the Act, in the regulations, or in a declaration [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Common surplus" means the excess of all receipts of the corporation over the expenses of the corporation [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Common interest" means the interest in the common elements appurtenant to (a) a unit, in the case of all corporations except a common elements condominium corporation, or (b) an owner's parcel of land described in the declaration, in the case of a common elements condominium corporation [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Corporation" means, unless the context otherwise provides or requires, a corporation created or continued under the Act [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].

### Part I definitions — declarant and instrument terms

- "Declarant" means a person who owns the freehold or leasehold estate in the land described in the description and who registers a declaration and description under the Act, including successors and assignees but excluding a good-faith purchaser of a unit who pays fair market value and that purchaser's successors and assignees [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Declarant affiliate" extends declarant status to a body corporate (with or without share capital), whether or not the Act applies to it, that is related to a declarant by being deemed (a) a subsidiary under s. 1(2) of the Business Corporations Act, (b) a holding body under s. 1(3) of the Business Corporations Act, or (c) affiliated under s. 1(4) of the Business Corporations Act [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Declaration" means a declaration registered under section 2 and all amendments to the declaration [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Description" means a description registered under section 2 and all amendments to the description [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Encumbrance" means a claim that secures the payment of money or the performance of any other obligation and includes a charge under the Land Titles Act, a mortgage, and a lien [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Claim" includes a right, title, interest, encumbrance, or demand of any kind affecting land, but does not include the interest of an owner in the owner's unit or common interest [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Deed" includes a transfer under the Land Titles Act [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].

### Part I definitions — types of condominium corporations

- "Freehold condominium corporation" means a corporation in which all the units and their appurtenant common interests are held in fee simple by the owners [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Leasehold condominium corporation" means a corporation in which all the units and their appurtenant common interests are subject to leasehold interests held by the owners [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Common elements condominium corporation" means a common elements condominium corporation described in subsection 138(2) [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Lessor," in relation to a leasehold condominium corporation, means the person who owns the freehold estate in the land described in the description [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- The TOC enumerates separate Parts for phased condominium corporations, common elements condominium corporations, vacant land condominium corporations, and leasehold condominium corporations, each with its own creation, declaration-content, description-content, and disclosure-statement provisions [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].

### Part I definitions — regulatory and oversight terms

- "Condominium authority" means the corporation that the Lieutenant Governor in Council has designated as such under clause 1.1(1)(a) [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Delegated provisions," when used in connection with the condominium authority, means the provisions of the Act and the regulations that the Lieutenant Governor in Council specifies under clause 1.1(1)(b) and of which the administration is delegated to the condominium authority under subsection 1.1(3) [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Administrative agreement" means the agreement described in subsection 1.2(1) [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Condominium management provider," "condominium management services," and "condominium manager" all have the same meaning as in the Condominium Management Services Act, 2015 [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Condominium guide" means a guide that is described in subsection 71.1(1) [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Auditor" means a person licensed as a public accountant under the Public Accounting Act, 2004 who is appointed as an auditor of a corporation under section 60 [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Annual general meeting" means a meeting of the owners of a corporation held in accordance with subsection 45(2) [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Approval authority" means the approval authority for the purposes of sections 51, 51.1, and 51.2 of the Planning Act [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Minister," in relation to a particular provision of the Act, means the Minister responsible for administration of the provision [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].

### Reserve fund operationalization (per Table of Contents headings)

- The Act enumerates "Reserve fund," "Reserve fund study," and "Use of reserve fund" as three distinct sections within the financial-obligations cluster of Part V, alongside Annual budget, Contribution of owners, Lien upon default, Priority of lien, Default with respect to leased unit, Mortgagee's rights, Repair after damage, Repair, and Maintenance [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- The same financial-obligations cluster also enumerates separate sections for Changes made by corporation, Modifications made by corporation, Changes made by owners, Modifications made by owners, Property insurance, Proceeds, Double coverage, Other insurance, Capacity to maintain insurance, Disclosure by insurer, and Deductible — collectively defining the maintenance, replacement, and risk-transfer architecture of an Ontario condominium corporation [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].

### Pending and proclamation-dependent amendments

- The consolidation flags numerous pending amendments enacted under 2015, c. 28, Sched. 1 (Protecting Condominium Owners Act, 2015) and 2017, c. 33, Sched. 2 that come into force on days to be named by proclamation of the Lieutenant Governor [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- A pending 2017, c. 33, Sched. 2, s. 75(1) amendment would add a definition of "guarantee fund" cross-referencing subsection 2(1) of the Protection for Owners and Purchasers of New Homes Act, 2017 [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- A pending 2015, c. 28, Sched. 1, s. 1(9) amendment would add a definition of "improvement" covering (a) any part of a unit that does not constitute a standard unit or part of a standard unit, and (b) any repair or modification to a standard unit done using materials higher in quality (as determined by current construction standards) [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
