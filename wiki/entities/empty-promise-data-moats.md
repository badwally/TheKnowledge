# "The Empty Promise of Data Moats" (Casado & Lauten, 2019)

## Summary

"The Empty Promise of Data Moats" is a May 2019 a16z essay by Andreessen Horowitz partners Martin Casado and Peter Lauten arguing that, for enterprise startups, data is rarely a durable competitive moat and that the popular narrative of "data network effects" largely misidentifies a weaker dynamic — a data scale effect — for a stronger one [[sources/web-2019-05-09-487]].

## Key facts

- The essay distinguishes data scale effects (no direct interaction between nodes; e.g. Netflix recommendations) from true network effects (direct interaction over a protocol or interface) and argues most claimed "data network effects" are scale effects [[sources/web-2019-05-09-487]].
- It introduces a four-phase framework for the data journey: minimum viable corpus, data acquisition cost, incremental data value, and data freshness — each of which works against, rather than for, durable defensibility as the corpus grows [[sources/web-2019-05-09-487]].
- It advances the thesis that defensibility is not inherent to data itself, and that founders treating data as a "magical moat" risk underinvesting in verticalization, go-to-market dominance, post-sales account control, and brand — areas the authors view as the actual sources of long-term defensibility [[sources/web-2019-05-09-487]].
- It acknowledges proprietary-data counterexamples — pharma, Equifax, LexisNexis, Experian — and attributes their defensibility to industry-structure features (scanty sources or sources reticent to provide data to more than one vendor, including government buyers) rather than to data volume per se [[sources/web-2019-05-09-487]].
- It recommends four practical strategies for founders working with data: bootstrap the initial corpus to compete with incumbents, understand the distribution of the data, evaluate the extent to which data actually improves the product, and weigh the quality/quantity tradeoff [[sources/web-2019-05-09-487]].
- The piece is partly a self-revision of a16z's own earlier writing endorsing data network effects [[sources/web-2019-05-09-487]].
