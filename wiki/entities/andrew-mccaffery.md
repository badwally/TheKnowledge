# Andrew McCaffery

## Summary

Global CIO of Asset Management at Fidelity International; author of the Overview chapter of the firm's Investment Outlook 2023, where he argues that overtightening by central banks risks an "inflation bust" and that a hard landing remains the most likely outcome in 2023 [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].

## Key facts

- Listed as Global CIO, Asset Management at Fidelity International on the Investment Outlook 2023 Overview chapter [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Frames the Overview chapter as "Rates overshoot risks inflation bust" [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues a hard landing remains the most likely outcome in 2023, against market hopes for a soft landing [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues the previous norm of central-bank "whatever it takes" intervention during the financial crisis and the pandemic "is going or has gone" [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Predicts sharp rallies on the back of expected Fed action will reverse when those expectations don't materialise — that rates should eventually plateau, but if inflation remains sticky above 2 per cent rates are unlikely to reduce quickly even if banks take other measures to manage debt piles [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Identifies the dollar's direction as a key factor to watch: a strong dollar has been a wrecking ball for other economies and emerging-markets borrowers in hard currency, and a marked change in direction could bring broad relief and increase overall liquidity across challenged economies [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues that any shift away from Japan's yield-curve control could lead to unintended consequences for the yen and add another layer of risk to already elevated FX-market volatility [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues China policymakers will continue focusing on reviving the economy and investing in longer-term areas such as green technologies and infrastructure, with any loosening of Covid restrictions causing consumption to pick up [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues "the deglobalisation that has arisen from the pandemic and tensions with the US will take time to work its way through but is a theme that will grow" [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues that as the dispersion of returns increases, investors will be able to seek out idiosyncratic elements in their portfolios rather than rely on whole-market moves to generate returns [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Identifies decarbonisation and reindustrialisation as longer-term themes that could draw investor attention sooner rather than later [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Recommends emerging markets and Asian countries (with weaker growth correlation to the US and Europe) as a way to increase diversification, while cash and quality investment-grade securities offer defensive characteristics [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
