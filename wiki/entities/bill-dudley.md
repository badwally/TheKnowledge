# Bill Dudley

## Summary

Former president of the Federal Reserve Bank of New York and former vice chair of the FOMC; in an April 6, 2022 column he argued that for hikes to be effective the Fed will have to shock markets and inflict more losses on stock and bond investors — an argument that Zoltan Pozsar treats as the closest a former Fed policymaker will get to endorsing the need for a "Volcker Moment" [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].

## Key facts

- Identified as the former president of the Federal Reserve Bank of New York in Pozsar's May 13, 2022 dispatch [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Also identified as the former vice chair of the FOMC, lending weight to his April 6, 2022 argument [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Argued on April 6, 2022 that for hikes to be effective, financial conditions have to tighten more — a lot more [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Argued that if the broader market expects rate hikes to undermine growth and force the Fed to cut rates in 2024, financial conditions mathematically won't tighten on their own, so "the Fed will have to shock markets to achieve the desired response" by inflicting "more losses on stock and bond investors than it has so far" [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Closed the April 6, 2022 column by saying "one way or another, to get inflation under control, the Fed will need to push bond yields higher and stock prices lower" [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Per Pozsar, a former vice chair of the FOMC arguing the Fed needs to shock markets is "as close as we will ever get to a former Fed policymaker endorsing the need for a 'Volcker Moment'" [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Interviewed Zoltan Pozsar in summer 2008 to join the Markets Group of FRBNY after Pozsar sent him a brief note describing concerns about the plumbing [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
