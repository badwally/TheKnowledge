# Corsica

## Summary

Corsica is a Mediterranean island promoted by its tourism agency as a cycling destination — its signature offering for riders is the [[entities/gt-20|GT 20]], a 600 km, 12-stage cyclo-tourist route crossing the island from north to south [[sources/pdf-432fe168a2d1]]. Beyond the GT 20, the island also hosts commercial supported-cycling holidays such as [[entities/marmot-tours|Marmot Tours]]'s 6-day [[entities/corsican-highlights|Corsican Highlights]] [[sources/pdf-f03d709c7ff7]] and [[entities/europe-active|Europe Active]]'s 7-day self-guided [[entities/cycling-corsica-self-guided-luxury|Cycling Corsica Luxury]] route [[sources/pdf-a78b30f3c6c3]].

## Key facts

- Corsica markets itself as 'THE destination' for cyclists, pitching a mix of seaside, mountain landscapes, authentic interior villages, and locally sourced products [[sources/pdf-432fe168a2d1]].
- The island is crossed end-to-end by the [[entities/gt-20|GT 20]] cycling itinerary, running ~600 km from Bastia in the north to Bonifacio (Bunifaziu) in the south [[sources/pdf-432fe168a2d1]].
- Major cities and stage towns on the cycling route include Bastia, Calvi, Corti, Aiacciu, Portivecchju, and Bunifaziu [[sources/pdf-432fe168a2d1]].
- The island is also the setting for the [[entities/corsica-cyclo-gt-20|Corsica Cyclo 'GT 20']], a four-day stage cyclosportive run on the GT 20 each May [[sources/pdf-432fe168a2d1]].
- [[entities/marmot-tours|Marmot Tours]] runs the 6-day [[entities/corsican-highlights|Corsican Highlights]] holiday on the island in September, looping clockwise from Bastia via St-Florent, Calvi, Porto, and Corte to Solenzara [[sources/pdf-f03d709c7ff7]].
- [[entities/europe-active|Europe Active]] runs a 7-day self-guided 'Cycling Corsica Luxury' tour daily from April through October, point-to-point from Bastia north around Cap Corse to Saint-Florent, across the Désert des Agriates, and on to Ile Rousse and Calvi [[sources/pdf-a78b30f3c6c3]].
- Notable cycling cols on the island include the [[entities/col-de-vergio|Col de Vergio]] (1,477 m — the highest road pass on Corsica), the Col de Sorba (1,314 m), the [[entities/col-de-bavella|Col de Bavella]] (1,218 m), the Col de Sevi (1,105 m), and the Col de Bataille (1,096 m) [[sources/pdf-f03d709c7ff7]].
- Distinctive coastal cycling sections include Cap Corse, the Désert des Agriates, the cliff-hugging road through the Calanque di Piana red cliffs, and the NW coast road from Calvi to the Col de Palmarella [[sources/pdf-f03d709c7ff7]].
- Cap Corse cycling waypoints, riding up the east side from Bastia, include Erbalunga (where commercial tours typically transfer riders out of Bastia traffic) and the Moulin de Mattei windmill before a descent down to Centuri on the west side [[sources/pdf-a78b30f3c6c3]].
- Centuri is described as the first lobster fishing port in France, with harbourside restaurants serving the local catch [[sources/pdf-a78b30f3c6c3]].
- The west-coast Cap Corse road from Centuri to Saint-Florent passes Nonza (with its Paoline tower and black pebble beach), Albo, and Patrimonio — the latter described as the birthplace of the best wine in Corsica, with cellar wine-tasting widely offered [[sources/pdf-a78b30f3c6c3]].
- Saint-Florent is positioned as 'the Corsican Saint-Tropez,' a small harbour town with a white sand beach and yacht moorings, from which boats run to the Saleccia beach in the Désert des Agriates [[sources/pdf-a78b30f3c6c3]].
- The Désert des Agriates, despite its name, is described by Europe Active as a 'luxurious granitic and green environment' covered in Maquis scrub, with the Vezzu pass marking the high point and views across the mountains before a descent into La Balagne [[sources/pdf-a78b30f3c6c3]].
- Ile Rousse is a lively coastal town with a market square and cafés under the plane trees, a common evening stop on the north-coast cycling itinerary [[sources/pdf-a78b30f3c6c3]].
- La Balagne contains hill villages — including Pigna, Sant'Antonino, Aregno, and Cateri — connected by an 'artisans' route'; Sant'Antonino is locally known for its lemon juice [[sources/pdf-a78b30f3c6c3]].
- Calvi is described as a 'perfect Mediterranean town' with a Genoese citadelle, a sandy bay backed by pines, and a busy harbourside [[sources/pdf-a78b30f3c6c3]].
- Bastia airport (BIA) is the standard arrival point for Bastia-based cycling holidays — Marmot's transfers run 45 min from BIA to its Pietranera start hotel and 1 hr 30 min back from Solenzara — with transfers to and from Bastia Port also offered on request [[sources/pdf-f03d709c7ff7]].
- Europe Active also uses Bastia airport as the arrival point for its self-guided luxury tour, with a Day-1 transfer from Bastia airport into the old town included in the tour price [[sources/pdf-a78b30f3c6c3]].
- Bastia is reachable by air from several European cities via Air France, Air Corsica, Easyjet, Volotea, and Ryanair, and by sea via Corsica Ferries, Corsica Linea, and La Méridionale [[sources/pdf-a78b30f3c6c3]].
- A 2019 GT 20 promotional kakemono additionally depicts the Corsican towns of Lisula (Île-Rousse), Centuri, Belgudè, Calinzana, Galeria, Portu, Venacu, Ghisoni, Zicavu, Zonza, Conca, and Figari as waypoints along the GT 20 route [[sources/pdf-c985b0372d8a]].
- The same kakemono shows the [[entities/gr20|GR20]] long-distance walking trail crossing Corsica alongside the GT 20 cycling route at multiple points [[sources/pdf-c985b0372d8a]].
