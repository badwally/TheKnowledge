# "No One Owns Data" (Determann, 2019)

## Summary

"No One Owns Data" is a 2019 Hastings Law Journal article by Lothar Determann arguing that data is not — and should not be — subject to property rights, with the analysis applied to both the United States and the European Union [[sources/web-2019-01-23-bbd]]. The article is a response to a wave of calls from businesses, policy makers, and scholars to recognize property rights in data, especially the data generated by connected cars, industrial machines, artificial intelligence, toys, and other Internet of Things (IoT) devices, in which many parties have overlapping interests [[sources/web-2019-01-23-bbd]].

## Key facts

- Cited as Lothar Determann, *No One Owns Data*, 70 Hastings L.J. 1 (2019); published online 2019-01-23 in the UC Law Journal (Hastings) repository [[sources/web-2019-01-23-bbd]].
- Identifies a pattern in which stakeholders in digital markets — businesses, individual data subjects, policy makers, and scholars — routinely frame data-access disputes as questions of ownership, with businesses asserting they own data and data subjects assuming they own data about themselves [[sources/web-2019-01-23-bbd]].
- Draws a conceptual distinction between "data" (the content of information) and three categories that ARE subject to property rights: (i) works of authorship and databases (intellectual property rights), (ii) physical embodiments of information such as data on a computer chip (personal property rights), and (iii) the physical objects or intangible items to which information relates [[sources/web-2019-01-23-bbd]].
- Argues that the standard rationale for granting property rights — incentivizing investments and improvements in works of authorship, databases, land, and chattels — does not exist with respect to data itself [[sources/web-2019-01-23-bbd]].
- Claims that existing US and EU law deliberately refrain from granting property rights in data, instead protecting the competing interests of individuals, businesses, governments, and the public through an "intricate net" of existing rules [[sources/web-2019-01-23-bbd]].
- Argues new property rights in data are not suited to promote better privacy, innovation, or technological advances, and would more likely suffocate free speech, information freedom, science, and technological progress [[sources/web-2019-01-23-bbd]].
- Concludes that the rationales for propertizing data are not compelling and are outweighed by the rationales for keeping data "open," so no new property rights need to be created for data [[sources/web-2019-01-23-bbd]].
