# Victor Niederhoffer

## Summary

Ex-Soros veteran and Asia expert who entered long OTC swaps on Thai equities in September 1997 as a bottom appeared to be developing during the Asian Financial Crisis; when the Thai Baht and equities fell further in mid-October, cascading margin calls on his Thai swap positions caused his U.S. clearing broker REFCO to liquidate his short SPX put spreads, producing an immediate impact on U.S. futures and options markets — a textbook margin-call cascade that Constan cites alongside LTCM and Archegos as demonstrations of how margin calls from one counterparty trigger demands from others [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].

## Key facts

- Identified as an "ex-Soros veteran and Asia expert" [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Entered long swaps on Thai equities in September 1997 as a bottom appeared to be developing [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- By mid-October 1997, the Thai Baht and Thai equities began another leg lower, triggering margin calls [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- The margin calls on his OTC Thai swaps quickly caused his U.S. futures and options clearing broker REFCO to liquidate his short SPX put spreads [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- The impact on U.S. markets was immediate [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Constan cites the episode as an important lesson — then relatively new — about "the speed at which margin calls by one counterparty result in other counterparties calling for more margin as well" [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Constan names LTCM and Bill Hwang at Archegos as later examples of the same mass margin-call dynamic [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- The resulting deleveraging in October 1997 produced a 10.8% SPX drawdown that was essentially over in 3 days, with markets retaking the prior high just 39 days later [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
