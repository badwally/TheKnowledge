# Fannie Mae Form 1076 (Condominium Project Questionnaire)

## Summary

Fannie Mae Form 1076 is the Condominium Project Questionnaire required for every conventional mortgage on a condominium unit; its Freddie Mac counterpart is Form 1077 [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]]. The form is organized into 8 sections (Project Information, Insurance, Financial Information, Delinquency, Ownership and Occupancy, Governance, Special Assessments and Litigation, Additional Disclosures) and is used by lenders to determine whether a condominium project meets eligibility requirements for conventional financing [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]]. The questionnaire is identified by CommunityPay as one of the most frequent sources of closing delays in condominium transactions, with stale financial data, incomplete insurance information, incorrect delinquency calculations, missing/outdated reserve studies, and inconsistent answers across sections as the five named common failure modes [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].

## Key facts

### Statutory framing

- Every conventional mortgage for a condo unit requires a completed Form 1076 (Fannie Mae) or Form 1077 (Freddie Mac) [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].
- Lenders use the questionnaire answers to determine whether the project meets eligibility requirements for conventional financing [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].

### Section structure (8 sections)

- Section 1 — Project Information: project name, address, number of units, unit types (attached/detached), year built, developer status (completed vs. phased), conversion status [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].
- Section 2 — Insurance: master hazard/property, general liability, fidelity/crime bond, flood (if in zone), workers' compensation (if employees), D&O — each requiring carrier name, policy number, coverage limits, deductible amounts, expiration dates [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].
- Section 3 — Financial Information: total annual budget, total annual assessment income, operating reserves balance, replacement reserves balance, reserve study completion status and date, any planned/pending special assessments [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].
- Section 4 — Delinquency: number of units 30+ days delinquent on assessments, total delinquency amount, delinquency percentage (units delinquent / total units), single-entity multi-unit ownership disclosure [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].
- Section 5 — Ownership and Occupancy: total units, owner-occupied units, investor-owned (non-owner-occupied) units, single-entity >10% ownership disclosure [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].
- Section 6 — Governance: board composition and meeting frequency, self-managed vs professionally managed, management company name (if applicable), developer-turnover status [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].
- Section 7 — Special Assessments and Litigation: pending/planned special assessments (amount, purpose, timeline), pending/threatened/settled litigation, FEMA disaster declarations affecting the project [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].
- Section 8 — Additional Disclosures: commercial space percentage (if mixed-use), right of first refusal or transfer restrictions, environmental hazards, completion status (if phased development) [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].

### Critical eligibility thresholds

- Section 4 is critical for Fannie Mae eligibility: projects with delinquency rates exceeding 15% may be ineligible for conventional financing [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].
- Delinquency percentage must be calculated based on units 30+ days past due, not dollar amounts [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].

### Operational failure modes (per CommunityPay)

- Five named common errors that delay closings: stale financial data, incomplete insurance information, incorrect delinquency calculations, missing or outdated reserve studies, inconsistent answers across sections [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].
- Lenders expect a current reserve study; if the most recent study is more than 3–5 years old, or if the reserve balance does not align with the study's recommendations, the lender may require a new study before proceeding [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].
- Mortgage rate locks typically expire in 30–60 days; questionnaire delays directly affect the buyer's cost of homeownership [[sources/web-2026-02-11-4eb]].
