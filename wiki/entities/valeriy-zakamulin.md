# Valeriy Zakamulin

## Summary

Finance researcher at the School of Business and Law, University of Agder, Kristiansand, Norway (also publishing under the name Valeri Zakamouline); author of the 2016 paper "Market Timing with Moving Averages: Anatomy and Performance of Trading Rules," which argues that every moving-average trading indicator can be reinterpreted as a weighted moving average of price changes and that the performance of any such rule depends exclusively on the shape of that weighting function [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].

## Key facts

- Affiliated with the School of Business and Law, University of Agder, Service Box 422, 4604 Kristiansand, Norway, with phone (+47) 38 14 10 39 and email Valeri.Zakamouline@uia.no [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].
- Sole author of "Market Timing with Moving Averages: Anatomy and Performance of Trading Rules" (first draft June 2014; revision May 29, 2016), available as SSRN abstract id 2585056 [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].
- The paper's first contribution is to "uncover the anatomy of market timing rules with moving averages," presenting a methodology that examines how the value of each trading indicator is computed and showing that all such indicators are computed in the same general manner [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].
- The paper's second contribution is to evaluate the out-of-sample performance of 300 various shapes of the weighting function for price changes using historical data on four financial market indices [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].
- Acknowledges Steen Koekebakker, Pete Nikolai, and participants of the 3rd Economics & Finance Conference (April 2015, Rome, Italy) for insightful comments on earlier drafts [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].
- Cited in his own paper as the author of an earlier 2014 study (Zakamulin, 2014), which the paper notes is — together with Sullivan, Timmermann, and White (1999) — one of only two papers known to the author to implement out-of-sample tests of profitability of trading rules in the stock market [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].
- Argues that prior academic studies on market timing have selected only an arbitrary and limited set of "most popular combinations" of trading rules with moving-average weighting schemes, so their conclusions cannot be generalized to the entire universe of all potential combinations [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].
