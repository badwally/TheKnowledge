# Florida Statute §718.111 (The Association)

## Summary

Florida Statute §718.111 is the section of the Florida Condominium Act (Chapter 718) that governs the corporate organization, powers, duties, and operational obligations of Florida condominium associations. The statute requires the association to be a Florida corporation (for-profit or not-for-profit), imposes a fiduciary relationship on officers and directors toward unit owners, criminalizes kickbacks as third-degree felonies, defines the association's powers to contract/sue/be sued and to manage common elements, governs association rights of access (including abandoned units), specifies title-acquisition procedures, and mandates adequate property insurance based on replacement cost re-appraised at least every 36 months [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].

## Key facts

- The operation of a Florida condominium must be by an association that is a Florida corporation for-profit or not-for-profit; associations in existence on January 1, 1977 need not be incorporated, per §718.111(1)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Unit owners are shareholders or members of the association, per §718.111(1)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Officers and directors of the association have a fiduciary relationship to the unit owners, per §718.111(1)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The Legislature explicitly disclaims providing or removing any fiduciary relationship between a manager employed by the association and the unit owners, per §718.111(1)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- An association may operate more than one condominium, per §718.111(1)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- A director present at a board meeting is presumed to have assented to the action unless the director votes against or abstains; abstention is presumed to be no position, per §718.111(1)(b) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Directors may not vote by proxy or by secret ballot at board meetings, except that officers may be elected by secret ballot, per §718.111(1)(b) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- A vote or abstention for each member present must be recorded in the minutes, per §718.111(1)(b) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- A unit owner has no authority to act for the association by reason of being a unit owner, per §718.111(1)(c) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- §718.111(1)(d) imports the duty-of-care standard from Florida §617.0830 for officers, directors, and agents [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The association's powers and duties include those in §718.111 and, except as limited by Chapter 718, those in the declaration, bylaws, and Part I of Chapter 607 and Chapter 617, per §718.111(2) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The association may contract, sue, or be sued in its own name with respect to the exercise or nonexercise of its powers, per §718.111(3)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- After developer control transitions, the association may institute actions concerning common elements, roof and structural components, mechanical/electrical/plumbing elements, and developer representations, per §718.111(3)(b) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The association may protest ad valorem taxes, defend ad valorem and eminent domain actions, and bring inverse condemnation actions, per §718.111(3)(b)2–4 [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- An association may not hire an attorney who represents the management company of the association, per §718.111(3)(f) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The association has the power to make and collect assessments and to lease, maintain, repair, and replace common elements or association property, per §718.111(4) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The association may not charge a use fee against a unit owner for use of common elements or association property unless the declaration so provides, the association votes by majority, or the charges relate to exclusive-use expenses, per §718.111(4) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The association has an irrevocable right of access to each unit during reasonable hours when necessary for maintenance, repair, or replacement of common elements or association-maintained portions of a unit, or to prevent damage, per §718.111(5)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Two or more residential condominiums whose initial declaration was recorded before January 1, 1977 may be operated by a single association as a consolidated financial unit, per §718.111(6) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The association has the power to acquire title to property; conveyance, lease, or mortgage of association real property requires the procedure in the declaration or 75% of total voting interests if the declaration is silent, per §718.111(7)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The board has limited power, subject to §718.112(2)(o), to convey a portion of common elements to a condemning authority for utility easements, right-of-way expansion, or other public purposes, per §718.111(7)(b) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The association has the power to purchase any land or recreation lease, subject to the §718.114 approval requirements for leasehold acquisitions, per §718.111(8) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The association may purchase units in the condominium and hold, lease, mortgage, and convey them; except in timeshare condominiums, a board member, manager, or management company may not purchase a unit at a foreclosure sale on the association's own lien or by deed in lieu, per §718.111(9) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Unless prohibited by the declaration, the board may grant, modify, or move any easement that constitutes part of or crosses the common elements or association property without unit-owner joinder, per §718.111(10) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- §718.111(11) governs property insurance and applies to every residential condominium in Florida regardless of the date of its declaration; the Legislature's stated intent is to encourage lower or stable insurance premiums [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
