# Brookfield Residential Services, Ltd.

## Summary

Brookfield Residential Services, Ltd. is the Canadian condominium management subsidiary of Brookfield Properties Corporation; in a May 15, 2007 Yardi Systems press release, the company was characterized as Canada's largest condominium management firm, managing more than 50,000 condominium units in the greater metropolitan Toronto area [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]]. The company's May 2007 selection of Yardi Voyager and Yardi Portal with Yardi ASP Hosting Service is the load-bearing primary-source evidence anchoring Yardi's long-standing Canadian-condo-channel relationship that supports the Yardi-as-acquirer thesis in the condo-capital-infra exit-thesis-acquirer-mapping under ADR-0004 [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]].

## Key facts

### Corporate identity and ownership (as of May 2007)

- Canada's largest condominium management firm as of May 2007 [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]].
- Manages more than 50,000 condominium units in the greater metropolitan Toronto area as of May 2007 [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]].
- Owned by Brookfield Properties Corporation, a publicly-traded company on the New York Stock Exchange and the Toronto Stock Exchange as of May 2007 [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]].
- Self-characterized as focusing on the individual needs and objectives of each condominium it manages, despite its size, with an "unwavering commitment to its clients and employees" [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]].

### Leadership (as of May 2007)

- Tracy Gregory served as Senior Vice President of Finance for Brookfield as of May 2007 [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]].

### Yardi Voyager adoption (May 15, 2007)

- On May 15, 2007, announced selection of Yardi Voyager and Yardi Portal with Yardi ASP Hosting Service, characterized by Brookfield's SVP of Finance Tracy Gregory as adoption of a "user-friendly, browser-based interface" that "can be customized to meet our needs" [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]].
- Stated objectives of the Yardi adoption: maximize efficiency using the speed of the Internet; enable Brookfield staff, property owners, and board members to retrieve information from anywhere at any time; provide enhanced services through browser-based technology integrated with centralized data [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]].
- Specific Yardi Portal use cases targeted: online fee payment, online maintenance requests, concierge services, real-time accounting and property-management data integration with the corporate Web site, and increased market exposure via dynamic Web presence [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]].
- Stated rationale for adopting Yardi ASP Hosting Service: benefit from "the power and performance of state-of-the-art technology without having to maintain the IT infrastructure, support services or staff necessary to keep their system running at peak efficiency" [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]].
- Yardi's Canadian operations point of contact at the time of announcement: Peter Altobelli, Vice President of Canadian Operations for Yardi Systems [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]].

### Implication for condo-capital-infra exit thesis

- The May 2007 selection of Yardi Voyager by Canada's largest condo-management firm establishes a ~19-year Yardi-Brookfield Canadian-condo-channel relationship at the country's largest book of business — load-bearing primary-source confirmation that Yardi has held an entrenched Canadian-condo-segment position over nearly two decades, supporting the Yardi-as-acquirer-target framing in the ADR-0004 Canada-first GTM exit-thesis-acquirer-mapping [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]].
- The 50,000+ Toronto-unit footprint under Brookfield's management in 2007 frames Toronto/Ontario as the historically dominant Canadian-condo concentration relevant to the Year-2 Ontario expansion arm of the Canada-first GTM, where Brookfield's installed Yardi base would represent a structural integration-target footprint for any Ontario-channel partnership ramp [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]].
- The Brookfield Properties Corporation parent's public-company status (NYSE + TSX dual-listing as of 2007) surfaces a structural pattern relevant to Associa's October 2025 acquisition of Douglas Elliman Property Management: non-CAM-native public-company parents have historically held property-management subsidiaries that become divestiture candidates — a pattern that may resurface for Brookfield's property-management arm over the engagement window [[sources/web-2007-05-15-70e]].
