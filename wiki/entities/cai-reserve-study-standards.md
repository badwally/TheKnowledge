# CAI Reserve Study Standards

## Summary

The Community Associations Institute (CAI) publishes the reserve-study standards (2023 and 2025 editions referenced in the engagement synthesis) that define the methodology baseline for the U.S. reserve-study industry [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]]. The condo-capital-infra synthesis identifies a structural gap in those standards: CAI does not require probabilistic methods, so vendors do not build them — making methodology the moat for any new entrant pursuing probabilistic reserve modeling [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]]. The August 2010 California Department of Real Estate Reserve Study Guidelines explicitly aligns its interpretive framework to CAI's methodology baseline alongside the Association of Professional Reserve Preparers (APRA), establishing state-government adoption of CAI's deterministic methodology convention as the operative interpretive frame for California Civil Code § 1365 / § 1365.5 (predecessor to Davis-Stirling § 5550) [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]]. PropFusion's April 2026 vendor-curated FL SIRS guide confirms that CAI's Reserve Specialist (RS) and Professional Reserve Analyst (PRA) designations are among the statutorily-recognized qualified-preparer categories under HB 913-amended Florida SIRS — vendor-curated evidence that CAI's credentialing system enjoys direct statutory recognition in Florida [[sources/web-2026-04-27-33b]]. Solume's January 2025 vendor-curated multi-state reserve-study state-requirements guide reinforces the deterministic-methodology framing in vendor education across 13 US states (CA, CO, DE, FL, HI, MD, NV, NJ, OR, TN, UT, VA, WA), surfacing CAI's deterministic methodology baseline as the operative interpretive frame in vendor-curated regulatory-interpretation content beyond California and Florida [[sources/web-2025-01-07-b6e]]. Condo Control's February 2025 practitioner-standard cross-province summary (Brown) extends the vendor-and-regulator-curated cross-reference set into the Canadian regulatory regime, framing reserve-fund-study practice across Ontario (Class 1/2/3 cadence), Alberta (5-year + 12-unit DIY exception), and British Columbia (5-year + six qualified-provider categories + Nov 2023 contribution rules + July 2026/2027 deadlines) entirely in deterministic point-estimate terms without engagement with probabilistic methodology — confirming that CAI's deterministic methodology baseline is also the operative interpretive frame for Canadian practitioner-standard educational content covering the project's Year-2 Toronto and Vancouver waves under Brief-0011 [[sources/web-2025-02-05-f51]]. The Condominium Authority of Ontario's December 2023 "Best Practices Guide: Ensuring Healthy Reserve Funds" is the regulator-curated regulatory-interpretation surface (alongside the U.S. California DRE 2010 Reserve Study Guidelines and the Florida DBPR SIRS-reporting framework) confirming deterministic point-estimate methodology — assumed annual inflation rate and assumed annual interest rate treated as scalar disclosed inputs in the Ontario Reg. 48/01 §29(3) financial analysis — as the operative baseline for North American reserve-study practice, with Ontario's primary regulator explicitly conceding that reserve fund studies are "only ever able to provide an estimate of future costs and may be an under-estimation of repair and replacement costs" [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]].

## Key facts

- Editions referenced in the engagement synthesis: 2023 and 2025 [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- The standards do not require probabilistic methods (Monte Carlo, Bayesian hierarchical, etc.); existing vendor implementations remain deterministic point estimates with fully-funded vs. baseline comparisons [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- The engagement plan reserves probabilistic depth for the six high-leverage components and treats CAI-compliant deterministic coverage as table stakes for the long tail [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- The synthesis explicitly cites the absence of a CAI requirement as the structural reason vendors have not built probabilistic engines [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- The August 2010 California DRE Reserve Study Guidelines were updated by Roy Helsing PRA, RS specifically to ensure alignment with current California law and the guidelines of the Association of Professional Reserve Preparers (APRA) and the Community Associations Institute (CAI) — an explicit state-government adoption of CAI's methodology baseline as the interpretive framework for the predecessor § 1365 / § 1365.5 regime [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
- The DRE Guidelines cite CAI alongside APRA as the two industry-standard reference frameworks for reserve study practice in California, demonstrating that the state regulator's interpretive guidance defers to CAI's methodology baseline even where the statute does not mandate it [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
- The DRE Guidelines describe both physical and funding analysis steps in deterministic point-estimate terms — consistent with CAI's deterministic methodology baseline, reinforcing that probabilistic methods are absent both in CAI standards proper and in state-government interpretive guidance that defers to CAI [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
- PropFusion's April 2026 vendor-curated FL SIRS guide explicitly identifies CAI's Reserve Specialist (RS) and Professional Reserve Analyst (PRA) designations as among the three statutory qualified-preparer categories authorized to perform or verify a Florida SIRS under HB 913 — vendor-curated regulatory-interpretation evidence that the CAI-credentialed reserve-specialist profession is recognized directly in Florida statute alongside the Florida-licensed Professional Engineer and Florida-licensed Architect categories, establishing FL SIRS as a second state-government adoption surface (after California's DRE Guidelines) for CAI's credentialing system [[sources/web-2026-04-27-33b]].
- PropFusion's 2026 FL SIRS guide also documents that HB 913 tightened conflict-of-interest rules for SIRS preparers: professionals who bid on a SIRS must disclose if they intend to bid on repair work, and undisclosed conflicts can render the contract voidable — a statutory enforcement provision that operationalizes the independence dimension CAI's standards historically framed as a methodology-baseline practice norm [[sources/web-2026-04-27-33b]].
- Solume's January 2025 vendor-curated multi-state reserve-study state-requirements guide reinforces the deterministic-methodology framing of CAI's standards across all 13 US states it covers (CA, CO, DE, FL, HI, MD, NV, NJ, OR, TN, UT, VA, WA): the guide describes reserve-study practice in deterministic point-estimate terms throughout, with no engagement with probabilistic deterioration modeling, Monte Carlo aggregation, or distribution-based forecasting — confirming that CAI's deterministic methodology baseline is operating as the interpretive default across vendor-curated multi-state regulatory-interpretation content well beyond the California DRE and Florida SIRS state-government adoption surfaces [[sources/web-2025-01-07-b6e]].
- Solume's guide is a second vendor-curated regulatory-interpretation cross-reference (after PropFusion's April 2026 FL SIRS guide) that frames CAI-baseline deterministic methodology as the unstated convention underlying vendor education content — surfacing the multi-state-vendor-thought-leadership channel as a competitive perimeter against probabilistic-methodology positioning across the US Year-2 expansion footprint [[sources/web-2025-01-07-b6e]] [[sources/web-2026-04-27-33b]].
- Condo Control's February 2025 practitioner-standard cross-province summary (Brown, condocontrol.com) is the first identified Canadian-jurisdiction practitioner-standard secondary cross-reference confirming deterministic methodology as the operative interpretive frame across Canada's three largest condominium / strata reserve-fund regimes (Ontario, Alberta, British Columbia) — the cross-Canada summary describes Ontario's Class 1/2/3 cadence schema, Alberta's 5-year cycle with 12-unit DIY exception, and British Columbia's 5-year cycle, six qualified-provider categories, Nov 1, 2023 minimum 10% CRF contribution rule, post-Nov 2023 owner-developer contribution formula, July 1, 2026 / July 1, 2027 grace-period deadlines, and the at-least-3-cash-flow-funding-models prescribed-contents requirement entirely in deterministic point-estimate terms, with no engagement with probabilistic deterioration modeling, Monte Carlo aggregation, or distribution-based forecasting [[sources/web-2025-02-05-f51]].
- The Condo Control summary frames Florida's HB 913-driven funding mandate as the structural-collapse-response anchor for Canadian readers ("a recent law is making prospective buyers think twice about buying a condo unit in Florida"), positioning Canada's existing mandatory-reserve-study regimes as protective against Florida-style funding shocks — a vendor-positioning frame that situates Canadian regimes as more mature than U.S. peers while preserving deterministic methodology as the unstated methodological baseline across both [[sources/web-2025-02-05-f51]].
- With Condo Control's February 2025 Canadian practitioner-standard summary added to the cross-reference set, the deterministic-methodology baseline is now confirmed across three vendor-curated practitioner-standard channels (PropFusion FL/CA/TX/GA/MI/MD/NJ; Solume CA/CO/DE/FL/HI/MD/NV/NJ/OR/TN/UT/VA/WA; Condo Control ON/AB/BC) and three regulator-curated channels (California DRE 2010 Guidelines; Florida DBPR SIRS framework; CAO Best Practices Guide December 2023) — six independent interpretive surfaces all framing reserve-study practice in deterministic point-estimate terms, making methodology-tier differentiation the operative competitive perimeter against an exceptionally well-established regulator-and-vendor-curated deterministic consensus that spans the U.S. and the Canadian jurisdictions targeted by the project's Year-1 NS arm, Year-2 ON/BC arms, and Year-2 FL channel partnership [[sources/web-2025-02-05-f51]] [[sources/web-2025-01-07-b6e]] [[sources/web-2026-04-27-33b]] [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]] [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]].
- The Condominium Authority of Ontario's December 2023 "Best Practices Guide: Ensuring Healthy Reserve Funds" is a third regulator-curated regulatory-interpretation surface confirming deterministic point-estimate methodology as the operative baseline: the Guide prescribes that the financial analysis under Ontario Regulation 48/01 §29(3) disclose an assumed annual inflation rate and an assumed annual interest rate as scalar per-year inputs over the at-least-30-year projection horizon — consistent with CAI's deterministic methodology baseline and the U.S. California DRE / Florida SIRS state-government adoption surfaces — and explicitly acknowledges that reserve fund studies are "only ever able to provide an estimate of future costs and may be an under-estimation of repair and replacement costs as unexpected expenses may arise," surfacing Ontario's primary regulator's concession of the under-estimation limitation that the project's probabilistic engine is designed to address [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]].
- The CAO Best Practices Guide adds Canada's largest condominium-corporation regulator (Ontario, 12,000+ corporations per CAO 2024 survey) to the state-government-and-vendor-curated adoption set, framing deterministic methodology as the operative baseline across the BC SPA depreciation-report regime, the ON Condo Act 1998 reserve-fund regime, the California Davis-Stirling § 5550 regime, the Florida SIRS regime, and U.S. vendor-curated regulatory-interpretation content in 13 states — making methodology-tier differentiation the primary competitive perimeter against the established regulator-and-vendor-curated deterministic-methodology consensus in both the project's Year-1 NS arm, Year-2 ON arm, and Year-2 FL channel partnership [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]] [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]] [[sources/web-2026-04-27-33b]] [[sources/web-2025-01-07-b6e]].
