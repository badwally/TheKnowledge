# Dale Copeland

## Summary

International-relations scholar and author of the book "Economic Interdependence and War," cited by Zoltan Pozsar in the August 24, 2022 Credit Suisse dispatch "War and Industrial Policy" as the analytical backbone for understanding both the U.S./Russia/China conflict and the inflation outlook [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].

## Key facts

- Author of the book "Economic Interdependence and War," which Pozsar cites as a 200-year history covering the Napoleonic and Crimean wars [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Pozsar quotes the book's central argument: "when great powers have positive expectations of the future trade environment, they want to remain at peace in order to secure the economic benefits that enhance long-term economic power. When, however, these expectations turn negative, leaders are likely to fear a loss of access to raw materials and markets, giving them an incentive to initiate crises to protect their commercial interests" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Pozsar labels this argument "the theory of trade expectations" and treats it as holding lessons "not only for today's conflict between the U.S. on the one hand, and Russia and China on the other, but also the outlook for inflation" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- The book's framing — "if there is trust, trade works. If trust is gone, it doesn't" — is the explicit logical bridge Pozsar uses to argue that distrust drives de-globalization and de-globalization drives "The Great Reflation" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
