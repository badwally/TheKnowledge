# Carsten Brzeski

## Summary

Global Head of Macro and Chief Economist for the Eurozone, Germany, and Austria at ING; lead author of the December 8, 2022 "ING global economic outlook 2023," which frames 2023 as a year of "different shades of recession" and articulates ING's three calls — recession, inflation, and central banks [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].

## Key facts

- Listed title at ING: Global Head of Macro and Chief Economist, Eurozone, Germany, Austria [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Listed contact email: carsten.brzeski@ing.de [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Authored ING's three calls for 2023: recession, inflation, and central banks [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Argues the "natural reflex of many forecasters" is to extrapolate recent trends, but ING's predictions reflect base-case median forecasts backed by 2022's events, explicitly not identifying potential new black, grey, or pink swans [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Frames 2022 as "Zeitenwende" — a German term he glosses as "game changer" — citing a war in the EU's backyard, the end of cheap energy, and the end of globalisation as Europeans knew it [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Argues that "interest rates entering restrictive territory in early 2023, the looming loss of economic wealth and a large need for investment" will force the ECB to stop hiking earlier than it might like, with the alternative risk being a policy mistake if it hikes far beyond mildly restrictive levels [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Closes the outlook by relaying that a friend told him "may he live in interesting times" is "actually not a Chinese proverb but more a curse" [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
