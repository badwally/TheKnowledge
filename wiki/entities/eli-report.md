# Eli Report

## Summary

Eli Report is the flagship product of OctoAI Technologies Corp (Vancouver, BC), an AI-powered condo document review platform that transforms hundreds of pages of condo documents into a single, clear report for Realtors, condo buyers, sellers, owners, lawyers, lenders, property managers, condo boards, and strata councils [[sources/web-2024-10-07-033]]. Effective October 7, 2024, OctoAI transitioned the product to a freemium pricing model across North America, with free essential tools (powered by a proprietary AI system enhanced with summaries from OpenAI's ChatGPT, plus a side-by-side viewer) and paid tiers for advanced capabilities and access to OctoAI's expert condo data review team [[sources/web-2024-10-07-033]]. As of the October 2024 freemium launch, Eli Report had delivered over 25,000 reports to thousands of users across the US and Canada [[sources/web-2024-10-07-033]]. The condo-capital-infra synthesis identifies Eli Report as the canonical workaround to the Canadian-HOA-data-scraping prohibition documented in the Ontario CAO Registry's Terms of Use — a transactional-data-acquisition product (rather than a scraping-based dataset) that uses AI to extract structured key-data-point information from condo Reserve Fund Studies, Board Meeting Minutes, and related corporate records uploaded by users who already hold legal access to those documents during the status-certificate or disclosure phase of a unit sale [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]]. The vendor's October 31, 2024 canonical product page (elireport.com/products/condo-document-review/) enumerates the full six-section product feature taxonomy — governing-document and bylaws decoding, historical meeting-minutes screening, technical inspection / engineering report summarization, automated budget benchmarking against similar buildings of the same age and type, insurance policy term evaluation against regional benchmarks, and special-assessment forecasting based on reserve studies and current budget contributions — establishing the canonical scope of the product's automated-reporting layer well beyond the three-category marketing-tier framing surfaced in the freemium-launch press release [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]]. The October 19, 2023 launch of Eli Report's Special Levy Forecast tool — a year preceding the October 2024 freemium launch and canonical product page — is the load-bearing earliest primary-source evidence for the feature later catalogued as Section 6 (special-assessment forecasting) of the canonical product taxonomy, with explicit deterministic-methodology positioning ("While our extraction is powered by AI, this feature is not a guess, it's simple math using the best available information"), three named inputs (Depreciation Report/Reserve Study; CRF/Reserve Fund balance; annual budget CRF contributions), per-unit average output framing, and a 15-item enumerated failure-mode taxonomy that the October 2024 product-page surface does not replicate [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]]. The four-tier evidence base — the October 19, 2023 Special Levy Forecast launch press surface plus the October 31, 2024 canonical product page plus the October 7, 2024 freemium-launch press release plus the synthesis's transactional-data-acquisition framing — confirms that Eli Report is operating as both a realtor/buyer-facing transactional tool and a property-management / board-facing capital-planning advisory channel through its Annual Benchmark Report extension, with at least one year of operational history on its special-assessment-forecasting feature before the freemium pivot [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]] [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]] [[sources/web-2024-10-07-033]] [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].

### Special Levy Forecast (October 19, 2023 launch)

- Launched October 19, 2023 on a dedicated product page (elireport.com/products/special-levy-forecast/) and framed by the vendor as "its most powerful tool yet" within the Eli Report platform at that time — establishing the special-assessment-forecasting capability as the company's flagship feature one year before the October 2024 canonical product page consolidated it under Section 6 of the broader six-section feature taxonomy [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]] [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]].
- Audience framing: targeted explicitly at Realtors selling condos, with secondary positioning for condo owners and buyers; the page opens "If you are a Realtor that sells condos, you know all about condo fees" — establishing a Realtor-primary audience for the 2023 feature launch that differs from the broader Realtor/buyer/owner/lawyer/lender/property-manager audience surfaced in the October 2024 freemium-launch press release [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]] [[sources/web-2024-10-07-033]].
- Explicit deterministic-methodology positioning: "While our extraction is powered by AI, this feature is not a guess, it's simple math using the best available information, displayed in a way that anyone can understand" — a load-bearing self-characterization that the special-levy-forecast feature is deterministic point-estimate forecasting layered on top of AI extraction, not probabilistic forecasting [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]].
- Output: projects the timing, amount, and primary expenditure for special levies, shown on a dollar-per-unit basis (average) [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]].
- Per-unit-average disclaimer: the average presented is based on the number of units, not a specific unit or entitlement — so penthouse owners will face relatively higher levies and studio owners will face relatively lower levies than the per-unit average [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]].
- Vendor framing of value to Realtors: enables clients to estimate the amount they may be asked to pay in a given year above-and-beyond the condo fees, and conveys the "likely best-case scenario as far as financial exposure to maintaining the health of common property" — with the explicit caveat "In life, things tend to cost more and take longer than expected" [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]].
- Three named inputs to the forecast model (the load-bearing methodology disclosure): (1) **Depreciation Report / Reserve Study** — the document written by a professional engineer that should be updated at least every 3-5 years (with various regions having different expectations); the projected expenditures estimated by the professional engineering company are inflation-adjusted on their assumptions; (2) **CRF / Reserve Fund balance** — provided via financial statements or information certificate (Form B in BC; status certificate in Ontario); if unavailable, the system assumes the CRF balance is $0 and overstates the amount of the first special levy accordingly; (3) **Annual budget CRF contributions** — current annual budget CRF contributions are used as the predictor of future CRF contributions, rather than depreciation-report funding-scenario contributions, on the explicit vendor view that current contributions are a more reasonable predictor [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]].
- Regional terminology surfaced in the methodology: "Form B in BC" (the BC Strata Property Act information certificate) and "status certificate in Ontario" (the Ontario Condominium Act 1998 disclosure document) are both named as the operational sources for the CRF/Reserve Fund balance input — confirming Eli Report's special-levy-forecast tool operates across both BC SPA and Ontario Condo Act 1998 regimes from inception [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]].
- Vendor liability disclaimer: "We do not provide any advice or opinions, so what is shown in the special levy forecast table is derived from the data made available to us" — an explicit no-advice-no-opinions framing that positions the tool as a data-presentation layer rather than a professional-services deliverable [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]].
- Vendor caveat to Realtors: "while this tool is designed to give you and your clients at least a good sense of exposure to special levies, it is important that Realtors always read the underlying documents and provide their clients with suitable advice"; the company "assumes no liability for any inaccuracies in the collection, presentation or display of data" [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]].
- Vendor-enumerated failure-mode taxonomy (15 named reasons the forecast could be inaccurate): (1) being supplied with an outdated depreciation report, or only one depreciation report when there are more (sectioned stratas, amenity buildings, etc.); (2) capital replacement not happening on the schedule prepared by the engineering company in the latest available depreciation report; (3) inflation meaning costs are higher or lower than estimated by the engineering company; (4) a strata choosing to increase or decrease the rate of CRF contributions; (5) the latest budget CRF contributions not being reflective of a typical budget year; (6) the latest CRF balance excluding funds set aside for capital projects (with vendor option to display forecast with total reserve funds); (7) a strata earning more or less on their CRF balances than assumed by the model; (8) a larger or smaller special levy being raised by the strata, changing future-period funds; (9) bad data from extraction, or human error from a failure to accurately review that data, especially when it comes to tables that may not have inflated costs or that exclude taxes and/or contingency; (10) sectioned stratas having more than one depreciation report or budget, causing a partial picture; (11) natural disaster, catastrophe, act of terrorism or otherwise that accelerates near-term capital expenditures; (12) inaccurate building information (such as number of units) causing under-or-over-stated per-unit levies; (13) the per-unit average being based on unit count rather than entitlement (penthouse / studio variance); plus aggregated framing across (14) bad-data risks and (15) model-assumption risks [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]].
- The 15-item failure-mode taxonomy is materially more granular than any failure-mode disclosure surfaced on the October 2024 canonical product page or freemium-launch press release — surfacing the October 2023 Special Levy Forecast launch page as the most defensibility-detailed primary-source artifact in the Eli Report evidence base, with explicit acknowledgment of inflation-deviation risk, regime-switching CRF-contribution risk, and natural-disaster acceleration risk that map directly onto failure modes the condo-capital-infra engine's probabilistic methodology is designed to address [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]].
- Structural implication for competitive positioning: the vendor's explicit "simple math, not a guess" framing of its special-levy forecast methodology, combined with the detailed failure-mode taxonomy that acknowledges 15 specific sources of forecast inaccuracy, is the load-bearing primary-source evidence that Eli Report's special-assessment forecasting is deterministic point-estimate forecasting overlaid on AI-extracted inputs — confirming the absence of probabilistic / Monte Carlo / Bayesian methodology in the vendor's flagship 2023 feature and surfacing methodology-tier differentiation as a viable competitive perimeter for an AI-native reserve-study firm operating against the same Canadian-condo special-assessment-forecasting use case [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]].

### Canonical product feature taxonomy (per October 31, 2024 product page)

- The canonical product page enumerates a six-section automated-reporting feature taxonomy that extends well beyond the three-category marketing-tier framing (lifestyle restrictions, budget benchmarks, possible upcoming special assessments) surfaced in the October 7, 2024 freemium-launch press release [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]] [[sources/web-2024-10-07-033]].
- **Section 1 — Decoding Governing Documents and Bylaws**: framed as the "foundational step in property evaluation," the automated review system extracts and categorizes critical lifestyle restrictions across three named sub-categories — Lifestyle Alignment (ensuring the property's governing rules match the buyer's specific requirements); Pet and Rental Policies (instant identification of occupancy and ownership constraints); Operational Rules (clear presentation of noise, parking, and renovation protocols) [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]].
- **Section 2 — Screening of Historical Meeting Minutes**: framed as accessing "the only true record of a community's internal health" through documents that are "often voluminous and difficult to parse manually"; uses intelligent data extraction to identify three named sub-categories — Recurring Maintenance (flagging persistent building issues that may indicate systemic failure); Community Dynamics (identifying resident disputes or litigation that could impact future value); Financial Red Flags (highlighting discussions regarding significant upcoming expenditures or mismanagement) [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]].
- **Section 3 — Technical Summarization of Inspection & Engineering Reports**: framed as translating professional inspection reports that "can be overly technical for the average stakeholder" into a concise summary across three named sub-categories — Known Deficiencies (identifying existing water damage, foundation concerns, or aging mechanical systems); Capital Projects (forecasting major renewals, such as roofing or envelope work, that require significant investment); Risk Mitigation (helping buyers anticipate maintenance costs before they become liabilities) [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]].
- **Section 4 — Comparing Budgets to Other Buildings of the Same Type and Age**: framed as an automated budget analysis providing data-driven comparison against similar buildings of the same age and type; reports three named sub-categories — Management Efficiency (whether the Strata is effectively allocating funds for repairs and maintenance); Financial Stability (identifying discrepancies or funding gaps relative to industry standards); Operational Health (confirming the building is well-maintained and financially sound) [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]].
- **Section 5 — Evaluating Insurance Policy Terms**: framed against the risk that "Inadequate insurance can lead to devastating personal liability for owners"; scrutinizes policy terms and compares them against regional benchmarks across three named sub-categories — Coverage Gap Identification (determining if the property is adequately protected against property damage and liability); Deductible Benchmarking (comparing insurance terms with other communities to assess relative risk); Liability Awareness (ensuring buyers are fully informed of their potential financial exposure) [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]].
- **Section 6 — Forecasting Special Assessments Based on Reserve Studies and Budgets**: framed as a predictive assessment forecast targeting "the most significant financial shock to a new owner"; uses reserve studies and current budget contributions to project across three named sub-categories — Financial Planning (projecting the timing and amount of potential assessments); Negotiation Power (equipping buyers with the data needed to negotiate a fair purchase price based on future costs); Long-term Security (eliminating "unpleasant surprises" through rigorous reserve fund analysis) [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]].
- Section 6 is the consolidated October 2024 canonical surface for the feature originally launched as the standalone Special Levy Forecast tool on October 19, 2023 — the two artifacts together establish a documented one-year operating history for the special-assessment-forecasting capability before its incorporation into the broader six-section product taxonomy [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]] [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]].
- The Section 6 framing — special-assessment forecasting built directly on reserve studies and current budget contributions — is the structural overlap point between Eli Report's transactional/realtor-facing product and the reserve-fund-study advisory channel that an AI-native reserve-study firm operates in, surfacing Eli Report not merely as a document-review tool but as a downstream consumer of reserve-fund-study outputs that re-packages them as buyer-facing predictive assessments [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]].
- The Section 3 framing — extracting capital projects "such as roofing, or envelope work" from inspection and engineering reports — places Eli Report's automated-reporting layer in direct adjacency to two of the six probabilistic components in the condo-capital-infra engine (roof, building envelope), surfacing the vendor as a downstream consumer of the same component-level capital-renewal data the engine produces [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]].

### Data-acquisition model and legal compliance

- Data-acquisition model: authorized users (typically realtors and buyers with legal access during status-certificate / disclosure phase of a unit sale) upload PDFs of Reserve Fund Studies or Board Meeting Minutes; the platform uses AI to extract key data points [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- The October 2023 Special Levy Forecast launch page operationalizes this user-upload data-acquisition model by explicitly framing the upload step as the user action that triggers analysis ("Ready to analyze your condo or HOA documents for special levies?") and by identifying the operative input documents as the depreciation report / reserve study, financial statements, and information certificate (Form B in BC, status certificate in Ontario) — all documents that authorized users would already hold legal access to during the status-certificate / disclosure phase of a unit sale [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]].
- Data extracted from uploaded documents includes: mentions of leaks, litigation, or structural theft in the minutes; the current reserve balance versus the recommended funding levels; benchmarking of the building's budget against similar structures of the same age [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- October 2024 marketing-tier framing of the same extraction layer surfaces three primary report categories: lifestyle restrictions, budget benchmarks, and possible upcoming special assessments — vendor-marketing taxonomy consistent with the synthesis's underlying extraction-feature inventory [[sources/web-2024-10-07-033]].
- The October 31, 2024 canonical product page extends the three-category framing into the full six-section taxonomy (governing-document decoding, meeting-minutes screening, inspection-report summarization, budget benchmarking, insurance-policy evaluation, special-assessment forecasting) — establishing that the vendor's full product scope is materially broader than the freemium-launch press-release surface and that the press-release framing was a marketing-tier abridgment rather than an exhaustive feature inventory [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]] [[sources/web-2024-10-07-033]].
- Access posture: private / transactional — full Reserve Fund Studies, meeting minutes, and bylaws are made available only to the user who uploaded them and their authorized counterparties, in contrast to the Ontario CAO Registry's public-but-administrative-only data surface [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- Legal compliance posture: the upload-based model circumvents the Canadian-HOA-data-scraping prohibition documented in the Ontario CAO Registry's Terms of Use ("users shall not conduct any systematic or automated data collection activities (including without limitation scraping, data mining, data extraction and data harvesting)") by relying on data provided by authorized users who already hold legal access [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].

### Strategic implication for condo-capital-infra GTM

- For an AI-native reserve-study software developer, the legal-compliant path to a high-fidelity Canadian HOA dataset is to partner with property management companies or insurance providers who hold large repositories of these documents under contract — the same transactional / bottom-up model Eli Report operates [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- OctoAI's Vancouver, BC headquarters and the Annual Benchmark Report's positioning for strata councils (the BC SPA terminology) suggest a structural BC / Western Canada operating base — relevant context for the ADR-0004 Canada-first GTM sequence as a precedent for a Canadian-headquartered AI-native condo software company achieving North America freemium scale [[sources/web-2024-10-07-033]].
- The freemium model — free essential tier across North America with paid advanced tier + human review team — is a competitive perimeter signal: Eli Report has commoditized the baseline document-summarization tier while preserving paid revenue through expert review, framing pricing-architecture choices an AI-native reserve-study competitor will face when entering the same market [[sources/web-2024-10-07-033]].
- The canonical product page's Section 6 (special-assessment forecasting based on reserve studies and budgets) and Section 3 (capital-project extraction including roofing and envelope work) confirm that Eli Report consumes reserve-fund-study outputs and inspection-report capital-renewal data as upstream inputs to its buyer-facing automated reporting — surfacing the vendor as a structural downstream beneficiary of the reserve-fund-study production pipeline an AI-native reserve-study firm would feed; this positions Eli Report less as a competitor and more as a candidate data-consumer / channel-partner in the Canada-first GTM sequence under ADR-0004 [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]].
- The full six-section product taxonomy framed against "hundreds of pages of complex strata and condo filings" — explicitly including BC SPA-flavored "strata" terminology alongside generic "condo" framing — confirms Eli Report's product is jurisdiction-agnostic across BC strata and other Canadian condo regimes, structurally aligned with the engine v0.5's jurisdiction-agnostic methodology framing under the Canada-first sequence [[sources/web-2024-10-31-4cf]].
- The October 2023 Special Levy Forecast launch's explicit "simple math, not a guess" deterministic-methodology framing — combined with the 15-item enumerated failure-mode taxonomy that acknowledges specific sources of forecast inaccuracy (inflation deviation, regime-switching CRF-contribution rates, natural-disaster acceleration, bad extraction data) — is the primary-source evidence base supporting methodology-tier differentiation as a viable competitive perimeter for an AI-native reserve-study firm operating against Eli Report's special-assessment-forecasting use case; the failure modes Eli Report acknowledges but does not model are precisely the failure modes the condo-capital-infra engine's probabilistic methodology is designed to handle [[sources/web-2023-10-19-a69]].
