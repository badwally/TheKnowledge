# Evaluating Pavement Deterioration Rates Due to Flooding Events Using Explainable AI

## Summary

A 2025 *Buildings* paper by Lidan Peng, Lu Gao, Feng Hong, and Jingran Sun that combines 20 years of TxDOT Pavement Management Information System (PMIS) pavement condition data with flood event data (duration and spatial extent) and uses explainable-AI techniques (SHAP and LIME) to quantify how flooding accelerates pavement roughness deterioration measured by the International Roughness Index (IRI).

## Key facts

- The paper is authored by Lidan Peng, Lu Gao, Feng Hong, and Jingran Sun [[sources/arxiv-2507.01056]].
- The paper was published in *Buildings* 15.9 (2025): 1452, with DOI 10.3390/buildings15091452 [[sources/arxiv-2507.01056]].
- The corresponding arXiv preprint identifier is 2507.01056, with primary category cs.LG, published 28 June 2025 [[sources/arxiv-2507.01056]].
- The motivating premise is that flooding can damage pavement infrastructure significantly, causing both immediate and long-term structural and functional issues [[sources/arxiv-2507.01056]].
- The study measures pavement roughness using the International Roughness Index (IRI) as its deterioration metric [[sources/arxiv-2507.01056]].
- The authors used 20 years of pavement condition data from the Texas Department of Transportation's PMIS database, integrated with flood event data including duration and spatial extent [[sources/arxiv-2507.01056]].
- Statistical analyses were performed to compare IRI values before and after flooding and to calculate deterioration rates influenced by flood exposure [[sources/arxiv-2507.01056]].
- The authors applied Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques, specifically SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) and Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations (LIME), to assess the impact of flooding on pavement performance [[sources/arxiv-2507.01056]].
- The results demonstrate that flood-affected pavements experience a more rapid increase in roughness compared to non-flooded sections [[sources/arxiv-2507.01056]].
- The authors argue the findings emphasise the need for proactive flood mitigation strategies, including improved drainage systems, flood-resistant materials, and preventative maintenance, to enhance pavement resilience in vulnerable regions [[sources/arxiv-2507.01056]].
