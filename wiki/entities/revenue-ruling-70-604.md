# IRS Revenue Ruling 70-604

## Summary

IRS Revenue Ruling 70-604 is the federal tax ruling that permits a non-exempt membership organization — including a homeowners association or condominium association filing Form 1120 under IRC §277 — to elect to "rollover" excess member assessment income from one tax year to the following year's assessments, thereby avoiding immediate taxation of the excess as net membership income [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]]. The ruling's operative language — "...have the excess applied to the following year's assessments" — uses "year's" in the singular, which the IRS interprets as authorizing a one-year rollover only, not a perpetual rollover scheme [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]]. As of the source article, the IRS Special Industry Group Liaison was taking an increasingly restrictive interpretation; associations and tax practitioners that abuse the rollover — by failing to adhere to ruling language, failing to document the rollover in Schedule M-1, or failing to absorb the rollover in the subsequent year — were having Rev. Rul. 70-604 elections disallowed in IRS audits and being taxed on net membership income that, in many cases, exceeded the tax that would have been owed under Form 1120-H [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].

## Key facts

- A federal IRS revenue ruling that authorizes a non-exempt membership organization to elect to rollover excess member assessment income to the following year's assessments [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Operates under IRC §277 as the mechanism for avoiding tax on excess net membership income in a non-exempt membership organization filing Form 1120 [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Source article characterizes the ruling as continuing to cause enormous problems for associations and CPA practitioners throughout the United States due to its very ambiguous wording, which without careful reading could be interpreted in a very liberal manner [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- The operative language is "...have the excess applied to the following year's assessments"; IRS interprets "year's" as singular, not plural — meaning the rollover is a one-year mechanism, not a perpetual rollover scheme [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- The five required elements of a proper Rev. Rul. 70-604 election: (1) election made by the members, not the board of directors; (2) election documented in writing; (3) election made prior to the filing of the tax return; (4) consideration in the subsequent year of the amount rolled over; (5) "absorption" of the rollover in the subsequent year [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Omission of any of the five required elements destroys the election [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Annual-meeting approval of the election (before year end, or after year end but before the tax return is filed) satisfies the first three required elements; documentation may be in the annual meeting minutes or, preferably, a separate resolution [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- The CPA must document the actual amount of the rollover in Schedule M-1 of the tax return so the calculations can be replicated and the rollover confirmed [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Worked example: if an association rolls over $50,000 from year 2001 to 2002, the association must generate a $50,000 membership loss in 2002, or it will be taxed on any excess carryover from 2001 [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- New IRS Special Industry Group Liaison interpretation: if the rollover is not absorbed in the subsequent year, the unabsorbed portion is taxed in the year of election (not the subsequent year), the election itself is treated as invalidated, and an amended return is required for the original year [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- The IRS interpretation analogizes the rollover to IRC §§1031 and 1034: completing the first part of the transaction (the election) without completing the second part (the absorption) invalidates the rollover treatment ab initio [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- The same IRS personnel making this interpretation will write any future Revenue Rulings or Private Letter Rulings for the HOA industry and will be relied upon as IRS technical experts in any Tax Court petitions, making the interpretation effectively binding on practitioners [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Revenue Rulings 75-370 and 75-371 must be considered in determining net membership income alongside Rev. Rul. 70-604; failure to do so has caused associations in IRS audit to discover net membership income they did not know existed [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Where a 70-604 election is disallowed after audit, the only escape route for the tax preparer is to plead ignorance and use Revenue Ruling 83-74 to amend the returns to Form 1120-H [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Gary Porter, CPA's author posture: Rev. Rul. 70-604 should be used "as little as possible, and with extreme caution in the future" — it is not the universal cure-all that many CPA practitioners previously believed [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Disallowances by IRS are not because Rev. Rul. 70-604 is no longer a viable alternative, but because associations and tax practitioners have abused the ruling — failing to adhere to its language, failing to document the rollover, and failing to consider the subsequent year "recovery" of amounts rolled over [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
