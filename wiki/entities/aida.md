# Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA)

## Summary

The Artificial Intelligence and Data Act (AIDA) was a proposed Canadian federal statute, tabled in the House of Commons in June 2022 as the third act in Bill C-27 (the Digital Charter Implementation Act), that aimed to establish a comprehensive regulatory framework for AI systems across Canada [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]]. AIDA died on the table of the House of Commons Standing Committee on Industry and Technology (INDU) following the early-2025 prorogation of Parliament, leaving Canada without comprehensive national AI legislation [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].

## Key facts

- AIDA was tabled in Parliament in June 2022 as the third of three acts bundled in Bill C-27, the Digital Charter Implementation Act [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].
- AIDA's regulatory framework required developers, providers, and operators of "high-impact" AI systems in Canada to comply with requirements for risk assessment and mitigation, recordkeeping, and disclosure of key system information, or face monetary penalties and criminal offences [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].
- Enforcement was to be carried out by a new federal official titled the "AI and Data Commissioner" [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].
- The INDU Committee began its study of AIDA in April 2023; over the course of that study, 137 witnesses appeared before the committee and 113 briefs were submitted by individuals and organizations [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].
- Critics cited an exclusionary public consultation process, vague scope and requirements, and a lack of independent regulatory oversight as fundamental flaws; records showed AIDA was developed primarily behind closed doors with a selective group of industry representatives [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].
- Submissions opposing AIDA's drafting were filed by, among others, the Canadian Labour Congress, Directors Guild of Canada, Writers Guild of Canada, Screen Composers Guild of Canada, Music Canada, Amnesty International, the Women's Legal Education and Action Fund, the Assembly of First Nations, and the Canadian Union of Public Employees [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].
- The federal government proposed amendments to AIDA in November 2023 that added specificity to the legislation's scope, requirements, and regulatory powers, but the amendments did not address its fundamental flaws [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].
- AIDA languished in INDU committee throughout 2024 and ultimately failed to become law alongside the rest of Bill C-27 after Parliament was prorogued [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].
