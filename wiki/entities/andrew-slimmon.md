# Andrew Slimmon

## Summary

Head of Applied Equity Advisors at Morgan Stanley; author of the Equity chapter "We See A Resilient Economy Without a Looming Collapse" in the December 12, 2022 Morgan Stanley "2023 Investment Outlook," in which he argues the consensus view of an early-2023 earnings collapse is contradicted by sectoral leadership and broader S&P 500 breadth, and uses that diagnosis plus a regulatory-scrutiny argument to scale back megacap exposure in favor of the S&P 500 equal-weighted [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].

## Key facts

- Listed title in the outlook: Head of Applied Equity Advisors [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Argues the consensus that early-2023 earnings will collapse and bring the stock market down is contradicted by sectoral leadership: financials, industrials, and materials all outperformed in October and November 2022 — "if the economy were going to collapse in the first quarter of 2023, these economically cyclical groups would not be leading today" [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Quotes market consultant John Raphael (November 20, 2022): "It's puzzling to me why these brokers and investment banks are acting so well. Would you buy these stocks if the SPX had 10% downside?" [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Reads the gap between S&P 500 equal-weighted (down -11%) and cap-weighted (down -17%) through November 29, 2022 as broader breadth and a bullish signal (Bloomberg) [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Expects earnings to "drip down slowly, frustrating market bears" rather than collapse, with continuing inflation improvements producing the ingredients for a strong first quarter of 2023 [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Reads yield-curve inversion as a fact that "requires attention and respect," but argues yield curves are not very good at predicting when a slowdown occurs, only that one will occur at some point in the future [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Floats — without endorsing as consensus — the possibility of a weaker second half following a surprisingly strong first half in 2023, framed as "weakness in the market — but from higher levels" [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Reports the five largest tech-related stocks comprised roughly 25% of the S&P 500 at their 2022 peak (Apple, Microsoft, Google, Amazon, Tesla) — versus the just-over-20% 2000 peak (Microsoft, Cisco, Nokia, Intel, IBM), whose constituents bottomed five years later at only 5% of the index [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Notes the 30x average valuations of the 2022 megacap five "will never reach the triple-digit valuations of 2000," but argues U.S. government regulatory scrutiny — "the U.S. government's desire to abate their dominance" — has historically stunted the growth of mega S&P 500 stocks [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Notes bolt-on acquisitions historically boosted megasized companies' sales growth at roughly 100/year during the 2010s, but only 22 acquisitions had been completed year-to-date 2022 (per Goldman Sachs US Weekly Kickstart, November 11, 2022) [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Concludes slowing growth rates combined with premium valuations will allow the S&P 500 equal-weighted to continue to outperform the S&P 500 cap-weighted, and is reducing megacap exposure on that basis [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Notes non-U.S. markets began outperforming the U.S. when the dollar peaked in late September 2022, with Asia ex Japan leading since the end of October — outperforming Europe and Japan, which both outperformed the U.S. (FactSet) [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Sees moderating China zero-COVID policies plus a weaker dollar as making China an intriguing equity area for 2023 [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
