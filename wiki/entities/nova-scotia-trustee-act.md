# Nova Scotia Trustee Act (R.S.N.S. 1989, c. 479)

## Summary

The Nova Scotia Trustee Act, R.S.N.S. 1989, c. 479, is the provincial statute consolidating and amending the enactments relating to trustees in Nova Scotia [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]]. The Act governs trustee investment authority, diversification, the prudent-investor standard, delegation, deposit of trust moneys pending investment, beneficiary notice for proposed investments, and trustee appointment and discharge [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]]. The version captured in the archived CanLII text has been in force since July 13, 2007 and reflects amendments by 1992 c. 8 s. 37; 1994-95 c. 19; 2002 c. 10 s. 45; and 2007 c. 17 ss. 22-25 [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]]. The 2002 c. 10 s. 45 amendment is structurally significant: it introduced §§3, 3A, 3B, 3C, 3D, 3E, and 3F, replacing a prior legal-list-style investment regime with a principles-based prudent-investor standard that permits investment in any form of property or security in which a prudent investor might invest, including securities issued by mutual funds [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

## Key facts

### Permitted investment — §3 (2002 c. 10, s. 45)

- §3(1) permits a trustee to invest trust property in any form of property or security in which a prudent investor might invest, including a security issued by a mutual fund as defined in the Securities Act [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3(2) provides that §3(1) does not authorize a trustee to invest in a manner that is inconsistent with the trust [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3(3) enumerates non-exclusive criteria a trustee may consider in planning the investment of trust property: general economic conditions; the possible effect of inflation or deflation; expected tax consequences of investment decisions or strategies; the role each investment or course of action plays within the overall trust portfolio; expected total return from income and the appreciation of capital; other resources of the beneficiaries; needs for liquidity, regularity of income, and preservation or appreciation of capital; and any asset's special relationship or special value to the purposes of the trust or to one or more of the beneficiaries [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

### Prudent investor standard — §3A (2002 c. 10, s. 45)

- §3A requires a trustee, in investing trust property, to exercise the care, skill, diligence, and judgment that a prudent investor would exercise in making investments [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

### Diversification — §3B (2002 c. 10, s. 45)

- §3B requires a trustee to diversify the investment of trust property to an extent that is appropriate having regard to (a) the requirements of the trust and (b) general economic and investment market conditions [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

### Trustee liability and damages — §3C, §3D (2002 c. 10, s. 45)

- §3C provides that a trustee is not liable for a loss to the trust arising from the investment of trust property if the conduct that led to the loss conformed to a plan or strategy for the investment of trust property — comprising reasonable assessments of risk and return — that a prudent investor could adopt under comparable circumstances [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3D permits a court assessing damages payable by a trustee for a loss arising from the investment of trust property to take into account the overall performance of the investments [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

### Advice — §3E (2002 c. 10, s. 45)

- §3E(1) permits a trustee to obtain advice in relation to the investment of trust property [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3E(2) provides that it is not a breach of trust for a trustee to rely upon advice obtained under §3E(1) if a prudent investor would rely upon the advice under comparable circumstances [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

### Delegation — §3F (2002 c. 10, s. 45)

- §3F(1) defines "agent" to include a stockbroker, investment dealer, investment counsel, and any other person to whom investment responsibility is delegated by a trustee [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3F(2) permits a trustee to delegate to an agent the degree of authority with respect to the investment of trust property that a prudent investor might delegate in accordance with ordinary business practice [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3F(3) requires the delegating trustee to exercise prudence in establishing the terms of the authority delegated and in monitoring the performance of the agent to ensure compliance with the terms of the delegation [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3F(4) requires the agent, in performing a delegated function, to exercise reasonable care to comply with the terms of the delegation [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3F(5) provides that a trustee who complies with §3F(3) is not liable to the beneficiaries or to the trust for the decisions or actions of the agent [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3F(6) does not authorize a trustee to delegate authority under circumstances in which the trust requires the trustee to act personally [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3F(7) provides that investment in a security issued by a mutual fund as defined in the Securities Act or in a similar investment is not a delegation of authority with respect to the investment of trust property [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

### Regulations and prohibited investments — §4, §5, §7

- §4 authorizes the Governor in Council to make regulations prescribing or prohibiting the investment of money by a trustee in particular investments, and prescribing investments or classes of investments in which money may be invested by a trustee for the sound and efficient management of a trust [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §5 provides that nothing in §3 or §4 permits a trustee to invest in investments that are expressly forbidden by the instrument, if any, creating the trust [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §7 permits a trustee, in addition to investments authorized by §3 or by the trust instrument and except where that instrument expressly prohibits such investment, to invest funds in such other securities as the Court or a judge upon application in any particular case selects as fit and proper, without relieving the trustee of the duty to take reasonable and proper care [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

### Deposit pending investment — §8, §9

- §8 permits a trustee, pending the investment of any trust moneys, to deposit them during such time as is reasonable in the circumstances in a trust account in the name of the trustee in any chartered bank of Canada, or in any approved trust company, loan corporation, or other like depository which has by order of the Governor in Council been approved as such depository [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §9 requires the trustee to maintain a ledger account in the trustee's books for the particular trust estate, or to require the account in the bank or other depository ledger to be opened and kept in the name of the trustee for the particular trust estate [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

### Effect on trustee authority — §10, §11

- §10(1) provides that the powers conferred by the Act relating to trustee investments are in addition to the powers conferred by the trust instrument [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §10(2) provides that nothing in the Act authorizes a trustee to do anything expressly forbidden, or to omit anything expressly directed, by the trust instrument [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §11(1) permits a trustee in discretion to call in trust funds invested in unauthorized securities and reinvest them in securities authorized by the Act, and to vary any investments authorized by the Act [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §11(2) provides that no trustee shall be liable for any breach of trust by reason only of continuing to hold an investment that has ceased to be authorized by the trust instrument or by the general law [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

### Concurrence in corporate reconstruction or sale — §12

- §12(1) permits a trustee holding securities under the Act to concur in any scheme or arrangement for the reconstruction of the company, winding up or sale or distribution of its assets, sale to another company, amalgamation, or release/modification/variation of rights attaching to the securities, with power to accept replacement securities of the reconstructed or purchasing or new company [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §12(2) provides that the trustee shall not be responsible for any loss occasioned by any act or thing done in good faith under §12(1), and may retain any securities so accepted for any period for which the trustee could have properly retained the original securities [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

### Improper mortgage advance — §13

- §13(1) provides that where a trustee improperly advances trust money on a mortgage security that would at the time of the investment be a proper investment in all respects for a smaller sum than is actually advanced, the security shall be deemed an authorized investment for the smaller sum, and the trustee shall only be liable to make good the sum advanced in excess thereof, with interest [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

### Beneficiary notice — §14

- §14(1) requires the trustee to give written notice of any proposed investment or change of investment to a beneficiary entitled in possession to receive income for life (or for a term determinable with life, or for any greater estate) who has given the trustee written notice of desire to be consulted [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §14(2) requires the notice to contain a request that, if the beneficiary objects, the beneficiary notify at least one trustee in writing within ten days after receipt of the notice [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §14(3) permits the trustee, on receiving notice of objection, to apply to the Court or a judge in a summary manner for leave to make the proposed investment or change [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §14(6) excludes from the notice requirement the release of a mortgage upon payment of the principal by the mortgage debtor, and the temporary deposit on interest of the whole or any part of a fund in any solvent chartered bank of Canada [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

### New trustee — §16

- §16(1) governs appointment of a new trustee when a trustee (original or substituted) is dead, remains out of the Province for more than twelve months, desires to be discharged from all or any of the trusts or powers, refuses, is unfit to act, or is incapable of acting; appointment is by the person nominated by the trust instrument, or, failing such person, by the surviving or continuing trustees (with beneficiary consent in writing) or the personal representatives of the last surviving or continuing trustee [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
