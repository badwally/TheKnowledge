# Quebec Bill 16 (2019, chapter 28)

## Summary

Quebec Bill 16 (2019, chapter 28) — "An Act mainly to regulate building inspections and divided co-ownership, to replace the name and improve the rules of operation of the Régie du logement and to amend the Act respecting the Société d'habitation du Québec and various legislative provisions concerning municipal affairs" — is the omnibus Quebec statute enacted by the 42nd Legislature, introduced 3 April 2019, passed in principle 16 May 2019, passed 5 December 2019, and assented to 11 December 2019 [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]]. For the condo-capital-infra Canada-first GTM sequence under ADR-0004, Bill 16 is the load-bearing in-scope regulatory source for the QC regime (Civil Code art. 1071 / Bill 16): it amends the Civil Code of Québec to subject syndicates of co-owners of immovables under divided co-ownership to additional obligations, including (i) keeping a maintenance log ("carnet d'entretien") for each immovable and (ii) obtaining a contingency fund study ("étude de fonds de prévoyance") that establishes the sums necessary for the fund to be sufficient to cover major repairs and the replacement of common portions, with the co-owners' annual contribution to the contingency fund determined on the basis of such a study [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]] [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]]. Per the condo-capital-infra synthesis the operational cadence is every 5 years, the legislation emphasizes a 25-year planning horizon, and annual contributions must be based on realistic professional assessments of the building's actual condition rather than arbitrary percentages of the operating budget [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]]. Bill 16 sits alongside the BC Strata Property Act, ON Condominium Act 1998, NB Condominium Property Act c16.05, NL Condominium Act 2009 + NLR 80/11, California Davis-Stirling § 5550, and Florida SIRS regime in the cross-jurisdictional reserve-study statutory comparator set [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

### Civil Code of Québec — divided co-ownership and contingency fund study (Bill 16 explanatory notes)

- The Civil Code is amended with respect to immovables under divided co-ownership in order to make syndicates of co-owners subject to certain additional obligations [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Each syndicate of co-owners must keep a maintenance log ("carnet d'entretien") for each immovable — the Quebec statutory analog to BC Housing's Maintenance Matters #11 "Maintenance and Renewals Plan" and the operational maintenance-records anchor for the contingency fund study [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]] [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- Each syndicate of co-owners must obtain a contingency fund study ("étude de fonds de prévoyance") that establishes the sums necessary for the fund to be sufficient to cover major repairs and the replacement of common portions [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]] [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- The co-owners' annual contribution to the contingency fund must be determined on the basis of such a contingency fund study — the Quebec analog to the BC SPA depreciation-report-driven contingency-reserve-fund regime and the Ontario Condo Act 1998 reserve-fund-study-driven common-expenses contributions regime [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Each syndicate must provide certain information on the immovable and the syndicate to a promisor who promises to buy a fraction of an immovable under divided co-ownership; the Government is given the power to determine information other than that required under the Civil Code that must be included in the memorandum or in the preliminary contract [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- A builder or developer must protect the deposits paid by buyers of a fraction of an immovable under co-ownership [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- A developer who has underestimated amounts in a budget forecast must pay certain amounts to the syndicate — a Quebec-specific developer-liability provision absent from the parallel Atlantic-Canada regime in NB Condominium Property Act c16.05 (CMHC NB fact sheet notes NB "legislation does not include rules governing the financial consequences of a developer inaccurately estimating the operational costs of a condominium") [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]] [[sources/web-2018-03-31-c63]].
- Various measures are introduced concerning the operation of co-ownerships, and certain rules governing general meetings of the co-owners are amended [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- The rules governing co-owners' contributions for common portions for restricted use are clarified; Civil Code terminology with regard to common expenses is standardized and certain interpretation difficulties are resolved [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

### Operational cadence, planning horizon, and contribution methodology (synthesis)

- The condo-capital-infra synthesis surfaces an operational contingency-fund-study cadence of every 5 years for Quebec syndicates under the Bill 16 / Civil Code regime, a cadence not surfaced in the Bill 16 explanatory notes text itself and set in the Civil Code article-level text and subordinate Quebec regulations [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- The legislation emphasizes a 25-year planning horizon — the shortest minimum forward-forecast horizon in the cross-jurisdictional Canadian comparator set (vs. BC SPA's 30 years, ON Condo Act 1998's 30 years, NB CPA c16.05's 30 years, NL NLR 80/11's 30 years, and NS Act's 30 years per the synthesis) [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- The legislation requires that annual contributions be based on realistic professional assessments of the building's actual condition rather than arbitrary percentages of the operating budget — making the contingency-fund-study output the binding determinant of annual contribution levels [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- Bill 16 introduces the "co-ownership certificate" required for unit sales as a legal passport for the property — detailing the reserve fund balance and planned repairs, and effectively linking building health directly to market liquidity [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].

### Building Act amendments (Chapter I, sections 1-15)

- The Building Act (chapter B-1.1) is amended to grant the Régie du bâtiment du Québec (the Board) new regulatory powers authorizing it to, among other things, regulate building inspections [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- The Board is empowered to order the suspension of construction work where the person carrying out the work or having it carried out does not hold the appropriate licence; a proceeding to contest such an order is to be heard and decided by preference by the Administrative Labour Tribunal [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- New grounds are introduced that allow the Board to render an unfavourable decision for any type of authorization within its purview [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Section 9.1 is inserted into the Building Act: "For the purposes of this Act, a syndicate of co-owners is considered an owner with respect to the responsibilities conferred on the syndicate under the Civil Code" — the statutory bridge that propagates Civil Code syndicate obligations into the Building Act enforcement perimeter [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Section 17.4 is inserted, authorizing the Board to require by regulation that a contractor or owner-builder obtain plans and specifications before construction work begins or final signed plans and specifications when the work is completed; such plans must be prepared by a person or body recognized by the Board [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Section 18 is replaced: a recognized person or body that prepares plans and specifications for construction work must ensure that they comply with the Construction Code (chapter B-1.1, r. 2) and, if applicable, the construction standards set by a municipality [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Section 124.1 is inserted: the Board may order the suspension of construction work where the person carrying out the work does not hold a licence or where the licence is not of the appropriate class or subclass; the work may not resume until the Board so authorizes [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

### Building inspector certification regime (Chapter V.1, sections 86.8-86.14)

- A new Chapter V.1 ("Building Inspection") is inserted into the Building Act after section 86.7, codifying a building-inspector certification regime administered by the Board [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Section 86.8: a natural person must, in the cases and on the terms and conditions determined by regulation of the Board, obtain from the Board a certificate to act as a building inspector; the section does not apply to a person who inspects a building under statutory powers of verification, inspection, supervision, control or inquiry, or to a category of persons prescribed by regulation [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Section 86.9: no person may give cause to believe that he holds a certificate authorizing him to act as a building inspector if he does not hold one [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Section 86.10: the Board must, by regulation, determine the terms and conditions for the issue, amendment or renewal of a certificate, its period of validity, and the standards, terms and conditions its holder must comply with [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Section 86.11 codifies nine grounds on which the Board may refuse to issue, amend or renew a certificate, or may suspend or cancel a certificate, including non-compliance with terms or conditions, submission of falsified facts or misrepresentation, non-compliance with orders, conflict of interest, failure to pay sums payable to the Board, conviction of an offence under the Building Act or the Consumer Protection Act, conviction of fiscal-law or indictable offences related to the building-inspector activities, foreign equivalent convictions, and failure to provide what is needed to carry out a verification or inspection — with an additional five-year post-imprisonment bar for sentences of imprisonment unless a pardon has been obtained, and a public-interest test for issuing or maintaining a certificate based on good moral character and capacity to exercise the activities with competence and integrity [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Section 86.12: the Board may recognize persons or bodies for the purpose of certifying building inspectors, and may by regulation determine the terms and conditions for such recognition [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Section 86.13: the Board must keep a public register in which the names and contact information of the certificate holders and their certificate numbers are entered [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Section 86.14: the Board may, by regulation, establish a public register of the main problems observed by certified building inspectors in the performance of their duties — a Quebec-specific public-aggregated-defect-data instrument that has no direct analog in the BC SPA, ON Condo Act 1998, NB CPA c16.05, NL NLR 80/11, California Davis-Stirling § 5550, or Florida SIRS regimes [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

### Régie du logement (renamed Administrative Housing Tribunal)

- The Act respecting the Régie du logement is amended to rename the Régie du logement the Administrative Housing Tribunal; other amendments include changes to certain procedural rules and the introduction of new rules to govern conciliation [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

### Société d'habitation du Québec and municipal-affairs provisions

- The Act respecting the Société d'habitation du Québec is amended to allow the provisional administration of any housing agency that receives financial assistance from it; municipalities are granted the power to contribute to housing projects carried out outside their territory and supported by the Société [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- The Act also amends various provisions concerning municipal affairs, specifies the scope of certain rules governing the awarding of contracts, makes certain amendments regarding property assessment and taxation, allows municipalities to assist seniors' residences, confirms that the Régime de retraite des policiers et policières de la Ville de Montréal and the Régime de rentes de l'Association de Bienfaisance et de Retraite de la Police de Montréal are subject to the Act to foster the financial health and sustainability of municipal defined benefit pension plans, allows Ville de Laval to replace its zoning and subdivision by-laws within two years after this Act is assented to, and clarifies the period of application of an interim control by-law adopted following the adoption of a regional wetlands and bodies of water plan [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

### Legislation amended

- Bill 16 amends fifteen statutes: Civil Code of Québec; Building Act (chapter B-1.1); Act to affirm the collective nature of water resources and to promote better governance of water and associated environments (chapter C-6.2); Cities and Towns Act (chapter C-19); Municipal Code of Québec (chapter C-27.1); Act respecting the Commission municipale (chapter C-35); Municipal Powers Act (chapter C-47.1); Act respecting municipal taxation (chapter F-2.1); Act respecting the Régie du logement (chapter R-8.1); Act to foster the financial health and sustainability of municipal defined benefit pension plans (chapter S-2.1.1); Act respecting the Société d'habitation du Québec (chapter S-8); Act respecting public transit authorities (chapter S-30.01); Act respecting Northern villages and the Kativik Regional Government (chapter V-6.1); Act mainly to improve the regulation of the financial sector, the protection of deposits of money and the operation of financial institutions (2018, chapter 23) [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Bill 16 also amends three regulations: Regulation respecting the procedure for the recruitment and selection of persons apt for appointment as commissioners to the Régie du logement and for the renewal of their term of office (chapter R-8.1, r. 4); Tariff of costs exigible by the Régie du logement (chapter R-8.1, r. 6); By-law respecting the allocation of dwellings in low rental housing (chapter S-8, r. 1) [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

### Implication for condo-capital-infra Canada-first GTM

- Bill 16 makes Quebec the third Canadian jurisdiction (after BC and ON, alongside the Atlantic cluster NB / NL / NS) to impose a statutory contingency-fund / reserve-fund-study regime grounded in a forward-looking funding plan for major repairs and replacement of common portions — establishing the regulatory floor for any Year-2+ Quebec expansion beyond the ADR-0004 NS → Atlantic Canada → Ontario → US sequence [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- The maintenance-log ("carnet d'entretien") requirement is the Quebec statutory analog to BC Housing's Maintenance Matters #11 "Maintenance and Renewals Plan" and codifies a per-immovable maintenance-records artifact directly usable as a work-order ingest covariate in the condo-capital-infra engine [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]] [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- The Bill 16 "co-ownership certificate" sale-disclosure passport creates a Quebec-specific transactional touchpoint (analogous to BC SPA's Form B Information Certificate and NB CPA c16.05's estoppel certificate) through which an AI-native reserve-study product can be surfaced to prospective buyers and their lenders at the point of unit-sale liquidity [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- The developer-liability-for-budget-forecast-underestimation provision is materially stricter than the NB regime (where CMHC's NB fact sheet documents that NB legislation does not include rules governing the financial consequences of a developer inaccurately estimating operational costs) and creates a Quebec-specific litigation-risk channel that an AI-native reserve-study firm can position against in budget-forecast-defensibility services [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]] [[sources/web-2018-03-31-c63]].
- The Bill 16 building-inspector certification regime (sections 86.8-86.14) and the Régie du bâtiment du Québec's section 86.14 public register of building-inspector-observed defects together create a Quebec public-data surface for defect-prevalence priors that does not exist in any other Canadian or US comparator jurisdiction in the project comparator set [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
