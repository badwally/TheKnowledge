# Henk-Jan Rikkerink

## Summary

Global Head of Solutions and Multi Asset at Fidelity International; author of the Multi asset chapter of the firm's Investment Outlook 2023, in which he argues defensive positioning, government bonds, and less-correlated exposures will be central to navigating a hard landing in 2023 [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].

## Key facts

- Listed as Global Head of Solutions and Multi Asset at Fidelity International on the Investment Outlook 2023 Multi asset chapter [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues defensive positioning will remain important for investors going into 2023, with cash and uncorrelated assets forming a key component of multi-asset portfolios until volatility subsides [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues government bonds are likely to have a role to play, "especially now that yields are much more attractive" — while structural tailwinds that drove the 40-year bond rally may have diminished, government bonds remain the go-to asset for portfolio diversification in a recession [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues the deteriorating environment is not yet reflected in earnings forecasts or valuations, implying further downside to come for equities [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Reports earnings estimates for 2022 have gradually declined as the year has progressed but estimates for 2023 have "barely budged," which he calls an anomaly given the challenging outlook [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Reports valuations have fallen somewhat with pockets of real value emerging, but many markets are "a long way from what we might call 'cheap' compared to history" [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Identifies signs that hard US data is moderating as a potential harbinger of the much-feted Fed pivot — when it arrives, it should boost risky assets such as equities and credit, as well as government bonds [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Cites the Atlanta Fed wage growth tracker and the headline US job-market data to argue the US labour market and wages have peaked but remain strong [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues the structural theme of deglobalisation and re-shoring will continue in 2023, with the gradual bifurcation of China and the West [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues the reconfiguring of supply chains will provide opportunities for other countries — especially Mexico and Canada, but also some Latin American economies and Thailand and Vietnam [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues less-correlated exposures will be an important part of the investment arsenal, and that the first rule of multi-asset investment is to understand the difference between asset behaviour over the long and the short term [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Maintains a long-term view that "over a longer time horizon, we see no reason for undue pessimism" — economies will survive [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
