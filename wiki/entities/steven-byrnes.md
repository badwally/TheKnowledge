# Steven Byrnes

## Summary

Steven Byrnes is an AI researcher whose motto "the brain is a machine that runs an algorithm" frames much of the contemporary brain-ANN convergence discourse, and whose somewhat iconoclastic reading of neuroscience holds that more than 90% of the brain by volume — including the cortex, striatum, cerebellum, amygdala, nucleus accumbens, and hippocampus — exists solely to run learning-from-scratch algorithms initialized in a way analogous to artificial neural networks [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].

## Key facts

- Coined the motto "the brain is a machine that runs an algorithm," meaning the brain and its components operate compatibly with the laws of physics and chemistry, and a minority of what the brain does is hardwired by evolution while the rest is dedicated to general learning algorithms [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- Argues that "much of the brain (>90% by volume) exists solely to run learning-from-scratch algorithms — namely, roughly the cortex, striatum, and cerebellum, but defined broadly so as to also include the amygdala, nucleus accumbens, hippocampus, and more" [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- Defines "learning-from-scratch" as end-to-end learning subsystems initialized in a way analogous to the initialization of an artificial neural network — not a "blank slate," since genetic hardcoding still influences architectures, algorithms, and hyperparameters [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- Holds that nature and nurture are tightly coupled: a handful of "controlling subsystems" in the brainstem and elsewhere come equipped with impressive functionality at birth, while the bulk of competence is learned [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- Cited by Hammond as the primary expositor of the case that the brain implements general learning algorithms — the prerequisite for the brain-ANN representational-convergence argument [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
