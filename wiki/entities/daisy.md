# Daisy

## Summary

Daisy is an AI-native property management company serving condos, co-ops, and HOAs, founded with the thesis that the property management industry is fundamentally broken — characterized by outdated systems, manual processes, and poor customer-service orientation — and that connecting all building stakeholders on one platform with streamlined workflows and full transparency can fundamentally change how buildings are managed [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]]. As of September 2025, Daisy manages over 4,000 units across New York City with expansion underway into New Jersey, and self-describes as "the fastest-growing AI-native property management company" [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]]. The company's stated expansion path is 10 additional U.S. states in 2027, national coverage by the end of 2028, and global expansion by 2030 [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].

## Key facts

### Founding and positioning

- Co-founded by Yotam Cohen, who serves as CEO [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Self-describes as "the fastest-growing AI-native property management company" [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Built as a technology-first company from day one, with the explicit positioning of having "rethought property management from the ground up" rather than adding technology to legacy processes [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Marketing framing: "Think of Daisy like ordering a car on Uber: you see what's happening in real time, and everything behind the scenes just works" [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Founding thesis articulated by Cohen: management companies were using outdated systems, processes were very manual, and providers weren't orienting service around customer satisfaction [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].

### Customer scale (as of September 2025)

- Manages over 4,000 units across New York City [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Active in New York and New Jersey at time of publication, with expansion underway into Long Island, NJ, and Yonkers [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Recently onboarded two of the largest buildings the company had worked with: one with over 420 units and another with more than 270 units [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].

### Product surface

- Core platform serves multiple stakeholder roles via dedicated connected apps: board members, residents, supers, vendors, accountants, and brokers [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Board-member experience: a dashboard showing finances, operations, and compliance with the ability to take action instantly [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Resident experience: the Daisy Neighbors app for paying charges, tracking packages, and connecting with community [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Built a dedicated on-site-teams platform in approximately one month after deciding to build rather than adopt the incumbent software used by two large-building onboardings (420+ unit and 270+ unit buildings) [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].

### AI agents and operations

- Operates AI agents framed as teammates rather than chatbots, with full ownership of repeatable workflows [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- "Steven" AI agent handles all communication with the prior management company when Daisy takes over management of a new building — chasing down documents, following up on missing items, and escalating to humans at decision points [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- "Lily" support agent handles 22% of all incoming requests [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- AI powers core operations including routing tasks by urgency, flagging issues early, and automating compliance [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Platform upgrades over the year preceding the September 2025 interview reduced resolution times by over 30% [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Cohen articulates that the AI-agent posture is reshaping the org structure: "Instead of throwing more people at problems, we're designing roles around what humans do best — and giving agents full ownership of repeatable workflows. That's allowed us to scale faster without sacrificing service" [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].

### NYC Local Law 97/84 customer success

- Conducted a full audit of Local Law 84 filings for a building it took over on the Upper West Side and discovered that the prior management company had submitted inaccurate energy/water usage estimates [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Based on those erroneous LL84 reports, NYC had projected approximately $58,000 in penalties under Local Law 97 [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- By correcting the data and resubmitting accurate reports, Daisy reduced the building's projected LL97 penalties to zero [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].

### Differentiation

- Operates a specialized-team CAM model rather than the conventional one-property-manager-handles-everything model: a dedicated resident-request team, an operations team that supports supers and on-site staff, and routing systems that ensure work goes to the right specialist [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Frames differentiation as the combination of human care + smart automation, transparency + efficiency [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].

### Market sizing and expansion plan

- Cohen's stated NYC market sizing: approximately 14,400 condo and co-op buildings in NYC alone [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Cohen's stated national market sizing: more than 40 million U.S. households in HOAs across 365,000 communities; ~73% professionally managed by ~18,000 management companies; the majority of those management companies are small mom-and-pop operations [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Cohen's stated industry inefficiency framing: 70% of CAM company revenue goes to labor performing manual tasks that technology could streamline; less than 1% is invested in technology itself [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Cohen's stated U.S. CAM TAM: $130 billion [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Stated expansion path: Long Island, New Jersey, and Yonkers as the first surrounding-area expansion beyond NYC; 10 additional U.S. states in 2027; national coverage by the end of 2028; global expansion by 2030 [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Cohen identifies HOA communities as "a massive opportunity" beyond the initial NYC condo/co-op focus [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].

### Financing

- Cohen does not publicly share revenue figures but states the company has experienced strong year-over-year growth since launching and secured funding from leading investors [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Stated capital uses: expanding the technology team, growing service coverage, and investing heavily in product development [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
