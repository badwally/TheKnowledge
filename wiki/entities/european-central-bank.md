# European Central Bank

## Summary

Eurozone central bank; per ING's December 2022 outlook, expected to lift its deposit rate to 2% in December 2022 — a level some Governing Council members consider the neutral rate — and to deliver another 50 bp of tightening in Q1 2023 alongside the start of a gradual, very-slow-paced balance-sheet reduction [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].

## Key facts

- ING expected the ECB to lift the deposit rate to 2% in December 2022 [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Some members of the ECB Governing Council consider 2% to be the neutral rate, per ING [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- ING expected another 50 bp of tightening in Q1 2023, alongside the start of gradual balance-sheet reduction at "a very slow pace in the beginning" [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Eurozone headline inflation fell back to 10% in November 2022 while underlying inflation remained stuck at 5%, per ING's reading [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Per ING, once interest rates enter restrictive territory in early 2023, the ECB will be forced to stop hiking earlier than it might like, given the looming loss of economic wealth and a large need for investment [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Per ING, an alternative risk is that the ECB "commits a policy mistake" if it hikes rates far beyond mildly restrictive levels [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- ING and JPM AM (M-am 2023 Investment Outlook) both treat the ECB as part of a Fed/ECB/BoE peer group whose paths to peak rates differ — the ECB peaking earlier than the BoE per JPM AM [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
