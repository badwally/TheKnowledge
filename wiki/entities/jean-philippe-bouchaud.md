# Jean-Philippe Bouchaud

## Summary

Quantitative-finance researcher at Capital Fund Management with academic affiliations at Ecole polytechnique and the France Académie des Sciences; co-author of the 2014 paper "Two centuries of trend following," which establishes a highly statistically significant trend-following anomaly across commodities, currencies, stock indices, and bonds using futures since 1960 and spot proxies back to 1800 [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]], and co-author of the 2024 paper "Ponzi Funds," which applies microstructure price-impact models to active-fund return decomposition [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].

## Key facts

- Affiliated with Capital Fund Management on both papers [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]] [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]], with Ecole polytechnique and the France Académie des Sciences listed alongside CFM on the 2024 paper [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- Co-authored "Two centuries of trend following" (April 15, 2014) with Y. Lempérière, C. Deremble, P. Seager, and M. Potters at Capital Fund Management's Paris office, 23 rue de l'Université, 75007 Paris, France [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- The 2014 paper reports an overall t-stat of ≈5 for trend-following excess returns since 1960 across futures markets and ≈10 since 1800 using spot proxies on commodities and indices, after accounting for the upward drift of these markets [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- The 2014 paper concludes that the existence of trends is "one of the most statistically significant anomalies in financial markets," stable across time and asset classes [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- The 2014 paper finds the trend-following signal saturates non-linearly for large signal values, suggesting fundamentalist traders do not attempt to resist weak trends but step in when their own signal becomes strong enough [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- The 2014 paper finds no sign of statistical degradation of long trends in the recent period, whereas shorter trends have "significantly withered" [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- The 2014 paper argues the excess return of trends cannot be classified as a risk premium and that, on the contrary, trend following is correlated with "long-vol" strategies [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- Co-authored "Ponzi Funds" (May 22, 2024; arXiv:2405.12768) with Philippe van der Beck and Dario Villamaina [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- The 2024 paper situates its empirical price-impact estimates in the higher-frequency literature including Bouchaud et al. (2018) and finds that a square-root specification with demand shocks scaled by daily volatility strongly dominates linear specifications at a daily frequency [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- The 2024 paper extends Brunnermeier and Pedersen (2009) by showing that the flow-performance relationship causes endogenous price spirals when future fund flows chase price pressure [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
