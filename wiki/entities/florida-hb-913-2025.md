# Florida CS/CS/HB 913 (2025) — Condominium Associations

## Summary

CS/CS/HB 913 (2025) is the Florida House of Representatives bill titled "Condominium Associations," sponsored by Rep. V. Lopez, with companion bill CS/CS/SB 1742 (Bradley); it materially rewrites Florida's Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) and milestone-inspection regime by adding local-government confirmation and reporting duties, requiring local ordinances on repair commencement, expanding alternative funding methods, permitting reserve-account pooling for SIRS components, codifying SIRS conflict-of-interest restrictions, raising thresholds, requiring DBPR rulemaking on useful-life criteria and a standard SIRS form, and providing boards with new special-assessment and loan authority [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]]. The bill is the operative 2025 statutory update layered on top of SB-4D (2022) and is named alongside SB-4D in the condo-capital-infra synthesis as the regulatory engine producing the engagement's market-window thesis [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]] [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]]. The Section 7 SIRS-form rulemaking mandate is layered on top of the current operative reporting instrument, the DBPR SIRS Reporting Form (Revised 9/2024), which functions as the pre-rulemaking baseline that the coordinated DBPR / Florida Building Commission standardized form is required to supersede [[sources/pdf-b922a12f47f0]].

## Key facts

### Bill identification and procedural posture

- Bill number: CS/CS/HB 913; title: Condominium Associations; sponsor: Lopez, V.; companion bill: CS/CS/SB 1742 (Bradley); linked bills and related bills: None [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- Committee references and votes: Housing 18 Y, 0 N, As CS; Agriculture & Tourism Commerce 23 Y, 0 N, As CS [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The bill has an indeterminate effect on state government and the private sector [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- Storage name h0913c.COM; bill analysis dated April 17, 2025 [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Section 1 — Confirmation and reporting by local governments

- Each local government that has authorized and permitted construction of a building three habitable stories or more in height subject to the condominium form of ownership as a residential condominium must confirm with each condominium association whether the SIRS and milestone inspection have been completed and filed with the Division of Florida Condominiums, Timeshares, and Mobile Homes, or whether the condominium has failed to complete and file the requisite SIRS or milestone inspection report [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- Each local government must report its findings to the Division by December 31, 2025 [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Section 2 — Local ordinances and electronic reporting to DBPR

- Counties and municipalities must adopt an ordinance requiring a condominium association to schedule or commence repairs for substantial structural deterioration within a specified timeframe after the local enforcement agency receives a phase two inspection report; the bill specifies repairs must be commenced within 365 days after receiving the report [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- If the owner fails to submit proof that repairs have been scheduled or commenced within the required timeframe, the local enforcement agency must review and determine whether the building is unsafe for human occupancy [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- On or before October 1, 2025 and on or before December 31 each year thereafter, the local enforcement agency responsible for milestone inspections must provide DBPR (in an electronic format determined by DBPR) the number of buildings required to have a milestone inspection within the agency's jurisdiction; the number for which a phase one milestone inspection has been completed; the number granted an extension of time; the number required to have a phase two milestone inspection; the number for which a phase two milestone inspection has been completed; the number, type, and value of permits applied for to complete phase-two-driven repairs; a list of buildings deemed unsafe or uninhabitable based on a milestone inspection; and the license number of the building code administrator responsible for milestone inspections [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- DBPR must provide all information obtained from the local enforcement agencies to the Office of Program Policy Analysis and Government Accountability (OPPAGA) by a date specified, and in a manner prescribed, by OPPAGA; OPPAGA may request additional information from a local enforcement agency [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Section 3 — Alternative funding method

- Amends the definition of "funding method" under the Florida Condominium Act to mean a method approved by the Division for funding capital expenditures and deferred maintenance obligations for any multicondominium association — removing the prior restriction that limited the alternative funding method to multicondominium associations operating at least 25 condominiums — which may reasonably be expected to fully satisfy the association's reserve funding obligations by allocation of funds in the annual operating budget [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Section 4 — Nonresidential declaration amendments

- A nonresidential condominium declaration formed after July 1, 2025 may be amended to change unit configuration or size in any material fashion, alter or modify appurtenances, or change the proportion or percentage of common-expense share and common-surplus ownership, if the record owners of all affected units and all record owners of liens on the affected units join in the execution of the amendment; approval of the owners of nonaffected units is not required [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Section 5 — Insurance coverage

- Every condominium association must provide adequate property insurance as determined under paragraph (11)(a) of s. 718.111, F.S., regardless of any requirement in the declaration of condominium for different coverage for the association [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The amount of adequate insurance coverage for full insurable value, replacement cost, or similar coverage may be based on the replacement cost of the property to be insured, as determined by an independent insurance appraisal or update of a previous appraisal [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The replacement cost of property covered must be determined every 3 years, at a minimum [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Section 6 — Board meeting transparency and website posting

- An association must post on its website the approved minutes of meetings of the board of administration over the preceding 12 months and copies of all assessments and special assessments for the previous 2 years, along with an itemized list indicating which assessments were related to the milestone inspection report and which were related to the association's most recent SIRS [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The website-posting requirements do not go into effect until January 1, 2025 [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Section 7 — Board meeting question opportunities and SIRS reforms

- At least four times each year, the meeting agenda for an association's board meeting must include an opportunity for members to ask questions of the board, including questions relating to the status of any construction or repair projects, the status of all revenue and expenditures during the current fiscal year, and any other issues affecting the condominium; the association may adopt written reasonable rules governing the frequency, duration, and manner of unit-owner statements and questions [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- Unit owners may vote to use a line of credit in lieu of fully funding reserves annually, provided the unit owners receive clear notice that reserves will not be funded annually, that the association will use credit instead, and the amount of projected future assessments; any line of credit secured by an association and certain related information must be disclosed to subsequent purchasers [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- It is a conflict of interest for any person who performs a SIRS or a milestone inspection to provide services for the repair or replacement of the condominium property that was the subject of such milestone inspection or SIRS, or to have a financial interest in the entity or person providing the repair or replacement services [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The threshold for non-specified items that must be included in the SIRS is increased from $10,000 to $25,000 [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- SIRS is required for condominium buildings that are three habitable stories or higher in height; four-family dwelling units are exempt from the SIRS requirements, similar to such units' exemption from the milestone inspection requirements [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- By October 1, 2025, DBPR must initiate rulemaking to establish criteria for determining the estimated useful life of the building components that are subject to SIRS, and to create — in coordination with the Florida Building Commission — the form to be used for SIRS [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The current operative reporting form pre-dating the Section 7 rulemaking mandate is the DBPR SIRS Reporting Form (Revised 9/2024), authorized under ss. 718.112(2)(g)9 & 10 and 719.106(1)(k)9 & 10, F.S., that codifies a four-category preparer-credential taxonomy (Architect / Engineer / Reserve Specialist / Qualified Person) and per-building data fields including SIRS cost, assessment outcome with per-unit breakdown, completion date, and unit-owner distribution method — the regulatory data-collection baseline the coordinated DBPR / Florida Building Commission standardized form is mandated to supersede [[sources/pdf-b922a12f47f0]].
- The base amount of an expense or replacement cost for a SIRS-related item must be modified annually based on the Consumer Price Index; DBPR must post on its website by February 1 of each year the revised expense or replacement cost [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- A vote of the association's members is not required to create the association's reserve accounts in accordance with the most recent SIRS [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- An association's reserve accounts for two or more required components may be pooled; the reserve funding indicated in the proposed annual budget must be sufficient to ensure that available funds meet or exceed projected expenses for all components in the reserve pool, based on the most recent SIRS [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- A board of an association may pause contributions to its reserves, without obtaining the prior approval of a majority of its members, if a local building official determines that a condominium building is uninhabitable due to a natural emergency (an emergency caused by a natural event including, but not limited to, a hurricane, storm, flood, severe wave action, drought, or earthquake — s. 252.34(8), F.S.) [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- Members of an association may vote to waive the maintenance of reserves recommended by the most recent SIRS if the association votes to terminate the condominium [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- An officer or director of an association must sign an affidavit acknowledging receipt of the completed SIRS [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Section 7 — Recall of board members

- A voting interest of the condominium may not be suspended when voting to recall a member of the board, and any prior suspension of voting rights pursuant to s. 718.303(5), F.S., has no effect on a recall vote [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- A proposed recall agreement must be served on the association by registered mail or in the manner authorized by ch. 48, F.S., and the Florida Rules of Civil Procedure; methods of service not authorized by ch. 48, F.S., and FRCP are invalid and any service that does not comply is void [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- Rejection of a unit owner's recall agreement applies when the recall agreement was improperly served, was executed by a person who was not a unit's record owner or designated voter, was previously marked for the removal of any board member, does not contain markings indicating selection to remove or retain a board member, or does not contain the signature of the unit owner [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- A rebuttable presumption is created that a unit owner executing a recall agreement is the designated voter for the unit; an association may not enforce a voting-certificate requirement if the association has not enforced such requirement in all matters requiring the use of voting certificates in the year immediately preceding service of the recall agreement [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- Rescission or revocation of a unit owner's recall agreement must be in writing and delivered to the association before the association is served with the written recall agreement [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- These provisions must be liberally construed to ensure a unit owner is not disenfranchised by an association in a recall and to prevent an association from failing to certify a recall agreement on a technical omission [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Section 7 — Director/officer delinquencies

- A director or officer is delinquent if a payment is not made by the due date specifically identified in the association's governing documents; in the absence of a specified due date, the due date is the first day of the assessment period [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Section 8 — Hurricane protection

- Unless otherwise provided in an association's declaration, a unit owner is not responsible for the cost of any removal or reinstallation of hurricane protection, including exterior windows, doors, or other apertures, if the removal is necessary for the maintenance, repair, or replacement of other condominium property for which the association is responsible [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The board must determine if removal or reinstallation of hurricane protection must be completed by the unit owner or the association if the declaration does not specify who is responsible [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The association's ability to assess a unit owner if the association removes or reinstalls hurricane protection where the unit owner is responsible is removed [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].

### Board's authority to levy special assessments and obtain loans

- The bill makes the legislative finding that in some circumstances an association's governing documents restrict the authority of its board to levy special assessments or obtain loans without the prior approval of its membership, which may preclude the association from obtaining immediate funding to carry out its obligations [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The bill allows the board of directors of an association to levy special assessments and obtain loans to perform necessary maintenance, as required by the milestone inspection report and SIRS report, without the prior approval of the condominium association's membership [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
- The members of a unit-owner-controlled association may approve, by majority vote, a secured line of credit to meet the reserve funding schedule recommended by the SIRS report [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]].
