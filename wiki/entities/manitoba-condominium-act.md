# Manitoba Condominium Act

## Summary

The Manitoba Condominium Act is the province's primary condominium statute and the reserve-fund-study regime under which Manitoba condominium corporations are required to obtain reserve-fund studies every five years, with a mandatory site inspection at each update [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]]. The minimum forward-forecast planning horizon for the Manitoba reserve-fund study varies by Regulation rather than being set in the Act-level summary [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]]. Manitoba sits alongside Alberta, BC, and Ontario in the central-Canada / western-Canada provincial comparator cluster downstream of the ADR-0004 primary NS → Atlantic Canada → Ontario GTM sequence.

## Key facts

- Primary condominium statute of the Province of Manitoba; cited in the condo-capital-infra synthesis as the regulatory anchor for Manitoba's reserve-fund-study regime [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- Mandatory reserve-fund-study frequency: every 5 years [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- Site inspection requirement: mandatory with each reserve-fund-study update [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- Planning horizon for the reserve-fund-study forward forecast: varies by Regulation (not specified at the Act level in the synthesis) [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- Manitoba authorizes Professional Engineers (P.Eng) and Registered Architects (RA) as authorized preparer categories for reserve-fund studies, consistent with the practice in all Canadian provinces [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- The Manitoba regime is the only province in the Canadian comparator set whose forward-forecast horizon is described as varying by Regulation rather than being set as a uniform Act-level minimum (BC SPA 30 years; ON Condo Act 1998 30 years; AB CPA 30 years; QC Civil Code / Bill 16 25 years; NS Act 30 years per synthesis) [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- Like the BC SPA, AB Condominium Property Act, ON Condo Act 1998, NB CPA c16.05, NL NLR 80/11, NS Condominium Act, and QC Civil Code divided-co-ownership regime, the Manitoba regime requires reserve-fund studies prepared by experts but does not mandate probabilistic methods in the statutory minimum — operational practice remains deterministic point-estimate cash-flow forecasting [[sources/docx-818ed0a0ce55]].
- Prince Edward Island sits at the opposite end of the Canadian provincial comparator spectrum from Manitoba — CMHC's PEI provincial fact sheet confirms that PEI provincial legislation/regulations do not require that all condominiums in Prince Edward Island have a reserve fund, the developer of a new condominium is not obligated to put aside reserves as soon as the condominium is registered, and there are no rules regarding special assessments — making PEI the structural outlier with no mandatory reserve-fund regime alongside MB's mandatory 5-year-study cadence [[sources/web-2018-03-31-9ad]].
