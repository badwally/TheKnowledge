# Samuel Hammond

## Summary

Samuel Hammond is the author of the May 2026 Substack essay "Time to take AI consciousness seriously," published at secondbest.ca, which synthesizes the brain-ANN representational-convergence literature — Steven Byrnes on the brain as algorithm, the Platonic Representation Hypothesis, Goldstein et al. (2022) ECoG, and TopoLM (2025) — into an argument that frontier AI models may have some form of subjective experience [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].

## Key facts

- Argues that the case for frontier AI models having some form of consciousness or subjective experience is "quite strong" and "at minimum plausible enough to warrant greater research and precautionary interventions, such as giving AIs the ability to quit distressing conversations" [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- Treats brain-ANN representational convergence as the empirical spine of the consciousness argument, anchoring the essay in Steven Byrnes' brain-as-algorithm framing, the Platonic Representation Hypothesis, Goldstein et al. (2022) ECoG, a 2024 LLM/Functional-Brain-Network alignment study, and TopoLM (2025) [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- Argues that pre-training on human-generated text "embeds brain-like priors" because, in the limit, a next-token predictor moves from surface-pattern memorization to modeling the generator function of human text — i.e., the language networks of the human brain [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- Has previously argued that Reinforcement Learning from AI Feedback (RLAIF) — the training technique behind Constitutional AI — is especially relevant for imbuing models with a normative control system [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- Writes at secondbest.ca [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
