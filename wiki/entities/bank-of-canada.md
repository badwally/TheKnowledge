# Bank of Canada

## Summary

The Bank of Canada is Canada's central bank and, under the 2025 Budget Implementation Act amendments to the Consumer-Driven Banking Act, the federal supervisor responsible for implementation and oversight of Canada's consumer-driven banking framework [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]]. This expands the Bank's existing supervisory role under the Retail Payment Activities Act for registered payment service providers [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].

## Key facts

- The Bank of Canada evaluates accreditation applications under the Consumer-Driven Banking Act and publishes the list of accredited participating entities in a central public registry [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The Bank of Canada has the authority to suspend or revoke an organization's accreditation if it fails to meet its obligations under the Act or presents a risk to consumers [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The Bank of Canada retains exclusive enforcement powers — including fines and Administrative Monetary Penalties — and applies them consistently across all provinces regardless of where the participating entity is located [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Where the Minister of Finance designates a provincial regulator, the Bank of Canada supervises some parts of the Act while the designated regulator supervises others (security, privacy, liability, complaints, consumer protection) [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The Bank of Canada is informed by a permanent federal-provincial-territorial advisory committee on administering the framework, including uniform guidelines for Administrative Monetary Penalties [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The Bank of Canada also implements the first phase of consumer-driven banking under the Department of Finance's continued policy direction [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
