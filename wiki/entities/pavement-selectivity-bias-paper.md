# Selectivity Bias in Modeling Highway Pavement Maintenance Effectiveness

## Summary

A 1998 *Journal of Infrastructure Systems* paper by Samer Madanat and Rabi Mishalani that presents a structured econometric approach for estimating the effectiveness of highway pavement maintenance and rehabilitation (M&R) activities while correcting for the self-selected nature of the observational sample. The methodology pairs a discrete model of the agency's choice of M&R activity with a set of continuous pavement-response equations — one per activity — and is demonstrated on a case study using Indiana Department of Transportation data.

## Key facts

- The paper is authored by Samer Madanat and Rabi Mishalani [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- It was published in the *Journal of Infrastructure Systems*, vol. 4, no. 3, on 20 September 1998 (print: September 1998), with DOI 10.1061/(ASCE)1076-0342(1998)4:3(134), under copyright of the American Society of Civil Engineers [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- The motivating premise is that engineers selecting an M&R activity for highway pavement sections rely on several items of information, foremost among which is the relative effectiveness of M&R activities in slowing pavement deterioration and improving condition [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- The paper observes that prior researchers quantifying M&R effectiveness have developed separate pavement deterioration models for each type of M&R activity, often obtaining unsatisfactory results — poor fits to data or counterintuitive signs of important variable coefficients [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- The authors attribute these poor results to the sample being self-selected: observations used to analyse a given M&R activity consist mainly of sections for which that activity was believed to be most effective, and so are not representative of the population of highway pavement sections [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- The paper proposes a structured econometric approach consisting of (i) a discrete model describing the choice of M&R activity by the highway agency, and (ii) a set of continuous models representing pavement response, with one equation for each activity [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- The authors argue that this joint model system accounts for the self-selected nature of the sample and consequently obtains consistent model parameter estimates [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- A case study using a data set provided by the Indiana Department of Transportation is presented to demonstrate the applicability and value of the methodology [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- The paper's reference list includes Mannering (1987) on selectivity bias in discrete and continuous choice, Greene (1993) *Econometric Analysis*, Al-Mansour, Sinha and Kuczek (1994) on routine maintenance effects on flexible pavements, Paterson (1987, 1990) on road deterioration and maintenance effects, Ramaswamy and Ben-Akiva (1990) on highway pavement deterioration estimation, Humplick (1986) on highway life-cycle costing, and Madanat, Mishalani and Wan Ibrahim (1995) on infrastructure transition probabilities [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
