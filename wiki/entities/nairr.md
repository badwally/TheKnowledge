# National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR)

## Summary

The National Artificial Intelligence Research Resource (NAIRR) is a U.S. National Science Foundation-led scalable national infrastructure that provides the U.S. research and education communities with access to computing, software, data, models, educational resources, and expertise needed to advance AI innovation [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]]. NAIRR is positioned to bridge a gap that neither the private sector nor government can address alone — making available tools for early, high-risk, high-reward research and building foundational AI capabilities long before they are commercially viable [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].

## Key facts

- NAIRR was initially established as a pilot in 2024 [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
- The pilot supported more than 600 research projects and 6,000 students across all 50 states, Washington, D.C., and Puerto Rico [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
- NAIRR partners with 13 federal agencies and 28 nongovernmental partners [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
- The pilot phase was recognized in the White House *Science and Technology Highlights: Year One* report [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
- Consistent with the White House AI Action Plan, NSF released an opportunity to establish the NAIRR Operations Center to transition NAIRR from a pilot to a sustained national capability [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
- NAIRR's stated goals are: accelerate AI-powered discovery and innovation; expand and train the AI workforce; increase capacity, integration, and use of world-class public and private-sector AI resources; and advance AI interpretability, security, and trust [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
- NAIRR operates through four focus areas: NAIRR for Research (NSF-led), NAIRR Secure (NIH and DOE co-led), NAIRR Data and Models (NSF-led), and NAIRR Classroom (NSF-led) [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
- NAIRR Secure explores the use of privacy/security-preserving infrastructure and secure environments for research areas using high-quality AI-ready controlled-access data [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
- Federal agency partners contributing to NAIRR include DARPA, NASA, NIST, NIH, NOAA, USDA, ED, DOE, VA, DoD, FDA, USGS, and USPTO [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
- NIST provides expertise on best practices for AI governance, risk management, and testing and evaluation for responsible AI [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
- Major industry contributions during the pilot included Microsoft ($20M in Azure compute credits), NVIDIA ($30M total support including $24M of compute on DGX Cloud), AWS (credits for at least 20 research projects), Cerebras (up to 4 EXAFLOPs of AI compute), and EleutherAI (100,000 hours of GPU access to train a scientific foundation model) [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
- Nongovernmental partners include AI2 (Dolma open dataset), AMD, Anthropic, Cerebras, Databricks, Datavant, EleutherAI, Google (Colab, Kaggle, Data Commons), Groq, HPE, Hugging Face, IBM, Intel, Lexset, Meta (Llama), Microsoft, MLCommons, NVIDIA, Omidyar Network, OpenAI, Palantir, SambaNova, and Vocareum [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
- Datavant contributed $2.6M in access to its privacy-preserving record linkage platform and privacy-preserving data discovery tools to support NAIRR Secure NIH and the future NAIRR software stack [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
- Palantir supports NAIRR Secure and the expansion of the National Clinical Cohort Collaborative via Foundry and AIP platforms deployed at NIH [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
- The community-facing portal is nairrpilot.org, hosting computational resources, cloud computing environments, AI-ready datasets, and pre-trained models [[sources/web-2021-07-29-89d]].
