# Credit Union Deposit Guarantee Corporation (CUDGC)

## Summary

The Credit Union Deposit Guarantee Corporation (CUDGC) is the Alberta provincial deposit guarantor and prudential regulator for Alberta credit unions and Alberta Central, operating under the Alberta *Credit Union Act* and backed by the Government of Alberta [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]]. CUDGC provides a comprehensive guarantee of 100% of all deposits — including chequing, savings, foreign currency, and registered and non-registered term deposits of any length — held at Alberta credit unions, with no dollar-amount ceiling [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]]. CUDGC sits structurally alongside CDIC (federal), CUDIC (BC), and FSRA (ON) in the Canadian deposit-protection regime, but is distinguished by the combination of (a) unlimited per-deposit coverage and (b) explicit Government-of-Alberta backstop as the third statutory level of protection [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].

## Key facts

### Deposit guarantee scope

- Backed by the Government of Alberta; the guarantee is described as comprehensive [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- 100% of all deposits held in Alberta credit union chequing, savings, and foreign currency accounts are guaranteed [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Registered and non-registered term deposits of any length are guaranteed [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- The guarantee does NOT cover non-deposit investments — including common shares, investment shares, or self-administered savings plans [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].

### Statutory authority — three-level protection

- The Alberta *Credit Union Act* establishes the legislation providing a three-level deposit protection system [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Level one: credit union capital — each credit union is required to build and maintain adequate levels of capital, comprised primarily of retained earnings, with common and investment shares owned by members also part of capital [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Level two: the Credit Union Deposit Guarantee Fund — Alberta credit unions fund the guarantee through regular assessments; the Fund is managed by CUDGC and invested in high quality, liquid investments [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Level three: the Government of Alberta — the Province ensures the Corporation's obligation to Alberta credit union depositors is carried out [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].

### Regulatory role

- CUDGC regulates Alberta credit unions and Alberta Central, and enforces the Alberta *Credit Union Act* [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Operates a risk-based regulatory approach including activities to review, advise, and direct credit unions on sound business practices [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Oversight practices are designed to assist credit unions to avert or alleviate financial difficulties and stabilize any credit union in financial difficulty [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Establishes credit union loan approval limits and provides adjudication for loans that exceed those limits [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Uses international and national best practices to establish its regulatory oversight framework [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Publishes a comprehensive Standards of Sound Business and Financial Practices and regulatory requirements through guidelines and guidances [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Conducts continuous monitoring and assessment of Alberta credit unions and Alberta Central through on- and off-site activities, including review of capital, liquidity, solvency and overall financial positions; board and management governance practices; enterprise risk management practices; internal control frameworks; and compliance with the Alberta *Credit Union Act* [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].

### Unclaimed balances administration

- The Alberta *Credit Union Act* establishes the requirements for managing inactive deposit accounts transferred to CUDGC as unclaimed balances from Alberta credit unions [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Every January, CUDGC publishes an advertisement in the Edmonton Journal and the Calgary Herald to provide notice that the list of unclaimed balances has been updated on its website [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Inactive account balances exceeding $100 may have been transferred to CUDGC [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Deposit principal and interest balances are transferred to CUDGC from Alberta credit unions once a year [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- CUDGC accrues monthly interest on transferred balances at a rate prescribed by the Alberta Minister of Finance; beneficiaries are entitled to both principal and accrued interest at time of payment [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- All claims are subject to validation [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
