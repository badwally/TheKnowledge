# EU Data Act

## Summary

The EU Data Act — formally the Regulation on harmonised rules on fair access to and use of data — is a cross-sectoral European Union regulation that entered into force on 11 January 2024 and entered into application on 12 September 2025 [[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]]. It is designed to empower users (both consumers and businesses) by giving them greater control over data generated by their connected (Internet of Things) devices, to lay the foundation for a fair and competitive European data economy, and to prohibit unfair contracts that impede data sharing [[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]]. For the data-collectives domain it functions as a reference precedent for statutorily mandated business-to-business data sharing, cloud-provider portability, and public-sector access to privately-held data.

## Key facts

- The Data Act is a Regulation of the European Union on harmonised rules on fair access to and use of data; it entered into force on 11 January 2024 and entered into application on 12 September 2025 [[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]].
- It is a cross-sectoral instrument: it lays out principles and guidelines that apply to all sectors and does not modify existing sectoral data-access obligations, although forthcoming sectoral legislation is expected to align with its principles [[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]].
- It requires connected devices placed on the EU market to be designed so that the data they generate can be shared [[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]].
- It gives individuals and businesses a right to access data produced through their use of smart objects, machines, and devices, and to choose to share that data with third parties such as independent aftermarket service providers (e.g. repair) [[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]].
- It establishes a framework for customers to effectively switch between providers of data-processing services (cloud providers), with the stated aim of unlocking the EU cloud market and improving overall data interoperability [[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]].
- It prohibits unfair contractual terms imposed on enterprises by counterparties wielding considerably stronger market positions, addressing contractual imbalances that impede equitable data sharing [[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]].
- The European Commission is developing non-binding Model Contractual Terms (MCTs) for data access and use and non-binding Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs) for cloud computing contracts to help market participants draft and negotiate fair data-sharing contracts [[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]].
- It enables public-sector bodies to access and use data held by the private sector for specific public-interest purposes — including responding quickly and securely to a public emergency — with minimal burden on businesses [[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]].
- It revisits aspects of the Database Directive, particularly the role of the sui generis database right, and extends that analysis to databases derived from data generated or acquired through IoT devices, with the goal of balancing data-holder and data-user interests [[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]].
- The Commission has published implementation guidance including FAQs, a draft Recommendation on the non-binding MCTs and SCCs, and guidance on vehicle data accompanying the Data Act [[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]].
- Further guidance is planned on reasonable compensation for mandatory business-to-business data sharing under Chapter III of the Data Act [[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]].
- The Commission has launched the Data Act Legal Helpdesk to offer stakeholders concrete guidance on legal questions related to the Data Act [[sources/web-2025-12-15-d5c]].
