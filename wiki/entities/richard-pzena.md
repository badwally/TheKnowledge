# Richard Pzena

## Summary

Value-investing money manager whose long-run benchmark of low-P/E and low-P/Sales returns (1960-2005) Greenblatt cites in class as the empirical foundation for the claim that value investing works in cycles — including a 1995-2000 stretch in which Pzena underperformed the S&P 500 by roughly 91% and lost 70% of his investors before returning to outperformance [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].

## Key facts

- Constructed a value benchmark on Low P/E, Low P/Sales returning 16.3% per year versus the S&P 500's 11% over 1960-2005, a 5.3-percentage-point spread cited by Greenblatt [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- During the 1995-2000 dot-com period the same value benchmark returned 71% versus the S&P 500's 163%, an underperformance of 91% that Greenblatt uses as the canonical cycle illustration [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Lost 70% of his investors during that drawdown; of the $14 billion he managed at the time of the class, only four of his original investors remained — Joel Greenblatt being one of them [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Greenblatt uses Pzena's record to argue that "value investing works because it doesn't work all the time" [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
