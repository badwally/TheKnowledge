# Bernie Gonzalez

## Summary

Bernie Gonzalez is the named author of the March 24, 2026 Kovitz Shifrin Nesbit (KSN) blog post translating Fannie Mae's March 18, 2026 reserve-funding guideline update (Lender Letter LL-2026-03) into board-level implementation guidance for condominium associations [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]]. Gonzalez's KSN-published commentary is the source of record for the quantitative framing of Fannie Mae's gatekeeper role over US condominium mortgages (75% governance share, 80-90% secondary-market sale share, 5-30% market-value-decrease range for unwarrantable-association status) [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]].

## Key facts

- Authored "Fannie Mae Updates Reserve Guidelines for Condominium Associations: Impact to Budgeting, Eligibility, and Property Values," published on the Kovitz Shifrin Nesbit (KSN) Law Firm blog on March 24, 2026 [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]].
- Authored the explainer six days after Fannie Mae's March 18, 2026 Lender Letter LL-2026-03 announcement, positioning the article as among the earliest legal-practitioner translations of the GSE update for community-association boards [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]].
- Categorized the article under KSN's "Community Associations" and "Finances" practice taxonomies, with geographic tags of Wisconsin, Indiana, Illinois, and Florida — the four jurisdictions in which KSN maintains offices [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]].
- Source of record for the 75% FNMA governance share of US condominium mortgages [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]].
- Source of record for the 80-90% secondary-market sale share of US mortgages and the corresponding cascade of unwarrantable-association loans being unsellable on the secondary market [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]].
- Source of record for the 5-30% market-value-decrease range estimate for associations with negative FNMA status ("unwarrantable" or "ineligible" or colloquially "blacklisted") [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]].
- Framed the GSE update as exposing non-compliant boards to potential fiduciary-duty claims arising from failure to plan for foreseeable funding and maintenance needs [[sources/web-2026-03-24-bb6]].
