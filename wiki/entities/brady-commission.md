# Brady Commission

## Summary

The Presidential Task Force on Market Mechanisms, informally known as the Brady Commission, formed after the October 1987 stock market crash to determine why the market had crashed and recommend regulatory changes; its most consequential recommendation was the introduction of circuit breakers in 1988, which Constan argues have been shown to slow down markets during times of stress and make a crash-style deleveraging less likely in the modern era [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].

## Key facts

- Formal name: Presidential Task Force on Market Mechanisms [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Formed in the aftermath of the 1987 stock market crash [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Mission was to determine why the stock market had crashed and recommend regulatory changes that could improve market function [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Andrew Constan, then a corporate finance analyst at Salomon Brothers, was assigned to the Commission soon after the crash [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Recommended the introduction of circuit breakers, which were put in place in 1988 [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Per Constan, "these circuit breakers aren't magic, and they won't keep markets from finding their equilibrium price, but they have been shown to slow down markets during times of stress" [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- The Brady Commission report details the causes of the 1987 crash in depth; Constan recommends it as "worth a read" [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
- Constan argues the circuit breakers are one of the primary reasons a crash-style deleveraging (like 1987's single-day 20%+ drop) is unlikely in the modern era, even if a broad deleveraging occurs [[sources/pdf-f139d7bd1924]].
