# Bank of England

## Summary

UK central bank; per JPM AM's 2023 Investment Outlook, the Bank of England is expected to take longer to reach a peak Bank Rate than the Fed or ECB given stickier UK inflation, and its successively-revised average weekly earnings forecasts are presented as the canonical illustration of how central-bank inflation errors are rooted in mis-reading labour-market tightness [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]]; per ING's December 2022 outlook, the BoE has begun to talk down market rate-hike pricing, with ING expecting a 50 bp hike in December 2022 and one further 50 bp move in February 2023 to mark the top of this tightening cycle [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].

## Key facts

- JPM AM forecasts a UK peak rate of 4.0%–4.5% reached in the second quarter of 2023, lagging the Fed and ECB peaks [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- Reasoning given: inflation is likely to prove stickier in the UK than in the US or euro area [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- The Bank's successive average-weekly-earnings forecasts (Aug-21, Feb-22, Aug-22, Nov-22) are reproduced in the outlook to argue that central-bank inflation misses came from underestimating how labour-market tightness would result in workers asking for more pay [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- UK is judged more vulnerable than the US to the rate hike's mortgage-channel impact: only 14% of mortgages are variable-rate today (vs 70% in 2005), but a further 25% are fixed for only two years — so transmission arrives with a delay [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- Per ING, the BoE "has begun to talk down market rate hike pricing, and investors have taken the hint, but are still probably overestimating what is to come" [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- ING expected the BoE to pivot back to a 50 bp hike in December 2022 and to deliver one further 50 bp move in February 2023, marking the top of this tightening cycle [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- ING reads UK financial conditions as having become calmer post-September-2022 mini-budget, with fresh tax rises in the Autumn Statement allowing the Chancellor to defer painful spending cuts until after the next election in 2024/25 [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Per ING, energy support will become considerably less generous for most UK households from April 2023 [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Per ING, despite the sharp fall in swap rates since the September mini-budget crisis, UK mortgage rates have fallen much more gradually [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- ING's base case is that a UK recession "now looks virtually inevitable," though more material signs of slowing might not appear until Q1 2023 [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
