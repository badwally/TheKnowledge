# Yotam Cohen

## Summary

Yotam Cohen is the Co-founder and CEO of Daisy, the AI-native property management company managing over 4,000 units across NYC as of September 2025 [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]]. Prior to Daisy, Cohen co-founded Wibbitz, a video technology company that grew to operate offices in New York, Tel Aviv, Paris, and London, and was eventually acquired by Vimeo [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]]. He studied business and entrepreneurship at Reichman University in Israel, and mentors young entrepreneurs alongside his Daisy operating role [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].

## Key facts

- Title: Co-founder and CEO of Daisy [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Co-founded Wibbitz, a video-tech company that grew into a global business with offices in New York, Tel Aviv, Paris, and London; Wibbitz was eventually acquired by Vimeo [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Studied business and entrepreneurship at Reichman University in Israel [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Mentors young entrepreneurs alongside his Daisy operating role [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Articulated the Daisy founding thesis as drawn from personal experience: having lived in two very different apartment-living realities, joining a condo board to try to fix things, and concluding that antiquated systems and lack of transparency were the norm, not the exception [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Articulated Daisy's strategic framing: "By connecting everyone on one platform, streamlining workflows, and making all actions transparent, we could fundamentally change how buildings are managed: making it proactive, efficient, and resident-focused" [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Articulated Daisy's AI-agent posture: "Instead of throwing more people at problems, we're designing roles around what humans do best — and giving agents full ownership of repeatable workflows" [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Articulated Daisy's market thesis on industry inefficiency: 70% of CAM-company revenue goes to labor performing manual tasks that technology could streamline; less than 1% is invested in technology itself [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
- Articulated Daisy's expansion-as-virtuous-cycle thesis: more buildings → more data → smarter systems → greater vendor buying power → better experience for boards and residents [[sources/web-2025-09-29-f2f]].
