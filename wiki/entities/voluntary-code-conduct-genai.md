# Voluntary Code of Conduct on the Responsible Development and Management of Advanced Generative AI Systems

## Summary

The Voluntary Code of Conduct on the Responsible Development and Management of Advanced Generative AI Systems is a voluntary instrument published by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) in September 2023 that commits signatories to risk-management, safety, fairness, transparency, oversight, and robustness measures for advanced generative AI [[sources/web-2026-06-04-a90]]. It distinguishes the obligations of developers from those of managers, and imposes additional measures on organizations whose systems are made widely available to the public [[sources/web-2026-06-04-a90]].

## Key facts

- Published by Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada in September 2023 [[sources/web-2026-06-04-a90]].
- Scoped to "advanced generative AI systems" with general-purpose capabilities, citing ChatGPT, DALL·E 2, and Midjourney as illustrative examples [[sources/web-2026-06-04-a90]].
- Defines six outcome principles: Accountability, Safety, Fairness and Equity, Transparency, Human Oversight and Monitoring, and Validity and Robustness [[sources/web-2026-06-04-a90]].
- Distinguishes two organizational roles — developers (who build systems) and managers (who operate them) — and assigns complementary measures across each role [[sources/web-2026-06-04-a90]].
- Assigns additional measures to organizations that make systems widely available for public use, including third-party audits prior to release, publication of capability and limitation information, publication of training-data descriptions, and a freely available method to detect content generated by the system, with near-term focus on watermarking audio-visual output [[sources/web-2026-06-04-a90]].
- Required measures across all advanced generative systems include: a comprehensive risk management framework proportionate to risk profile; ecosystem information sharing; adverse-impact assessment including malicious-use risk; adversarial red-teaming; cyber-security assessment with explicit attention to data poisoning; and benchmarking against recognized standards [[sources/web-2026-06-04-a90]].
- Requires managers of systems that could be mistaken for humans to clearly and prominently identify them as AI systems [[sources/web-2026-06-04-a90]].
- Explicitly does not change existing legal obligations under instruments such as the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act (PIPEDA) [[sources/web-2026-06-04-a90]].
- Cites the G7 Data Protection and Privacy Authorities' Statement on Generative AI as context for privacy implications [[sources/web-2026-06-04-a90]].
- Signatories include Ada, AlayaCare, Alberta Machine Intelligence Institute (Amii), Alloprof, AltaML, Appen, BlackBerry, BlueDot, CGI, CIBC, Clir, CNIB, Cofomo Inc., Cohere, Council of Canadian Innovators, Coveo, Dayforce Canada Ltd., Dimonoff Inc., Geotab Inc., Hewlett Packard Enterprise, IBM, Intel Corporation, Interac Corp., Jolera Inc., kama.ai, Kyndryl, Lenovo, Levio, MaRS Discovery District, Mastercard, Mila, Nuvei, OpenText, Organisme d'autoréglementation du courtage immobilier du Québec (OACIQ), PaymentEvolution, Protexxa Inc., Ranovus, Resemble AI, Responsible Artificial Intelligence Institute, Salesforce, SAP Canada, Scale AI, TELUS, TELUS Digital, Vector Institute, and Workday [[sources/web-2026-06-04-a90]].
- Signatories also commit to supporting Canada's AI ecosystem through standards contribution, information sharing, research collaboration, public-education work, and to prioritizing human rights, accessibility, and environmental sustainability in development and deployment [[sources/web-2026-06-04-a90]].
