# CRA Interpretation Bulletin IT-304R2 (Condominiums)

## Summary

CRA Interpretation Bulletin IT-304R2, *Condominiums*, dated June 2, 2000, is the CRA's primary administrative statement on the federal income tax treatment of Canadian condominium corporations and condominium-unit owners. It explains the legal basis of the condominium system across Canada's ten provinces and three territories, describes the purpose and function of a condominium corporation (a syndicate in Quebec), specifies the federal income-tax returns that must be filed by a condominium corporation, and discusses the unit owner's deduction of expenses (including CCA) when a unit is used to earn business or rental income [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]]. The bulletin cancels and replaces IT-304R (*Capital Cost Allowance — Condominiums*, May 13, 1991) and was archived without supersession; it remains the authoritative CRA position because no superseding interpretation bulletin has been issued [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]]. CRA's subsequent severed-letter technical interpretations — including External T.I. 2014-0528171E5 (August 18, 2014) — have applied the IT-304R2 framework to specific third-party-revenue fact patterns (e.g., leasing of common-element roofs for solar-panel installations), elaborating an incidental-vs-non-incidental analytic test and an "agent for unit owners" doctrine grounded in provincial condominium law [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]]. Industry-side BC restatements such as VISOA's practitioner guide operationalize the IT-304R2 framework for strata-corporation use, adding jurisdiction-specific application detail (T1044 trigger thresholds, T2 short-form eligibility, NPO Code 10, Business Number registration channel) consistent with the bulletin's broader doctrine [[sources/web-2025-10-20-a96]]. Québec-side practitioner guidance (e.g., T2inc.ca's November 2025 CO-17.SP guide) extends the IT-304R2 paragraph 9 doctrine — that a Québec syndicate is a corporation for federal purposes — into the operational parallel-filing regime where Québec syndicates file CO-17.SP with Revenu Québec alongside the federal T2 and (threshold-conditional) T1044 with the CRA [[sources/web-2025-11-06-d1c]].

## Key facts

- Bulletin number: IT-304R2 [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Date of issue: June 2, 2000 [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Subject: Income Tax Act — Condominiums [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- References paragraphs 20(1)(*a*), 149(1)(*l*) and 150(1)(*a*) of the *Income Tax Act*, paragraph 20(1)(*aa*), Class 1 of Schedule II of the *Income Tax Regulations*, and subsections 1100(11), 1100(14), 1101(1ac) and 1101(1ae) of the Regulations [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Cancels and replaces Interpretation Bulletin IT-304R, dated May 13, 1991, formerly titled *Capital Cost Allowance — Condominiums* [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Revision driven by a change in CCRA's position on condominium corporations announced in Income Tax Technical News No. 4 (February 20, 1995) [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Uses Ontario and British Columbia *Condominium Act* terminology as representative of the terminology likely to be encountered in most provincial and territorial legislation, with separate treatment for Quebec under the Civil Code of Québec at ¶ 9 [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Paragraph 3 establishes that a condominium corporation is a corporation without share capital whose members are the unit owners, and that any excess of members' fees and contributions over the corporation's expenditures for the year is not considered income of the corporation [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Paragraph 3 also establishes that interest earned on operating or reserve funds, plus rental and other incidental income, IS income of the corporation [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Paragraph 3 establishes that if a condominium corporation carries on a business, any profits from that business must be included in its income and the corporation will not be considered a non-profit corporation [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Paragraph 4 establishes that all corporations (including condominium corporations) must file an income tax return each year under paragraph 150(1)(*a*), even if exempt from Part I tax [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Paragraph 4 establishes that a residential condominium corporation qualifying as a non-profit organization under paragraph 149(1)(*l*) is exempt from Part I tax but must file Form T1044 with its T2 return [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Paragraph 4 explicitly states that 'most residential condominium corporations qualify as non-profit organizations within the meaning of' paragraph 149(1)(*l*), though qualification remains a question of fact [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Paragraph 6 establishes that for CCA purposes, the building portion of a condominium unit acquired after 1987 is generally included in Class 1 of Schedule II to the Regulations [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Paragraph 7 establishes that a condominium unit meeting the 'rental property' definition is subject to the subsection 1100(11) CCA restriction and that subsection 1101(1ac) imposes a separate-class requirement for rental properties with capital cost ≥ $50,000 [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Paragraph 8 establishes that no deduction or capitalization of reserve-fund contributions or extraordinary levies is permitted by the unit owner until the amount is laid out to earn income by the condominium corporation, because prepaid expenses are not deductible per IT-417 [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Paragraph 9 establishes that for federal income tax purposes a Quebec syndicate of co-owners is considered to be a corporation, and the bulletin's comments apply equally to condominiums established under the Civil Code of Québec [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Cross-references IT-220 (CCA — proceeds of disposition of depreciable property), IT-79 (CCA — buildings or other structures), IT-128 (CCA — depreciable property), IT-417 (prepaid expenses and deferred charges), and IT-496 (non-profit organizations) [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Bears the bulletin-wide disclaimer that 'Bulletins do not have the force of law' [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Archived by CRA without supersession; no later interpretation bulletin has been issued on condominium corporations [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].

### Subsequent technical interpretations applying the IT-304R2 framework

- CRA External T.I. 2014-0528171E5 (August 18, 2014), authored by Roger Filion (Manager, Non-Profit Organizations and Aboriginal Issues), applied the IT-304R2 paragraph 149(1)(l) framework to a fact pattern involving a condominium corporation entering into a leasing contract for the installation of third-party solar panels on common-element roofs [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- The 2014 T.I. articulated a conjunctive two-prong test for "incidental" income — minor AND directly related to NPO objectives — under which incidental rental income from common areas may be treated as income of the Corporation without affecting tax-exempt status [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- The 2014 T.I. articulated an "agent for unit owners" doctrine grounded in subsection 11(2) of the Ontario *Condominium Act, 1998*, under which non-incidental lease income is generally treated as income of the unit owners (not the Corporation) where supported by the relevant provincial law [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- The 2014 T.I. flagged CRA's specific concern that lease-agreement income flowing to a freeze or reduction in members' condominium fees would likely be available for the personal benefit of members of the Corporation, jeopardizing the 149(1)(l) exemption [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- The 2014 T.I. bears the standard CRA disclaimer that it may not represent the current position of the CRA [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].

### Industry-side practitioner restatements

- VISOA's October 2025 practitioner guide on tax filing is an industry-side BC restatement of the IT-304R2 framework, framing the §149(1)(l) NPO classification as applying to "most residential strata corporations" and tying it to the practical observation that interest earned on bank accounts, contingency reserve fund, and GICs is generally non-taxable [[sources/web-2025-10-20-a96]].
- VISOA's guide elaborates the §150 T2 filing obligation with BC-strata-specific operational detail (T1044 trigger thresholds, T2 short-form vs long-form, NPO Code 10 "Residential condominium corporation," RC-1 E (23) Business Number registration channel) absent from the federal-level IT-304R2 bulletin itself [[sources/web-2025-10-20-a96]].
- VISOA's guide consistently refers stratas with non-incidental commercial revenue or non-owner clubhouse/roof-leasing arrangements to the Jonathan Wright article "Income of Strata Corporations: How Not To Lose Tax-Exempt Status" in the November 2015 VISOA Bulletin — mirroring the IT-304R2 ¶3 doctrine that operating a business jeopardizes NPO status [[sources/web-2025-10-20-a96]].
- T2inc.ca's November 2025 practitioner guide is the Québec-side analogue: it operationalizes the IT-304R2 paragraph 9 doctrine (that a Québec syndicate is a corporation for federal purposes) into a parallel-filing regime where the syndicate files the provincial CO-17.SP with Revenu Québec alongside the federal T2 (with schedules 100, 125, 141) and threshold-conditional T1044 with the CRA [[sources/web-2025-11-06-d1c]].
- T2inc.ca's guide restates the IT-304R2 paragraph 4 doctrine that condominium syndicates are recognized as NPOs ("usually exempt from income tax") but must still file an annual information return, and operationalizes the federal T1044 threshold logic as a mirror disclosure obligation in Section 3 of the CO-17.SP [[sources/web-2025-11-06-d1c]].
