# California Davis-Stirling Act § 5550

## Summary

California Civil Code § 5550, part of the Davis-Stirling Common Interest Development Act, is California's primary reserve study statute: it requires HOA and condo boards to commission a triennial visual inspection of major components and produce a reserve study identifying components with remaining useful life of less than 30 years, cost estimates, an annual contribution estimate, and a reserve funding plan; the section was most recently amended by SB 900 (Stats. 2024, Ch. 288, Sec. 2), effective January 1, 2025. [[sources/web-2025-01-01-246]] The predecessor regime — California Civil Code § 1365 (pro forma operating budget) and § 1365.5 (reserve study) — is documented in the August 2010 California Department of Real Estate "Reserve Study Guidelines for Homeowner Association Budgets," which remains the state's authoritative interpretive guidance on the pre-Davis-Stirling-renumbering content requirements and is the bridge between the modern § 5550 framing and its statutory ancestry. [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]] Solume's January 2025 vendor-curated multi-state reserve-study state-requirements guide independently summarizes § 5550's triennial-inspection-plus-annual-review framing and characterizes California as having "one of the most stringent reserve study requirements for HOAs" — a vendor-curated regulatory-interpretation cross-reference comparable in role to PropFusion's vendor-curated FL SIRS guide. [[sources/web-2025-01-07-b6e]]

## Key facts

- Codified at California Civil Code § 5550 within the Davis-Stirling Common Interest Development Act. [[sources/web-2025-01-01-246]]
- Most recently amended by SB 900 (Stats. 2024, Ch. 288, Sec. 2), effective January 1, 2025. [[sources/web-2025-01-01-246]]
- At least once every three years, the board shall cause to be conducted a reasonably competent and diligent visual inspection of the accessible areas of the major components the association is obligated to repair, replace, restore, or maintain, as part of a study of the reserve account requirements. [[sources/web-2025-01-01-246]]
- The triennial visual inspection requirement applies if the current replacement value of the major components is equal to or greater than one-half of the gross budget of the association, excluding the association's reserve account for that period. [[sources/web-2025-01-01-246]]
- The board shall review the study, or cause it to be reviewed, annually and shall consider and implement necessary adjustments to its analysis of reserve account requirements as a result of that review. [[sources/web-2025-01-01-246]]
- The study must, at minimum, identify the major components the association is obligated to repair, replace, restore, or maintain that, as of the date of the study, have a remaining useful life of less than 30 years. [[sources/web-2025-01-01-246]]
- The study must identify the probable remaining useful life of the in-scope components as of the date of the study. [[sources/web-2025-01-01-246]]
- The study must include an estimate of the cost of repair, replacement, restoration, or maintenance of the in-scope components. [[sources/web-2025-01-01-246]]
- The study must include an estimate of the total annual contribution necessary to defray the cost to repair, replace, restore, or maintain the in-scope components during and at the end of their useful life, after subtracting total reserve funds as of the date of the study. [[sources/web-2025-01-01-246]]
- The study must include a reserve funding plan that indicates how the association plans to fund the contribution to meet its obligation for the repair and replacement of all major components with an expected remaining life of 30 years or less, excluding components the board has determined will not be replaced or repaired. [[sources/web-2025-01-01-246]]
- For purposes of § 5550, "major components" includes gas, water, and electrical service to the extent the association is responsible for repair or replacement of those lines pursuant to Civil Code § 4775. [[sources/web-2025-01-01-246]]
- The statutory minimum study contents do not require probabilistic forecasting methods — the required outputs are point estimates of remaining useful life, cost, and annual contribution. [[sources/web-2025-01-01-246]]
- Solume's January 2025 vendor-curated multi-state reserve-study state-requirements guide independently summarizes § 5550 as requiring a triennial reserve study with annual review, framing California as having "one of the most stringent reserve study requirements for HOAs" — a vendor-curated regulatory-interpretation cross-reference parallel to PropFusion's vendor-curated FL SIRS guide and supporting the interpretive consensus that § 5550 imposes a deterministic point-estimate methodology baseline. [[sources/web-2025-01-07-b6e]]
- Solume's guide additionally notes that § 5550 does not differentiate between HOAs and condominium associations — broadly using "common interest development" to encompass both — a vendor-curated framing consistent with the statute's CID-scoped application within the Davis-Stirling Act. [[sources/web-2025-01-07-b6e]]
- British Columbia's parallel reserve regime under the Strata Property Act and Strata Property Regulation operates on a five-year cycle rather than § 5550's triennial visual-inspection cadence, scopes the report to all common property and assets rather than to "major components with a remaining useful life of less than 30 years," and requires a 30-year forward financial forecast — also without mandating probabilistic methods. [[sources/web-2013-11-28-cda]]
- New Brunswick's parallel reserve-fund-study regime under the Condominium Property Act (c16.05, effective 2010-01-01) operates on a 10-year primary cadence with 3-year interim updates and a 30-year forward horizon — a longer base cycle than § 5550's triennial visual inspection — and applies only to corporations with more than 10 units rather than to associations whose current replacement value of major components is at least half of the gross budget; CMHC's NB provincial fact sheet additionally surfaces that substantial alterations and improvements to common elements approved by 60%-vote are paid from the reserve fund, broadening reserve-fund scope beyond § 5550's narrower "repair, replace, restore, or maintain" framing. [[sources/web-2017-07-26-1f3]] [[sources/web-2018-03-31-c63]]
- New Brunswick's regime is also two-tier in a way § 5550 is not: under NB CPA c16.05, condominium corporations with fewer than 11 units must maintain a reserve fund equal to 100 percent of the annual operating budget of the corporation (or a greater percentage if specified in the corporation's bylaws) in lieu of obtaining a reserve fund study, while corporations with 11 or more units must obtain a full reserve fund study — a unit-count-keyed two-tier structure that has no analog in § 5550's gross-budget-half threshold for triggering the reserve-study requirement; CMHC's NB fact sheet also documents an additional NB conversion-condominium disclosure asymmetry — for conversion condominiums, developers must provide buyers the most current building inspection report if the building has 10 units or less and the most current reserve fund study if the building has 11 units or more, a unit-count-keyed disclosure asymmetry without direct analog in California's Davis-Stirling disclosure regime. [[sources/web-2018-03-31-c63]]

### Predecessor regime — Civil Code § 1365 and § 1365.5 (pre-Davis-Stirling renumbering)

- The predecessor statute to § 5550 was California Civil Code § 1365 (pro forma operating budget) coupled with § 1365.5 (reserve study); the August 2010 California DRE Reserve Study Guidelines documents the operative requirements under those sections prior to the Davis-Stirling renumbering. [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]]
- The reserve study requirement under the predecessor regime sits within California's Common Interest Development Act, which defines a CID as a development governed by a mandatory association that administers shared property and enforces deed restrictions; the association is responsible for repairing, replacing, or maintaining the common areas, and each separate-interest owner is responsible for maintaining that separate interest and any exclusive use common area appurtenant to it (per Civil Code § 1364). [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]]
- Under § 1365 (predecessor regime), the association was required to prepare and distribute a pro forma operating budget 30-90 days prior to the start of the association's next fiscal year. [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]]
- The § 1365 pro forma operating budget was required to contain, at a minimum: estimated revenue and expenses on the accrual basis of accounting; identification of total cash reserves currently set aside; estimated remaining life of major components; estimated current replacement cost of major components; if applicable, the amount of any construction-defect-related award or settlement and the disposition of such funds; the total cash reserves expressed as a percentage of the current replacement cost and the current deficiency in reserve funding on a per-unit basis; identification of methods of funding for future repair, replacement, or additions to major components (including notification of any deferred repairs or replacements, anticipated special assessments, or certain outstanding loans to the association); and a statement of methods used to develop estimates and funding plan. [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]]
- Civil Code § 1365.2.5 (predecessor regime) required a specific form to be used in disclosing the pro forma operating budget items. [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]]
- The 2010 DRE Guidelines state that a reserve study alone (containing the elements prescribed by § 1365.5) is sufficient to constitute legal compliance under the predecessor regime — but that an association will be stronger financially if regular assessments are collected to help assure funding of replacement requirements as they occur. [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]]
- The 2010 DRE Guidelines disclaim being the "standard of care" for Reserve Studies or interpreting the California Civil Code; the document is characterized by the DRE as a manual to help readers better understand Reserve Studies rather than a regulatory rulemaking. [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]]
- The 2010 DRE Guidelines characterize the reserve study as a means to enable three core determinations: examination of the association's repair and replacement obligations; determination of costs and timing of replacement; and determination of the availability of necessary (reserve) cash resources. [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]]
- The DRE Guidelines describe both the physical analysis and the funding analysis methodology in deterministic point-estimate terms — useful life, remaining life, replacement cost, and inflation rate are treated as scalar inputs rather than distributions — establishing that California's predecessor-regime interpretive guidance, like CAI's standards, did not historically require probabilistic methods. [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]]
- The 2010 DRE Guidelines were updated by Roy Helsing PRA, RS specifically to ensure alignment with the guidelines of the Association of Professional Reserve Preparers (APRA) and the Community Associations Institute (CAI) — making the predecessor-regime interpretive framework an explicit state-government adoption of the CAI / APRA methodology baseline. [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]]
