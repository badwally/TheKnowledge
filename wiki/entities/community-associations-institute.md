# Community Associations Institute (CAI)

## Summary

Community Associations Institute (CAI) is a US organization that publishes educational and informational materials on community-association governance, including a recurring jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction summary of state reserve fund laws used by managers, boards, and reserve professionals as a reference baseline [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]]. CAI also operates an advocacy arm at advocacy.caionline.org that publishes policy explainers — authored by senior CAI executives such as Chief Advocacy Officer Dawn Bauman, CAE — that translate federal regulatory and GSE-policy developments (e.g., Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2026-03 and Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-C) into implementation guidance for boards, managers, and business partners [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]]. CAI further operates a research arm — the Foundation for Community Association Research — that conducts industry-wide surveys quantifying the real-world impact of GSE underwriting standards on condominium communities [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].

## Key facts

- Publishes a "Summary of State Reserve Fund Laws" reference document; the September 2024 edition is the version of record used here [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- The September 2024 edition is authored by Phoebe Neseth [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- The publication explicitly disclaims that its contents are intended for general educational and informational purposes only, may not reflect the most recent developments, and are not a substitute for advice from a lawyer, community manager, accountant, insurance agent, reserve professional, or lender [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- The publication tracks two distinct statutory requirements separately: (a) whether reserve studies (or a reserve schedule) are required, and (b) whether reserve funding itself is required [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- The publication tracks developer-side reserve requirements separately from ongoing association requirements [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Italicizes the names of states whose reserve laws changed in response to the June 2021 collapse of Champlain Towers South in Surfside, FL — Florida, Maryland, and Tennessee in the September 2024 edition [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Operates an advocacy arm at advocacy.caionline.org that publishes federal-policy explainers; Dawn Bauman, CAE authored the March 18, 2026 explainer on Fannie Mae LL-2026-03 and Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-C [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Characterized the March 2026 GSE updates as consequential for community associations, board members, managers, and business partners, and noted that without thoughtful implementation and support the changes have the potential to slow transactions and place additional strain on already-constrained communities [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Committed to continue monitoring GSE-policy implementation and provide resources to support community associations navigating the evolving requirements [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Recognizes a roster of professional credentials in its implementation guidance — CMCA®, AMS®, PCAM®, AAMC® (management), CIRMS® (insurance), RS™ (reserve professional), CCAL™ (legal fellows) — that associations are encouraged to engage when preparing for GSE-policy compliance [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Has been described by an Ohio CAM operator as "the leading national organization for community association professionals and volunteers" [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Operates the Foundation for Community Association Research as its research arm, which has tracked the real-world impact of post-Surfside GSE underwriting standards on condominium communities closely [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Foundation for Community Association Research conducted a survey of over 700 board members, managers, and business partners on the impact of Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac condo lending standards [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Survey finding: 42% of respondents were unsure whether their community was eligible for federally-backed financing [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Survey finding: 40% of respondents acknowledged characteristics that could trigger ineligibility [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Survey finding: among respondents already deemed ineligible, 64% reported that the denial had negatively impacted home sales or property values [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Maintains a dedicated GSE-policy resource hub at caionline.org/advocacy/condo-safety/fannie-mae-freddie-mac-condo-and-housing-cooperative-lender-appraiser-requirements/ [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
