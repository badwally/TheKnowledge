# Elinor Ostrom

## Summary

Elinor Ostrom is a political scientist who developed the empirical framework of design principles for successfully managed common-pool resources, formalized in her 1990 book *Governing the Commons: The Evolution of Institutions for Collective Action* [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]]. Her framework is the upstream reference for subsequent work on knowledge commons and data commons.

## Key facts

- Argued against Garrett Hardin's "Tragedy of the Commons" thesis on the grounds that Hardin's model was too abstract and failed to account for real-world commoning institutions that had regulated CPR access for centuries or even millennia [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Identified eight design principles characterizing successful CPR institutions by reviewing commoning practices including irrigation communities in the Philippines, village commons in Japan, and the common pasture of Torbel, Switzerland [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Used the Swiss alpine village of Torbel — managed in common since the 15th century — as a primary case study, bringing it to international attention with the 1990 book [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
