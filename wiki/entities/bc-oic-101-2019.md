# British Columbia Order in Council 101/2019 (Strata Property Regulation Amendment)

## Summary

British Columbia Order in Council 101/2019 (OIC 101/2019) is the BC Cabinet instrument announced March 6, 2019 that amended the Strata Property Regulation to clarify deposit-insurance requirements for strata-corporation funds [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]]. The amendment was a targeted response to a widespread misinterpretation of §6.11 under which some strata corporations believed they were required to divide their investments across many accounts to keep each balance under the $100,000 CDIC insurance limit; OIC 101/2019 confirmed that compliance does not require full-account insurance — partial eligibility for CDIC or CUDIC coverage is sufficient [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]]. The amendment took effect immediately on announcement [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].

## Key facts

- Announced March 6, 2019 (BCLI commentary published March 7, 2019) [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- Amended the definition section of the Strata Property Regulation (§1.1) to define 'insured account' for the purposes of the Strata Property Act as 'an account with a deposit, all or part of which is eligible to be insured by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation or guaranteed by the Credit Union Deposit Insurance Corporation of British Columbia' [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- Amended §6.11(a) of the Strata Property Regulation by repealing paragraph (a) and substituting: 'a savings account or chequing account at a financial institution outside of British Columbia with a deposit, all or part of which is eligible to be insured by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation' [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- Amended §6.11(b) by repealing the text before subparagraph (ii) and substituting: 'a term deposit or a guaranteed investment certificate, if all or part of the deposit or certificate (i) is eligible to be insured by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation or guaranteed by the Credit Union Deposit Insurance Corporation of British Columbia' [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The 'all or part' language is the central operative change — it explicitly recognizes that an account whose balance exceeds the $100,000 CDIC limit is still a compliant investment vehicle for CRF or special-levy money [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- Stated rationale: reduce confusion and the perceived administrative burden on strata corporations that had interpreted Regulation 6.11 to mean the entire amount on deposit must be insured [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The Ministry's strata-housing webpage explained that many strata corporations have account balances well in excess of $100,000, and it was never intended that strata corporations administer a multitude of accounts to keep balances under the deposit insurance limit [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The 2014 predecessor amendment (B.C. Reg. 68/2014) had modernized and simplified §6.11 to specify that investments must be in accounts at CDIC- or CUDIC-member financial institutions and that the deposit must be eligible for deposit insurance — but did not address the partial-vs-full eligibility ambiguity that OIC 101/2019 later resolved [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- Took effect immediately on the date the order in council was made [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
