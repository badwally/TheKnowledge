# California Department of Real Estate

## Summary

The California Department of Real Estate (DRE) is the California state regulatory agency that publishes the "Reserve Study Guidelines for Homeowner Association Budgets" (August 2010 edition), providing the state's authoritative interpretive guidance on California Civil Code § 1365 and § 1365.5 — the predecessor regime to the Davis-Stirling Common Interest Development Act § 5550 [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]]. The DRE Guidelines establish state-government adoption of CAI and APRA methodology baselines for reserve study practice and remain a primary reference document for understanding the California reserve-study regulatory framework relevant to the Year-2 California expansion arc of the condo-capital-infra GTM [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].

## Key facts

- California state agency, mailing address Department of Real Estate Publications, 651 Bannon Street, STE 500, Sacramento, CA 95811; web www.dre.ca.gov [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
- Publishes the "Reserve Study Guidelines for Homeowner Association Budgets," originally developed under contract for the DRE by Eva Eagle Ph.D. and Susan Stoddard Ph.D., AICP (both of the Institute for the Study of Family, Work and Community), and David H. Levy M.B.A., C.P.A.; Janet Andrews MBA was responsible for the original design, layout, and typography [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
- Revised the publication in August 2010 with updates by Roy Helsing PRA, RS to ensure alignment with current California Law and the guidelines of the Association of Professional Reserve Preparers (APRA) and the Community Associations Institute (CAI) [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
- The Guidelines explicitly state that the report "does not necessarily reflect the position of the Administration of the State of California" — surfacing that DRE-published interpretive guidance is not equivalent to a regulatory rulemaking or formal statutory interpretation [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
- The Guidelines disclaim being the "standard of care" for Reserve Studies and disclaim interpreting the California Civil Code; the document is characterized as a manual to help readers better understand Reserve Studies [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
- The Guidelines were developed with the assistance of numerous industry professionals, association board members and managers and the Department of Real Estate, drawing on examples of current reserve studies and the comments and suggestions of industry leaders [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
- The Guidelines recommend that boards consult with the association's own attorney, accountant, or other advisors as necessary when following the procedures presented [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
- The Guidelines flag that, before a homeowners' association decides to prepare its own Reserve Study, it should consider seeking professional advice — citing volunteer board member indemnification, reliance on expert advice, and other factors as the rationale [[sources/pdf-dre-2010-reserve-study-guidelines]].
