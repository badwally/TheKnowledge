# Alberta Condominium Property Regulation (Alta Reg 168/2000)

## Summary

The Alberta Condominium Property Regulation (Alberta Regulation 168/2000, made under the Condominium Property Act, RSA 2000, c C-22) is the omnibus regulation governing condominium plan registration, board administration, and the capital replacement reserve fund regime in Alberta [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]]. Part 2 (§§21–25) operationalizes §38 of the Condominium Property Act by defining the reserve fund, the qualified-person standard, and the reserve fund study / report / plan trilogy, including a self-study exemption for corporations with ≤12 units [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]]. The regulation also defines the universe of securities in which an Alberta condominium corporation may invest its reserve fund at the Authorized Corporation Investments provision (Page 48 of the regulation as cited in the source). Alberta operates the most permissive of the three Canadian eligible-investment regimes documented (ON, BC, AB), explicitly allowing dividend-paying common shares up to a 15% portfolio cap [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].

## Key facts

### Reserve fund — Part 2 (§§21–25)

- Part 2 governs the Capital Replacement Reserve Fund regime [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §21(1)(d) defines "reserve fund" as the capital replacement reserve fund required to be established and maintained by the corporation under §38 of the Condominium Property Act [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §21(1)(b) defines "depreciating property" as the property to which §38(1) of the Act applies [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §21(1)(c) defines "qualified person" as an individual who, based on reasonable and objective criteria, is knowledgeable with respect to (i) the depreciating property or that type of depreciating property, (ii) its operation and maintenance, and (iii) the costs of replacement or repair [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §21(2) extends the qualified-person concept to a corporate entity, provided the entity employs or retains an individual who meets the qualified-person standard to carry out the functions [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §22 allows a corporation whose condominium plan consists of not more than 12 units to carry out the functions of a qualified person itself, if authorized to do so by a special resolution [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §23(1) requires the board to retain a qualified person to carry out a study of depreciating property determining: an inventory of depreciating property that may need to be repaired or replaced within the next 25 years, present condition and state of repair, estimated repair/replacement costs (using costs not less than those existing at the time of the reserve fund report), and life expectancy of each component once repaired or replaced [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §23(2) requires the qualified person also to determine the current reserve fund balance, recommend the amount of funds (if any) that should be included or added to the reserve fund to establish or maintain it for §38 purposes, and describe the basis for those determinations [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §23(3) requires the qualified person to prepare and submit to the board a written reserve fund report setting out the person's qualifications, whether the person is an employee/agent of (or otherwise associated with) the corporation or its manager, the findings of the study, and any other matters the person considers relevant [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §23(4) requires the board, after reviewing the report, to approve a reserve fund plan (i) under which a reserve fund is to be established if not already, and (ii) setting forth the method and amounts needed for funding and maintaining the reserve fund [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §23(5) requires the plan to provide that sufficient funds will be available — by owners' contributions or any other reasonable method — to repair or replace depreciating property in accordance with the reserve fund report [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §23(6) requires the corporation to provide owners with copies of the approved plan prior to collecting any funds for the matters dealt with in the underlying reserve fund report [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §23(7) allows a corporation that has not yet approved a plan to continue to collect or otherwise receive funds for a fund similar in nature to a reserve fund, and to make expenditures from it [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §24(1) required corporations existing immediately before September 1, 2000 to meet §23(1)–(6) requirements before September 1, 2002 [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §24(2) requires corporations whose condominium plan is registered on or after September 1, 2000 to meet §23(1)–(6) requirements not later than 2 years from the day the condominium plan is registered [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §24(3) provides a transitional rule: if a corporation completed a substantially-similar study within the 5 years before September 1, 2000, the board needed only to meet §23(4) (plan-approval) requirements before September 1, 2002, treating that prior study as if it were a reserve fund study [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].

### Cost consultant (§1(2))

- §1(2) defines the cost-consultant qualification requirement for §14(1)(b) of the Act: an individual cost consultant must be knowledgeable about construction costs of units/common property and the determination of substantial completion [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- A corporate cost consultant must employ or retain a qualifying individual to carry out the functions [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- A cost consultant must act at arm's length from the developer of the unit or common property [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].

### Part 1 — registration mechanics

- §2–§3 require the Registrar to keep a register of condominium plans and govern the form of plans presented for registration, including the contents of the first sheet, diagram sheets, and Schedule (Form 1) under §8(1) of the Act [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §6 requires unit factors to be assigned such that the total of all unit factors in the parcel equals 10,000 [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §11 requires a certificate of title to a unit to be in Form 2 or in any other form that the Registrar specifies [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §15 governs the filing requirements when an administrator, receiver, or receiver and manager is appointed under §14(14) or §58 of the Act, requiring a certified copy of the court order to be filed with the Registrar [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §16 requires notice of termination of condominium status of a building or parcel to be in Form 5 [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].

### Authorized corporation investments

- Governs authorized investments for Alberta condominium corporations under the Condominium Property Act [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- Permits investments in US, Canadian, and UK government bonds [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- Permits municipal bonds [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- Permits some Canadian corporate bonds [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- Permits preferred shares of Canadian corporations [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- Permits some dividend-paying common shares, limited to a maximum of 15% of the market value of the corporation's overall investment portfolio [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- Per CCI's June 2016 statistics, Alberta at the time of the 2017 source article had approximately 8,000 condominium corporations representing approximately 440,000 units [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- The source-of-record author cites Alberta's regime alongside BC's as a model for expanding Ontario's eligible-securities definition under Section 115(5) [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].

### Regulatory history

- Originally enacted as AR 168/2000; consolidated up to AR 151/2006; the archived 2011-06-02 source captures the version posted between January 17, 2007 and May 27, 2011 [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- §1 of the regulation has been amended by AR 108/2004 and AR 151/2006; multiple Part 1 sections (§3, §4, §7, §12, §13, §14, §15, §17, §18, §19, §20) carry AR 108/2004 amendment markers [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
