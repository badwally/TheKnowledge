# Cliff Asness

## Summary

Managing principal at AQR Capital Management in Greenwich, CT and author of "The Less-Efficient Market Hypothesis" (forthcoming in the 50th Anniversary Issue of The Journal of Portfolio Management); argues that over the past 30+ years markets have become less informationally efficient in the relative pricing of common stocks, particularly over medium horizons, with technologies such as social media as likely the biggest culprit [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].

## Key facts

- Listed as managing principal at AQR Capital Management in Greenwich, CT [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Listed contact email cliff.asness@aqr.com on the paper [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Author of "The Less-Efficient Market Hypothesis," forthcoming in the 50th Anniversary Issue of The Journal of Portfolio Management [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Career length stated as "almost 35 years," anchored to his dissertation that ended its data in 1990 (27 years of data, 1963-1990) — "you know you've been around a while when you have more out-of-sample data than you originally had in-sample data" [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Attended Eugene Fama's two-class Finance series at the University of Chicago three years in a row — first as a student, then as a T.A. for two years — describing himself as "terrified to miss anything I needed to remember" [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Co-authored with John Liew a piece in Institutional Investor (Asness and Liew, 2014) proposing to inject the word "reasonable" into the EMH testing process, citing law's "reasonable man" standard [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Argues bubbles are rare, the word is used too promiscuously, and the standard should be very high — requiring that no reasonable model and no reasonable expectations can justify the price [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Identifies as qualifying bubbles in his career: Japanese stock prices in the late 1980s; US stock prices in general and the high-vs-low-multiple spread in 1999-2000; high-vs-low-multiple stocks again in 2019-2020 [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Co-introduced the "value spread" — the ratio of valuation of expensive stocks to cheap stocks — in Asness, Friedman, Krail, and Liew (2000) during the dot-com bubble [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- AQR began trading basic value and momentum strategies in 1994; Asness argues nanosecond-like speed is irrelevant to medium-term holding-period strategies, with only modest deterioration if a one-year strategy is lagged a full week [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Self-describes as "a fan, but not a Panglossian one" of the market system, paraphrasing Churchill on democracy: the market system is "the worst way to carry this out, excepting for all others that have ever been conceived of or tried" [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Acknowledges the piece is "as much an op-ed as it is quantitative research" and may be "the harrumphing of an old man," but believes it is an accurate op-ed [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Implication of the LEMH thesis: investors willing to take the other side of these inefficiencies should rationally be rewarded with higher expected returns, but also greater risks [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
