# Akash Network

## Summary

Decentralized cloud-computing marketplace launched by Overclock Labs and described in the May 13, 2018 position paper "Akash Network: Decentralized Cloud Infrastructure Marketplace" (Version 0.0.3) as "the world's first global spot market for cloud computing," using a blockchain-anchored public orderbook to match client deployment orders with provider fulfillment orders, settling in the network-native AKASH token [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].

## Key facts

- Documented in the May 13, 2018 position paper "Akash Network: Decentralized Cloud Infrastructure Marketplace" (Version 0.0.3) authored by Overclock Labs, with research contact research@akash.network [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- Self-described as a "super" cloud platform (supercloud) that provides a unified layer above all providers on the marketplace, presenting clients with a single cloud platform regardless of which particular provider they may be using [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- Connects clients (those who need computing resources) with providers (those that have computing capacity to lease) via a publicly-verifiable blockchain record of transactions [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- Workloads in Akash are defined as Docker containers, chosen for highly-isolated and configurable execution environments [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- The marketplace consists of a public orderbook and a matching algorithm; clients place deployment orders and providers place fulfillment orders to bid on them [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- Among eligible fulfillment orders, the one offering the lowest price wins; if multiple are eligible at the same price, the earliest-submitted wins [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- A match between a deployment and fulfillment order creates a lease, which is the binding agent in fulfilling the deployment [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- The native token AKASH is used to pay for leased compute infrastructure and to mitigate foreign-exchange risk that usually results from cross-border payments [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- A staking system requires new providers to lock up a meaningful value in AKASH; there is no minimum stake amount, but participation in governance and a provider's reputation score are stake-weighted [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- A homomorphic encryption layer is added to the marketplace to guard against competitor data mining and other attack vectors [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- Cites Cisco (2016) projecting that by 2020 cloud infrastructure will account for 53% of global internet traffic, and Forrester (2017) projecting that Amazon, Google, and Microsoft will deliver 80% of the payload [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- Cites Gartner (2017) sizing the market at $737 billion with well over 21% annual growth [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
