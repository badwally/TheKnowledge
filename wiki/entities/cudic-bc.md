# Credit Union Deposit Insurance Corporation of British Columbia (CUDIC)

## Summary

The Credit Union Deposit Insurance Corporation of British Columbia (CUDIC) is the BC provincial deposit insurer for credit union deposits. CUDIC eligibility — alongside CDIC — is one of the two insurance criteria that qualify term deposits and GICs as eligible investments for a BC strata corporation's contingency reserve fund or special levy under Strata Property Regulation §6.11(b)(i), as substituted by B.C. Reg. 68/2014 effective July 16, 2014 [[sources/web-2014-07-16-7c3]] [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]] and further amended by Order in Council 101/2019 (effective March 6, 2019) to clarify that partial CDIC or CUDIC eligibility is sufficient for compliance [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]]. CUDIC's unlimited deposit insurance protection — in contrast to CDIC's $100,000-per-deposit ceiling — means accounts held at BC credit unions are not exposed to the partial-coverage problem that drove the OIC 101/2019 clarification [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].

## Key facts

- BC Strata Property Regulation §6.11(b)(i) requires that term deposits and GICs be insured by CDIC or by the Credit Union Deposit Insurance Corporation of British Columbia in order to be eligible investments for a strata corporation's CRF or special levy [[sources/web-2014-07-16-7c3]] [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- The §6.11(b)(i) CUDIC eligibility text was first introduced in its modern form by B.C. Reg. 68/2014, the amendment that substituted §6.11 of the Strata Property Regulation effective July 16, 2014 [[sources/web-2014-07-16-7c3]], and was further amended by Order in Council 101/2019 (effective March 6, 2019) to clarify that the deposit need only be eligible for insurance 'in whole or in part' rather than in its entirety [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The BC regulation defines insured investments to include deposits or certificates insured by CUDIC [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- CUDIC offers unlimited deposit insurance protection on accounts at member credit unions, in contrast to CDIC, which limits insurance to the first $100,000 of total amount on deposit [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- Because CUDIC coverage is unlimited, CRF and special-levy balances held at BC credit unions are not exposed to the partial-coverage problem that drove the OIC 101/2019 clarification — that clarification was operatively addressed to CDIC-member institutions whose customers' balances routinely exceeded the $100,000 ceiling [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The OIC 101/2019 amendment of §1.1 added a statutory definition of 'insured account' for the purposes of the Strata Property Act, naming both CDIC and CUDIC as the two eligibility-conferring insurance regimes [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
