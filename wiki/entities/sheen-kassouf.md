# Sheen T. Kassouf

## Summary

Economist and Assistant Professor of Economics at the University of California at Irvine; co-author with Edward O. Thorp of the 1967 book "Beat the Market: A Scientific Stock Market System," and the trader whose October 5, 1961 first investment is the start of the book's five-year, 25%/year cash record [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].

## Key facts

- Identified on the title page of "Beat the Market" (1967) as Sheen T. Kassouf, Ph.D., Assistant Professor of Economics at the University of California at Irvine [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Listed as the author of two prior works on the title page: "Evaluation of Convertible Securities" and "A Theory and an Econometric Model for Common Stock Purchase Warrants" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Chapter 1, "A System Is Born," opens with: "On October 5, 1961, Sheen Kassouf began a series of investments which averaged 25% a year over the next five years" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Recounts in chapter 1 that in 1957 he subscribed to a respected advisory service, bought 100 shares of Emerson Radio on its "promising" rating, took a $1,500 unrealized loss as the market declined, and eventually sold in 1958 for a $500 profit — after which Emerson tripled within a year [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- States that by 1961 he had "sold my business and plunged into the financial maelstrom," subscribing to services and publications, emptying library shelves, and spending the hours between 10:00 A.M. and 3:30 P.M. in boardrooms — describing himself as "a 'boardroom bum'" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Conducted a personal test of chart reading: he selected pages at random from a chart book, covered the name of the corporation and the last half of the chart, and asked self-described chart readers what price change the "pattern" indicated, finding their predictions "no better than those of someone making random guesses" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
