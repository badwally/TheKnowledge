# Stanford University

## Summary

U.S. research university in Stanford, California, that publishes the Stanford Emerging Technology Review (SETR), an annual survey of ten emerging technology fields and their policy implications produced jointly by its School of Engineering, the Hoover Institution, and the Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence (HAI) [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]]. Stanford-affiliated researchers also contributed four of the eight authors of the 2026 paper "Multi-User Large Language Model Agents" [[sources/pdf-shu-yang-2026-multi-user-large]].

## Key facts

- The 2026 SETR report is described as the third annual report and the flagship product of the SETR initiative [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- The SETR initiative is the first-ever collaboration among Stanford's School of Engineering, the Hoover Institution, and Stanford's Institute for Human-Centered Artificial Intelligence (HAI) [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- The SETR education campaign for policymakers involves more than 100 Stanford scholars across 40 departments and research institutes [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- The 2026 report cites Google as having emerged from a National Science Foundation grant to Stanford professors who were conducting fundamental research on digital libraries in 1993 — when there were 100 total websites on Earth — as an example of how university-based basic research seeds future commercial innovation [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- Four of the eight authors of "Multi-User Large Language Model Agents" — Hao Zhu, José Ramón Enríquez, Alex Pentland, and corresponding co-author Jiaxin Pei — list Stanford University as their primary or joint institutional affiliation [[sources/pdf-shu-yang-2026-multi-user-large]].
