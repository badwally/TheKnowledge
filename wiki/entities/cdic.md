# Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC)

## Summary

The Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) is the Canadian federal Crown corporation that provides deposit insurance for eligible deposits held at CDIC member institutions. CDIC coverage is free and automatic — depositors do not sign up to receive protection — and eligible deposits are protected up to $100,000 per deposit insurance category, with deposits held in trust treated as a separate category whose protection can extend to each beneficiary of the trust when disclosure requirements are met [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]]. CDIC eligibility is one of the two qualifying insurance regimes (alongside the Credit Union Deposit Insurance Corporation of British Columbia) under BC Strata Property Regulation §6.11(b)(i) for term deposits and GICs held as eligible investments by a BC strata corporation's contingency reserve fund or special levy [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].

## Key facts

- CDIC coverage is free and automatic — depositors do not have to sign up to receive protection [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- Eligible deposits are protected up to $100,000 per deposit insurance category [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- Eligible deposits include savings accounts, chequing accounts, GICs or other term deposits, money orders, certified cheques, and bank drafts issued by CDIC members [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- CDIC protects deposits held in trust separately from deposits in other deposit insurance categories — the trust-deposit category does not consume any other CDIC coverage category at the same member institution [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- CDIC's trust-deposit protection applies to the trustee (the depositor) but can extend to each beneficiary of the trust deposit if key information is disclosed by the trustee to the CDIC member institution [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- For deposits held in trust, CDIC aggregates all deposits placed by the trustee for the same beneficiary at the failed CDIC member institution; the total is insured to a limit of $100,000 per beneficiary if the trustee meets CDIC's disclosure rules [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- Where no beneficiary information is provided on the records of the Member Institution, CDIC only protects up to $100,000 in the name of the trustee (the depositor) [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- CDIC trust-deposit protection is conditional on two requirements: (1) a valid trust established with or without formal documentation under the laws of the province or territory in which the trust is established; and (2) required information about the trustee and the beneficiaries is disclosed on the records of the CDIC member institution prior to the failure of a member institution [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- CDIC relies on the latest information disclosed on the records of the Member Institution to make an insurance determination [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- CDIC recognizes three distinct trustee categories with differing disclosure requirements: a trustee (e.g., a grandmother who deposits money in trust for her grandchildren), a professional trustee (e.g., a lawyer holding a down payment in trust for clients buying a house), and a nominee broker (a broker holding a GIC in nominee name for clients) [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- Nominee brokers face specific disclosure requirements set out in CDIC's "Changes to deposit insurance affecting Nominee Brokers" guidance [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- BC Strata Property Regulation §6.11(b)(i) requires that term deposits and GICs held by a strata corporation be insured by CDIC or by the Credit Union Deposit Insurance Corporation of British Columbia in order to qualify as eligible investments for the strata corporation's CRF or special levy [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
