# Bryan Kelly

## Summary

Finance researcher jointly affiliated with the Yale School of Management, AQR Capital Management, and the NBER; first-listed author of the 2023 SSRN survey "Financial Machine Learning" (with Dacheng Xiu), which surveys the literature on machine learning in financial markets and argues that finance is fertile soil for machine learning because of large conditioning information sets and ambiguous functional forms [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].

## Key facts

- Listed institutional affiliations on the paper: Yale School of Management, AQR Capital Management, and NBER [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- Listed contact email: bryan.kelly@yale.edu [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- First-listed author of "Financial Machine Learning" (2023, SSRN abstract id 4501707), co-authored with Dacheng Xiu of the University of Chicago Booth School of Business [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- The paper is designed for both financial economists interested in grasping machine learning tools and statisticians/machine learners seeking interesting financial contexts where advanced methods may be deployed [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- Argues that the modern analysis of financial markets centers on the Euler equation P = E[M X | I] and that, in words, "prices are predictions" reflecting investors' best guesses for the discounted future payoffs of an asset [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- Argues two conditions of finance research make it fertile soil for machine learning: large conditioning information sets and ambiguous functional forms [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- Adopts the definition of machine learning from Gu et al. (2020b): (i) a diverse collection of high-dimensional models for statistical prediction, (ii) regularization methods for model selection and mitigation of overfit, and (iii) efficient algorithms for searching among a vast number of potential model specifications [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- Argues that machine learning need not be differentiated from econometrics or statistics more generally, citing Israel et al. (2020) for the observation that many ideas underlying machine learning have lived comfortably under the umbrella of statistics for decades [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- The paper acknowledges Doron Avramov, Max Helman, Antti Ilmanen, Sydney Ludvigson, Yueran Ma, Semyon Malamud, Tobias Moskowitz, Lasse Pedersen, Markus Pelger, Seth Pruitt, and Guofu Zhou for helpful comments [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- States that the views expressed in the paper are those of the authors and not necessarily those of AQR Capital Management [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
