# BCFSA Advisory 24-014

## Summary

BCFSA Advisory 24-014 — 'Strata Property Regulation Changes - Strata Depreciation Reports' — is the May 21, 2024 regulator-curated interpretive advisory published by the BC Financial Services Authority notifying real estate licensees and developers of amendments to the Strata Property Regulation under the Strata Property Act affecting depreciation report requirements for strata corporations with five or more strata lots, with the amendments effective July 1, 2024 subject to transitional provisions carried by Order of the Lieutenant Governor in Council 204 - 2024 [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]]. The Advisory replaces an earlier Advisory 24-012 (May 6, 2024) on the same subject, updated specifically to clarify disclosure requirements for real estate developers regarding strata depreciation reports — establishing a fifteen-day reissuance window pattern that operationalizes BCFSA's regulator-curated interpretive iteration channel [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].

### Supersession history

- This Advisory replaces Advisory 24-012 issued on May 6, 2024 [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- The reissuance window between Advisories 24-012 and 24-014 was fifteen days [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- The stated reason for the reissuance: 'It has been updated to clarify disclosure requirements for real estate developers regarding strata depreciation reports' [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].

### Background framing

- Depreciation reports are framed by BCFSA as 'comprehensive assessments of the condition of strata buildings and infrastructure, aiding in long-term financial planning and maintenance strategies for strata corporations' [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- The amendments to the Strata Property Regulation are characterized as serving three purposes: (1) discontinue indefinite deferrals of strata depreciation reports; (2) lengthen the timeline for acquiring new depreciation reports; (3) increase the quality of new depreciation reports [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].

### Four substantive regulatory amendments enumerated

- (1) Remove the ability of strata corporations to indefinitely defer getting a depreciation report by obtaining an annual three-quarters vote [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- (2) Require strata corporations to obtain a depreciation report every five years instead of every three years — establishing the historical pre-July-2024 cadence as every three years and the post-amendment cadence as every five years [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- (3) Require strata corporations to obtain depreciation reports from a list of qualified professionals [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- (4) Require developers to provide new strata corporations (established on or after July 1, 2027 with five or more strata lots) with funding for depreciation reports — required funding minimum $5,000, plus $200 per strata lot, up to a maximum of $30,000, paid into the strata contingency reserve fund no later than the date of the strata corporation's first annual general meeting [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].

### Effective dates and transitional provisions

- The amendments come into effect on July 1, 2024 [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- Although the amendments come into effect on July 1, 2024, there are transitional provisions specifying dates by which various existing or new strata corporations must obtain depreciation reports [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- The transitional provisions are formally carried in Order of the Lieutenant Governor in Council 204 - 2024 [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- Developers are required to provide funding for depreciation reports effective July 1, 2027 — a three-year regulatory-lag window between the regulation's effective date and the developer-funding-obligation effective date [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].

### Licensee considerations

- Real estate licensees, especially those licensed to provide strata management services, should familiarize themselves with the regulatory amendments [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- Transitional provisions provide strata corporations time to obtain a depreciation report; licensees working with strata councils should carefully review the timelines [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].

### Real estate developer considerations and REDMA disclosure bridge

- Developers filing disclosure statements or disclosure statement amendments under the Real Estate Development Marketing Act (REDMA), for development properties completed on or after July 1, 2027, should ensure the disclosure statement provides for the developer's contribution to the strata contingency reserve fund for a depreciation report [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- If a developer has already filed a disclosure statement with BCFSA but does not expect to complete the development property until on or after July 1, 2027, the developer may be required to file a disclosure statement amendment if the Strata Property Regulation changes would result in a misrepresentation in the disclosure statement [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
- The REDMA misrepresentation-driven amendment-trigger is the operative enforcement mechanism BCFSA uses to propagate the SPA developer-funding obligation into pre-sale compliance for development properties completing on or after July 1, 2027 [[sources/docx-f70cc64d728a]].
