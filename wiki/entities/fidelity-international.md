# Fidelity International

## Summary

Global asset manager whose late-2022 Investment Outlook 2023 — "Navigating the polycrisis" — frames 2023 as a year shaped by a confluence of monetary tightening, geopolitical conflict, energy crisis, and deglobalisation, and argues that a hard landing remains the most likely outcome rather than the soft landing markets are pricing [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].

## Key facts

- Published "Investment Outlook 2023: Navigating the polycrisis" in late 2022 ahead of the new year [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Anne Richards is the CEO of Fidelity International and authored the foreword to the outlook [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Andrew McCaffery is the Global CIO of Asset Management at Fidelity International and authored the Overview chapter [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Salman Ahmed is the Global Head of Macro and Strategic Asset Allocation at Fidelity International and authored the Macro chapter [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Henk-Jan Rikkerink is the Global Head of Solutions and Multi Asset at Fidelity International and authored the Multi asset chapter [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- The outlook is structured into chapters on Macro, Multi asset, Equities, Fixed income, Private credit, and Real estate [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Fidelity's house view in the outlook is that a hard landing remains the most likely outcome in 2023, against market hopes for a soft landing [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- The outlook recommends a defensive posture as the firm's near-term stance until volatility subsides — favouring cash, government bonds, and uncorrelated assets [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Identifies decarbonisation and reindustrialisation as longer-term themes that could draw investor attention sooner rather than later [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Maintains proprietary activity trackers that, through Q4 2022, indicated a continuing slowdown with a recession likely in the US and near certain in Europe and the UK [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Recommends emerging markets and Asian countries (with weaker growth correlation to the US and Europe) as a way to increase diversification, while cash and quality investment-grade securities offer defensive characteristics [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
