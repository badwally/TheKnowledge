# Martin Martens

## Summary

Quantitative researcher at Robeco Quantitative Investing in Rotterdam; co-author of the 2020 paper "Hedging demand and market intraday momentum," which establishes that the last-half-hour futures return is positively predicted by the rest-of-day return across all major asset classes, and that the predictability mean-reverts over the next three days — a signature of transitory price pressure consistent with hedging rather than informed trading [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].

## Key facts

- Affiliated with Robeco Quantitative Investing, Weena 850, 3014 DA Rotterdam, on the paper [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
- Co-author of "Hedging demand and market intraday momentum" (26 August 2020; SSRN abstract 3760365) with Guido Baltussen, Zhi Da, and Sten Lammers [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
- The paper notes that for equities, bonds, and commodities the next-three-day reversal of last-half-hour returns is highly statistically significant — consistent with transitory price pressure from hedging — whereas an informed-trading explanation would predict a permanent price impact [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
- The authors further argue that the predictability of rest-of-day returns does not extend to S&P 500 futures returns beyond 4pm ET (when most options and leveraged ETFs settle), which is hard to reconcile with an informed-trading channel since futures keep trading actively until 4:15pm [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
