# Tom Hougaard

## Summary

Financial trader and author of "Best Loser Wins" (subtitled "Freedom Road"), in which he argues that consistent profitability in markets requires an internal psychological transformation — being "exceptionally good at losing" — rather than better tools, software, or analysis [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].

## Key facts

- Holds a master's degree in Economics; began his career in 1997 at Chase Manhattan Bank as an analyst [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
- Three years later, left Chase with savings to begin his "business of dreams" as a day trader; lost it all in thirteen months [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
- Joined a CFD broker in London with the official title of market analyst, with a large part of his role serving as the educational bridge between the company and trading clients [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
- Headhunted to a second London brokerage in 2002, where he spent the next seven years working with trading clients [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
- Reports observing over 100,000 people and close to 100 million trades over a decade on the London trading floor, watching markets from 7am to 9pm [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
- Now trades full-time from his own four-screen office, with no employer, no debt, and no sick pay; battles markets from 5am until 9pm and sometimes late into the night [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
- Self-summary: "I am here because of my relationship with pain" — claims his edge is being "exceptionally good at losing" rather than IQ, technical analysis, software, insider information, or a secret charting method [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
- Defines success personally as freedom — "the freedom to spend time with my family and live life on my terms" — rather than money [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
