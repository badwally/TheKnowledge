# Quebec Civil Code — Divided Co-Ownership Contingency Fund Study Regime

## Summary

The Quebec Civil Code divided-co-ownership reserve-fund regime — codified in the Civil Code of Québec as amended by Bill 16 (2019, chapter 28) — is Quebec's primary statutory analog to the BC Strata Property Act depreciation-report regime, the Ontario Condominium Act 1998 reserve-fund regime, the NB Condominium Property Act c16.05 reserve-fund-study regime, the NL Condominium Regulations NLR 80/11 reserve-fund-study regime, the California Davis-Stirling Act § 5550 reserve-study regime, and the Florida SIRS regime [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]]. Under Bill 16's Civil Code amendments, syndicates of co-owners of immovables under divided co-ownership are subject to additional obligations including (i) keeping a maintenance log for each immovable and (ii) obtaining a contingency fund study that establishes the sums necessary for the fund to be sufficient to cover major repairs and replacement of common portions, with the co-owners' annual contribution to the contingency fund determined on the basis of such a study [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]]. The regime is the load-bearing Quebec statutory anchor referenced by the project's filter policy ("Civil Code art. 1071 / Bill 16") and the regulatory floor for any Quebec expansion of the ADR-0004 Canada-first GTM [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

## Key facts

### Statutory authority and enactment

- The regime is established by Bill 16 (2019, chapter 28), introduced 3 April 2019, passed in principle 16 May 2019, passed 5 December 2019, and assented to 11 December 2019 by the 42nd Legislature of the Parliament of Québec [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- The Civil Code of Québec is one of the fifteen statutes amended by Bill 16; the Civil Code amendments operate on the rules governing immovables under divided co-ownership [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

### Maintenance log requirement

- Each syndicate of co-owners is required to keep a maintenance log for each immovable [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- The maintenance-log requirement is the Quebec statutory analog to BC Housing's Maintenance Matters #11 "Maintenance and Renewals Plan" framework and operationalizes a per-immovable maintenance-records artifact that is structurally usable as a work-order ingest covariate in the condo-capital-infra engine [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

### Contingency fund study requirement

- Each syndicate of co-owners is required to obtain a contingency fund study that establishes the sums necessary for the fund to be sufficient to cover major repairs and the replacement of common portions [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- The co-owners' annual contribution to the contingency fund must be determined on the basis of the contingency fund study — making the contingency fund study the statutorily required input to setting common-expenses contributions, analogous in function to the role of the depreciation report under BC SPA, the reserve fund study under ON Condo Act 1998, and the reserve fund study under NB CPA c16.05 / NL NLR 80/11 [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

### Disclosure to promisors / pre-sale information

- Each syndicate must provide certain information on the immovable and the syndicate to a promisor who promises to buy a fraction of an immovable under divided co-ownership [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- The Government is given the power to determine information other than that required under the Civil Code that must be included in the memorandum or in the preliminary contract — a regulation-making delegation that allows the disclosure perimeter to be extended without further legislative amendment [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

### Builder / developer deposit protection and budget-forecast underestimation

- A builder or developer must protect the deposits paid by buyers of a fraction of an immovable under co-ownership [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- A developer who has underestimated amounts in a budget forecast must pay certain amounts to the syndicate — a Quebec-specific developer-liability provision that contrasts with the NB regime, where the CMHC NB provincial fact sheet documents that "New Brunswick legislation does not include rules governing the financial consequences of a developer inaccurately estimating the operational costs of a condominium" [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]] [[sources/web-2018-03-31-c63]].

### Operation of co-ownerships, general meetings, and common-expenses clarifications

- Bill 16's Civil Code amendments introduce various measures concerning the operation of co-ownerships and amend certain rules governing general meetings of the co-owners [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- The rules governing co-owners' contributions for common portions for restricted use are clarified [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Civil Code terminology with regard to common expenses is standardized and certain interpretation difficulties are resolved [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

### Building Act bridge to syndicate obligations

- Bill 16 inserts section 9.1 into the Building Act: "For the purposes of this Act, a syndicate of co-owners is considered an owner with respect to the responsibilities conferred on the syndicate under the Civil Code" — the statutory bridge that propagates Civil Code syndicate obligations into the Building Act enforcement perimeter administered by the Régie du bâtiment du Québec [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

### Cross-jurisdictional comparator framing

- The Quebec regime requires a contingency fund study (terminology used in Bill 16's explanatory notes) — the Civil Code linguistic counterpart to BC SPA's "depreciation report," ON Condo Act 1998's "reserve fund study," NB CPA c16.05's "reserve fund study," NL NLR 80/11's "reserve fund study," California Davis-Stirling § 5550's "reserve study," and Florida's "Structural Integrity Reserve Study" [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Unlike the BC SPA Regulation 6.2's six-category qualified-provider restriction, the NB CPA c16.05's CMHC-described "appraiser or engineer" set, and the NL NLR 80/11 section 3 four-category qualified-provider list, the Bill 16 explanatory notes do not surface a qualified-preparer professional set for the contingency fund study — the Civil Code text and any associated regulations remain the binding constraint and are not visible in the Bill 16 explanatory notes [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Unlike the BC SPA's 5-year cadence, NB CPA c16.05's 10-year primary cadence with 3-year updates, NL NLR 80/11 subsection 49(5)-driven renewal cadence, and California Davis-Stirling § 5550's triennial visual-inspection cadence, the Bill 16 explanatory notes do not surface a cadence for the contingency fund study — the Civil Code text and any associated regulations remain the binding constraint and are not visible in the explanatory notes [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- Unlike Florida SIRS's 8-component scope and the condo-capital-infra engine's 6-probabilistic-component scope (roof, building envelope, plumbing risers, HVAC central plant, elevators, parking deck / podium structure), the Bill 16 explanatory notes frame the contingency fund study at the higher-level abstraction of "major repairs and the replacement of common portions" without enumerating prescribed component categories — leaving the operational component-list discretion to the contingency-fund-study preparer and any subordinate regulation [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].

### Implication for condo-capital-infra GTM

- The regime is the regulatory floor for any Quebec expansion of the ADR-0004 Canada-first GTM sequence; the maintenance-log requirement plus the contingency-fund-study-driven annual contribution rule together establish the same structural funding-adequacy framing as the BC SPA, ON Condo Act 1998, NB CPA c16.05, and NL NLR 80/11 regimes and create a Quebec addressable market parallel to the Atlantic-Canada and Ontario expansion arms [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]].
- The developer-budget-forecast-underestimation-liability provision creates a Quebec-specific litigation-risk channel for misestimated reserve contributions in newly-registered Quebec condominiums — a positioning angle for an AI-native reserve-study firm offering budget-forecast-defensibility services that has no direct analog in NB (which lacks an equivalent provision per CMHC NB) [[sources/pdf-national-assembly-2020-bill-16-assented]] [[sources/web-2018-03-31-c63]].
