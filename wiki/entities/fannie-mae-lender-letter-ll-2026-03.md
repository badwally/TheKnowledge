# Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2026-03 (and Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-C)

## Summary

Lender Letter LL-2026-03 is the Fannie Mae policy instrument — issued March 18, 2026 in coordination with Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-C — that revises GSE project standards and property insurance requirements for condominium loans. The combined guidance retires the limited review process (effective August 3, 2026), raises the minimum reserve funding requirement from 10% to 15% of annual budget (effective January 4, 2027), eliminates the 50% investor concentration limit, retires Florida-specific new-project review rules, restructures property insurance flexibility, and imposes a $50,000-per-unit deductible cap (effective July 1) along with new servicer obligations fully effective by January 1, 2027 [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].

## Key facts

- Issued March 18, 2026 by Fannie Mae as Lender Letter LL-2026-03; Freddie Mac issued parallel guidance as Bulletin 2026-C the same day [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Reflects the realities of today's housing market and focuses on the long-term financial sustainability of condominium communities based on Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac criteria [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- CAI Advocacy characterizes the GSE criteria as not always aligned with actual financial stability and performance [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Retires the limited review process; full elimination applies to loans with application dates on or after August 3, 2026 [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Limited review historically represented roughly 40% of all project reviews, so the retirement shifts a significant portion of transactions toward full review and increases administrative burden on associations, managers, and volunteer leaders [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Going forward, lenders rely on either full review or waiver of project review; waiver eligibility is expanded to projects with up to 10 units [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Eliminates the long-standing 50% investor concentration limit [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Retires Florida-specific review requirements for new, attached condominium projects [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Raises the minimum reserve funding requirement from 10% to 15% of the annual budget, effective January 4, 2027 [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Provides a reserve-study carve-out: 15% allocation is not required if the association has a reserve study conducted or updated within the last three years and is following the highest recommended level of funding (baseline funding is not allowed) [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Eliminates strict replacement cost documentation requirements for property insurance [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Removes the requirement to insure roofs at full replacement cost [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Introduces greater flexibility in how coverage sufficiency is determined [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Removes the inflation guard requirement for condominium association property insurance [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Caps deductibles at a new maximum of $50,000 per unit, effective July 1 [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Requires unit owners to carry insurance (usually H06) when gaps exist in the master policy or when deductibles apply; coverage must align with either interior exposure or the master policy deductible [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Introduces new loan-servicer expectations: annual verification of insurance coverage, monitoring for reductions in coverage, and a new requirement to remind borrowers annually to maintain insurance — fully effective by January 1, 2027 [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Has the potential, per CAI Advocacy, to slow transactions and strain already-constrained communities without thoughtful implementation and support [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
