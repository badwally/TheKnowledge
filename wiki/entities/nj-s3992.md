# New Jersey S3992 (Amendments to Structural Integrity and Reserve Funding Act)

## Summary

S3992 is the New Jersey statute signed into law by Governor Phil Murphy on August 21, 2025 that amends and clarifies S2760 — the Structural Integrity and Reserve Funding legislation signed into law in January 2024 — for planned real estate developments. S3992 defines the previously-undefined term "adequacy," requires inclusion of a zero-dollar 30-year balance plan in every capital reserve funding plan, provides a transitional 85%-funding option (capped at five fiscal years) for associations existing as of January 8, 2024, imposes new buyer disclosure obligations on sellers, and removes the prior S2760 language granting associations authority to impose a special assessment or obtain a loan when a capital asset's useful life ends sooner than projected [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].

## Key facts

- Signed into law by NJ Governor Phil Murphy on August 21, 2025 [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Amends and clarifies S2760, the Structural Integrity and Reserve Funding legislation signed into law in January 2024 [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Defines "adequate" and "adequacy" as a sum of money held by an association sufficient to balance the association's reserve fund such that the fund is never projected to fall below zero dollars in the 30-year capital reserve funding plan [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Amends the prior statutory phrase "covered building owners" to "an association of a planned real estate development" for purposes of reserve-study obligations [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Requires inclusion of a zero-dollar balance plan with all capital reserve funding plans; the zero-dollar plan must show a 30-year funding plan that permits the reserve to reach zero but not fall below it [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Allows associations existing as of January 8, 2024 to fund either according to the most recent capital reserve study or at 85% of the recommended plan [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Requires 20-point bold-font notice to all unit owners when an association uses the 85% funding option, including the year a special assessment or loan is anticipated and the anticipated amount [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Caps the 85% funding option at five fiscal years, after which the association must transition to a plan satisfying the zero-dollar requirement [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Requires sellers of units within a planned real estate development to provide the buyer with the most recent unit-owner notice regarding reduced capital reserve funding before the sale completes [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Removes the prior S2760 language granting an association authority to impose a special assessment or obtain a loan if a capital asset reached the end of its useful life sooner than projected in the reserve study [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Stated legislative intent: provide clarity to existing law while providing relief for associations that have been historically underfunded [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
