# Philipp Lisibach

## Summary

Head of Global Investment Strategy at Credit Suisse; co-author of the editorial in the Credit Suisse Investment Outlook 2023 articulating CS's view that the global economy has undergone a fundamental and lasting reset and that 2023 will be a year in which fixed income reasserts diversification value and bond/equity performance again diverges [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].

## Key facts

- Listed title at Credit Suisse: Head of Global Investment Strategy [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- Co-authored the editorial in the Investment Outlook 2023 with Michael Strobaek and Nannette Hechler-Fayd'herbe [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- States that the 'Great Transition' CS foresaw for 2022 played out to a much greater extent than originally envisioned, resulting in a new reality [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- Argues the global economy has undergone a fundamental and lasting reset due to the COVID-19 pandemic, shifting demographics, climate change, and weakening business investment in the wake of geopolitical ruptures [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- Forecasts the global economy will grow at a much slower pace than in the 2010-2019 period [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- Argues inflation will remain an issue in 2023, though it is expected to eventually peak and start to decline [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- Argues that as inflation peaks and monetary policy reaches restrictive territory, fixed income should become more attractive again, which means the performance of bonds and equities should again diverge [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- Argues equity markets could still be volatile in the first half of 2023 as slower economic growth hits company earnings [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
