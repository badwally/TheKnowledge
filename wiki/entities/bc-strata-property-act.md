# British Columbia Strata Property Act (SPA)

## Summary

The British Columbia Strata Property Act (SPA) is the provincial statute governing strata corporations in British Columbia. The SPA permits a strata corporation to invest its contingency reserve fund (CRF) and special levies in insured accounts with savings institutions in British Columbia, plus those permitted by the regulations [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]]. Section 95 governs the CRF and the treatment of interest earned on CRF money; Section 108 governs special levies. Strata Property Regulation §6.11 — made under the SPA — defines the eligible-investment universe for both CRF and special-levy money [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]]. The §6.11 regime has been amended twice in the modern era: B.C. Reg. 68/2014 modernized and simplified the eligible-investment definitions effective July 16, 2014, and Order in Council 101/2019 (March 6, 2019) further amended §6.11 alongside the §1.1 definitions section to clarify that partial deposit-insurance coverage is sufficient for compliance [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]]. SPA §35 imposes a records-retention duty on strata corporations that extends to copies of any income tax returns filed, with Strata Property Regulation §4.1(3) setting a six-year minimum retention period; SPA §36 provides owners and authorized agents a corresponding right of inspection and copying [[sources/web-2025-10-20-a96]].

## Key facts

- Permits a strata corporation to invest its contingency reserve fund and special levies in insured accounts with savings institutions in British Columbia, plus those permitted by the regulations [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- Section 95 (referenced as §95(2)(a) by the regulation) governs CRF investments and requires that any interest or income earned on money in the CRF become part of the fund and be reported as part of the strata corporation revenue, with no other use permitted [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- Section 108 (referenced as §108(4)(b)(i) by the regulation) governs special levies; interest earned on a special levy becomes part of the special levy [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- Requires a strata corporation to account for the money collected for a special levy separately from other money of the strata corporation [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- Contributions to the CRF are determined annually as part of the budget approval process at the annual general meeting [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- A special levy is money raised by the owners for any purpose established by a 3/4-vote resolution passed by the strata corporation at an annual or special general meeting [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- If money collected for a special levy (including interest earned) exceeds the amount required for the resolution and no owner is entitled to receive a refund from contributions made, the strata corporation may deposit the excess in the CRF [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- Owners may by a majority vote direct the strata council in its exercise of powers and performance of duties [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- The §6.11 eligible-investment regime made under the SPA defines 'insured account' at §1.1; the 'insured account' definition was added by Order in Council 101/2019 (effective March 6, 2019) to mean an account with a deposit, all or part of which is eligible to be insured by CDIC or guaranteed by CUDIC [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- Order in Council 101/2019 also substituted the text of §6.11(a) and the prefatory language of §6.11(b) to extend the 'all or part' partial-eligibility standard uniformly across savings/chequing accounts at out-of-BC financial institutions, term deposits, and GICs [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The Ministry of Housing characterized the OIC 101/2019 amendment as a clarification responsive to a multi-year interpretive dispute under which some strata corporations had read §6.11 to require dividing investments across many accounts to keep each balance under the $100,000 CDIC insurance limit [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The OIC 101/2019 amendment took effect immediately on the date the order in council was made [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].

### Tax-record retention (§35 + Reg 4.1(3)) and inspection (§36)

- SPA §35 requires the strata corporation to retain copies of any income tax returns filed [[sources/web-2025-10-20-a96]].
- Strata Property Regulation §4.1(3) sets the minimum retention period for those tax records at six years; this includes the T2, T1044, and any supporting documents [[sources/web-2025-10-20-a96]].
- SPA §36 provides that any owner — or a person authorized by an owner — may inspect or request copies of records referred to in §35, including income tax returns [[sources/web-2025-10-20-a96]].
- The §36 inspection right operates by reference: it inherits whatever records are governed by §35, so the tax-return retention obligation and the tax-return inspection right travel together [[sources/web-2025-10-20-a96]].

### Strata-corporation legal-formation construct

- A BC strata corporation is established at the time the strata plan is deposited in a Land Title Office; the strata plan is the corporation's legal-formation document, not an incorporation document [[sources/web-2025-10-20-a96]].
- The legal-formation construct is operationally relevant when a strata corporation registers for a CRA Business Number, because CRA registration channels (e.g., Business Registration Online) presume an incorporation-document framework that does not match the strata-plan construct [[sources/web-2025-10-20-a96]].

### Federal tax interaction

- A BC strata corporation, like other Canadian corporations, must file the T2 corporate income tax return annually under §150 of the federal *Income Tax Act* regardless of taxable income, and most residential stratas qualify as non-profit organizations under §149(1)(l) such that interest earned on CRF, GICs, and other bank balances is generally non-taxable and becomes part of those funds [[sources/web-2025-10-20-a96]].
- A BC strata corporation that operates a commercial business (e.g., hotel, golf course) or that generates revenue such as renting a clubhouse to non-owners or leasing roof space for communication relay stations may jeopardize its NPO status and may have to report revenue and pay tax [[sources/web-2025-10-20-a96]].
- Residential strata corporations are not generally required to collect and remit GST on strata fees, because they qualify as NPOs under the federal *Income Tax Act*; GST may apply to commercial strata lots, strata lots in 'strata hotels' used for short-term rental accommodation, and stratas operating commercial businesses [[sources/web-2025-10-20-a96]].
