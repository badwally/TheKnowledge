# James P. O'Shaughnessy

## Summary

Quantitative investor and author of "What Works on Wall Street"; cited by Joel Greenblatt as the canonical example of how investors can be too emotionally involved to follow their own systems — having underperformed the market by 25% over three years after starting a fund in 1996-1997 and then selling his company to Hennessy Funds at the end of 1999, just before the bubble he had bet against collapsed [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].

## Key facts

- Author of the book "What Works on Wall Street," recommended by Greenblatt as having borne out its wisdom [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Started a fund in 1996-1997 based on the strategies in his book; the fund underperformed the market by 25% over its first three years [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Sold his company to Hennessy Funds at the end of 1999 — "the bottom of the cycle" in Greenblatt's framing — after the strategy failed to surpass the bubble's returns [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Greenblatt summarizes the lesson as: "the guy who wrote the book quit his system" — investors are often too emotionally involved to have the discipline to see the strategy through [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Two patented Hennessy Funds based on O'Shaughnessy's top strategies subsequently outperformed: HFCGX (Cornerstone Growth) returned an average of 13.44% per year vs. the Vanguard 500's -2.01% per year over 6/1/00 through 5/31/05; HFCVX (Cornerstone Value) returned 6.47% per year over the same period [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
