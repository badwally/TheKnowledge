# "Should We Treat Data as Labor? Moving beyond 'Free'" (Arrieta-Ibarra et al., 2018)

## Summary

"Should We Treat Data as Labor? Moving beyond 'Free'" is a 2018 paper by Imanol Arrieta-Ibarra, Leonard Goff, Diego Jiménez-Hernández, Jaron Lanier, and E. Glen Weyl, published in AEA Papers and Proceedings, volume 108, pages 38–42 [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]]. The paper argues that user data in the digital economy is typically treated as capital created by corporations observing willing individuals, and proposes instead treating data (at least partially) as labor to restore a functioning market for user contributions [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].

## Key facts

- Published May 2018 in AEA Papers and Proceedings 108: 38–42, DOI 10.1257/pandp.20181003 [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].
- Authored by Imanol Arrieta-Ibarra, Leonard Goff, Diego Jiménez-Hernández, Jaron Lanier, and E. Glen Weyl [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].
- JEL classifications: C80 (Data Collection and Data Estimation Methodology), D63 (Equity, Justice, Inequality, and Other Normative Criteria), and J42 (Monopsony; Segmented Labor Markets) [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].
- Diagnoses four harms of treating data as capital: it neglects users' roles in creating data, reduces incentives for users, distributes the gains from the data economy unequally, and stokes fears of automation [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].
- Proposes that treating data (at least partially) as labor could resolve these issues and restore a functioning market for user contributions [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].
- Acknowledges that the data-as-labor frame may run against the near-term interests of dominant data monopsonists who have benefited from data being treated as "free" [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].
- Identifies three forms of countervailing power to restore balance: competition, a data labor movement, and thoughtful regulation [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].
