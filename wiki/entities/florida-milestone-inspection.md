# Florida Milestone Inspection

## Summary

A Florida-mandated structural inspection of aging residential condominium and cooperative buildings codified at Florida Statutes § 553.899 (Section 1803 of the Florida Building Code), legally distinct from the SIRS reserve study but commonly paired with it. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]] CS/CS/HB 913 (2025) overlays the milestone-inspection statute with a local-government confirmation regime, a mandatory local ordinance requiring repair commencement within 365 days of a phase two report, an electronic-reporting pipeline from local enforcement agencies to DBPR (and onward to OPPAGA), and an enforcement gate by which a building may be deemed unsafe for human occupancy if repairs do not commence within the required window. [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]]

## Key facts

- Statutorily defined as a structural inspection of an aging building used to determine if substantial structural deterioration exists. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- Applies to all residential condominium and cooperative buildings (including mixed-ownership buildings) of three or more habitable stories. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- Triggered by building age measured from the date the certificate of occupancy was issued: 30 years and every 10 years thereafter, or 25 years and every 10 years thereafter if the local enforcement agency has determined that local circumstances require the initial inspection at 25 years instead of 30. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- Buildings that reached 30 years of age before July 1, 2022 were required to have an initial milestone inspection performed by December 31, 2024. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- Buildings reaching 30 years of age between July 1, 2022 and December 31, 2024 must have the initial milestone inspection performed before December 31, 2025. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- Single-family, two-family, three-family, and four-family dwellings with three or fewer habitable stories above ground are exempt. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- Performed by a licensed architect, licensed engineer, or by a team of professionals with a licensed architect or licensed engineer acting as the registered design professional in responsible charge. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- The condominium or cooperative association and any owner of a portion of the building not owned by the association are responsible for arranging and paying for the inspection. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- The local enforcement agency (typically the local building department) must notify the association and any other building owners of the required inspection by certified mail per Section 1803 of the Florida Building Code. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- The association must then notify unit owners of the required inspection within 14 days after receiving the local enforcement agency notice. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- Two-phase structure: Phase 1 is a visual assessment to determine whether evidence of substantial structural deterioration is present and produces a report submitted to the local enforcement agency; Phase 2 is required only if Phase 1 identifies substantial structural deterioration, and may involve destructive testing along with a recommended program for assessing and repairing damage. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- The required milestone inspection report must include a list of material findings and recommendations, a description of how the inspection was performed, an identification of any substantial structural deterioration with repair recommendations, a determination of whether unsafe or dangerous conditions were identified, recommendations for remedial or preventive repair of non-substantial deterioration, identification of items requiring further inspection, a separate summary of findings, and the signature and seal of the licensed professional who performed the inspection. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- The condominium or cooperative association must distribute the inspector-prepared summary report to each unit owner within 45 days of receiving the report, post the summary in a conspicuous place, and publish both the full report and the inspector-prepared summary on the association's website if the association is required to maintain one. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- The building owner must commence repairs of structural deficiencies identified by a Phase 2 milestone inspection within 365 days after receiving the Phase 2 report, unless the local governing body requires repairs to commence earlier. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- Once repairs are completed, a professional must reinspect the building and provide an amended report stating that all required repairs have been completed and the building is acceptable for continued occupancy. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- The local enforcement agency may extend the initial milestone inspection deadline if the owner has contracted with an architect or engineer but cannot reasonably complete the inspection before the deadline, or if other circumstances justify an extension. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- Associations required to complete a milestone inspection on or before December 31, 2026 may complete the SIRS simultaneously with the milestone inspection; in that case the SIRS must be completed by December 31, 2026. [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]
- Florida Building Code questions about the program go to the Florida Building Commission at 850-487-1824 (www.floridabuilding.org). [[sources/web-2024-07-09-f2a]]

### CS/CS/HB 913 (2025) — local-government and ordinance overlay

- Each local government that has authorized and permitted construction of a building three habitable stories or more in height subject to the condominium form of ownership as a residential condominium must confirm with each condominium association whether the SIRS and milestone inspection have been completed for each building on the condominium property and filed with the Division, or whether the condominium has failed to complete and file the requisite report. [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]]
- Each local government must report to the Division by December 31, 2025 its findings on whether each condominium association within the local government's jurisdiction has completed or failed to complete the SIRS or milestone inspection. [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]]
- A board of county commissioners or municipal governing body must adopt an ordinance requiring that a condominium association schedule or commence repairs for substantial structural deterioration within a specified timeframe after the local enforcement agency receives a phase two inspection report; the bill specifies that repairs must be commenced within 365 days after receiving the report. [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]]
- If an owner of the building fails to submit proof to the local enforcement agency that repairs have been scheduled or have commenced for substantial structural deterioration identified in a phase two inspection report within the required timeframe, the local enforcement agency must review and determine if the building is unsafe for human occupancy. [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]]
- On or before October 1, 2025 and on or before December 31 each year thereafter, the local enforcement agency responsible for milestone inspections must provide DBPR — in an electronic format determined by DBPR — the number of buildings required to have a milestone inspection within the local enforcement agency's jurisdiction; the number for which a phase one milestone inspection has been completed; the number granted an extension of time; the number required to have a phase two milestone inspection; the number for which a phase two milestone inspection has been completed; the number, type, and value of permits applied for to complete repairs based on a phase two milestone inspection; a list of buildings deemed unsafe or uninhabitable based on a milestone inspection; and the license number of the building code administrator responsible for milestone inspections for the local enforcement agency. [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]]
- DBPR must provide to the Office of Program Policy Analysis and Government Accountability (OPPAGA) all information obtained from the local enforcement agencies, and OPPAGA may request additional information from a local enforcement agency. [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]]
- Under HB 913, it is a conflict of interest for any person who performs a milestone inspection to provide services for the repair or replacement of the condominium property that was the subject of the inspection, or to have a financial interest in the entity or person providing the repair or replacement services. [[sources/pdf-fletcher-olivia-2025-h0913c-com]]
