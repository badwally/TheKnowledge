# James Knightley

## Summary

Chief International Economist for the Americas at ING; contributing author of the December 8, 2022 "ING global economic outlook 2023," responsible for the US chapter "Markets doubt the Fed's intent" [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].

## Key facts

- Listed title at ING: Chief International Economist, Americas [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Listed contact email: james.knightley@ing.com [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Argues the Fed's hawkish rhetoric in late 2022 is the result of concern that recent steep falls in Treasury yields and the dollar, coupled with a narrowing of credit spreads, is loosening financial conditions — "the exact opposite of what the Fed wants to see" [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- States the Fed's forward guidance "needs to be taken with huge handfuls of salt given their recent track record" [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Argues softer core inflation prints in October 2022 combined with bad housing-market data and weaker business confidence have led the market to anticipate rate cuts from H2 2023, in line with ING's "long-held view" [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Reads the late-2022 US labour market as still creating jobs in significant numbers with wages continuing to rise and households keeping spending [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
