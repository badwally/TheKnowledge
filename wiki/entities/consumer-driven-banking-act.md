# Consumer-Driven Banking Act (Canada)

## Summary

The Consumer-Driven Banking Act is the Canadian federal statute that establishes the legal foundation for an open-banking ("consumer-driven banking") framework in which approved service providers can access individuals' and businesses' financial data at the holder's direction via secure APIs rather than via screen scraping [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]]. The 2025 Budget Implementation Act adds the remaining provisions — accreditation, common rules on security and national security, liability, and consent — needed to make the Act operational [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].

## Key facts

- The Act provides the Minister of Finance with authority to address national security risks within the consumer-driven banking framework, supported by Canada's security and intelligence agencies [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Amendments through the 2025 Budget Implementation Act reinforce competition as a key objective and clarify the inclusion of small- and medium-sized businesses [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Implementation and oversight of the framework are delegated to the Bank of Canada, leveraging its existing supervisory role under the Retail Payment Activities Act [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Provincial credit unions and crown corporations that act as banks may "opt-in" to the federal framework while remaining subject to applicable provincial requirements [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The Act authorizes the Minister of Finance to designate provincial regulators to oversee certain provisions (security, privacy/consent/authentication, liability, complaints, consumer protection) for entities within their jurisdiction; accreditation, suspension/revocation, and national security remain federal [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Initial mandated participation is for banks above a retail-volume threshold; other federally regulated financial institutions, credit unions, crown corporations acting as banks, and registered payment service providers may opt-in upon accreditation [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- In-scope data covers consumer data, account information, balance data, transaction data, and product data for deposit (chequing and savings), payment products, investments (registered and non-registered), and lending accounts (secured and unsecured) [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Derived data — e.g., credit ratings, spending categorization, saving/budgeting recommendations, and predicted shortfalls/surpluses — is explicitly excluded from the Act's scope [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- All participating entities are equally subject to data-sharing requests (reciprocal access); in-scope data must be shared free of charge in a standardized, machine-readable format on consumer request [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Oversight will operate on a cost-recovery model once the framework is in place [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The Act prohibits screen scraping once the framework is fully operational; the Department of Finance will continue consulting on the timeline for bringing the prohibition into force [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The existing prohibition on the sharing of customer information by banks for the business of insurance is maintained [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
