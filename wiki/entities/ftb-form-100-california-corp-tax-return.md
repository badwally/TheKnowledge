# FTB Form 100 — California Corporation Franchise or Income Tax Return

## Summary

FTB Form 100 is the California corporation franchise or income tax return that a California tax-exempt homeowners' association under R&TC §23701t must file when its taxable income exceeds $100 [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]]. For exempt HOAs, Form 100 is the vehicle for reporting non-exempt-function taxable income — including interest income (including interest earned on member dues), dividends, nonmember receipts, and gains from the sale of property — which is taxed even though the association itself remains tax-exempt under §23701t [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]]. The Form 100 obligation runs independent of the Form 199 / FTB 199N information-return obligation [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].

## Key facts

- Form number: FTB Form 100 [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Title: *California Corporation Franchise or Income Tax Return* [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Issued by the California Franchise Tax Board [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Used by California HOAs to report the entity's taxable income (the non-exempt-function income that is taxed even when the HOA holds §23701t tax-exempt status) [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Required when taxable income exceeds $100; no Form 100 required when taxable income is equal to or less than $100 [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Form 100 obligation is independent of the Form 199 / FTB 199N annual-information-return obligation; the amount and type of income received determine which forms are required [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Taxable income is defined as total gross income minus exempt function income (including dues, fees, and assessments from members) minus any deductions directly connected with the production of nonexempt function income [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Taxable-income inclusions enumerated by FTB Form 1028: interest income (including interest from members), dividends, nonmember receipts, gains from the sale of property, health service fees from members, meals or food service fees from members, housekeeping service fees from members, laundry-use fees from members, and amounts received from members for use of a facility for a discrete period (evening, weekend, week, etc.) [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Organizations that fail to receive California tax-exempt status remain subject to the minimum franchise or income tax — Form 100 is the vehicle for that liability as well [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
