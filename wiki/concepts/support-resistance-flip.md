# Support/Resistance Flip After Breakout

## Summary

Price-action principle that, after a stock breaks through a resistance with sufficient buying pressure, that prior resistance level often acts as a support on subsequent retests because the buying pressure now constitutes the majority at that level — the same flip works in the inverse direction for a broken support [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].

## Key claims

- A support is a price level at which a downtrending stock can be expected to stop and most likely bounce, indicating an area of heavy buying pressure; a resistance is the price level at which an uptrending stock can be expected to hit a ceiling because of heavy selling pressure [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
- These levels are not always respected — when buying pressure increases at a resistance and overcomes the selling pressure, the result is a "breakout" and the stock continues uptrending [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
- After a breakout, the previous resistance often becomes a support, as the buying pressure is now the majority at that level [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
- Worked example: on the $TSLA daily chart, after the initial breakout of the $540 level the stock came back down to test it three times and bounced off it each time [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
- Another worked example: a resistance on the $TSLA daily chart was tested multiple times and, after it was broken, the same resistance became a support [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
- Workflow: find key levels of support and resistance on larger timeframes such as the 1D chart, then drop down to the 15M chart to find more local levels usable for daytrading [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
