# Portfolio vs. Tactical Approach

## Summary

A two-state market-exposure framework used by Scott Redler in which the SPY's position relative to the 8 & 21 day EMAs determines whether the trader runs a long-biased Portfolio Approach (broad equity exposure, no SPY short hedge unless extended) or shifts to a defensive Tactical Approach (book profits, scout shorts, hedge with a SPY short); the regime switch is mechanical — when SPY breaks the 8 & 21 day EMAs, the trader steps from Portfolio mode into Tactical mode [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].

## Key claims

- When SPY trends above the 8 & 21 day EMAs, Redler is typically in a Portfolio Approach: 4–12 long positions in individual stocks showing relative strength, occasionally call options, and no short hedge unless the market gets extended [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- When SPY breaks the 8 & 21 day EMAs, Redler shifts to a Tactical Approach: take profits (especially in weaker names), look for potential shorts, and hedge with a SPY short [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- In the Portfolio Approach the regime check is whether SPY is trending above the 8/21/50/200 day EMAs collectively, while the regime exit only requires SPY to break the 8 & 21 day — i.e., the 8 & 21 day act as the early-warning trigger [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- The October 2019 SPX gap over the 8 & 21 day EMAs is cited as the canonical Portfolio-Approach signal: Redler treats it as a green light to boost long exposure into year-end and reports the quarter as one of the most profitable of his career, despite shake-out headlines about China and Iran [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- The framework is explicit that "plenty of tech exposure" is the default tilt during the Portfolio Approach [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- The framework treats the 8 & 21 day reclaim/break as a discrete state machine — there is no "partial" or sized response to a break; the regime flips and exposure is recalibrated wholesale [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
