# Upper-Body Strength for Cyclists

## Summary

Upper-body strength work for cyclists — bent-over rows, push-ups, bench press, lateral/front raise combos, tricep kickbacks, and tricep dips — builds the postural and pressing strength needed to stay strongly supported on the bike during long and hilly rides, not aesthetic upper-body mass [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].

## Key claims

- **Bent-over row** targets lats, rhomboids, scapular stabilizers, shoulders, biceps, spinal erectors, glutes, and hamstrings — with the cycling-specific rationale of strengthening the back and core stabilizers for postural endurance on long rides [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- **Push-up** trains pectorals, shoulders, triceps, and core for powerful upper-body support on the bike; can be regressed to knee or three-quarter variants and progressed with added load or higher reps [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- **Bench press** builds pectoral, shoulder, and tricep strength to support long rides, executed with a slightly-wider-than-shoulder overhand grip lowering to mid-chest; riders whose backs arch are advised to elevate the knees with feet on a foot rest [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- **Lateral & front raise combo** strengthens the deltoids — both lateral and front heads — to keep the upper body strongly supported on the bike, performed in alternating sets of one lateral and one front raise per rep [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- **Tricep kickback** and **tricep dip** target the triceps for long and hilly rides, with the dip also recruiting the chest and anterior shoulders [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Across all upper-body lifts the cycling-relevant rationale is postural endurance and stable arm support on the bars — i.e., the bike-specific reason to lift up top, not aesthetic upper-body mass [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
