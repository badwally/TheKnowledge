# Streetlight Effect in Machine Learning

## Summary

The concern that ML models, trained on existing knowledge, will direct scientific search toward well-understood but low-value areas of the design space rather than unlocking genuinely new discoveries [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].

## Key claims

- A central worry about using ML for science is that models trained on existing examples may amplify the streetlight effect, biasing research toward familiar regions (Khurana 2023; Kim 2023; Hoelzemann et al. 2024) [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
- Empirically, in a randomized materials-discovery rollout, AI-generated compounds had more distinct physical structures than existing materials, contradicting the streetlight hypothesis in this setting [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
- Originality of compounds was measured using the chemical-similarity approach of De et al. (2016) [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
- Patents filed by AI-treated scientists are more likely to introduce novel technical terms, a leading indicator of transformative technologies (Kalyani 2024) [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
- AI shifts the share of prototypes toward new product lines rather than improvements to existing ones, engendering a shift to more radical innovation (Acemoglu et al. 2022a) [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
