# Derived Data Exclusion (Open Banking Scope)

## Summary

The derived-data exclusion is the design choice in Canada's Consumer-Driven Banking Act to exclude from data-portability scope any data about a consumer, product, or service that a participating entity has enhanced to significantly increase its usefulness or commercial value [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]]. The exclusion preserves the proprietary returns to data refinement while opening up raw account, balance, transaction, and product data for portability [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].

## Key claims

- Derived data is data about a consumer, product, or service that has been enhanced by a participating entity to significantly increase its usefulness or commercial value [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Illustrative examples of derived data named in the framework include credit ratings (whether produced in-house or by a third-party ratings agency); categorization of spending (e.g., rent, groceries, car payments); saving and budgeting recommendations generated from account-data analysis; and highlighting of upcoming fund shortfalls or surpluses based on past spending behaviour [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Portable in-scope data covers consumer data, account information, balance data, transaction data, and product data for deposit (chequing/savings), payment products, investments (registered and non-registered), and lending accounts (secured and unsecured) [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
