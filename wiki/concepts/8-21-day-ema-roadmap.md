# 8 & 21 Day EMA Roadmap

## Summary

A short-term-trend roadmap centered on the 8 day exponential moving average (very short-term trend) and the 21 day exponential moving average (short-term trend), used by Scott Redler as the primary visual indicator for when to be aggressively long versus when to step into hedges and shorts; in Redler's framing, the 8 & 21 day EMAs are "the most accurate short-term road map" he has found [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].

## Key claims

- Traditional technicians focus on the 10, 20, 50, and 200 day simple moving averages, with the 10 day SMA labeled the very short-term trend and the 20 day SMA labeled the short-term trend [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Redler uses 8 EMA (very short-term), 21 EMA (short-term), 50 EMA (intermediate), and 200 EMA (long-term), preferring exponential because EMAs are more sensitive to recent action [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- The 8 day EMA and the 10 day SMA are not very different in most cases; what matters is not the exact moving averages used but how well they are used to manage risk [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Redler picks 8 and 21 specifically because his brain is trained to judge action on those time frames, not because they have intrinsically superior signal [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Beyond Meat (BYND) case: in early 2020, BYND had two big failures at the 8 & 21 day EMAs that led to declines even while the broader market rose; it then formed a big WRB above the 8 & 21 day, broke a two-month consolidation channel, and rallied $22 in 4 days on the McDonald's expansion announcement [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Tesla (TSLA) Cybertruck case: after the November-2019 earnings gap-up, the stock dipped on the Cybertruck unveiling backlash but held the 8 & 21 day EMAs and rallied after Musk announced large pre-order numbers — the held EMAs were the leading indicator that the stock's holders disagreed with the media narrative [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- SPY post-Brexit case (June 2016): SPY fell from $210.87 the day before the vote to $188.65 the day after, then on June 30, 2016 reclaimed the 8 & 21 day EMAs, briefly retested them, and rallied above $219 — Redler treats the reclaim as the canonical "statement of strength" lesson [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- October 2019 SPX case: when SPX gapped over the 8 & 21 day EMAs, Redler treats it as a green light to boost long exposure into year-end, and reports that quarter as one of the most profitable of his career, with one minor slip below the 21 day quickly reclaimed [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
