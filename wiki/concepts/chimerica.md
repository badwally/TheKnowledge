# Chimerica

## Summary

"Chimerica" is Niall Ferguson's name for the commercial-and-financial entanglement between the United States and China, in which the U.S. paid U.S. dollars for cheap Chinese imports and China dutifully recycled its earnings into G7 claims; Zoltan Pozsar's August 24, 2022 Credit Suisse dispatch treats Chimerica as one of the two "heavenly match" unions whose breakdown drives de-globalization and inflation [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].

## Key claims

- Coined by Niall Ferguson and adopted by Pozsar to describe the U.S.-China economic union [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- The structural deal: "the U.S. paid U.S. dollars for cheap Chinese imports, and Russia and China dutifully recycled their earnings into G7 claims" — leaving both sides entangled commercially as well as financially [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- The "cartoon version" of Chimerica's failure: "China got very rich making cheap stuff, and then wanted to build 5G networks globally and make cutting-edge chips with cutting-edge lithography machines, but the U.S. said 'no way'. As a result, Chimerica is going through a messy divorce. The two sides don't talk anymore: 'Pentagon chief's calls to China go unanswered amid Taiwan crisis'" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Pozsar argues that without Chimerica the U.S. cannot sustain the lowflation regime that licensed QE: cheap Chinese exports were one of the three pillars of low inflation [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- The breakdown of Chimerica is one half of the divorce that pushes China into the Chussia alliance with Russia — "one disenfranchised spouse (China from the Chimerica union) found another (Russia from the Eurussia union) to form an economic union out of revenge" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
