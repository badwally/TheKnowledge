# Short-trend degradation, long-trend persistence

## Summary

Finding from Lempérière et al. (2014) that recent (post-2008) underperformance of trend-following CTAs is not evidence of an "end of trends": within the same dataset, long-horizon trends show no statistical degradation, while short-horizon trends "have significantly withered" — implying the recent weak run is concentrated in short trends and is statistically compatible with a fluctuation rather than a regime change [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].

## Key claims

- The paper finds "no sign of a statistical degradation of long trends, whereas shorter trends have significantly withered" [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- Recent CTA underperformance ("the relatively poor performance of CTAs over the last five years," written in 2014) is shown to be fully compatible with a statistical fluctuation [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- The authors cannot exclude that the recent period is a precursor of an "end of trends," but argue theoretically that this is an unlikely scenario [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- For the >2000 decade, the diversified 5-month trend Sharpe is 0.75 (t-stat 2.8) — lower than the 1980–2000 decades but still significantly positive [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
