# Asness's Bubble Criteria

## Summary

Cliff Asness's high-bar definition of a market bubble — articulated in his 2014 piece and reprised in "The Less-Efficient Market Hypothesis" — requiring (a) that no reasonable set of expectations about the future and no reasonable model can justify the price, and (b) that the mispricing affect a large swath of the market rather than a single stock [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].

## Key claims

- Bubbles are rare, and the word is used "far too promiscuously" — the standard for calling something a bubble should be very high [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- The threshold should not be "this small stock is expensive so it's in a bubble" but rather "I have tried hard and I cannot come up with any reasonable set of expectations about the future that justifies the price today under any reasonable model" [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- A bubble must affect a large swath of the market — one stock, even if grossly overvalued, doesn't constitute a "bubble of one" [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Qualifying bubbles Asness identifies in his career: Japanese stock prices in the late 1980s; US stock prices in general and the high-vs-low-multiple stock spread in 1999-2000; high-vs-low-multiple stocks again in 2019-2020 [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- He is "less sure" about real estate going into the GFC (subsequent prices have gone way higher), though pockets like securitized mortgage-backed securities probably qualify and were probably big enough to overcome the "broad" criterion [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- He is unwilling to call the AI boom a bubble, because while the stocks look very expensive on many typical measures, he can come up with assumptions about the future that justify these prices in the realm of reasonable possibility — "not that I'm saying they look good!" [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Asness rejects the cynical view that bubbles don't exist because they are hard to profit from — he treats whether one can practically profit from bubbles as a different question from whether bubbles exist [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
