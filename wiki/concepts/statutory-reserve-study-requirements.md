# Statutory Reserve Study Requirements (US)

## Summary

Statutory reserve study requirements are state-law mandates that condominium or community associations conduct a reserve study (or maintain a reserve schedule) on a defined cadence and to a defined minimum scope. As of September 2024, twelve US states impose such requirements on condominium associations, with substantial variation in cadence, scope, and the strength of the mandate [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].

## Key claims

- Twelve states require condominium associations to conduct a reserve study or maintain a reserve schedule: California, Colorado, Delaware, Florida, Hawaii, Maryland, Nevada, Oregon, Tennessee, Utah, Virginia, and Washington State [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Washington State's requirement is framed statutorily as encouragement rather than a hard mandate: associations are encouraged to perform a reserve study every three years unless doing so would impose an unreasonable hardship [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- California requires a visual inspection-based reserve study at least once every three years and an annual board review of that study; the study must identify major components with remaining useful life under 30 years, their probable remaining useful life, repair/replacement cost, and an annual contribution plan, per California Civil Code §5550–5520 [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Hawaii requires that the reserve study be reviewed by an independent reserve study preparer and updated every three years, with cash-flow plans based on thirty-year projections, per HRS §514B-148 [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Florida requires a Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS) and mandates that accounting records include all SIRS documents, retained for at least 14 years per Section 718.501(1)(c); the SIRS itself was required to be completed by December 31, 2024 [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Delaware requires that condominium reserves be calibrated to a current reserve study, with minimum contributions varying based on either the reserve study or a statutory formula based on the number of common area components, per Section 81-315 [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Colorado does not require a reserve study, but where an association does have one prepared, an internally conducted reserve study is statutorily sufficient, per Section 38-33.3-209.5 [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Several states (Connecticut, Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky) require reserve funding without separately requiring a reserve study; the two requirements are statutorily distinct [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
