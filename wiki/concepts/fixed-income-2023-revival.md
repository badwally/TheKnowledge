# Fixed Income 2023 Revival

## Summary

Credit Suisse's 2023 thesis that, with inflation likely to normalize, fixed income should become more attractive to hold and offer renewed diversification benefits in portfolios after a 2022 in which bond/equity correlation broke the asset class's traditional diversification role [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].

## Key claims

- With inflation likely to normalize in 2023, fixed income assets should become more attractive to hold [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- Fixed income should offer renewed diversification benefits in portfolios after 2022's broken bond/equity correlation [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- 2022 broke the traditional diversification role of bonds: there was a stronger correlation between bonds and equities due to the turbulent macroeconomic environment and tighter monetary policy regime [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- Specific 2023 fixed-income opportunities flagged by CS: US curve 'steepeners,' long-duration US government bonds (over Eurozone government bonds), emerging market hard currency debt, investment grade credit, and crossovers [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- Risk to the thesis: a renewed phase of volatility in rates due to higher-than-expected inflation [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
