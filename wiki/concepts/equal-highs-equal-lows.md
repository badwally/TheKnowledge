# Equal Highs and Equal Lows

## Summary

When swing-high or swing-low formations occur adjacent to one another at roughly the same price, ICT-style analysis labels them "equal highs" or "equal lows"; "Lesson 1: Price and Liquidity" argues that these formations create the optical illusion of support and resistance while in fact accumulating buyside or sellside liquidity that algorithmic order flow is designed to engage [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].

## Key claims

- Adjacent swing-high or swing-low formations are referred to as equal highs or equal lows [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].
- Equal highs and equal lows create the illusion of support and resistance when in fact they are areas of buyside and sellside liquidity [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].
- The lesson's worked example shows that, in a downtrend, retail traders expect the upper equal-highs band to act as resistance and the lower equal-lows band to act as support — but price typically breaks the upper band first as smart money accumulates shorts above retail resistance and then targets the lower band where retail "support" sits [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].
- Combining adjacent SH / SL formations with a higher-timeframe context is presented as the way to identify "really significant" areas of liquidity [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].
