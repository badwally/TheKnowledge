# Authority-Misinfluence Tendency

## Summary

Munger's twenty-second psychological tendency: man's automatic, evolution-derived tendency to follow leaders in dominance hierarchies, which becomes dangerous when the leader is wrong or when instructions are misunderstood — as demonstrated by Milgram's obedience experiments, copilot deference in aviation, and corporate settings where dysfunctional CEOs remain in power long after they should be removed [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].

## Key claims

- "Living in dominance hierarchies as he does, like all his ancestors before him, man was born mostly to follow leaders, with only a few people doing the leading" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Milgram's experiment showed that a man posing as a professor could trick ordinary people into giving what they believed were massive, torturous electric shocks to innocent citizens; the result involved about six psychological tendencies acting in confluence [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- In aviation: a new pilot for a general misinterpreted a minor shift in the general's position as a direction, crashed the plane, and became a paraplegic; even after simulator training to ignore foolish boss-pilot orders, copilots "too often allow the simulated plane to crash because of some extreme and perfectly obvious simulated error of the chief pilot" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Warren Buffett "always acts like an overquiet mouse around his pilots" as a conscious counterweight to this tendency [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- A fishing guide saying "Give him the rod" (meaning apply more pressure) caused an angler to literally throw his expensive rod at a tarpon — "This example shows how powerful is the tendency to go along with an authority figure and how it can turn one's brain into mush" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- In business: a psychology Ph.D. who became CEO of a major company went wild with an expensive headquarters and wine cellar; when told money was running short, he said "Take the money out of the depreciation reserves" — and was allowed to remain in control long after he should have been removed [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- The obvious implication: "Be careful whom you appoint to power because a dominant authority figure will often be hard to remove, aided as he will be by Authority-Misinfluence Tendency" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
