# HOA Federal Flat Tax Rate (§528)

## Summary

Under the §528 regime, an electing US homeowners association is taxed at a flat rate on its non-exempt taxable income — 30% for condominium management associations and residential real estate management associations, and 32% for timeshare associations [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]]. The flat rate applies to both ordinary income and capital gains [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]]. Because exempt function income (member dues, fees, and assessments from owners as owners) is excluded from the §528 tax base entirely, the flat rate effectively falls on the association's investment income — chiefly interest, dividends, capital gains, and certain non-member or special-use revenue — net of deductions directly connected with producing that income and the §528 $100 specific deduction [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]]. The flat-rate-on-investment-income economics make the Form 1120-H vs. Form 1120 comparison a recurring annual decision: a board may file whichever return produces the lowest total tax [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].

## Key claims

- The taxable income of a homeowners association that files its tax return on Form 1120-H is taxed at a flat rate of 30% for condominium management associations and residential real estate management associations [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- The flat rate is 32% for timeshare associations [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- The §528 flat rates apply to both ordinary income and capital gains [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- A homeowners association should compare its total tax computed on Form 1120-H with its total tax computed on Form 1120 and may file the form that results in the lowest tax [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- Because interest on amounts in a sinking fund is NOT exempt function income, reserve-fund interest is taxed at the §528 flat rate (30% for condominium and residential real estate management associations; 32% for timeshare associations) when the association elects Form 1120-H treatment [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
