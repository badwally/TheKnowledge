# VC Graduation Rate Decline

## Summary

The secular decline in the rate at which venture-backed companies progress from one funding round to the next; per SVB's H2 2025 "State of the Markets," companies are graduating at the lowest rates in history and taking far longer to do so, with the report framing this as a natural-selection dynamic following a "Cambrian explosion" of startup formation in the prior cycle [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].

## Key claims

- Companies are graduating from one series to the next "at the lowest rates in history, and when they do graduate, it takes them far longer than it used to" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- SVB characterizes the distribution of funding rounds as having expanded: "There is no typical series today" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- Per Jon Sakoda (Founding Partner): "Intuitively, everything used to be cool: consumer, crypto, fintech, etc. Everything was firing on all cylinders. A big number of startups were generated. Can the world support 100,000 startups? You had the Cambrian explosion of ideas, now you're seeing the natural selection. People tried a bunch of ideas and not everything is working. How could they all graduate?" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- Many of the down rounds that have occurred are characterized as part of this winnowing process [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- The moderate uptick in Series A deal activity off 2024 lows represents the first growth in investment activity seen since 2021, fueled by a robust cohort of seed companies seeking to raise Series A [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
