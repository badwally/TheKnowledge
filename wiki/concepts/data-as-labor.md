# Data as Labor

## Summary

"Data as labor" is an economic framing — proposed by Arrieta-Ibarra, Goff, Jiménez-Hernández, Lanier, and Weyl (2018) — that treats user-generated data as the product of labor by users, rather than as capital created by corporations observing willing individuals [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]]. The frame is presented as an alternative to the dominant "data as free input" treatment, with the goal of restoring a functioning market for user contributions [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].

## Key claims

- The data-as-labor frame characterizes users as producers of data through their activity, not merely as passive sources observed by data-collecting firms [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].
- The dominant alternative frame — data as capital created by corporations — is argued to neglect users' roles in creating data, reduce user incentives, distribute the gains from the data economy unequally, and stoke fears of automation [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].
- Treating data (at least partially) as labor is proposed as a way to resolve these problems and restore market function for user contributions [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].
- Adoption is expected to run against the near-term interests of dominant data monopsonists who benefit from data being treated as "free" [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].
- Arrieta-Ibarra et al. (2018) propose three forms of countervailing power to support the transition: competition, a data labor movement, and thoughtful regulation [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].
- The frame sits within the economics of monopsony and segmented labor markets (JEL J42) [[sources/web-2018-05-10-3b3]].
