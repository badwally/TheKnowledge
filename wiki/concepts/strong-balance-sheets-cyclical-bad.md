# Strong Balance Sheets as a Cyclical Bad

## Summary

Zoltan Pozsar's contrarian May 2022 claim — drawing on Charlie Munger's "Invert. Always invert" — that strong household and business balance sheets are not a hedge against recession but a reason the Fed must lean against the wind harder, since stronger balance sheets force the Fed to inject more volatility and damage to slow demand [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].

## Key claims

- The market is ripe with narratives that strong household and business balance sheets (and strong corporate profits) mean there won't be a recession; Pozsar argues the opposite — there can be a recession precisely because balance sheets are so strong [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Strong balance sheets mean the Fed needs to lean against the wind harder to shock demand lower [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- If low rates, QE, and low volatility healed balance sheets, the opposite will hurt balance sheets — by design [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Charlie Munger's maxim "Invert. Always invert" is invoked by Pozsar to motivate the reframe [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Strong balance sheets are a "cyclical bad," not a "cyclical good" — implying more discipline from the Fed: more hikes and more volatility injected by the Fed, by design, until financial conditions tighten more and demand slows enough [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- The risk of recession, whether real or merely implied by an inversion of the yield curve, won't deter the Fed from hiking rates higher faster, and signs of a recession might not mean immediate rate cuts to ramp demand back up — cuts may have to wait until the Fed is certain that inflation is dead [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
