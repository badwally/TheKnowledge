# Ontario Comprehensive Reserve Fund Study

## Summary

Under Ontario Regulation 48/01 (General) made under the Condominium Act, 1998, the reserve fund study regime is built around three distinct, formally defined study types: the comprehensive study, the updated study based on a site inspection, and the updated study not based on a site inspection. Each type is a defined term that determines the methodology, currency, and disclosure characteristics of the reserve fund analysis a condominium corporation must perform and disclose to owners [[sources/web-2023-10-01-0bc]].

## Key claims

- "Comprehensive study" means a comprehensive reserve fund study that meets the requirements of Ontario Regulation 48/01 [[sources/web-2023-10-01-0bc]].
- "Updated study based on a site inspection" means a comprehensive study that has been revised so that it is current as of the date of the revision, where the revision is based on a site inspection of the property and has been conducted in accordance with the Regulation's requirements [[sources/web-2023-10-01-0bc]].
- "Updated study not based on a site inspection" means a comprehensive study that has been revised so that it is current as of the date of the revision, where the revision is not based on a site inspection of the property and has been conducted in accordance with the Regulation's requirements [[sources/web-2023-10-01-0bc]].
- The three-tier framework — comprehensive, updated-with-inspection, updated-without-inspection — is the regulatory substrate for Ontario's reserve-fund disclosure cadence and is the definitional basis for §94 of the Condominium Act, 1998's reserve-fund obligations [[sources/web-2023-10-01-0bc]].
- The current plan proposed by the board under §94(8) of the Act for future funding of the reserve fund is itself a "core record" of the corporation under the Regulation, sitting alongside the financial statements and auditor's report in the disclosure inventory [[sources/web-2023-10-01-0bc]].
- The distinction between updated studies with versus without a site inspection is the regulatory mechanism by which Ontario differentiates re-fits of an existing study from a fresh on-site re-baseline, with downstream consequences for both the validity period and the cost of the engagement [[sources/web-2023-10-01-0bc]].
