# Canadian Condominium Corporation Reserve Fund Interest Tax Treatment

## Summary

Interest earned on a Canadian condominium corporation's operating or reserve funds IS income of the corporation for federal income-tax purposes — paragraph 3 of CRA Interpretation Bulletin IT-304R2 is unambiguous on this point [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]]. However, because most residential condominium corporations qualify as non-profit organizations under paragraph 149(1)(*l*) of the *Income Tax Act*, they are exempt from Part I tax on that income, with the practical consequence that reserve-fund interest is generally non-taxable for qualifying Canadian condominium corporations [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]]. The non-taxability flows from the NPO exemption rather than from any characterization of reserve-fund interest as non-income. Loss of NPO status — for example by carrying on a business — would expose reserve-fund interest to Part I tax in the year it ceases [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].

## Key claims

- Interest earned on the corporation's operating or reserve funds is income of the corporation, per IT-304R2 ¶ 3 [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Rental and other incidental income earned by the corporation is also income of the corporation under the same paragraph [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Because most residential condominium corporations qualify as NPOs under paragraph 149(1)(*l*), reserve-fund interest is generally exempt from Part I tax in their hands [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- The non-taxability of reserve-fund interest is contingent on continued NPO qualification, which is a question of fact and can be lost — for example if the corporation carries on a business, in which case it will not be considered a non-profit corporation, per IT-304R2 ¶ 3 [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- The classification of reserve-fund interest as income (taxable but typically exempt) is structurally distinct from the treatment of members' fee surpluses, which are not considered income of the corporation at all under IT-304R2 ¶ 3 [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
