# ETF arbitrage mechanism

## Summary

The ETF arbitrage mechanism is the same-day creation/redemption process by which authorized participants keep an ETF's price aligned with the value of its underlying holdings; van der Beck, Bouchaud, and Villamaina (2024) treat it as a laboratory for measuring flow-induced price impact because it propagates ETF demand into the underlying basket securities at a daily frequency [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].

## Key claims

- The vast majority of ETFs perfectly reinvest flows in their existing positions on the same day, which makes their flow-induced trades observable at a daily frequency and thus an ideal laboratory for estimating price impact [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- The mechanism propagates ETF demand into the underlying securities, causing a non-fundamental price impact that enhances the ETF's own return — the basis for self-inflated ETF returns [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- When ETFs hold concentrated positions in individual stocks, they accumulate substantial ownership in those securities as the ETF grows in size; the arbitrage mechanism then translates ETF demand into flow-induced price impact on the underlying [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- The literature on ETFs offers two competing views — that ETFs improve price discovery in the underlying basket by offering superior liquidity (per Blackrock's "ETF Market Realities") versus that ETF liquidity attracts a clientele that introduces non-fundamental volatility in the underlying (Ben-David et al. 2018); the paper argues that the second channel produces considerable price distortions when ETFs hold concentrated positions [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- ETF investors return-chase and cannot differentiate the mechanical price impact due to ETF arbitrage from fund-manager skill, hence purchasing further ETF shares and producing endogenous boom-and-bust cycles in both the ETF price and the underlying [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
