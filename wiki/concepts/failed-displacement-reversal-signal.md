# Failed-Displacement Reversal Signal

## Summary

In Lesson 2: Market Structure, the failure of price to displace in one direction — typically by running stops just past a swing level and then refusing to follow through — is read as a signal that price is positioning to displace and seek liquidity in the opposite direction, with two worked examples (January-2022 bearish and a $ES bullish reversal) supporting the inference [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].

## Key claims

- Lesson 2 states the rule directly: "price failing to displace in one direction is indicating it might want to go in the other direction" [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The symmetric form of the rule: if price fails to displace up and takes buyside liquidity, then by default price may be seeking to take out sellside liquidity below [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The bearish January-2022 worked example uses the rule: the swing high is taken out on low volume, price consolidates 6–8 days, attempts to continue the trend up but fails (red x), then falls back into the consolidation and produces the bearish displacement [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- In that example, the failure to break out is interpreted as a move designed to stop out eager shorts, after which smart money uses the shorts' stops to position for the ensuing move down [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The economic-mechanism narrative attaches a confirmation rule: the failed-breakout direction is confirmed by the next move's displacement creating an imbalance, which means the consolidation phase has smart money positioned in the opposite direction [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The bullish-reversal example shows the symmetric case: price hits new yearly lows (sellside liquidity), then "shows that it does not want to displace down from the consolidation" — and "immediately comes back up" — leading to a weak move up that leaves a faint bullish OB before stronger displacement up follows [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- A weakness diagnostic is built in: the lesson labels the initial bullish move "weak" because it does not leave behind an imbalance; the trader should wait for a stronger displacement before treating the OB and imbalance as a tradable POI [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The signal is the engine behind Lesson 2's stop placement — if the direction were wrong, the order blocks left behind would be taken out quickly with displacement in the opposite direction, invalidating the trade [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
