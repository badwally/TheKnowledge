# Canadian Condominium Accrual Accounting Requirement

## Summary

Per generally accepted accounting principles in Canada, financial statements for condominium corporations must use the accrual method of accounting rather than the cash method [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]]. Under accrual accounting, revenues and expenses are recorded when the transaction occurs rather than when payment is received or made — an obligation that affects both the timing of expense recognition at year-end and the integrity of the corporation's reported financial position, and which is positioned by CAO as the structural defense against manipulation of financial results [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].

## Key claims

- Generally accepted accounting principles in Canada require condominium corporation financial statements to use the accrual method [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- There are two major accounting methods — cash accounting and accrual accounting — and the accrual method is the one mandated for condominium corporations [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Under the accrual method, revenues and expenses are recorded when the transaction occurs rather than when payment is received or made [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Example given by the CAO Guide: if a condominium hires a company to make a repair completed in May, the expense must be recorded in May even if the invoice is not received or paid until June [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- The most common accounting errors to look for when reviewing the financial statements are in the liabilities section — particularly invoices dated after the corporation's year-end for work completed before year-end, which must be included in the year-end financial statements [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- When such liabilities are not recorded, the effect is to understate the expenses for the year [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Directors should review invoices that have been received late or are expected to arrive after the corporation's year-end to determine whether the underlying work was done prior to year-end and, if so, include the expenses in the same fiscal year [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- The accrual method exists to ensure the condominium corporation's financial position is accurate [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Accrual accounting ensures the recording of an expense incurred in a particular year cannot be avoided by arranging to delay payment until after the year, and the reverse — an expense cannot simply be recorded in a year because it was budgeted if the condominium did not actually incur the expense [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- The accrual method is positioned by CAO as the structural mechanism that eliminates the possibility of manipulation of financial results [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
