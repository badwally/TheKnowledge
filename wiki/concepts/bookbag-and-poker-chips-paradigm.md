# Bookbag-and-Poker-Chips Paradigm

## Summary

The most commonly used experimental setup for studying belief updating, originating with Green, Halbert, and Robinson (1965); participants see cards drawn from one of two decks with known compositions and must estimate the probability of which deck was chosen — a setup that cleanly isolates Bayesian updating from other cognitive processes but has historically been run predominantly with strong signals (diagnosticity of 3/5 or higher), which per Augenblick, Lazarus, and Thaler (2024) has biased the literature toward finding underreaction as the dominant direction of bias [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].

## Key claims

- Originated with Green, Halbert, and Robinson (1965) and has since become the canonical experimental paradigm for studying belief updating under symmetric binary signals about a binary state [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
- Benjamin's (2019) survey of the literature includes 500 experimental treatment blocks across 21 papers that study inference from symmetric binary signals about a binary state using this paradigm [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
- Benjamin's "Stylized Fact 1" from the literature states: "Underinference is by far the dominant direction of bias" — but the vast majority of this evidence uses strong signals; in all papers where people receive one symmetric binary signal, diagnosticity is never lower than 3/5 [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
- Benjamin notes that "Underinference...is more severe the larger is the diagnosticity," hinting at the pattern that Augenblick, Lazarus, and Thaler formally test and confirm — that the direction of bias flips for weak signals [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
- In Augenblick, Lazarus, and Thaler's Study 1a, 500 participants are presented with two decks of cards — a green deck with more spades than diamonds and a purple deck with more diamonds than spades — and must estimate the probability of which deck was chosen after seeing one drawn card [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
- Signal strength is varied by changing the number of spades and diamonds in each deck; the direction to update is fairly clear (a spade is evidence for the green deck), but the correct magnitude requires clear understanding of the data-generating process, correct use of Bayes' rule, and exact calculation of suit proportions [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
- Almost all participants update in the right direction but there is substantial heterogeneity in how much they revise their beliefs — interpreted as knowing the direction but differing in perceived signal strength [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
- Estimates of underinference for high-strength signals are consistent with the prior literature; it is only for previously understudied low-strength signals (diagnosticity below 3/5) that overinference emerges, with participants acting as if signals are twice as strong as they truly are [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
