# Hormetic Exercise Stress

## Summary

The clinical framing of why exercise tolerance differs between healthy and inflamed bodies: exercise temporarily increases inflammation and oxidative stress, which in a healthy uninflamed body is hormetic or "good" stress that builds muscle and cardiovascular strength via SIRT-gene activation, but in an already-inflamed body — such as a Long COVID patient — even mild-to-moderate exercise can be "the straw that breaks the camel's back" and increase cell damage [[sources/pdf-e8351bcf5615]].

## Key claims

- Exercise temporarily increases inflammation and oxidative stress [[sources/pdf-e8351bcf5615]].
- In a healthy uninflamed body, this is a hormetic or "good" stress that builds muscle and cardiovascular strength through activation of SIRT genes [[sources/pdf-e8351bcf5615]].
- Overexercising can result in cell damage and excess inflammation [[sources/pdf-e8351bcf5615]].
- In an inflamed body, even mild-to-moderate exercise can be the straw that breaks the camel's back and increases cell damage [[sources/pdf-e8351bcf5615]].
- This framing motivates Dr. Carla Kuon's pacing recommendation in Long COVID — building strength slowly before cardiovascular exercise [[sources/pdf-e8351bcf5615]].
