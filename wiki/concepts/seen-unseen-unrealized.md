# Seen, Unseen, and Unrealized

## Summary

Per Bylund's three-category framework for analyzing the effects of regulatory intervention, extending Bastiat's classic seen-versus-unseen dichotomy by adding "The Unrealized" as a third category — the innovations, businesses, and value that never come into existence because intervention prevents them from being attempted; Bylund devotes Chapter 9 of his primer to this framework [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].

## Key claims

- Bylund structures Chapter 9 ("Regulatory Intervention") into three sections: "The Seen," "The Unseen," and "The Unrealized" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- The framework extends Bastiat's classic economic argument about the seen effects of intervention (direct, visible consequences) and the unseen effects (indirect, hidden consequences harder to trace) by adding the unrealized — effects that never come into existence because intervention forecloses them [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- The chapter falls under Part III ("Intervention") alongside Chapter 8 on monetary intervention, framing regulatory intervention as a companion distortion to monetary policy [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
