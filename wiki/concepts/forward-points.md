# Forward Points

## Summary

The differential between an FX forward rate and the corresponding spot rate, expressed in "points" — small adjustments added to or subtracted from spot to obtain the forward rate, reflecting the interest-rate differential between the two currencies and forming the building block for both forward-outright and FX-swap pricing [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].

## Key claims

- Forward points are the adjustments to the spot rate that yield the forward rate; rates for any value date other than spot are calculated relative to spot [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- Forward points may be at a premium or a discount to spot — the convention determines whether they are added to or subtracted from the spot rate [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- "Pay or earn the points" is the trader's mnemonic for whether a given side of a swap or forward earns or pays the carry implied by the points [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- Forward points change as a function of interest-rate-differential moves and time-to-delivery [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- "Which side of the market" — bid versus offer — is determined by the customer's intention and the market-maker's quoting convention, with rules of thumb the manual lays out specifically for swap points [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- Forward-rate conventions and worked examples ("sample problems") in the manual show how to apply the points to obtain a tradable forward rate [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
