# Process-oriented trading

## Summary

Process-oriented trading is the ICT Month 2 discipline that good trading comes from a defined sequence — patience, environment definition, parameter selection, executable criteria, thesis articulation, and post-trade review — rather than reactionary or impulsive decision-making [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].

## Key claims

- Good trading comes by way of process-oriented thinking, not by reactionary or impulsive thinking or rushing to prematurely take trades [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- The ICT Month 2 high-reward setup process specifies six steps: patience, define trade environment, determine trade parameters, executable criteria, understand why it should pan out, and experience for future reference [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- Trading with fear promotes fear-based decision making, prevents traders from taking losses, focuses on the adverse, and fosters paralysis or inability to execute [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- The complementary discipline is to treat losses as a cost of doing business — risk management combined with high-probability setups produces large percentage returns even when individual trades fail [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- The half-size recovery rule (half size on the trade after a loss, breakeven at 2R, profit at 3R) operationalizes process-oriented trading after adverse outcomes [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
