# College Sports Rent-Sharing

## Summary

The empirical framework developed by Garthwaite, Keener, Notowidigdo, and Ozminkowski (2020) for estimating how economic rents generated by NCAA football and men's basketball programs are distributed across athletic departments; using panel fixed effects OLS regressions with school and year fixed effects on FBS data from 2006 to 2019, they find that $0.31 of each additional dollar of revenue stays in the revenue sports, $0.20 goes to facilities, $0.11 flows to all other sports, $0.09 to administrative compensation, $0.03 to football coaches, and $0.03 to non-football coaches — with institutional support from the university essentially unchanged [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].

## Key claims

- Own-sport rent-sharing elasticity of 0.82, corresponding to $0.31 of every additional dollar of football and men's basketball revenue spent on those programs [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Income limits for athletes imply that very little of the increased own-sport spending directly flows to the athletes; instead the spending goes to facilities, coaches' salaries, and other factors within the programs [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Cross-sport rent-sharing elasticity for all other sports: 0.42 ($0.11 per dollar of revenue-sport revenue) [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Cross-sport rent-sharing elasticity for women's sports: 0.41 ($0.07 per dollar) [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Cross-sport rent-sharing elasticity for other men's sports: 0.42 ($0.04 per dollar) [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Rent-sharing elasticity for football coaches' salaries: 0.40 ($0.03 per dollar) [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Rent-sharing elasticity for non-football coaches' salaries: 0.31 ($0.03 per dollar); the equal aggregate spending does not reflect equal individual benefits since there are far more non-football coaches in an athletic department [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Rent-sharing elasticity for administrative compensation: 0.45 ($0.09 per dollar) [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Rent-sharing elasticity for facilities spending: 0.86 ($0.20 per dollar) [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Institutional support elasticity close to zero (ranging from -0.2 to 0.01, corresponding to -$0.01 to $0.01 per dollar), demonstrating that increased revenues are not simply reducing university transfers — not surprising since Power Five conferences are largely self-contained enterprises [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Methodology follows the recent rent-sharing elasticity literature in labor economics, citing Lamadon, Mogstad, and Setzler (2019) and Kline et al. (2019) [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Uses within-school-over-time variation in total revenue generated from football and men's basketball programs as the identification strategy [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
