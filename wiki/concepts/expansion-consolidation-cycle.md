# Expansion-Consolidation Cycle

## Summary

In Lesson 2: Market Structure, price is framed as cycling between consolidation areas via expansion: from one consolidation, an energetic move out (expansion) creates an imbalance and order block; price then either consolidates and continues or consolidates and reverses, with the displacement signature distinguishing the two [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].

## Key claims

- The Lesson 2 framework states that "price likes to move from one area of consolidation to the next" and does so through expansion [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Once price hits its target liquidity, it can consolidate and continue further or consolidate and reverse — both options begin with a consolidation phase [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- When price expands away from consolidation, an energetic move should occur and create an imbalance — this is the imbalance the trader uses to enter the trend that is forming [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The displacement-creating expansion phase is where Lesson 1's expansion-phase framing applies: explosive moves are seen near breakouts, reversal breakouts, or during trends [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- A failed expansion in one direction is read as a sign of intent in the other direction — "price failing to displace in one direction is indicating it might want to go in the other direction" [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The cycle is calibrated by liquidity: a sellside-liquidity target hit triggers consolidation, then a stop-run, then expansion up — Lesson 2's bullish reversal walk-through follows exactly that sequence [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The cycle is calibrated by stop-runs: in Lesson 2's bearish example, the failed breakout up was a move to stop out eager shorts — smart money sees this liquidity and uses the shorts' stops to short the ensuing move down [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Lesson 2 cautions traders to avoid "trading in chop (consolidation phase of price)," framing chop as the failure case the rest of the framework is designed to detect and skip [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
