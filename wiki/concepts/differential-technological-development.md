# Differential Technological Development

## Summary

Bostrom's (2002) principle that existential risk depends on the relative rate of development of dangerous technologies versus technologies that ameliorate those hazards; Aschenbrenner (2020) operationalizes this principle through directed technical change between a consumption (risk-creating) sector and a safety (risk-mitigating) sector [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].

## Key claims

- Bostrom (2002), as cited by Aschenbrenner, articulates the idea of "differential technological development": existential risk depends on the relative rate of development of potentially dangerous technologies versus technologies that ameliorate these hazards [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Aschenbrenner's model is explicitly inspired by this principle and operationalizes it as directed technical change between a consumption sector — whose technologies generate risk via emissions, bioengineering, and AI capabilities — and a safety sector whose technologies mitigate that risk [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Concrete instances of the principle in the paper: AI safety investment can offset risks from AI run amok; biosecurity investment can offset risks from bioengineered pandemics; permissive action links can offset risks from accidental nuclear war; and emissions abatement can offset climate-change risk [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Growth in consumption is thus not purely positive, but creates risks; the differential-technological-development frame says safety must keep pace with capability for risk to remain bounded [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
