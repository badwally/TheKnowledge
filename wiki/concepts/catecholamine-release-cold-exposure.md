# Catecholamine Release in Cold Exposure

## Summary

Reliable and easily measured chemical signature of cold-induced eustress: cold exposure co-releases norepinephrine, epinephrine (adrenaline), and dopamine — the catecholamines — in high quantities, with norepinephrine alone increasing roughly five-fold (530%), and does so with minimal cortisol increase, which is what makes cold a "good stressor" rather than a chronic-stress liability [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].

## Key claims

- Norepinephrine, epinephrine (adrenaline), and dopamine are collectively called catecholamines, and "literally nothing triggers them together like CWI" per Brass Monkey [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].
- Norepinephrine increases up to five-fold (a 530% increase) with cold-water immersion; noradrenaline and adrenaline are the unique chemical definition of stress and can be observed in cold exposure whether or not the exposure is deliberate [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].
- Norepinephrine acts as both a hormone and a neurotransmitter — as a neurotransmitter mobilizing the body and waking us up; as a hormone priming metabolism for heat conservation and production [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].
- Dopamine is misunderstood as a "happy hormone" — it is in fact a motivation molecule, a reward for pursuit designed to make us repeat the behaviour, which is why people consistently report feeling great after cold exposure [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].
- The catecholamine release happens with minimal cortisol increase, which is the condition that makes cold a eustress; cortisol initiates fight-or-flight and raises heart rate and blood pressure, both undesirable in chronic doses [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].
- Catecholamines are responsible for drive, desire, and focus — Brass Monkey's framing for why CWI builds mental resilience and why Navy SEALs and SAS training rely on cold above heat [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].
- Brass Monkey labels norepinephrine "the master molecule" for its dual role in cold-driven white-to-brown fat conversion and metabolic adaptation [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].
