# Multi-Manager Fund

## Summary

A hedge fund structure in which a single fund deploys capital across many distinct portfolio managers running diverse strategies, typically under a "full pass-through" expense model; ClearAlpha argues the structure's outperformance versus traditional 2/20 hedge funds and balanced hedge fund portfolios is driven by portfolio construction math — assembly of low-correlation strategies and cash-efficient implementation — rather than by superior manager selection [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].

## Key claims

- Multi-manager funds with "full pass-through" expense models delivered 11.8% net returns, 10.8% alpha, and 2.1 net Sharpe Ratio versus 6.3% / 4.1% / 0.6 for traditional 2/20 hedge fund structures, per Barclays' July 2022 survey of 290 hedge funds [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
- Multi-manager fund assets grew 18.8% per year from 2017 to 2022 versus 2.6% per year for the rest of the hedge fund industry, yet still account for only 8% of total industry assets, per Goldman Sachs Global Markets Division's December 2022 report "The Multiplier Effect" [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
- ClearAlpha argues the inherent edge is structural — assembly of a significant number of diverse investment strategies with low correlations, and cash-efficient implementation — not the ability to attract top portfolio managers or to quickly cut underperformers [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
- A multi-manager fund earns the sum of the excess returns of its constituent strategies plus the risk-free return — not the sum of total returns, because cash earns interest only once even when the fund holds the combined positions of multiple strategies [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
- Running a multi-manager fund requires sophisticated risk management expertise and systems to maintain low strategy correlations not only in normal periods but, more importantly, in tail scenarios — often requiring intra-month adjustments to portfolio positioning and hedges at the strategy or fund level [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
- Per ClearAlpha, the structure has "delivered more alpha than other structures, and as a result, are likely here to stay" [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
