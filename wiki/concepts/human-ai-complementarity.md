# Human-AI Complementarity in Innovation

## Summary

The empirical finding that AI tools and human expertise act as complements in the innovation production function: top scientists capture disproportionate gains from AI by leveraging domain knowledge to evaluate model output, while less-skilled evaluators see little benefit [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].

## Key claims

- In a randomized rollout, the bottom third of researchers saw minimal gains from AI while top-decile scientists' output rose by 81% [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
- 90:10 performance inequality more than doubles after AI introduction [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
- Top scientists leverage expertise to identify promising AI suggestions and investigate the most viable candidates first [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
- A significant minority of researchers order tests no better than random chance, seeing little benefit from the tool [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
- Researcher discretion boosts discovery rates for two-thirds of scientists, indicating experts observe features of the design problem not captured by the algorithm [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
- Evaluation ability is positively correlated with initial productivity, explaining the widening inequality in scientists' performance [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
- Findings support Agrawal et al. (2018): improvements in machine prediction make human judgment and decision-making more valuable, not less [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
