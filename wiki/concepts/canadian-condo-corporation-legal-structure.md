# Canadian Condominium Corporation Legal Structure

## Summary

A Canadian condominium corporation created under provincial or territorial legislation is a corporation *without share capital* whose members are the unit owners — a legal construct distinct from the share-capital corporate form that governs most other Canadian for-profit and not-for-profit entities [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]]. The corporation comes into existence on registration of a 'declaration' and 'description' (or a 'strata plan') in the appropriate land registry or land titles office, simultaneously creating the units (or strata lots) and vesting their ownership in fee simple in the unit owners [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]]. The corporation's mandate is to manage the real property and other assets of the corporation, to control and administer the common elements and assets, and to ensure unit-owner compliance with the corporation's registered condominium documents, by-laws, and provincial legislation [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].

## Key claims

- Each of Canada's ten provinces and three territories has statutes that recognize the condominium system of ownership [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- The condominium system combines separate ownership of a condominium unit with shared ownership of common property within the condominium development [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- IT-304R2 uses terms drawn from the Condominium Acts of Ontario and British Columbia as representative of the terminology likely to be encountered in most other provincial and territorial legislation, with Quebec treated separately under the Civil Code of Québec [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- A unit owner normally owns the unit in fee simple and shares ownership of the common areas with all other unit owners [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- A condominium is legally created by the acceptance and registration of a 'declaration' and 'description' or a 'strata plan' in the appropriate land registry or land titles office [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Registration of the declaration/description or strata plan brings the condominium corporation into existence and creates the units or strata lots whose members become the unit owners [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- A Canadian condominium corporation is a corporation *without share capital* whose members are the unit owners [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- The corporation's objects include management of the real property and any other assets of the corporation [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- The corporation has a duty to control, manage, and administer the common elements and assets of the corporation, and to ensure unit-owner compliance with the corporation's registered condominium documents, by-laws, and the provisions of the relevant condominium legislation [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- The board may pass by-laws to govern (subject to the relevant condominium legislation and registered documents) the management of the property, maintenance of the common elements, use and management of the corporation's assets, and the assessment and collection of condominium fees and common-expense contributions [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Condominium developments are not limited to residential use; they may also be commercial, industrial, resort, or mixed-use, with purpose and type set out in the declaration, by-laws, and rules [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- A unit is either a separate unit structure or, in a multi-unit structure, all the space between the partitions, floors, and ceilings separating it from other units and from the common elements [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Each unit owner's proportional interest in the common elements (parking lots, landscaped areas, laundry rooms, hallways, elevators, stairwells) is established in the documents filed in the land registry or land titles office [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
