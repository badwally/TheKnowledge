# Wage-price spiral

## Summary

Self-perpetuating feedback loop in which an inflationary mindset causes consumers to pull demand forward, firms to pass through higher costs and offer higher wages, and inflation expectations to entrench — invoked by Kai Volatility's Q2 2022 commentary as the central mechanism modern Fed policymakers cite when invoking the "Volcker Moment," and as the period (1965-1980) that three pre-Volcker Fed chairs — William McChesney Martin, Arthur Burns, and William Miller — failed to stop because, Kai Volatility argues, the inflation was structurally driven by political forces rather than psychology [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].

## Key claims

- Per Kai Volatility, the conventional model of the wage-price spiral runs: "consumers pull forward demand in order to beat anticipated future price increases. Firms readily pass along higher costs to consumers, including the future cost increases that they anticipate, and they become less resistant to offering higher wages. Left unchecked, this naturally plays out as a self-perpetuating, wage-price spiral" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- "Once long-term inflation expectations take hold they become a self-fulfilling prophecy" — and the policy implication is that secular inflation can be relegated to rare cyclical phenomena if the loop can be short-circuited early [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- The "seemingly perpetual wage-price spiral from 1965-1980" is the period that William McChesney Martin, Arthur Burns, and William Miller failed to stop and which Volcker is remembered for vanquishing [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Per Kai Volatility, this prevailing narrative implies that previous Fed chairs were derelict and that, with foresight and gumption, "10 to 15 painful years of inflation could have theoretically been bypassed" — leading to the modern policy prescription "circumvent a potential wage-price spiral before it starts, at all costs" / "Go big, early, and do whatever it takes" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Kai Volatility's critique: this cookie-cutter response "ignores several critical structural factors" — what was the initial structural driver of the inflation, what are the current structural realities of the economy, what is the economic cost of a Fed-generated slowdown, and what political feedback loop will the slowdown trigger [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Kai Volatility argues the 1965-1980 spiral was structurally driven by LBJ-era fiscal spending and 1970s populism, not psychology — and that 2022 inflation is structurally driven by 40 years of monetary-policy-driven inequality and the resulting populist fiscal response, again not psychology [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
