# Dispersion of Returns

## Summary

Andrew McCaffery's argument in Fidelity's 2023 Investment Outlook that as broad-market returns are no longer driven by easy-money tailwinds, the dispersion across securities will increase, allowing investors to seek idiosyncratic alpha rather than relying on whole-market moves [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].

## Key claims

- McCaffery argues that as the dispersion of returns increases, investors will be able to seek out idiosyncratic elements in their portfolios rather than rely on whole-market moves to generate returns [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues opportunities should also begin to emerge among securities driven by longer-term themes such as decarbonisation and reindustrialisation, which could draw investor attention sooner rather than later [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Henk-Jan Rikkerink's Multi asset chapter reinforces the thesis that less-correlated exposures will be an important part of the investment arsenal in 2023 [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Cash and quality investment-grade securities are framed as offering defensive characteristics, while emerging markets and Asian countries with weaker growth correlation to the US and Europe present a way to increase diversification [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Rikkerink argues the first rule of multi-asset investment is to understand the difference between asset behaviour over the long and the short term — implying dispersion-driven idiosyncratic opportunity is judged across both time horizons [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
