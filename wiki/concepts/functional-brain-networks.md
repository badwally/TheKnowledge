# Functional Brain Networks (FBNs)

## Summary

Functional Brain Networks (FBNs) are well-established organizational patterns of co-activated brain regions; a 2024 study cited by Hammond found that LLMs "exhibit brain-like functional architecture, with sub-groups of artificial neurons mirroring the organizational patterns of well-established Functional Brain Networks (FBNs)," and that more capable LLMs achieve an improved balance between behavioral diversity and consistency of functional specializations [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].

## Key claims

- FBNs are well-established functional-organization patterns of co-activated brain regions, mapped in the human brain across many tasks and used as a reference grid for testing brain-LLM organizational alignment [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- A 2024 study established that LLMs exhibit brain-like functional architecture, with sub-groups of artificial neurons mirroring FBN organizational patterns [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- More capable LLMs in that study achieve an improved balance between the diversity of computational behaviors and the consistency of functional specializations — a brain-like trade-off that scales with capability [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- The Default Mode Network specifically shows improved LLM alignment after basic instruction tuning, suggesting that brain-like specialization is latent in pretraining and amplified by post-training [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
- FBN alignment forms the middle step of the three-study brain-LLM alignment arc (Goldstein et al. 2022 ECoG → 2024 FBN study → TopoLM 2025) [[sources/web-2026-05-27-cee]].
