# Alberta Reserve Fund Study

## Summary

The Alberta reserve fund study is the §23 statutory study an Alberta condominium corporation's board must commission to identify depreciating property requiring repair or replacement within a 25-year horizon, estimate present condition, repair/replacement costs (priced no lower than current cost at the time of the report), and life expectancy of each component once repaired or replaced [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]]. The study, the §23(3) written report, and the §23(4) board-approved plan together form the regulatory trilogy that implements §38 of the Condominium Property Act [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].

## Key claims

- The board must retain a qualified person to carry out the §23(1) study of depreciating property [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- The study must determine an inventory of all depreciating property that, under the circumstances under which it will be or is normally used, may need to be repaired or replaced within the next 25 years [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- The study must determine the present condition or state of repair of the depreciating property and an estimate of when each component will need to be repaired or replaced [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- The study must determine estimated repair or replacement costs using as a basis costs that are not less than the costs existing at the time the reserve fund report is prepared [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- The study must determine the life expectancy of each component once that property has been repaired or replaced [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- The qualified person must additionally determine the current reserve fund balance, recommend the amount of funds (if any) to be included in or added to the reserve fund to establish or maintain it for §38 purposes, and describe the basis for those determinations [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- The qualified person must prepare and submit a written reserve fund report disclosing the person's qualifications, whether the person is an employee/agent/associate of the corporation or its manager, the study findings, and any other relevant matters [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- Corporations existing as of September 1, 2000 were required to meet §23(1)–(6) requirements before September 1, 2002 [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- Corporations whose condominium plan is registered on or after September 1, 2000 must meet §23(1)–(6) requirements within 2 years of registration [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- A study completed within the 5 years before September 1, 2000 that was the same as, or substantially the same as, a reserve fund study could be used to satisfy §23(4) (plan-approval) only, provided the board met that requirement before September 1, 2002 [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- Alberta's 25-year reserve-fund-study horizon is materially shorter than Hawaii's 30-year cash-flow projection requirement under §514B-148 [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]] [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
