# Information Blocking (21st Century Cures Act)

## Summary

"Information blocking" is the US regulatory term for a practice by a covered "actor" that is likely to interfere with the access, exchange, or use of electronic health information (EHI), except as required by law or specified in a regulatory exception [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]]. The definition originated in the 21st Century Cures Act (2016) and is implemented at 45 CFR Part 171 [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]]. As a regulatory regime it operates as a compelled-disclosure mechanism: covered actors must permit access, exchange, or use of EHI on lawful request, and refusing or impeding access is presumptively unlawful unless a regulatory exception is met or law requires withholding [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].

## Key claims

- Covered "actors" are healthcare providers, health IT developers of certified health IT, and health information exchanges (HIEs) / health information networks (HINs) [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- The statute applies two distinct knowledge standards: for health IT developers of certified health IT and for HIEs/HINs, the standard is whether the actor "knows, or should know" that a practice is likely to interfere with EHI access, exchange, or use; for healthcare providers, the standard is whether the actor "knows that the practice is unreasonable and is likely to interfere" with EHI access, exchange, or use [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- Exceptions to the information-blocking prohibition are enumerated at 45 CFR Part 171 and include (among others) the Fees Exception, Manner Exception, Preventing Harm Exception, and Privacy Exception [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- The exceptions function as voluntary safe harbors: meeting an exception guarantees the practice is not information blocking, but failing to meet any exception does not automatically mean information blocking has occurred — such cases are evaluated case-by-case [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- Claims of possible information blocking are submitted to the Office of the National Coordinator (ONC) through its Report Information Blocking Portal [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- Information received by ONC in connection with a claim that could identify the submitter is exempt from mandatory disclosure under the Freedom of Information Act [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- ONC has authority to review information-blocking claims against health IT developers of certified health IT as potential non-conformities under the ONC Health IT Certification Program [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- HHS Office of Inspector General (OIG) has authority to investigate information-blocking claims across all three actor classes — providers, networks/exchanges, and certified-health-IT developers [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- HHS issued a final rule under section 4004 of the Cures Act establishing disincentives for healthcare providers found by OIG to have committed information blocking [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
- A health IT developer participating in the ONC Health IT Certification Program may not take any action that constitutes information blocking as defined in Section 3022(a) of the Public Health Service Act and 45 CFR 171.103 [[sources/web-2026-04-08-4dc]].
