# US HOA Form 1120 vs Form 1120-H Election

## Summary

The US HOA Form 1120 vs Form 1120-H election is the annual federal tax filing decision facing a homeowners or condominium association: file Form 1120 under IRC §277 (non-exempt membership organization regime, with Revenue Ruling 70-604 available as a rollover mechanism), or file Form 1120-H under IRC §528 (HOA-specific elective regime). Gary Porter, CPA characterizes the §277 / 70-604 path as historically attractive — protecting net membership income from immediate taxation via the rollover election — but increasingly risky under the IRS's restrictive interpretation; for most associations, filing Form 1120-H approximately doubles the tax owed but eliminates the audit risk of a disallowed 70-604 election that, when triggered, often results in far greater tax than 1120-H would have produced [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].

## Key claims

- Form 1120 filing places an HOA under IRC §277 (non-exempt membership organization tax regime), where excess member assessment income is taxable absent a properly-executed Revenue Ruling 70-604 rollover election [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Form 1120-H filing avoids the §277 / 70-604 framework entirely, eliminating the audit risk associated with disallowed rollover elections [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- For most associations, filing Form 1120-H approximately doubles the tax compared to Form 1120 with a proper Rev. Rul. 70-604 election, but with materially less audit risk [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- When a Rev. Rul. 70-604 election is disallowed in IRS audit, the resulting tax under §277 is in many cases far greater than the tax that would have been owed had Form 1120-H been filed originally [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Where a 70-604 election is disallowed, Revenue Ruling 83-74 provides the escape route — the tax preparer pleads ignorance and amends the returns to Form 1120-H [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- The election decision interacts with two other tax-planning paths: (a) IRC §118 capital-reserve allocation to avoid Rev. Rul. 70-604 altogether, and (b) IRC §501(c)(4) exemption for the rare HOA that qualifies as a social welfare organization [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Gary Porter's overall posture: Rev. Rul. 70-604 should be used "as little as possible, and with extreme caution in the future" — it is no longer the universal cure-all many CPAs previously believed; for many associations, the elevated 1120-H tax is preferable to the audit-disallowance risk on 1120 [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
