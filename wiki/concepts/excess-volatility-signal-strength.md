# Excess Volatility and Signal Strength

## Summary

The market-level implication of Augenblick, Lazarus, and Thaler's (2024) signal-strength-dependent updating framework: asset prices exhibit excess volatility when information is weak (because market participants overreact to weak signals), but this effect reverses with sufficiently strong information (because participants underreact to strong signals) — a pattern confirmed in both sports betting markets and financial markets and offering a unified explanation for why observational evidence of excess volatility in asset prices appears contradictory to laboratory findings of underreaction [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].

## Key claims

- The experimental and empirical literatures on belief updating produce seemingly contradictory results: experiments commonly find underreaction to information, while observational evidence from real-world settings such as asset prices often appears more consistent with overreaction and excess volatility [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
- Augenblick, Lazarus, and Thaler's framework resolves this contradiction by showing that signal strength is the key mediating factor: experiments predominantly use strong signals (where underreaction is expected), while many real-world information flows are weak signals (where overreaction is expected) [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
- In sports betting markets, the paper demonstrates excess volatility when information is weak and a reversal of this effect with sufficiently strong information, consistent with the laboratory findings [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
- In financial markets, the paper similarly finds excess volatility when information is weak, but this effect reverses with sufficiently strong information [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
- The constant stream of information faced by market participants daily — polls, earnings reports, economic data, stock-market movements — is typically understood directionally but not in precise magnitude, creating the conditions under which overreaction to weak signals and underreaction to strong signals will emerge [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
