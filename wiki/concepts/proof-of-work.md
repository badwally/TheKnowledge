# Proof of Work

## Summary

A consensus and anti-spam mechanism that forces agents to expend computer time in order to qualify for a transaction, used in Bitcoin to deter spam and to compensate miners who validate transactions on the public ledger [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].

## Key claims

- Proof of work was originally devised to deter spam by forcing agents to use computer time in order to qualify for a transaction [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- In Bitcoin, the proof-of-work method has an adjustable degree of difficulty based on the speed of blocks, aimed in theory at keeping incentives sufficiently high for miners to keep operating the system [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Such adjustments lead to an exponential increase in computer-power requirements, "making at the time of writing onerous energy demands on the system — energy that could find alternatives in other computational and scientific uses" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Miners derive their compensation from both seignorage (the market value of a bitcoin minus its mining costs) and transaction fees upon validation, with the plan to switch to transaction fees as the sole revenues upon eventual depletion of the fixed number of coins [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Bitcoin "depends on the existence of such miners for perpetuity" — a structural dependence Taleb uses to argue that bitcoin is path-dependent in a way that gold is not [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Taleb explicitly excludes Ethereum and other non-proof-of-work cryptocurrencies from his analysis, noting that the discussion is focused on proof of work [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
