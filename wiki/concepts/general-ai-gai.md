# General AI (GAI, 通用人工智能)

## Summary

China's term for AI systems with broad, cross-domain capabilities, focused on developmental goals such as increasing manufacturing productivity and tailoring models to industry-specific use cases — distinct from the U.S. discourse around Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), which centers on rivaling or surpassing human cognition [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].

## Key claims

- The Chinese term 通用人工智能 places "general" before "artificial," and is commonly translated as either "general artificial intelligence" or "artificial general intelligence," but in Chinese policy and research usage refers to broadly capable AI without equating capabilities to human intelligence (though they may be comparable in some or all domains) [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
- Unlike AGI's focus on rivaling human cognition, GAI focuses on developmental goals such as increasing manufacturing productivity and prioritizes tailoring models toward industry-specific use cases [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
- China has organized its AI development around "general AI" as an integrating rubric across the economy, in contrast to U.S. labs' focus on compute-intensive frontier models [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
- The USCC paper contrasts GAI with the Commission's 2024 Annual Report definition of AGI as systems "as good as or better than human capabilities across all cognitive domains" that "would surpass the sharpest human minds at every task" [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
