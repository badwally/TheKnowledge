# Large Conditioning Information Sets in Finance

## Summary

Observation, foundational to Kelly and Xiu's 2023 financial-machine-learning survey, that price-relevant information in financial markets is vast in both time-series and cross-sectional dimensions — and that the gap between investor and researcher information sets is precisely what machine learning is suited to narrow [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].

## Key claims

- Studying asset prices is inextricably tied to information; the guiding question of financial economics is "what information do market participants have and how do they use it?" [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- The expanse of price-relevant information is compounded by the panel nature of financial markets — both time-series variation in the market environment (high vs. low risk regimes, policy regimes) and cross-sectional asset-level attributes shape prices [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- Kelly and Xiu cite Gu et al. (2020b) for the observation that "the profession has accumulated a staggering list of predictors": stock-level predictive characteristics number in the hundreds and macroeconomic predictors of the aggregate market number in the dozens [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- Cochrane (2009) is quoted to argue that "we obviously don't even observe all the conditioning information used by economic agents, and we can't include even a fraction of observed conditioning information in our models" [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- Hansen and Richard (1987), and more recently Martin and Nagel (2021), are cited for highlighting differences between information accessible to investors inside an economic model and information available to an econometrician on the outside looking in [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- Kelly and Xiu argue that "machine learning is a toolkit that can help narrow the gap between information sets of researchers and market participants by providing methods that allow the researcher to assimilate larger information sets" [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
- They note that the information content of news text has only recently emerged as an input to empirical asset-pricing models, and that there is much room for expansion on this and other frontiers [[sources/pdf-e2068d89c2a7]].
