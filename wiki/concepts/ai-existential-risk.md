# AI Existential Risk

## Summary

The class of catastrophic risk arising from sufficiently powerful AI systems behaving in ways that imperil human civilization; one of the canonical anthropogenic existential risks featured in Aschenbrenner's (2020) growth-and-risk model, where AI capability investment sits in the consumption (risk-creating) sector and AI safety investment sits in the safety (risk-mitigating) sector [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].

## Key claims

- Aschenbrenner identifies AI run amok as one of the key existential risks created by economic progress, alongside nuclear war, extreme climate change, and bioengineered pandemics [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- In the next century, powerful AI might engender amazing advances, but some worry that it could run amok — making AI a paradigmatic case of growth-driven existential risk in the model [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Risk increases with more consumption: "more artificial intelligence technology that could go awry" is listed alongside more carbon emissions, more air travel facilitating disease spread, and more bioengineering as channels by which growth raises x-risk [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- AI safety investment is treated as part of the safety sector in Aschenbrenner's model, alongside emissions abatement, biosecurity, and pandemic preparedness — operationalizing Bostrom's (2002) principle of differential technological development for AI [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Whether accelerating AI capability research raises or ultimately lowers x-risk depends on the scale-effect parameters in the model: for moderate scale effects, faster growth initially raises AI x-risk but improves long-run survival probability via increased eventual safety spending [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
