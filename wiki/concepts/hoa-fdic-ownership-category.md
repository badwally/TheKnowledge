# HOA FDIC Ownership Category (Corporation/Partnership/Unincorporated Association Accounts)

## Summary

Homeowners associations — whether incorporated or unincorporated — typically fall under the FDIC's Corporation/Partnership/Unincorporated Association Accounts ownership category, which provides $250,000 of FDIC coverage per Taxpayer Identification Number per bank [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]]. All accounts owned by the same HOA at the same bank are aggregated under this category, meaning HOAs cannot use multiple deposit account types (savings + checking + CDs, for instance) at the same bank to circumvent the per-bank limit [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]]. The aggregation rule extends across the bank's branches, so distributing deposits across multiple branches of the same institution does not extend coverage [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]]. Exceeding the limit at a single bank may be noted in the HOA's independent auditor's report [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].

## Key claims

- HOAs (incorporated or unincorporated) typically fall under the FDIC's Corporation/Partnership/Unincorporated Association Accounts ownership category [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- HOAs in this category have a $250,000 reserve-funds FDIC coverage limit per Taxpayer Identification Number per FDIC-insured bank [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- All accounts owned by the same HOA at the same bank are aggregated for FDIC-limit purposes [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- HOAs cannot use multiple deposit account types at the same bank to circumvent the FDIC per-bank coverage limit [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- The aggregation rule extends across the same bank's branches [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- If an HOA exceeds the FDIC limit at a single bank, the excess may be noted in the independent auditor's report [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- The ownership-category framework is what creates the binding constraint that CDARS and ICS are designed to relax through IntraFi-network multi-bank distribution under a single banking relationship [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
