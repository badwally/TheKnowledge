# Nova Scotia Prudent Investor Standard

## Summary

The Nova Scotia Prudent Investor Standard is the statutory trustee-investment standard codified at §§3, 3A, 3B, 3C, 3D, 3E, and 3F of the Trustee Act (R.S.N.S. 1989, c. 479), as introduced by the 2002 c. 10 s. 45 amendment [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]]. The standard authorizes investment in any form of property or security in which a prudent investor might invest, subject to a care-skill-diligence-judgment obligation (§3A), a diversification requirement (§3B), a plan-conditional liability safe harbor (§3C), discretion for courts to assess damages on portfolio-wide performance (§3D), an advice-reliance protection (§3E), and a delegation regime for investment agents (§3F) [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

## Key claims

- The prudent-investor regime was introduced into the NS Trustee Act by the 2002 c. 10 s. 45 amendment, which added §§3, 3A, 3B, 3C, 3D, 3E, and 3F [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3(1) permits a trustee to invest trust property in any form of property or security in which a prudent investor might invest, including a security issued by a mutual fund as defined in the Securities Act [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3A requires a trustee to exercise the care, skill, diligence, and judgment that a prudent investor would exercise in making investments [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- The standard is not unconditional: §3(2) provides that §3(1) does not authorize a trustee to invest in a manner that is inconsistent with the trust [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §5 preserves any express prohibition on particular investments contained in the instrument creating the trust [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3(3) enumerates non-exclusive criteria the trustee may consider in planning investments: general economic conditions; the possible effect of inflation or deflation; expected tax consequences; the role each investment or course of action plays within the overall trust portfolio; expected total return from income and the appreciation of capital; other resources of the beneficiaries; needs for liquidity, regularity of income, and preservation or appreciation of capital; and any asset's special relationship or special value to the purposes of the trust or to one or more of the beneficiaries [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3E(2) protects a trustee from breach-of-trust liability for reliance on investment advice if a prudent investor would rely upon the advice under comparable circumstances [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §11(1) permits a trustee in discretion to call in trust funds invested in unauthorized securities and reinvest them in securities authorized by the Act, and to vary any investments authorized by the Act [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
