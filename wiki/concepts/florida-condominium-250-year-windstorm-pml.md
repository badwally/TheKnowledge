# Florida Condominium 250-Year Windstorm Probable Maximum Loss Standard

## Summary

The 250-year windstorm probable maximum loss (PML) standard, codified in Florida Statute §718.111(11)(a)2, is the methodological gating threshold for a Florida residential condominium association — or group of associations — to provide property insurance via a multi-community group program rather than standard per-association coverage. To qualify, the program must cover at least three communities created and operating under Chapter 718, 719, 720, or 721, and the coverage amount must be sufficient to cover the probable maximum loss for the communities computed against a 250-year windstorm event. The PML computation must rely on a competent model that has been accepted by the Florida Commission on Hurricane Loss Projection Methodology (FCHLPM), and a policy or program providing such coverage may not be issued or renewed after July 1, 2008 unless reviewed and approved per statutory procedures [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].

## Key claims

- An association may provide adequate property insurance coverage for a group of at least three communities created and operating under Chapter 718, 719, 720, or 721 by obtaining and maintaining insurance coverage sufficient to cover an amount equal to the probable maximum loss for the communities for a 250-year windstorm event, per §718.111(11)(a)2 [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The probable maximum loss for a 250-year windstorm event must be determined through the use of a competent model that has been accepted by the Florida Commission on Hurricane Loss Projection Methodology, per §718.111(11)(a)2 [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- A policy or program providing 250-year-windstorm-PML coverage may not be issued or renewed after July 1, 2008 unless it has been reviewed and approved per the statute's referenced procedures [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The 250-year-PML pathway is an alternative to (a) standard per-association replacement-cost insurance under §718.111(11)(a) and (b) self-insurance funds complying with §§624.460–624.488 under §718.111(11)(a)1 [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The three-community minimum aggregates across Florida statutory regimes — Chapter 718 (condominiums), Chapter 719 (cooperatives), Chapter 720 (homeowner associations), and Chapter 721 (vacation and timeshare plans) — making the standard cross-applicable to the four Florida common-interest-community regimes [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
