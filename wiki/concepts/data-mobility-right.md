# Right to Data Mobility

## Summary

A right to data mobility is a statutory right entitling consumers to direct data holders to move their data to other approved providers, on demand, in a standardized machine-readable format [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]]. Canada's 2025 Budget Implementation Act amends PIPEDA to establish an economy-wide right to data mobility that activates sector-by-sector as each sector develops a secure and interoperable framework; consumer-driven banking is the first such activation [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].

## Key claims

- The data-mobility right is structured as an enabling power: PIPEDA grants the right at the economy level, but it takes effect only in sectors that have built compliant secure and interoperable frameworks [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The Government frames data mobility as the foundation for "open finance" (cross-financial-sector portability) and "open data" (cross-sectoral portability) as the next stages beyond consumer-driven banking [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- In-scope data must be shared at consumer instruction, free of charge, and in a standardized machine-readable format [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The right is bounded by an explicit exclusion of derived data — analytics, scores, and inferences computed by the holder are not portable [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The Department of Finance cites the UK's Data (Use and Access) Bill (projected £10 billion GDP boost over 10 years) as comparable evidence for the downstream economic value of a statutory data-mobility right [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
