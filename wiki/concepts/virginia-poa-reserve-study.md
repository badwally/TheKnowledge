# Virginia POA Reserve Study (five-year cycle, annual review)

## Summary

The Virginia Property Owners' Association Act imposes a two-layered reserve-planning obligation on POA boards: a full reserve study at least once every five years, plus an annual review of the existing study's results to determine whether reserves remain sufficient. The annual review is paired with an explicit board duty to adjust the annual budget and annual assessment as needed to maintain reserves [[sources/web-2026-05-23-01f]]. The statutory framing treats the reserve study as a planning input rather than a fixed funding mandate — funding discretion is preserved at the board level under §55.1-1826(D) — distinguishing Virginia's POA regime from prescriptive funding-floor regimes such as the Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac 15% requirement for condominium projects.

## Key claims

- Virginia POA boards must conduct a reserve study "at least once every five years" to determine the necessity and amount of reserves required to repair, replace, and restore capital components as defined in §55.1-1800 [[sources/web-2026-05-23-01f]].
- The five-year cadence is a statutory floor ("at least once"), allowing more-frequent updates at board discretion [[sources/web-2026-05-23-01f]].
- Boards must review reserve-study results at least annually to determine if reserves remain sufficient, independent of the five-year full-study cycle [[sources/web-2026-05-23-01f]].
- The annual review is paired with an affirmative duty to make adjustments to the annual budget and annual assessment that the board deems necessary to maintain reserves, as appropriate [[sources/web-2026-05-23-01f]].
- Where the reserve study indicates a need to budget for reserves, the annual budget must disclose: (1) current estimated replacement cost, estimated remaining life, and estimated useful life of capital components; (2) accumulated cash reserves at fiscal-year start plus expected contribution; (3) the estimation/accumulation procedures used; and (4) the recommended reserve amount per the study compared to current cash for replacement reserves [[sources/web-2026-05-23-01f]].
- The statute does not mandate a percentage funding floor or require the board to fully fund the recommended reserve amount; the board retains discretion to meet repair and replacement requirements through replacement reserves, additional assessments, or borrowed funds [[sources/web-2026-05-23-01f]].
- The reserve-study requirement applies only to POAs governed by the Property Owners' Association Act (Title 55.1, Chapter 18); the Virginia Condominium Act governs condominium associations under a separate statutory title and is not the controlling reference for §55.1-1826 obligations [[sources/web-2026-05-23-01f]].
