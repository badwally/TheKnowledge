# Two Loops framework (digital + physical AI loops)

## Summary

An analytic frame, developed in Ngor Luong's March 2026 USCC paper, that distinguishes a "digital loop" of frontier model training (compute-, data-, and algorithm-bound) from a "physical loop" of deployment-driven data creation across factories, robotics, and embodied AI — and argues that U.S. export controls primarily target the former while leaving the latter unaddressed [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].

## Key claims

- The digital loop is the conventional AI training loop: advanced chips → frontier model training → model improvement → demand for more chips; U.S. export controls on advanced semiconductors primarily target this loop [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
- The physical loop is the deployment loop: low-cost AI deployment across factories, logistics networks, and robotics generates real-world proprietary data, which feeds back into model improvement, which enables further deployment [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
- China's open-model strategy and its manufacturing dominance are mutually reinforcing across the two loops: open models accelerate low-cost deployment across China's industrial base, which generates real-world data that compounds model and deployment advantages [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
- China's strategy prioritizes data curation and refinement through the deployment of embodied AI in manufacturing, robotics, and research, where specialized real-world data from widespread use may compound into advantages that proprietary U.S. models cannot easily replicate, even if those U.S. models maintain technical superiority on benchmarks [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
- As open models reduce the compute required for effective deployment, China's ability to generate proprietary industrial data at pace and scale becomes increasingly independent of access to cutting-edge hardware — a structural gap in the U.S. policy framework [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
- Even if U.S. export controls on training compute succeed in their stated goal, they may not prevent China from building AI advantages rooted in its physical economy [[sources/pdf-ngor-luong-2026-two-loops-how]].
