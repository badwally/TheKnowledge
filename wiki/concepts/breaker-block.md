# Breaker block

## Summary

A breaker block is a specific type of order block that forms when a run on external or internal liquidity is followed by a market structure shift in the opposite direction; the original order block "fails to do its job" and inverts into a launch pad in the new direction [[sources/pdf-042302b20a5d]].

## Key claims

- A breaker block forms when there is a run on external or internal liquidity followed by a market structure shift in the opposite direction [[sources/pdf-042302b20a5d]].
- A bullish breaker block is what was originally a bearish order block — when price runs liquidity below and then displaces back above the high that led to the raid, the bearish order block becomes a bullish breaker block [[sources/pdf-042302b20a5d]].
- A bearish breaker block is what was originally a bullish order block — when price runs liquidity above and then displaces back below the low that led to the raid, the bullish order block becomes a bearish breaker block [[sources/pdf-042302b20a5d]].
- Bullish breaker blocks are colored green and bearish breaker blocks black in the lesson's diagrams, capturing the inversion of the order block's original role [[sources/pdf-042302b20a5d]].
- Breaker blocks act as mini launch pads because, on a retracement back into the zone, smart money places orders in the new trend direction while trapped traders mitigate losses — both flows push price the same way (in a bullish breaker, longs entering and shorts covering are both buying) [[sources/pdf-042302b20a5d]].
- A breaker block combined with a Fair Value Gap (FVG) — when the draw on liquidity is in the same direction — creates a "giant launch pad" for price [[sources/pdf-042302b20a5d]].
- Bullish breaker block sequence: price takes out external liquidity into a key area expected to be support, then trades back above the high that led to the liquidity raid; that level becomes the bullish breaker block [[sources/pdf-042302b20a5d]].
- Bearish breaker block sequence: price takes out external liquidity into a key area expected to be resistance, then trades back below the low that led to the liquidity raid; that level becomes the bearish breaker block [[sources/pdf-042302b20a5d]].
- In the bearish $ES example, candles wick above the breaker block but bodies never close above, indicating that price will seek more sell-side liquidity before another move up [[sources/pdf-042302b20a5d]].
