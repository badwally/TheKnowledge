# Governing Knowledge Commons (GKC) Framework

## Summary

The Governing Knowledge Commons (GKC) framework is an analytical approach for studying the construction and governance of commons over information, data, and other cultural-environment resources. Its foundational statement is Madison, Frischmann, and Strandburg's article "Constructing Commons in the Cultural Environment," published as 95 Cornell L. Rev. 657 (2010), which adapts Elinor Ostrom's institutional-analysis work on natural-resource commons to the "cultural environment" — knowledge, information, and data pooled and governed by communities rather than allocated solely through private property or state provision [[sources/web-2014-08-29-013]].

## Key claims

- The framework treats commons construction in the cultural environment as a tractable object of structural analysis, paralleling Ostrom's treatment of natural-resource commons [[sources/web-2014-08-29-013]].
- Madison, Frischmann, and Strandburg are the canonical co-authors of the framework's foundational statement, the 2010 Cornell Law Review article "Constructing Commons in the Cultural Environment" [[sources/web-2014-08-29-013]].
- The framework is foundational for analyzing how data collectives — including data commons, data trusts, and data cooperatives — are constructed and governed as institutional arrangements over shared informational resources [[sources/web-2014-08-29-013]].
