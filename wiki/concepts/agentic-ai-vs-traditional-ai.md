# Agentic AI vs Traditional AI

## Summary

A structured comparison of Agentic AI against classical rule-based AI, supervised-learning AI, and reinforcement-learning agents, framed by the Acharya et al. survey as the analytical lens that motivates Agentic AI's distinctness [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].

## Key claims

- Traditional AI systems are typically integrated into specific tasks such as image analysis, language translation, and recommendation engines — narrow scope, supervised learning on large datasets, behavior determined by inputs and instructions [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].
- Traditional AI is best applied in controlled environments and has limited ability to micro-manage situations with significant outcomes [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].
- Agentic AI is open-ended: there is no prescribed method for achieving a task, and systems work with and adapt to rapidly changing conditions [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].
- Conventional AI may be accurate but lacks the situational awareness and goal-directed dynamics inherent in Agentic AI [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].
- A factory equipment-failure model illustrates the gap: rule-based AI cannot incorporate adaptive shifts driven by manufacturing-schedule changes or evolving wear patterns, whereas Agentic AI can change its metric-estimation processes by context [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].
- Classical agents excel in controlled environments — e.g., rule-based financial trading algorithms with constant predefined parameters — but struggle with volatile market changes or unpredictable disruptions [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].
- Agentic-AI-powered trading systems dynamically adjust strategies based on real-time data, historical trends, and unexpected market shifts, making them more resilient and adaptive than rule-based equivalents [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].
- Reinforcement-learning agents focus on optimizing cumulative rewards in a specific task environment, while language-model-based Agentic AI extends this by interpreting complex natural-language inputs and interacting with humans seamlessly [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].
- Example contrast: an RL agent excels at optimizing gameplay strategies, whereas a language-model-based agent can generate dialogue, interpret rules, and adapt strategies during a live game [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].
