# Screen Scraping (Financial Data Access)

## Summary

Screen scraping is the data-access practice in which consumers share their confidential banking credentials with third-party financial service providers, who then log into the consumer's bank account as if they were the consumer and extract data from the rendered interface [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]]. Canada's consumer-driven banking framework is designed in part to replace screen scraping with API-based access, citing increased security, liability, and privacy risks for consumers who currently rely on it [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].

## Key claims

- Roughly nine million Canadians currently share their financial data via screen scraping [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Screen scraping exposes consumers to increased security, liability, and privacy risks, and leaves them without recourse if something goes wrong [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- APIs are positioned as the replacement substrate — a more secure communications connection between entities than credential-sharing [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Canada's Consumer-Driven Banking Act will prohibit screen scraping once the framework is fully operational; the timing of the prohibition is subject to further Department of Finance consultation with stakeholders [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
