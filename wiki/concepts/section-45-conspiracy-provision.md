# Section 45 Criminal Conspiracy Provision (Competition Act)

## Summary

Section 45 of Canada's Competition Act is the criminal prohibition on agreements between competitors to fix prices, allocate markets, or restrict output [[sources/web-2024-06-27-57a]] [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]]. The provision is reserved for "naked restraints" — agreements that are not implemented in furtherance of a legitimate collaboration, strategic alliance, or joint venture — and these agreements are often referred to as "hard core" cartels [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].

## Key claims

- Section 45's modern form was created by the 2009 amendments to the Competition Act, which established a more effective criminal enforcement regime for the most egregious cartel agreements while removing criminal-sanction risk from legitimate competitor collaborations [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Only agreements between competitors to fix prices, allocate markets, or restrict output may be subject to section 45 prosecution; other competitor collaborations (joint ventures, strategic alliances) are routed to the civil track under section 90.1 [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Prosecutorial discretion under section 45 is exercised by the Director of Public Prosecutions (DPP), not the Commissioner of Competition; the Commissioner's role is to investigate and refer [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Vertical agreements between suppliers and customers are generally not assessed under section 45 — even where the supplier competes with the customer in respect of the supplied product (dual distribution), the arrangement is routed to Part VIII civil review [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Bid-rigging is assessed under section 47 or section 90.1 rather than section 45 — except where the bid-rigging is bundled with additional restraints (e.g., market allocation) or is part of a broader conspiracy, in which case section 45 can apply alongside section 47 [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Agreements between federal financial institutions described in subsection 49(1) are assessed under section 49, not section 45 [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
