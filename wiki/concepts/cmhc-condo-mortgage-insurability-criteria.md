# CMHC Condominium Mortgage Insurability Criteria

## Summary

CMHC (Canada Mortgage and Housing Corporation) imposes additional financial-reporting and reserve-funding criteria on condominium corporations whose unit owners hold CMHC-insured mortgages, beyond the baseline statutory obligations under the Ontario Condominium Act, 1998. Per CPA-practitioner guidance, the criteria collectively form an "insurability" standard that governs whether prospective buyers in a given condominium corporation can obtain CMHC mortgage insurance — making the criteria load-bearing for unit resale value, buyer mortgage eligibility, and property insurance rates [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]]. The criteria operate as a private-sector overlay on top of the public statutory regime, raising the effective floor on reserve funding and financial-reporting quality for any condo corporation with even a single CMHC-insured unit [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].

## Key claims

- CMHC compliance imposes additional financial-reporting requirements on condominium corporations beyond those in the Ontario Condominium Act, 1998 [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- CMHC requires an "adequate reserve fund balance," typically benchmarked at 25%+ of the corporation's annual budget — higher than the baseline 10% threshold referenced in the same source for statutory compliance [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- CMHC requires no pending special assessments [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- CMHC requires financial statements prepared by qualified accountants [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- CMHC requires the corporation's most recent reserve fund study be less than 3 years old [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- Non-compliance with CMHC criteria carries cascading market consequences: impacts on resale values, buyer mortgage eligibility, and property insurance rates [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- The practitioner framing positions CMHC compliance as "essential for protecting property values and ensuring buyer financing" in luxury condominium segments in Mississauga and downtown Toronto [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
