# Pricing the Damage Matrix

## Summary

The BlackRock Investment Institute's tactical allocation framework in its 2023 Global Outlook: a two-by-two matrix crossing market risk sentiment (on/off) with the assessment of how much economic damage is reflected in asset prices (yes/no), used to guide how and when to shift positioning across equities, credit, short-term government bonds, and long-term government bonds [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].

## Key claims

- The framework asserts that navigating markets in the new regime "will require more frequent portfolio changes" driven by two assessments: market risk sentiment and how much economic damage is in the price [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- BII positioned itself in November 2022 in the "risk off, damage not priced" quadrant — underweight equities, underweight long-term government bonds — their "most defensive stance" [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- From this starting position, all other quadrant options involve turning more positive, especially on equities [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- BII is underweight nominal long-term government bonds in every quadrant — this is their "strongest conviction in any scenario" [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- The framework allows turning positive via two independent channels: either market risk sentiment improves, or valuations get closer to reflecting the economic damage [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- BII finds that equity earnings expectations as of late 2022 don't yet price in even a mild recession — the reason they stay underweight DM equities tactically [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- Evidence of damage building: surging mortgage rates cratered U.S. new-home sales — with the 2022 decline already steeper than past mega Fed hiking cycles (1970s, early 1980s) and the mid-2000s housing boom unwind [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- Other warning signs: deteriorating CEO confidence, delayed capital spending plans, consumers depleting savings, and European incomes hit by the energy shock amplified by tightening financial conditions [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- The framework calls for a "continuous reassessment of how much of the economic damage being generated by central banks is in the price" — not a buy-and-hold posture [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
