# Bias vs Narrative (ICT)

## Summary

In ICT's framework, "bias" and "narrative" are distinct: bias is the trader's directional view limited to a roughly 5-day horizon, while narrative is the explanatory model — what price should do, why, and what it must encounter to confirm the assumption — and ICT teaches that an asymmetric bias (easy to argue one way, hard to argue the other) is the prerequisite for a high-probability trade [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].

## Key claims

- Bias should keep the trader's perspective limited to a 5-day time horizon — ICT instructs not trying to predict more than a week in advance [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
- Narrative is defined as "the understanding of what price should do, why, and what things will it encounter to prove that the narrative you are assuming is in place is in fact underway" [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
- Narrative is based on climate, economic calendar events, and volatility on that day, and on the why of market structure (e.g., a swing low after a low making a return to the most recent short-term high probable) [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
- If a bullish stance is easily communicated and a bearish stance is a hard stretch, that's high-probability; if the trade could go either way and the trader enters anyway, they will regret it later — easy to blow accounts that way [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
- ICT instructs starting bias analysis on the daily chart and only waiting for setups consistent with that bias (long only or short only) unless absolutely proven wrong [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
- If the trader cannot reasonably outline whether the day is going higher or lower, they are gambling [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
- Once daily-chart consolidation begins, bias becomes hard to form; the trader should drop to smaller timeframes, look for liquidity pools, and be more nimble — taking money and running rather than overstaying [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
- It's important to know what to look for ahead of time so the trader isn't changing gears intraday and chasing impulsive price swings [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
- Anytime the market trades above an old high it's at a premium (going into liquidity); anytime it trades into an old low it's at a discount — but premium and discount can still extend, which is why narrative-within-market-structure determines actionable bias [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
