# U.S. HOA Non-Exempt Function Income (Form 1120-H)

## Summary

Non-exempt function income is the Form 1120-H tax category that an HOA is taxed on under the homeowners-association election regime. Non-exempt function income includes interest, nonmember fees, and per-use rental income, net of expenses, and is taxed at a flat rate of 30% under Form 1120-H [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]]. A consequential property of this category is that the type of reserve project — whether the reserve project does or does not fall under the IRS definition of a "capital" project — does not matter for tax purposes under Form 1120-H, enabling associations to responsibly fund non-capital projects through reserves without CPA involvement during tax season [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].

## Key claims

- Non-exempt function income includes interest, nonmember fees, and per-use rental income, net of expenses [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Non-exempt function income is taxed at a flat rate of 30% under Form 1120-H [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Because taxes under Form 1120-H are paid based on non-exempt function income, the type of reserve projects in the Reserve Study — those that do or do not fall under the IRS definition of a "capital" project — does NOT matter for tax purposes [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- This means associations filing Form 1120-H can responsibly fund painting, asphalt sealing, tree trimming, and other major "non-capital" (by IRS definition) projects through reserves without needing to involve a CPA during tax season [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Under Form 1120 (the alternative filing election), the capital vs. non-capital categorization DOES matter and requires CPA involvement during tax season [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
