# HOA 60% Gross Income Test (§528 Qualification)

## Summary

The 60% gross income test is one of the two threshold tests an association must satisfy to qualify as a homeowners association under §528 and file Form 1120-H [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]]. The test requires that at least 60% of the association's gross income for the tax year consist of exempt function income — principally membership dues, fees, and assessments from owners in their capacity as owners [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]]. The test is administered as Item B on Form 1120-H and operates in tandem with the 90% expenditure test (Item C) and the no-private-benefit requirement [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]]. An association whose investment income (interest, dividends, capital gains) or non-member revenue (e.g., laundry-room collections, guest fees, rental income from non-association property) grows large enough to push exempt function income below 60% of gross income loses §528 eligibility for that year and must file Form 1120 instead [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].

## Key claims

- To qualify as a homeowners association under §528, at least 60% of the association's gross income for the tax year must consist of exempt function income [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- The 60% test is administered as Item B on Form 1120-H — the form's structural index reflects Item A (type of homeowners association), Item B (60% gross income test), Item C (90% expenditure test), Item D, and Item E [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- Exempt function income for purposes of the 60% test consists of membership dues, fees, or assessments from owners of condominium housing units (condominium management associations); owners of real property (residential real estate management associations); or owners of timeshare rights to use or timeshare ownership interests (timeshare associations) — and must come from the members as owners, not as customers, of the association's services [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- Income that does NOT count toward the 60% exempt function income threshold includes: amounts that are not includible in the organization's gross income other than under §528 (e.g., tax-exempt interest); payments from nonmembers; payments from members for special use of the organization's facilities apart from use generally available to all members; interest on amounts in a sinking fund; payments for work done on nonassociation property; and members' payments for transportation [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- The 60% gross income test operates jointly with the 90% expenditure test under §528: failure of either test disqualifies the association from §528 status for that tax year [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
