# Three Funding Mechanisms for the Reserve Fund

## Summary

A condominium reserve fund can only be funded through three distinct mechanisms: (i) a special assessment, (ii) an increase in regular common-expense fees, or (iii) improved investment returns on the existing reserve balance. The first two transfer capital from unit owners; the third leverages the existing reserve principal. Will MacKay (CIBC Wood Gundy) frames the third mechanism as the politically and operationally preferred starting point for Ontario boards, since it raises reserves without imposing a new financial demand on owners — provided the investments remain compliant with Condominium Act §115(5) eligibility rules [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].

## Key claims

- There are only three ways to get money into a condominium reserve fund: special assessments, raising fees, or investing better [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
- Of the three mechanisms, investing better is the only one that does not transfer additional capital from unit owners; the other two require fresh owner contributions [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
- Will MacKay characterizes improved investment returns as the preferred starting point among the three mechanisms for Ontario boards seeking to bolster the reserve fund [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
- The investing-better mechanism operates only within the constrained universe of "eligible securities" defined by §115(5) of the Ontario Condominium Act, 1998 [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
- An investment plan is required prior to engaging in investing the reserve fund per the Ontario Condominium Act, 1998 — the investing-better mechanism cannot operate without a formalized plan [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
