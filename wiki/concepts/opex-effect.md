# OpEx effect

## Summary

The OpEx effect is the empirical drag on equity-index returns observed around monthly option-expiration dates; Ambrus Capital attributes it to dealers unwinding gamma hedges as large option positions roll off, temporarily removing the stabilizing force that gamma reflexivity normally provides [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].

## Key claims

- Equity options have a monthly expiry calendar; as large option positions are closed on these dates, dealers reduce their hedging — usually starting before option expiry [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- The stabilizing effect that dealer gamma hedging normally provides is massively reduced on option-expiration dates, causing increased volatility and a corresponding drop in price [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- Ambrus Capital reports a backtest in which a portfolio that buys the S&P 500 at the close the night before option expiry and sells at the close on the option-expiry date loses roughly 15% over a three-year window [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- The CBOE has been adding Tuesday and Thursday expirations for S&P index-based trading vehicles, meaning there will be an option expiration every day of the week — implying the effect is becoming more diffuse across the calendar [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
