# Ontario Reserve Fund Investment Plan (Section 115(8))

## Summary

Section 115(8) of the Ontario Condominium Act, 1998 imposes a statutorily distinct obligation — separate from the Reserve Fund Study and its funding plan — for the Board to develop an investment plan based on the anticipated cash requirements of the reserve fund as set out in the most recent RFS. The investment plan is the operational liquidity-and-return overlay on top of the funded reserve balance, crafted with a licensed investment professional rather than the reserve fund study provider [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].

## Key claims

- Section 115(8) of the Ontario Condominium Act, 1998 states: "Before investing any part of the money in the corporation's reserve fund accounts, the board shall develop an investment plan based on the anticipated cash requirements of the reserve fund as set out in the most recent reserve fund study." [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]]
- The investment plan is separate and distinct from the reserve fund study and the related funding plan: the Act specifies that the RFS shall be conducted by a reserve fund study provider, whereas the investment plan should be crafted with the help of a licensed investment professional [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- The investment plan outlines a strategy ensuring that funds are available when needed for major projects while enhancing investment returns to the corporation [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- As a best practice, the investment plan should be reviewed annually and refreshed at minimum every time the reserve fund study is updated [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- As buildings age and components require replacement, the liquidity requirements of the reserve-fund investment plan increase [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- Some fixed-term investments such as GICs can be sold prior to maturity, but at the discretion of the GIC provider and typically with surrender of all interest earned to date plus processing fees [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- A reserve fund investment portfolio should consist of both fixed-term and non-fixed-term securities, with fixed-term securities laddered by maturity and paying interest annually [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- A rule-of-thumb cited for sizing the non-fixed-term portion is to double the age of the corporation (e.g., a 15-year-old corporation would hold approximately 30% of its overall investment balance available within a given fiscal year) [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- The investment plan is the statutory grounding for the CMRAO-defined licensee obligation to support the Board in the reserve fund investment process [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
