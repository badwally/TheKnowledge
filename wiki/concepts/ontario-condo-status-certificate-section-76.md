# Ontario Condo Status Certificate (Section 76)

## Summary

Section 76 of the Ontario Condominium Act, 1998 requires a condominium corporation to issue a status certificate when a unit is sold, containing prescribed financial disclosures that prospective buyers, real estate lawyers, mortgage lenders, and home inspectors rely on for due diligence [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]]. The status certificate functions as the corporation's authoritative point-in-time financial-condition disclosure to the transaction counterparties; inaccurate financial information in a status certificate can delay real estate transactions, expose the corporation to liability, undermine buyer confidence, and trigger lender concerns [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].

## Key claims

- Section 76 of the Condominium Act, 1998 governs the status-certificate issuance requirement triggered by the sale of a condominium unit [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- Required financial disclosures in the status certificate include: current reserve fund balance, reserve fund study summary, outstanding special assessments, unit owner's account balance (arrears), pending legal actions, and insurance certificates [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- The status certificate is relied upon by four named counterparty categories: prospective buyers conducting due diligence, real estate lawyers, mortgage lenders, and home inspectors [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- Inaccurate financial information in a status certificate can: delay real estate transactions, expose the corporation to liability, undermine buyer confidence, and trigger lender concerns [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- The practical implication for board governance is that maintaining accurate, up-to-date financial records is a precondition for status certificates to be issued quickly and reliably — making the §76 disclosure quality downstream of the corporation's underlying bookkeeping and reserve-fund accounting practices [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
