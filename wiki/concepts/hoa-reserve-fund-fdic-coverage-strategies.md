# HOA Reserve Fund FDIC Coverage Strategies

## Summary

Staying within the FDIC's $250,000 per-bank reserve-funds coverage limit is the only way an HOA can fully protect reserve funds against bank failure; uninsured excess is permanently lost in the event of insolvency [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]]. The Clark Simson Miller framework enumerates four named strategies HOAs can use to maximize coverage while still maintaining deposits well above the per-bank cap: the IntraFi Certificate of Deposit Account Registry Service (CDARS), the IntraFi Cash Service (ICS), money market accounts, and CDs and CD ladders [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]]. CDARS and ICS allow an HOA to maintain a single banking relationship while distributing funds across multiple member banks for FDIC-coverage purposes; money market accounts offer tiered interest rates and can serve as an operating overflow or reserve account; and CD ladders provide fixed rates while still permitting access to funds every few months [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]]. The alternative — opening multiple bank accounts at unrelated institutions — is described as inconvenient, confusing, and prone to fraud because multiple bank accounts are more challenging to track [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].

## Key claims

- Staying within the FDIC reserve-funds limit is the only way an HOA can fully protect its funds in the event of bank failure [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Funds exceeding the FDIC per-bank limit at a failed institution are lost forever [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Loss of uninsured reserve funds may leave the HOA unable to cover unexpected expenditures, forcing sudden monthly-dues increases or large special assessments [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Losing uninsured reserve funds could jeopardize an HOA's compliance with state law in jurisdictions that require HOAs to follow the recommendations of their reserve study [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Some states require HOAs to keep reserve funds within FDIC limits [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Four named strategies for maximizing FDIC coverage while maintaining deposits above the per-bank cap: CDARS, ICS, money market accounts, and CDs and CD ladders [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- CDARS distributes the HOA's funds across CDs at various member banks while permitting a single banking relationship for all CDARS transactions [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- ICS distributes the HOA's funds into Demand Deposit Account-backed savings accounts at multiple member banks; the savings accounts bear interest at a higher rate than traditional savings accounts and remain FDIC-insured [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Money market accounts offer tiered interest rates depending on the account balance and can serve as an operating overflow account or a reserve account [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- CDs and CD ladders use the CD's length and deposit amount to determine rates and terms; ladders offer fixed rates while still providing access to funds every few months [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Opening multiple bank accounts at unrelated institutions is an inferior alternative — inconvenient, confusing, and prone to fraud because multiple accounts are more challenging to track [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- The structural advantage of CDARS and ICS over multi-bank-account approaches is the preservation of a single banking relationship as the operational front-end despite multi-bank distribution at the FDIC-coverage layer [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
