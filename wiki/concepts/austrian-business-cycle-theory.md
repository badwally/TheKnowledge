# Austrian Business Cycle Theory

## Summary

The Austrian explanation of the boom-bust cycle, presented by Per Bylund in Chapter 8 ("Monetary Intervention") of his primer as a theory of how artificial credit expansion distorts the rate of return on capital investments, creates an unsustainable boom, reaches a turning point, and necessitates a corrective bust [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].

## Key claims

- Bylund structures the Austrian business cycle theory across five sections in Chapter 8: "The Boom-Bust Cycle," "The Rate of Return and Capital Investments," "The Cause and Nature of the Artificial Boom," "The Turning Point," and "The Corrective Bust" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- Chapter 8 is titled "Monetary Intervention" and falls under Part III ("Intervention"), framing the boom-bust cycle as a consequence of monetary policy interference rather than an inherent feature of market economies [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- The theory builds on Chapter 6's treatment of "Fiat Currency and Price Inflation," which covers fiat money as a precondition for the monetary distortions that drive the cycle [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
