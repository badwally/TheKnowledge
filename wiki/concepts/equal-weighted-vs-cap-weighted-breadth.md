# Equal-Weighted vs Cap-Weighted Breadth Signal

## Summary

Andrew Slimmon's reading that the gap between S&P 500 equal-weighted (-11%) and S&P 500 cap-weighted (-17%) returns through November 29, 2022 indicates broader market breadth and is a bullish signal arguing against the consensus early-2023 earnings-collapse narrative; reinforced by sectoral leadership of cyclical groups (financials, industrials, materials) that would not be leading if the economy were about to roll over [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].

## Key claims

- Per Slimmon, the S&P 500 equal-weighted was down -11% while the S&P 500 cap-weighted was down -17% (Bloomberg, November 29, 2022) [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Slimmon reads the divergence — equal-weighted holding up better than cap-weighted — as broader breadth, and as a bullish signal [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Sectoral confirmation: financials, industrials, and materials all outperformed in October and November 2022 — economically cyclical groups Slimmon argues would not be leading if the economy were going to collapse in Q1 2023 [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- Slimmon ties the breadth signal to a bullish process conclusion: "the economy is proving too resilient, causing the 'looming collapse' in earnings to remain elusive for yet another quarter" [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
- The breadth signal is the empirical lever Slimmon uses to scale back megacap exposure (which dominates the cap-weighted index) in favor of equal-weighted exposure [[sources/pdf-bf1afc73ac8f]].
