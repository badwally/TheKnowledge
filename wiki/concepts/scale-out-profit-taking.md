# Scale-out profit taking

## Summary

Scale-out profit taking is the practice of selling a portion of an open winning position at a predetermined target while allowing the remainder to run, typically with a trailing stop — Matthew Ryan codifies a specific tick-based scale-out (sell 3/4ths at +20-30 ticks, run the remaining 1/4th with a trailing stop) and frames its purpose as teaching the trading brain to focus on process rather than dollar amounts [[sources/pdf-2248d6cdc39f]] [[sources/pdf-6ba2dc608ac8]].

## Key claims

- Ryan sets his target to a precise tick level and scales out of his position once that target is hit — selling back 3/4ths of position to secure profits, while letting the remaining contracts run with a trailing stop [[sources/pdf-2248d6cdc39f]] [[sources/pdf-6ba2dc608ac8]].
- The tick level is +20-30 ticks in his favor before he begins to scale out, depending on entry location and trade type [[sources/pdf-2248d6cdc39f]] [[sources/pdf-6ba2dc608ac8]].
- Some brokers let the trader configure scale-out mechanically with bracket orders, but the default settings may need adjustment [[sources/pdf-2248d6cdc39f]] [[sources/pdf-6ba2dc608ac8]].
- Ryan uses ticks rather than dollar amounts so his trading brain focuses on process and not outcome — his stated belief is that profits will come once the process is solidified [[sources/pdf-2248d6cdc39f]] [[sources/pdf-6ba2dc608ac8]].
