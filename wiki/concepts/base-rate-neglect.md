# Base-Rate Neglect

## Summary

The tendency for people to insufficiently weight prior probabilities when updating beliefs in response to new evidence; Augenblick, Lazarus, and Thaler (2024) estimate modest base-rate neglect in their Study 1b (which varied the prior across values of 1/4, 1/3, and 1/2) but demonstrate that it is a distinct phenomenon from signal-strength-dependent overinference and underinference — the core finding that people overinfer from weak signals and underinfer from strong signals holds even after disentangling base-rate biases [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].

## Key claims

- Study 1b in Augenblick, Lazarus, and Thaler (2024) systematically varies the prior probability (1/4, 1/3, and 1/2) in addition to signal strength, finding that participants exhibit modest base-rate neglect [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
- Although base-rate biases can impact how people react to new information, disentangling base-rate neglect from signal-strength-dependent updating does not substantially affect the core conclusion that people overinfer from weak signals and underinfer from strong signals [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
- The paper explicitly distinguishes between "overinference" (perceiving a signal as more informative than a Bayesian would) and "overreaction" (the resultant behavior of reacting too strongly), noting that a contaminating force such as base-rate neglect might cause beliefs to react too strongly for a reason other than overinference [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
