# Urban Water Infrastructure Planning

## Summary

Urban water infrastructure planning is the application of infrastructure asset management methods to the water, wastewater, and stormwater systems serving urban populations. It operates across strategic, tactical, and operational horizons and is increasingly supported by dedicated software platforms (e.g., AWARE-P) that couple hydraulic simulation with risk and cost analysis.

## Key claims

- Urban water infrastructure planning is motivated by the combination of deferred maintenance, ageing infrastructure, and overwhelming investment needs that characterise water services worldwide [[sources/web-2012-01-01-57d]].
- Effective urban water planning requires assessing water systems over multiple planning horizons, with methods and models hosted in dedicated open-source software platforms such as AWARE-P [[sources/web-2012-01-01-57d]].
- Key metrics for evaluating urban water infrastructure performance — including hydraulic performance, supply interruption risk, and service cost — are derived from comprehensive hydraulic simulation models such as EPANET [[sources/web-2012-01-01-57d]].
- Urban water infrastructure planning methodology integrates separate analytical foundations for water distribution and for sewer/stormwater networks, drawing on the EU-funded CARE-W and CARE-S projects respectively [[sources/web-2012-01-01-57d]].
