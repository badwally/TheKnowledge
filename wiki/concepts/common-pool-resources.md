# Common-Pool Resources (CPRs)

## Summary

Common-pool resources (CPRs) are shared resource pools whose access is regulated by a community of commoners through what Elinor Ostrom termed "CPR institutions" — durable governance arrangements that have, in many documented cases, regulated community access for centuries or millennia [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]]. In the data-collectives domain, framing pooled data as a CPR brings Ostrom's institutional-design vocabulary (boundaries, monitoring, graduated sanctions, conflict resolution) to bear on data governance.

## Key claims

- Ostrom used "CPR institutions" as the analytic unit for studying successful commons governance, foregrounding the institution — rules, monitoring, sanctions, decision-making — rather than the physical resource itself [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- The CPR institutions Ostrom studied ranged from over 100 years to over 1,000 years old at the time of her 1990 book, providing existence proofs that durable CPR governance is empirically possible [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Documented CPR cases include irrigation communities in the Philippines, village commons in Japan, and the common pasture of Torbel, Switzerland [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
