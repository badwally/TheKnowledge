# Failure Swing

## Summary

In ICT-style price-action analysis, a failure swing is one of the two institutional swing point theories: a swing low (or, by symmetry, a swing high) framed by a higher candle on each side, formed when price trades through a support/resistance level, returns to retest it, and is unable to make another pass through — used as the fallback pattern when the optimal breaker swing point does not develop [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].

## Key claims

- Failure swings happen when the market makes a swing low with a higher candle to the left and right of it [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- The failure swing is one of two conceptual ways to view the marketplace in terms of swing points, alongside the breaker swing point [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- The failure swing is when there is a resistance level, the market trades through it, comes back and retests it, but it can't make another pass through [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- Because traders cannot know with great conviction whether a breaker setup will materialize, while waiting for it to occur they could be anticipating the failure swing [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- The aim is to get breaker swing points, but if they do not occur, traders can still trade off the failure swing pattern [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- Breaker and failure swing are framed as the only two conditions in the marketplace [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
