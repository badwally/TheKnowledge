# Lectin Elimination Diet

## Summary

Food-elimination protocol — labeled "Modification #1" in Dr. Carla Kuon's UCSF Long COVID Group Medical Visit handout — used for patients with autoimmunity or gut disorders; rests on the molecular-mimicry hypothesis that food lectins entering systemic circulation alter the structure of self-tissue and increase the likelihood of autoimmune attack [[sources/pdf-5bef1fceb745]].

## Key claims

- Indicated specifically for autoimmunity and gut disorders [[sources/pdf-5bef1fceb745]].
- Required eliminations: gluten (100% strict — no wheat or barley products), soybean (tofu, edamame, tempeh, soy sauce), dairy, peanuts (a legume), lentils, kidney beans and jack beans (other beans must be pressure cooked; Eden canned beans are noted as acceptable), and nightshades — specifically tomato (skin and seeds) and white potato (sweet and purple potatoes are allowed) [[sources/pdf-5bef1fceb745]].
- Lectins are defined as proteins in foods that bind to carbohydrates; the problem is that they can enter systemic circulation and bind to selective tissue targets/organs [[sources/pdf-5bef1fceb745]].
- Through this binding mechanism, lectins alter the structure of self-tissue by the addition of the lectin, yielding a new structure that the immune system may not recognize as self — a process called molecular mimicry, said in the handout to be present in all cases of auto-immune disease [[sources/pdf-5bef1fceb745]].
- Different food lectins tend to have affinity for binding to particular tissue targets; the named foods are the most commonly problematic and many come from the nightshade and legume families [[sources/pdf-5bef1fceb745]].
- Reference text given on the handout: Brostoff & Challacomb, "Food Allergy and Intolerance," cited as an immunology textbook with a chart of lectin-tissue affinities [[sources/pdf-5bef1fceb745]].
