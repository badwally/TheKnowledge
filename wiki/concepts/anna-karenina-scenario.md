# Anna Karenina Scenario

## Summary

The Anna Karenina scenario, introduced by Bansal et al. (2021), is the possibility that all well-performing neural networks represent the world in the same way [[sources/pdf-minyoung-huh-2024-the-platonic-representation]].

## Key claims

- The name borrows from Tolstoy's 1877 novel; the broader "Anna Karenina principle" was popularized by Diamond (1998) to explain animal domestication [[sources/pdf-minyoung-huh-2024-the-platonic-representation]].
- The Platonic Representation Hypothesis refers to the specific case where (i) we are in an Anna Karenina scenario and (ii) the "happy representation" that is converged upon reflects a statistical model of underlying reality [[sources/pdf-minyoung-huh-2024-the-platonic-representation]].
- Huh et al. paraphrase the framing as: "all strong models are alike, each weak model is weak in its own way" — invoked to describe their empirical VTAB clustering result [[sources/pdf-minyoung-huh-2024-the-platonic-representation]].
