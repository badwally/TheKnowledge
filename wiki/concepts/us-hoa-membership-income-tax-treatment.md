# U.S. HOA Membership Income Tax Treatment

## Summary

Membership income — including assessments — is generally non-taxable for U.S. homeowners associations under federal tax law, in contrast with interest income (always taxable) and certain other income items (variably taxable) [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]]. The treatment can differ depending on the tax filing election: under Form 1120-H, membership income is generally non-taxable; under Form 1120, filing can potentially cause membership net income to be taxable [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].

## Key claims

- Membership income, including assessments, is generally non-taxable for U.S. HOAs [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- This non-taxable treatment applies generally under the Form 1120-H regime [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Filing under Form 1120 (regular corporation) can potentially cause membership net income to be taxable, in contrast with the Form 1120-H treatment [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Other income items that may or may not be taxable, depending on the tax form filed, include laundry income, rental income for the clubhouse or other common areas, and sales of goods or services to non-members [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Interest income, in contrast, is ALWAYS taxable regardless of which form is filed [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
