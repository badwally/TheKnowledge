# Data Supply Chains for AI

## Summary

Data supply chains are the institutional, legal, and technical infrastructure that enables AI developers to access large, high-quality datasets for model training under privacy and governance controls; Sen (2026) frames data supply chains as the "missing pillar" of Canadian federal AI strategy [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].

## Key claims

- Sen (2026) characterizes data supply chains as a "foundational pillar of AI innovation" alongside research capacity, talent, and computing infrastructure [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
- A secure AI/ML data supply chain should be: (1) statistically representative and follow well-defined collection standards; (2) stored securely; and (3) available to non-government organizations under strict privacy protocols and, when appropriate, on the basis of privacy-enhancing technologies [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
- Canada's current legal and institutional frameworks lack clear, standardized rules governing data sharing for AI development, limiting firms' ability to access critical datasets [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
- Coordinated policies enabling responsible access to large, high-quality datasets are framed as a competitive advantage for Canadian startups and scaleups, which most acutely need training data [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
- Sen's proposed policy framework rests on three principles: expanded-but-guarded data access; integration of privacy-enhancing technologies; and transparent governance grounded in cost-benefit analysis [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
- Specific recommendations include carefully regulated private-sector access to confidential Statistics Canada datasets on a pilot basis; accelerated national production of synthetic datasets; social-benefit tests embedded in privacy legislation; and regulated AI sandboxes aligned with regional strengths [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
- Sen (2026) argues that without such frameworks Canada risks falling behind initiatives such as the US Genesis Mission (an integrated AI platform built on federal scientific datasets) and China's AI governance action plan [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
- Council of Canadian Academies (2015, 2023), cited by Sen, indicates that overly cautious data-custodian access restrictions can reduce the incentive to conduct large-dataset research and thus reduce innovation [[sources/web-2026-03-26-f82]].
