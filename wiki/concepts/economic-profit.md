# Economic Profit

## Summary

Measure of corporate value creation defined as (return on invested capital minus the weighted-average cost of capital) times invested capital — a single number that captures both the spread between ROIC and WACC and the size of the opportunity. Mauboussin and Callahan (2024) use it as the fundamental yardstick for testing whether the current U.S. market-cap concentration is supported by the economics of the leading companies [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].

## Key claims

- Defined as Economic Profit = (ROIC – WACC) × Invested Capital; e.g., a company with ROIC of 13%, WACC of 8%, and invested capital of $1,000 has economic profit of $50 [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Measures both value creation (the difference between ROIC and WACC) and the size of the opportunity (invested capital) [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- The spread between ROIC and WACC provides a sense of the appropriate multiple of enterprise value to invested capital; that multiple times invested capital gives an estimate of enterprise value, from which equity market capitalization can be calculated — establishing a reasonably direct link between economic profit and equity market capitalization [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- ROIC and invested-capital calculations in Mauboussin and Callahan's analysis include adjustments for intangible investments [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- In 2023, aggregate economic profit for U.S. public companies in their universe was $481 billion; the top 10 by market cap contributed $331 billion of that total — i.e., 27% of market cap was earning 69% of economic profit [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Over the decade ending 2023, the top 10 averaged 19% of market cap and 47% of economic profit; from 1990 to 2023, the figures are 17% of market cap and 46% of economic profit — establishing a long-standing pattern of leading firms earning a disproportionate share of the universe's economic profit [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- The implication used by Mauboussin and Callahan: it would be hard to argue the market capitalizations of the largest companies are wildly mispriced when those companies are also earning the lion's share of the economic profit [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
