# Decentralized Cloud Marketplace

## Summary

Market structure proposed by the 2018 Akash Network position paper in which clients and datacenter providers exchange compute resources without a centralized intermediary, using a public blockchain-anchored orderbook and matching algorithm priced in a native token, with on-chain provider fulfillment history serving as the trust substitute for brand-equity-based trust in incumbent cloud providers [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].

## Key claims

- Akash is "first and foremost, a platform that allows clients to procure resources from providers," enabled by a blockchain-powered distributed exchange where clients post desired resources and providers bid [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- The marketplace is implemented through a decentralized exchange consisting of a public orderbook and a matching algorithm [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- The Akash blockchain provides a layer of trust in a decentralized and trustless environment — clients today trust large infrastructure providers based primarily on brand equity, but Akash should not require that same leap of faith [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- "Data on the chain is an immutable and public record of all transactions, including each Provider's fulfillment history" [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- Akash is also "politically decentralized" — "No single entity controls the network and no intermediary facilitates transactions. Therefore no entity is incentivized to control or to extract marginal revenue from the network" [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- A large company such as Coca-Cola can participate as a Provider, providing compute to another large company or to an individual developer, with all three parties on equal footing [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- The foundational design objective is to maintain a low barrier to entry for providers while ensuring that clients can trust the resources offered to them [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
