# Cumulative Ruin Principle

## Summary

Taleb's Principle 1 in "Bitcoin, Currencies, and Fragility": if any non-dividend-yielding asset has the tiniest probability of hitting an absorbing barrier — causing its value to become 0 — then its present value must be 0 [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].

## Key claims

- Stated formally as Principle 1: "If any non-dividend yielding asset has the tiniest probability of hitting an absorbing barrier (causing its value to become 0), then its present value must be 0" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Derived from a traditional rational-bubble model: given a discount rate r and a probability of absorption π over a period, the asset must grow at a rate r + π forever, "without remission, and with total certainty," to escape the barrier — and even then, "should it grow then stabilize, it still would be prone to extinction" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- The absorbing barrier need not be 0 itself for the price to spiral to 0 upon hitting it — Taleb's analogy is a heart rate dropping below ten beats per minute, which then proceeds to 0 (death) — and the barrier need not be caused by a drop in price, nor be endogenous [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Traditional rational-bubble models rule out continuous growth at an exponential rate faster than r + π, because the security or asset would then represent the entire economy; Taleb argues bitcoin is distinguished from other assets because of "its fragility as a mere book entry on a virtual ledger that requires constant refreshing ad infinitum" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Implication for bitcoin: "if we expect that at any point in the future the value will be zero when miners are extinct, the technology becomes obsolete, or future generations get into other such 'assets' and bitcoin loses its appeal for them, then the value must be zero now" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Taleb summarizes the corollary as "Comment 1: Why BTC is worth exactly 0," contrasting bitcoin's perpetual maintenance requirement with gold and other precious metals that "are largely maintenance free, do not degrade over an historical horizon, and do not require maintenance to refresh their physical properties over time" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
