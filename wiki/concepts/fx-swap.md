# FX Swap

## Summary

A foreign-exchange transaction in which two parties simultaneously agree to exchange one currency for another at one value date and to re-exchange the same two currencies at a second, later value date — typically a spot leg paired with a forward leg, priced via swap points that reflect the interest-rate differential between the two currencies under cost-of-carry [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].

## Key claims

- A swap is an exchange and re-exchange of two currencies — for example, one party gives the other dollars for Euros for immediate delivery and simultaneously agrees to re-exchange Euros for dollars at a specified rate at some time in the future [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- Swap pricing requires identifying the two value dates (typically spot and a later forward) and computing swap points relative to spot [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- Bid-offer spreads on swaps are quoted in points, with rules of thumb that govern whether the customer pays or earns the points based on which side of the market is hit [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- The "rationale behind the chart" of swap-point quoting connects swap points back to the interest-rate differential between the two currencies via cost-of-carry [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- The Lehman manual organizes swap teaching around five learning points: what a swap is, how value dates work, how bid-offer spreads are constructed, how swap points are calculated, and the underlying rationale tying it all together [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
