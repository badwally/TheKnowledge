# Data Cooperative

## Summary

A data cooperative is a member-owned organisation that holds and administers personal data on behalf of its members, who govern the entity democratically and pool their data to leverage economies of scale and consolidate negotiating power vis-à-vis data collectors [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].

## Key claims

- Data cooperatives are one of four commonly proposed collective data intermediaries — alongside data trusts, decentralised autonomous organisations, and data unions — that build on individualist data rights by embracing data as a form of collective value and redistributing benefits to members [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Cooperatives align the social and economic value of aggregated data with the normative interests of the individuals described in it, complementing rather than replacing individual data rights [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Demand for cooperative data governance spans many jurisdictions and a wide range of otherwise divergent ideological positions [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Some data cooperatives act primarily as brokers — negotiating contractual limits on how members' data can be used without ever controlling it — while others seek to build or seize the underlying infrastructures of data collection and storage [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
