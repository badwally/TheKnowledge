# Liquidity-based bias

## Summary

Liquidity-based bias is the ICT framing in which higher-timeframe (monthly/weekly/daily) direction sets the dominant bias while lower timeframes (4-hour and below) retrace into the opposing extreme and re-engage entries off orderblocks or fair value gaps before targeting the next pool of liquidity [[sources/pdf-08bcb2d549ba]].

## Key claims

- When the monthly, weekly, and daily charts are bearish, the 4-hour chart or less will be retracing higher; the trader anticipates the market entering a premium and seeking buy-side liquidity, returning to a bearish orderblock (-OB) or FVG, providing a low-resistance liquidity run that targets a recent low [[sources/pdf-08bcb2d549ba]].
- When the monthly, weekly, and daily charts are bullish, the 4-hour chart or less will be retracing lower; the trader anticipates the market entering a discount and seeking sell-side liquidity, returning to a bullish orderblock (+OB) or FVG, providing a low-resistance liquidity run that targets a recent high [[sources/pdf-08bcb2d549ba]].
- The framework couples HTF directional bias with LTF entry mechanics — the entry zone is always a return to an orderblock or fair value gap inside the appropriate premium or discount [[sources/pdf-08bcb2d549ba]].
