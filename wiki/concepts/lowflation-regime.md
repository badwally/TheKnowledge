# Lowflation Regime

## Summary

The "lowflation regime" is Zoltan Pozsar's August 24, 2022 framing of the post-1989 era of structurally low inflation in the developed world, which he argues rested on three pillars (cheap immigrant labor, cheap Chinese goods, cheap Russian gas) and on Pax Americana underwriting globalization; with Chimerica and Eurussia both broken, Pozsar argues the regime must end [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].

## Key claims

- The three pillars: "the low inflation world had three pillars: cheap immigrant labor keeping nominal wage growth 'stagnant' in the U.S., cheap Chinese goods raising real wages amid stagnant nominal wages, and cheap Russian natural gas fueling German industry and Europe more broadly" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Origin time-stamp: economists "associate [the Fall of the Berlin Wall on November 9th, 1989] with the birth of the 'lowflation' regime," the same event Pozsar associates with the birth of globalization [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Pozsar argues the lowflation regime "licensed" U.S. quantitative easing: "the U.S. got very rich by doing QE. But the license for QE came from the 'lowflation' regime enabled by cheap exports coming from Russia and China" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- End condition: "if Chimerica and Eurussia are over as unions, the lowflation regime will have to end, period" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Pozsar argues the U.S. — "the top of the global economic food chain" — does not want the lowflation regime to end, but the geopolitical decoupling has removed the supply-side conditions that made it possible [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Pozsar pairs the end of the lowflation regime with the start of "The Great Reflation," the inflationary regime he argues is downstream of de-globalization [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
