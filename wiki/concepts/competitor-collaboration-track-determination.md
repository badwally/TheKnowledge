# Criminal vs Civil Track Determination for Competitor Collaborations

## Summary

Under the Canadian Competition Act, the Bureau's first analytical step in reviewing a competitor collaboration is to route the arrangement to the appropriate provision of the Act before assessing it on the merits [[sources/web-2024-06-27-57a]] [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]]. Routing precedes evaluation: the Bureau decides between section 45 (criminal conspiracy), section 90.1 (civil agreements), and other Act provisions, and may investigate under multiple provisions until enough facts are uncovered to settle the track [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].

## Key claims

- Mergers — proposed or completed acquisitions of control or significant interest in a business through share/asset purchase, amalgamation, or combination are assessed under the section 92+ merger provisions, not section 90.1 or 45 [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Where an arrangement bundles a merger with separate competitor-collaboration agreements, the Bureau may invoke its sections 11 and 15 information-gathering powers to determine which provisions of the Act apply [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Agreements between federal financial institutions described in subsection 49(1) are routed to section 49 and not section 45; if exempted by subsection 49(2), they are not assessable under section 45 but may still be reviewable under section 90.1 if likely to substantially lessen competition [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Purely vertical agreements between suppliers and customers are generally assessed under the reviewable-matters provisions in sections 75 through 84, not section 45 — including dual-distribution arrangements where the supplier competes with the customer in the supplied product [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Bid-rigging agreements between competitors are generally assessed under section 47 or section 90.1, not section 45; bids made known to the requester at or before submission/withdrawal sit outside section 45 entirely, though residual section 90.1 exposure remains for the consortium itself [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Where an agreement bundles bid-rigging with additional restraints (e.g., market allocation) or is part of a broader conspiracy, sections 45 and 47 may apply jointly [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Agreements between jointly dominant competitors may be examined under section 79 (abuse of dominance) in addition to or instead of section 90.1, where the agreement has or facilitates exclusionary, predatory, or disciplinary conduct having or likely to have the effect of substantially lessening or preventing competition [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- After routing to either section 45 or 90.1, the Bureau then evaluates whether the agreement is a naked restraint (criminal track) or a collaboration integrated with a legitimate joint venture or strategic alliance (civil track) [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
