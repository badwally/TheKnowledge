# Core Stability for Cyclists

## Summary

Core stability work for cyclists — plank, bird dog, renegade row, and lying hip abduction — trains the abdominals, back, and hip stabilizers to hold a stable platform on the bike while the legs deliver power [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].

## Key claims

- **Plank** targets abdominals and back; the practice goal is core stability — holding a straight line from shoulders to heels with elbows under shoulders, not letting hips sag or back arch [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- **Bird dog** trains the ability to stabilize the core while mobilizing the extremities — opposite arm and leg extended from a hands-and-knees position with the pelvis held stable, reaching through the heel to engage hamstrings and glutes [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- **Renegade row** combines a push-up plank with a row to strengthen abs, biceps, triceps, back, and chest, with stability as the primary trained quality — riders are cued to keep the core engaged and avoid swinging from one side to the other [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- **Lying hip abduction** trains the glutes, hamstrings, TFL, and hip abductors for stability and injury prevention; raising the top leg above 45° starts recruiting the whole hip and detracts from the target muscles [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Standard progressions: longer plank holds and alternating leg raises during the plank, adding a crunch to the bird dog, adding a push-up to the renegade row, and ankle-weight or banded variants for hip abduction [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
