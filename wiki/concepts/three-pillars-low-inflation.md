# Three Pillars of the Low Inflation World

## Summary

Zoltan Pozsar's framing of the structural conditions that produced decades of low inflation in the West: cheap U.S. immigrant labor that kept service-sector wages stagnant, cheap Chinese goods that raised real living standards amid those stagnant wages, and cheap Russian gas that powered German industry and the EU more broadly — three pillars whose collapse, in Pozsar's view, drives today's inflation more than monetary stimulus does [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]] [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].

## Key claims

- The low-inflation world stood on three pillars: cheap immigrant labor keeping service-sector wages stagnant in the U.S., cheap goods from China raising living standards amid stagnant wages, and cheap Russian gas powering German industry and the EU [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- The asset-rich, benefiting from decades of QE, bought high-end stuff from Europe produced using cheap Russian gas, while lower-income U.S. households bought all the cheap stuff coming from China — a configuration that worked for decades "until nativism, protectionism, and geopolitics destabilized the low inflation world" [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Pillar 1 (immigrant labor) was undermined by Trump-era immigration policy, which Pozsar credits with costing the U.S. about two million jobs and driving labor shortages and wage pressures, exacerbated further by Covid-driven early retirements [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Pillar 2 (Chinese goods) was undermined by a U.S. shift from tariffs on cheap goods to a broader technology war (e.g., banning ASML from selling state-of-the-art lithography to China), and by Xi's zero-Covid policy frustrating supply chains [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Pillar 3 (Russian gas) was undermined by U.S. sanctions on Nord Stream 2 in November 2021 and then by the Ukraine war that began February 24, 2022, after which the U.S. weaponized the dollar and Russia weaponized commodities [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- In Pozsar's August 24, 2022 dispatch the same three-pillar framing is restated as "cheap immigrant labor keeping nominal wage growth 'stagnant' in the U.S., cheap Chinese goods raising real wages amid stagnant nominal wages, and cheap Russian natural gas fueling German industry and Europe more broadly" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
