# Infrastructure Asset Management (IAM)

## Summary

Infrastructure Asset Management (IAM) is the discipline of planning, operating, and rehabilitating long-lived civil infrastructure (water, wastewater, transport, bridges) to deliver required levels of service at acceptable risk and cost over its full life cycle. Modern IAM treats infrastructure as a system rather than a collection of components, is operationalised through dedicated planning software and technical guidance documents, and relies on flexible deterioration models — increasingly built on stochastic processes such as the gamma process — to forecast asset condition under usage and environmental loads.

## Key claims

- IAM in urban water services must address high levels of deferred maintenance and rehabilitation alongside overwhelming investment needs, requiring wise spending and innovative, efficient planning rather than ad-hoc reactive maintenance [[sources/web-2012-01-01-57d]].
- Modern IAM methodology treats performance (level-of-service), risk, and financial effort as joint objectives whose best compromise must be sought through system-centric rather than component-centric methods [[sources/web-2012-01-01-57d]].
- The IAM methodology underlying AWARE-P assesses water systems over multiple planning horizons and is documented in Portuguese national technical guides — Technical Guide n.16 for water services (Alegre & Covas, 2010) and Technical Guide n.17 for wastewater and stormwater services (Almeida & Cardoso, 2010), both co-published by ERSAR, LNEC, and IST [[sources/web-2012-01-01-57d]].
- EU-funded predecessor projects CARE-W and CARE-S developed analytical foundations for rehabilitation planning of water and sewer/stormwater networks respectively, and their methodologies were integrated into the AWARE-P platform [[sources/web-2012-01-01-57d]].
- IAM extends beyond water services to transport infrastructure such as bridges, where asset management systems require flexible deterioration models that can handle various degradation patterns in a unified way [[sources/arxiv-2508.13359]].
- Stochastic-process deterioration models — in particular the gamma process and its bounded transformed (BTGP) and bounded nonstationary (BNGP) variants — are a central methodological component of modern IAM, with model selection and parameterisation driven by both deterioration-modelling fidelity and asset-management decision-making considerations [[sources/arxiv-2508.13359]].
- Real-world historical bridge condition data is one of the empirical settings used to benchmark competing IAM deterioration models, evidencing the role of bridge condition records as a primary data source for transport-infrastructure asset management research [[sources/arxiv-2508.13359]].
