# Institutional Reference Points

## Summary

In ICT-style price-action analysis, the institutional reference points are the five chart locations that mark where institutional order flow is hidden and where stop runs / failure swings are most likely to develop: order blocks, breakers, mitigation blocks, liquidity voids, and fair value gaps; locating price relative to these — together with key support and resistance on high time frames — is described as essential context for trade selection [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].

## Key claims

- Knowing the institutional reference points (order blocks, breakers, mitigation blocks, liquidity voids, and fair value gaps) helps anticipate stop-run and failure-swing patterns unfolding [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- Once the pattern breaks down, it becomes a trigger to sell or buy [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- Stop runs can be anticipated when price is hovering just below a key institutional reference point [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- Institutional order flow on higher time frames helps in looking at the effects of these patterns [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- It is essential to understand where the orders are relative to institutional order flow reference points, key support, and resistance on high time frames [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- Institutional trading creates two types of swing points — stop runs and failure swings — that play out around these reference points [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
