# Blockchain-Based Competitor Data Sharing

## Summary

Blockchain is used as infrastructure for sharing data between competitors when trust between parties is low, providing tamper-proof distributed records that support cross-firm collaboration without a centralized intermediary [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].

## Key claims

- Blockchain is fundamentally a way to record, process, and authenticate data without centralization or manual processing, using cryptographic methods that make individual manipulation difficult [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- The technology is particularly useful when trust between parties is low, including between anonymous internet counterparties and between two competitors [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- Potential cross-competitor applications span banking, legal services, real estate, stock trading, health data, food production, supply chain, and autonomous vehicles [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- In autonomous vehicle development, blockchain can catalogue, label, and immutably brand each data point with the company that generated it, enabling trading of test data between competitors in an open market [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- Supply chain use cases include product source tracking (an indisputable record of shipment status, storage conditions, and milestones) and automated transactions between unknown parties [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- IBM and Oracle are actively developing private blockchain solutions for firms managing complex supply chains [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- Real-world automotive consortia include MOBI (BMW, Ford, GM, Honda, Renault), Toyota's partnership with MIT, and IOTA's partnerships with Volkswagen and Jaguar Land Rover [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- Competitive effects depend on network design and regulatory oversight rather than on the technology itself — blockchain serves primarily as a data management tool [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
