# War-Economy Macro Investing

## Summary

Zoltan Pozsar's reframing of macro investing in his August 1, 2022 "War and Interest Rates" dispatch: post-Cold War macro had a "golden age" of trading central-bank reaction functions and the first three prices of money (par, interest, FX), but in a war economy the relevant model is John Maynard Keynes during the interwar period and Nathan Meyer Rothschild during the Napoleonic Wars — investors who applied political and geopolitical lenses rather than bottom-up Fed-watching to wager on interest rates [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].

## Key claims

- Macro investing had its "golden age in the post-Cold War era," and investors like George Soros, Stanley Druckenmiller, Paul Tudor Jones, and Louis Bacon "traded in a peaceful world, punctuated only by relatively small military conflicts" [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- The big conflicts those investors traded "were all 'nominal conflicts'" involving markets and central banks, and the first three of the four prices of money: par, interest, and foreign exchange [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Today's conflict — a complex economic war between empires — drives the fourth price of money: the price level and its derivative, inflation [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- John Maynard Keynes "is one investor who comes to mind that we know practiced macro investing during the interwar period," drawing on "The Economic Consequences of the Peace" [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Nathan Meyer Rothschild "loaded up on gilts when he learned before everyone else of Wellington's victory over Napoleon at the Battle of Waterloo" — applying political/geopolitical lenses to victory and peace [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Neither Keynes nor Rothschild approached their investments using a bottom-up, micro understanding of central banks' inflation targets and reaction functions — "that's a peacetime game" [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Pozsar's prescription: "think more about the risk of inflation staying higher for longer due to economic warfare, and less about inflation being driven by a messy re-opening process and stimulus" [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Recommends reading "less Friedman and Schwartz and much-much more Brzezinski and Mackinder," because the special relationship between China and Russia covers most of Eurasia, which has most of the stuff needed to meet inflation targets in the West [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
