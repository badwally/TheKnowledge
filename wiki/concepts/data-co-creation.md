# Co-creation of Data

## Summary

Co-creation of data is Wendy H. Wong's (2023) framing that data extracted from people are necessarily produced jointly by an actor (the human whose behaviour is observed) and an observer (the collector and its infrastructures), drawing attention to the asymmetries and the mutual interests held by data collectors and data subjects in the data they jointly produce [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].

## Key claims

- Wong (2023) observes that data extracted from people are necessarily co-created by an actor and an observer, highlighting both the asymmetries that characterise data production and the mutual interests held by collectors and subjects in the resulting data [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Digital data are both ideational and material: artefacts of conceptual norms about what is important to observe and how to measure it, requiring physical infrastructures such as servers and network cables to record and store observations (Raso & Sheffi, 2021) [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Co-created data give rise simultaneously to moral claims about autonomy and dignity ('I object to this') and economic claims about the distribution of value derived from data ('I deserve to be compensated for this') [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- For actions to become data, observers and infrastructures are required to transform them into information or actionable knowledge (Rowley, 2006), and collectors typically set the priorities about what to observe, how to observe it, and where to store the observations [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Collectors invest in incentivising data subjects to make themselves amenable to data collection — to want to share intimate details with sensor-enabled devices — which preserves economic and informational asymmetries between subjects and collectors (Lyon, 2018) [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- While rights-based protection like the GDPR recognises individuals as co-creators of data, the capacity to make collective claims remains mostly limited to restitution after harm (Casarosa, 2020), motivating intermediary mechanisms that can act ex ante [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
