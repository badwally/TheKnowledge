# Unanchoring of Inflation Expectations

## Summary

The argument — central to the July 13, 2022 Q3-2022 IACFM presentation — that survey-based measures of both near-term and long-term inflation expectations had become unanchored by mid-2022, with one-year-ahead expectations between 4% and 7% and longer-term expectations around 3%–4%; this matters because expectations shape firm price-setting and worker wage demands and because the Burns-era 1970s precedent shows that once expectations come unanchored, monetary policy loses inflation-fighting credibility [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].

## Key claims

- Survey-based measures of near-term inflation expectations had become unanchored as of mid-2022, anticipating approximately 4% to 7% inflation over the next year [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Median one-year-ahead inflation expectations cited: Atlanta Fed Business Inflation Expectations 3.6% (n ≈ 300 businesses of various sizes), University of Michigan Consumer Inflation Expectations 5.3% (n ≈ 600 households), NY Fed Survey of Consumer Expectations 6.8% (n ≈ 1,300 households) [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Although the Federal Reserve and market participants place greater importance on long-term inflation expectations, one-year-ahead inflation expectations likely play a pivotal role in shaping price-setting behavior by firms and increased wage demands of employees [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Survey-based measures of longer-term inflation expectations had also risen sharply over the trailing twelve months, projecting approximately 3% to 4% inflation per annum over the next five- to ten-year period [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Median long-term inflation expectations cited: Atlanta Fed Business Inflation Expectations next 5–10 years ~2.9%, Survey of Professional Forecasters 5-Year PCE Inflation 3.1% (n=34 forecasters), University of Michigan Consumer Inflation Expectations next 5–10 years 3.5%, NY Fed Survey of Consumer Expectations next 3 years 3.6% [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- The IACFM presentation invokes the Burns era as the historical analog: when monetary policy is insufficiently restrictive in the face of high inflation, inflationary expectations become unanchored and the Federal Reserve loses its inflation-fighting credibility [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- By contrast, under the Volcker-era tightening cycle of the early 1980s, inflation expectations stabilized as the Federal Reserve maintained a high Fed Funds rate even in the face of slowing real GDP growth [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
