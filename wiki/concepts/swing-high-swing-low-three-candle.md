# Three-Candle Swing High / Swing Low

## Summary

A precise structural definition used in ICT-style technical analysis: a swing high (SH) is a three-candle pattern in which the middle candle is the highest; a swing low (SL) is the mirror, with the middle candle the lowest [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]]. "Lesson 1: Price and Liquidity" treats this as the atomic structural unit on which buyside/sellside liquidity, equal highs/lows, and higher-timeframe market structure are subsequently built [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]]. Ali Khan's "The ICT Bible" (2023) presents the same definition under the heading "3 Bar Swing" [[sources/pdf-ali-khan-2023-the-ict-bible]].

## Key claims

- A swing high is formed when three candles create a point with the middle candle being the highest [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].
- A swing low is formed when three candles create a point with the middle candle being the lowest [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].
- The lesson uses this three-candle definition as the prerequisite for locating buyside liquidity (above the high of the middle candle of an SH) and sellside liquidity (below the low of the middle candle of an SL) [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].
- The three-candle structure exists on every timeframe; the lesson directs traders to scan from a higher timeframe down through hourly, 15-minute, 5-minute, and 3-to-1-minute charts to find swings of varying significance [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].
- Per Ali Khan, a swing high is the highest high (middle candle) with a lower high to the left and a lower high to the right [[sources/pdf-ali-khan-2023-the-ict-bible]].
- Per Ali Khan, a swing low is the lowest low (middle candle) with a higher low to the left and a higher low to the right [[sources/pdf-ali-khan-2023-the-ict-bible]].
