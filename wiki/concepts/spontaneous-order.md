# Spontaneous Order

## Summary

The principle that the economy is an "unplanned order" — what emerges when people go about their own business, acting and interacting as they see fit — without any central plan, planner, or goal; Per Bylund uses Bastiat's "How does Paris get fed?" to illustrate that a complex system of food production and distribution arises from decentralized individual decisions rather than central coordination [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].

## Key claims

- "The economy is an unplanned order. It is what emerges when people go about their own business, when we act and interact as we see fit" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- "The economy is a decentralized and distributed system where all people — farmers and city folk alike — make their own plans and decisions. They do not simply carry out orders from some central command" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- "The economy lacks both plan and planner. It doesn't even have a goal. It just is" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- Bastiat's "How does Paris get fed?" illustrates the point: "there is no central plan for what types and quantities of foods are to be offered to Parisians and when. There is no one telling farmers when and what to sow, which land to use for each crop, what tools to use or develop, or in what cities, towns, or market squares to sell their produce and at what prices. All of this just happens" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- Bylund argues there is "a natural order" and "structure to the apparent chaos" — the economy "has something of a life of its own: it has a nature," which means "we are not free to tamper with it at will and cannot make it work in ways that we might prefer but that are not in line with its nature" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- The economy has immutable "laws" that economics over the past three centuries has been about "identifying, learning, and understanding" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
