# Phosphocreatine System

## Summary

The phosphocreatine system is the fastest of the three [[concepts/atp|ATP]]-resynthesis pathways — it draws on muscle phosphocreatine stores to refill ATP almost instantly, but the supply runs out within seconds, so it dominates only the very start of an effort or a short sprint [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].

## Key claims

- Phosphocreatine is stored directly in the muscles and is broken down to release energy that reforms ATP molecules when ATP must be resynthesised very rapidly [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
- The system can only operate at a high rate for roughly 2–3 seconds before phosphocreatine stores are depleted [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
- It dominates explosive efforts such as Olympic lifting and the first few pedal strokes of a race start, while "plugging the gap" before the slower glycolytic and aerobic systems catch up [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
- Once depleted, phosphocreatine is resynthesised by the [[concepts/aerobic-energy-system|aerobic system]]; it typically takes around 30 seconds to replenish 50% of the depleted stores (Sahlin et al., 2014), and this rate depends on aerobic strength [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
- It is sometimes called the *anaerobic alactic system* because it does not require oxygen and does not produce [[concepts/lactate|lactate]] [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
