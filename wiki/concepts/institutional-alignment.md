# Institutional Alignment

## Summary

An alignment paradigm proposed by Evans, Bratton, and Agüera y Arcas (2026) as an explicit alternative to Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback: rather than dyadic parent-child correction, scalable AI ecosystems should be aligned through persistent institutional templates — roles and norms borrowed from courtrooms, markets, and bureaucracies — that constrain agent behavior regardless of which agent occupies which role [[sources/pdf-james-evans-2026-agentic-ai-and]].

## Key claims

- The dominant paradigm for AI alignment, Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), resembles a parent-child model of correction: it is fundamentally dyadic and unable to scale to billions of agents [[sources/pdf-james-evans-2026-agentic-ai-and]].
- The social-intelligence perspective suggests an alternative — institutional alignment — drawing on Ostrom's work on institutions for collective action [[sources/pdf-james-evans-2026-agentic-ai-and]].
- Just as human societies rely not on individual virtue but on persistent institutional templates — courtrooms, markets, bureaucracies — defined by roles and norms, scalable AI ecosystems will require digital equivalents [[sources/pdf-james-evans-2026-agentic-ai-and]].
- The identity of any agent matters less than its ability to fulfill a role protocol, just as a courtroom functions because "judge," "attorney," and "jury" are well-defined slots independent of who occupies them [[sources/pdf-james-evans-2026-agentic-ai-and]].
- The authors argue AI developers should put as much effort into building agent institutions as into building agents themselves [[sources/pdf-james-evans-2026-agentic-ai-and]].
- The framing draws on North's work on institutional change and economic performance, treating institutional templates as the durable substrate within which agents — human or artificial — operate [[sources/pdf-james-evans-2026-agentic-ai-and]].
