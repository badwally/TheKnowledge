# Ontario Condominium Common Expenses

## Summary

Ontario condominium common expenses (often called condo fees or common element expenses) are the contributions that each unit owner is responsible for paying toward the corporation's operating and reserve-fund obligations. Collection is backed by a statutory lien mechanism, and managers may impose chargebacks for unit-attributable costs incurred by the corporation [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].

## Key claims

- Each unit owner is responsible for contributing a portion of the common expenses; these contributions are often referred to as condo fees or common element expenses [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- The corporation may impose special assessments or levies to cover unexpected or extraordinary expenses that arise outside of the normal budgeting process [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Common-expense collection is backed by a statutory lien that the corporation may register against a delinquent owner's unit [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- The lien will include all outstanding and subsequent common expenses, interest owing in accordance with the corporation's bylaws, and all reasonable legal costs and expenses incurred by the corporation to collect the outstanding amount [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Before registering the lien, the corporation must give 10 calendar days' notice to the owner using the prescribed Notice of Lien form; this notice period is included in the expiry-of-lien period [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Chargebacks are a separate mechanism by which the corporation can charge an individual unit owner for costs the corporation incurred that are attributable to that unit [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- The Practice Guide includes Appendix C, "Chargeback Process: Investigation, Remediation and Compensation for Repairs and Maintenance," defining the procedural workflow for chargebacks [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
