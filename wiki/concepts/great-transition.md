# Great Transition

## Summary

Credit Suisse term for the regime transition foreseen in the 2022 Investment Outlook away from the post-2010 globalization-era macro regime; in the 2023 Investment Outlook CS judges the Great Transition to have played out to a much greater extent than originally envisioned, ushering in a 'new reality' defined by the return of geopolitics and a multipolar world [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].

## Key claims

- 'The Great Transition that we foresaw for 2022 has played out to a much greater extent than we originally envisioned, resulting in a new reality' [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- Geopolitics has made a comeback as a key driver of the global economy under the Great Transition [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- The West-Russia confrontation over Ukraine triggered an energy crisis and soaring food prices [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- International commerce, far from normalizing, has reorganized according to political alliances, marking the dawn of a multipolar world [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- The new economic reality has more elevated inflation and a monetary policy regime prioritizing inflation stability over growth [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- Rate consequence: interest rates are at their highest in years while economic growth is slowing — a direct implication of the regime change [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
