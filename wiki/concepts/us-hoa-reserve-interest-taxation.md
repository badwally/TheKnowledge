# U.S. HOA Reserve Interest Taxation

## Summary

U.S. homeowners associations, despite being organized as non-profit corporations, generally owe federal income tax on their interest earnings — including ALL bank and investment interest and interest earned on reserve accounts. The notion that non-profit status exempts an HOA from income tax is a common misconception among board members, especially first-time volunteers. Reserve interest income is taxable regardless of whether the association files Form 1120-H or Form 1120, and regardless of whether the underlying reserve project falls under the IRS definition of a "capital" project [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].

## Key claims

- U.S. associations are typically non-profit corporations [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Non-profit corporate status does NOT exempt a U.S. association from federal income tax — this is a common misconception among board members [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- At minimum, a U.S. association generally needs to pay federal income tax on its interest earnings [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Taxable interest includes ALL bank and investment interest, including interest earned on reserve accounts [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Reserve interest income is taxable whether the association files Form 1120 or Form 1120-H [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- The only exceptions to interest taxability arise when the association has very minimal interest income or is an exempt organization, which is less than 1% of associations [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Other income items that may or may not be taxable include laundry income, rental income for the clubhouse or other common areas, and sales of goods or services to non-members [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Generally membership income, including assessments, is non-taxable [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
