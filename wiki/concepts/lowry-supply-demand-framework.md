# Lowry Supply/Demand Framework

## Summary

A four-component daily decomposition of NYSE trading activity — Upside Volume, Downside Volume, Points Gained, and Points Lost — maintained continuously by Lowry's Reports since 1938 and forming the empirical foundation for the Lowry Analysis; the framework separates market activity into its basic supply (sellers) and demand (buyers) forces and enables intensity-based indicators like the 90% Days methodology [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].

## Key claims

- The four daily totals are: Upside (buyers) Volume, Downside (sellers) Volume, Points Gained (full and fractional dollars of price change for all NYSE-listed stocks that advanced), and Points Lost (full and fractional dollars of price change for all NYSE-listed stocks that declined) [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- These four totals represent the basic components of Demand and Supply and have been an integral part of the Lowry Analysis since 1938 [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- An industrious statistician can compile these totals independently from the NYSE stock tables published in each day's Wall Street Journal [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- The framework's key analytical insight is that panic selling must be measured in terms of intensity — the ratio of selling pressure to total activity — rather than just activity levels such as unusually active volume, massive declining stocks, or large numbers of new lows [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- Desmond's 2002 paper reviewed the complete daily record for every NYSE-listed stock over 69 years (1933–2002) using this decomposition [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- The framework enables a combined Price-and-Volume intensity oscillator (presented in the paper's charts as a single indicator combining the upside/downside percentages from both the volume and points dimensions) that visually highlights 90% extremes in either direction [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
- The Lowry Daily Market Trend Analysis Report publishes these four totals alongside derived percentages — the April 4, 2001 report is reproduced in the paper showing how April 3, 2001 qualified as a 90% Downside Day with Downside Volume at 90.8% of total and Points Lost at 90.7% of total [[sources/pdf-bd-2002-microsoft-word-dow]].
