# Data Intermediary

## Summary

A data intermediary is an organisation that sits between data producers (e.g. individuals, firms) and data users (queriers, researchers, commercial buyers), brokering access, consent, and trade in the data on the producers' behalf [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]]. Data cooperatives are one specific type of data intermediary defined by cooperative legal form and governance [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].

## Key claims

- The European Commission and collaborators (2023) identify six distinct types of data intermediary, of which the data cooperative is one [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].
- The EU Data Governance Act (DGA) explicitly regulates data intermediaries — including limits on reselling data for profit — to increase trust in voluntary data sharing [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].
- Some entities that describe themselves as data cooperatives are more accurately classed as other kinds of data intermediary because they are not constituted under cooperative legal regimes [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].
- Data intermediaries are characterised by their role mediating among personal-data storers, queriers, and the institution itself; their viability depends on resolving issues of sharing management, consent, security, and regulatory compliance [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].
