# QE for Stocks

## Summary

The policy implication of the inelastic markets hypothesis that government purchases of equities have large effects on stock prices — by approximately the multiplier of 5 — making them potentially useful as a "quantitative easing" policy targeted at stocks rather than long-term bonds [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].

## Key claims

- If the government buys stocks, stock prices go up by approximately the multiplier of 5 — Gabaix and Koijen note this may be useful as a policy tool, "a 'quantitative easing' policy for stocks rather than long-term bonds" [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- The framework can also be used to analyze previous policy experiments — in Hong Kong, Japan, and China — and to give a quantitative framework to complement the previous qualitative discussions of policy proposals of this kind, citing Tobin (1998), Farmer (2010), and Brunnermeier et al. (2020) [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- This implication is interesting only because equity markets are inelastic; in traditional models the question is irrelevant or uninteresting [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
