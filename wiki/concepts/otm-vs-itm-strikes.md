# Out-of-the-money vs in-the-money strike selection

## Summary

The binary classification of option strike prices relative to the current spot price of the underlying — OTM (out of the money) and ITM (in the money) — and the cost-volatility tradeoff between them. Nikonomics teaches a default beginner heuristic: "only trade the 5 closest OTM strike prices" because they are the contracts most other participants are trading and therefore the safest [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].

## Key claims

- There are two types of strike prices: OTM (out of the money) and ITM (in the money) [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- The further out the strike is from the current stock price, the cheaper the contract — but also the more volatile [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- A general rule of thumb for beginners: "only trade the 5 closest OTM strike prices if you have the money for it, these are the contracts most people are trading, so they are the safest" [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- The strike-price decision is positioned as the fourth step (after choosing a stock, expiration, and option type) in Nikonomics's beginner trade-execution loop on Webull [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- The choice interacts with expiration selection: shorter-dated and further-OTM contracts together compound the cheap-but-volatile profile, which is why Nikonomics couples the OTM heuristic with a separate "AT LEAST 5 day expirations" rule [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
