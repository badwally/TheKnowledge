# Different shades of recession

## Summary

ING's framing of the 2023 global growth outlook as not a single recession but several differently-textured downturns — a "textbook-style" recession in the US, a recession in China that "feels but doesn't read like a recession," and a mild eurozone recession followed by very subdued growth with double-dip risk [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].

## Key claims

- The US is expected to have a "rather textbook-style recession" with the central bank hiking rates until the real estate and labour markets start to weaken, inflation comes down, and the Fed can actually cut policy rates again [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- China is expected to have a recession that "feels but doesn't read like a recession," with Covid restrictions, a deflating real estate market, and weakening global demand bringing economic activity to almost unprecedented low levels [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- The eurozone is expected to see "an end to the typical cycle" — a mild recession followed by only very subdued growth, with risk of a "double dip" as the region has to shoulder structural challenges and transitions [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- The eurozone transitions are framed as first weighing on growth before, if successfully mastered, increasing the bloc's potential and adding to growth again [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- The framework is presented as a base case — median forecasts backed by 2022's events and assumptions — rather than an attempt to identify new black, grey, or pink swans [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- ING accompanies the base case with both upside and downside scenarios for energy prices, US, eurozone, UK, and Chinese economies as part of its tradition of stress-testing assumptions [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
