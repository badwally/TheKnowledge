# External to Internal Range Liquidity

## Summary

The "external to internal range liquidity" framework, in daytradingrauf's DOL system, is a two-stage targeting routine: identify external liquidity at swing highs and lows in price action, wait for a stop hunt into those swings, then target internal liquidity at fair value gaps (FVGs) or other imbalances such as volume imbalances or gaps; daytradingrauf calls it his favorite way to identify draws on liquidity because it is "the most forgiving concept" — even when wrong, the setup can still be profitable [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].

## Key claims

- The framework is described as the author's favorite way to identify draws on liquidity, and "the most forgiving concept; when you are wrong you can be profitable" [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
- For external range liquidity, the rule is kept simple: "the best way to identify external liquidity is by looking for swing highs and lows in price action and wait for a stop hunt into them to target internal range liquidity" — illustrated on a DXY Monthly Chart [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
- For internal range liquidity, the targeting set is fair value gaps (FVGs) or any kind of imbalance such as V.I (volume imbalance) or gaps — taken as targets after the external stop hunt has occurred [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
- The framework is explicitly bidirectional, illustrated as both "Internal liquidity to external" and "External sell side liquidity to buyside" in the same document [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
- The framework sits inside the broader DOL system as the third identification layer, after "what is liquidity" and "market structure liquidity" [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
