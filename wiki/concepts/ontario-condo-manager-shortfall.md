# Ontario Condominium Manager Shortfall

## Summary

The Ontario Condominium Manager Shortfall is the structural gap, articulated by ACMO in May 2023, between the number of CMRAO-licensed general managers currently available (2,543) and the number required (an estimated 3,333) to manage Ontario's existing condominium inventory — a gap intensified by ongoing condo construction, Baby Boomer retirements, and education-pipeline delays [[sources/web-2023-05-31-64e]].

## Key claims

- ACMO warned in a May 29, 2023 statement that the severe shortfall of licensed condo managers will intensify as Ontario plans to build 1.5 million homes in 10 years, including a large number of new condos [[sources/web-2023-05-31-64e]].
- Urbanation reported that 125,000 new condo units are planned for completion in Toronto between 2023 and 2028, with a fifth on target for 2023 alone [[sources/web-2023-05-31-64e]].
- The CMRAO reported 2,543 general license holders managing more than 12,400 condo corporations representing 900,000-plus units throughout Ontario, equating to 5.3 condo corporations per general licensee [[sources/web-2023-05-31-64e]].
- ACMO estimates that 3,333 general license holders are required to manage the current inventory of units in Ontario, implying a gap of roughly 790 licensees against the present stock alone — before accounting for new builds or retirements [[sources/web-2023-05-31-64e]].
- An ACMO membership survey found 57% of responding managers were aged 55 or higher, 13% were aged 65 and nearing retirement, and 5% were under 34, signalling a retirement wave concentrated in the next decade [[sources/web-2023-05-31-64e]].
- The 1,638 limited license holders only partially relieve the shortfall: they require no management experience or training, must be supervised by a general licensed manager, and not all will pursue the general licence [[sources/web-2023-05-31-64e]].
- Compensation dynamics worsen the shortfall: condo corporations pressured by owners to keep fees low prioritize cost-cutting over quality management, forcing firms to limit manager pay or increase workloads, which in turn pushes managers to switch employers for better pay and forces firms to raise compensation to retain or attract talent [[sources/web-2023-05-31-64e]].
- ACMO recommends government recruitment and education programs (including the Ontario Bridge Training Program), low licensing fees, public awareness campaigns coordinated with the Canadian Condominium Institute (CCI) and the Community Associations Institute (CAI), and competitive salaries set jointly by corporations and management firms [[sources/web-2023-05-31-64e]].
