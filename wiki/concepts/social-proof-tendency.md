# Social-Proof Tendency

## Summary

Munger's fifteenth psychological tendency: the automatic tendency to think and act as one sees others around him thinking and acting — a followership mechanism that generally works well (e.g., following a crowd to a stadium) but produces severe errors in investing and business when triggered by puzzlement or stress, causing herding behavior, corporate mimicry, and bystander inaction [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].

## Key claims

- Social-Proof Tendency is most easily triggered "in the presence of puzzlement or stress, and particularly when both exist" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Corporate herding: "If one oil company foolishly buys a mine, other oil companies often quickly join in buying mines. So also if the purchased company makes fertilizer. Both of these oil company buying fads actually bloomed, with bad results" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Bystander inaction: "In social proof, it is not only action by others that misleads but also their inaction. In the presence of doubt, inaction by others becomes social proof that inaction is the right course" — as in the death of Kitty Genovese [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Corporate boards display "the near ultimate form of inaction" — failing to object to anything short of an axe murder until public embarrassment forces intervention; Munger's friend Joe Rosenfield described board culture: "They asked me if I wanted to become a director of Northwest Bell, and it was the last thing they ever asked me" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Disreputable sales organizations and destructive cults exploit the tendency by manipulating targets into situations combining isolation and stress — the isolation strengthens social proof from early converts and the stress augments susceptibility [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Bad behavior is made contagious: Munger's father nearly signed up for a phony hunting license after watching every other Nebraska hunter in the group do so; "Serpico Syndrome" names the state of a near-totally corrupt New York police division where social proof plus incentives drove corruption so thoroughly that the one honest officer (Frank Serpico) was nearly murdered [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Judith Rich Harris demonstrated that superrespect by young people for peers (rather than parents or adults) is substantially ordained by genes, making it wise for parents to focus on manipulating the quality of peers rather than exhorting their own children [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Munger's recommended lesson for self-improvement: "Learn how to ignore the examples from others when they are wrong, because few skills are more worth having" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
