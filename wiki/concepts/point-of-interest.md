# Point of Interest (POI)

## Summary

In Lesson 2: Market Structure, the Point of Interest (POI) is the area where the trader wants to go long or short based on the direction of displacement and the next liquidity target — operationally, the imbalance-and-order-block zone that price retraces into and then leaves with energy in the trend direction [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].

## Key claims

- Lesson 2 defines POI as "the area where you want to go long or short based on the direction of the displacement and which liquidity price is targeting next" [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The trader wants price to return to the imbalance, enter, and then move away from it with energy — that price action is the POI confirmation [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Displacement away from the POI in the trend direction confirms the trade idea — in a bearish setup, displacement down from the imbalance confirms the new trend down [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- In the bearish January-2022 example, the POI sits at the imbalance (the red box) plus the green up-closed candles inside the consolidation that act as the bearish order block; the protective stop goes at the most recent swing high, which happens to coincide with the order-block boundary [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- In the bullish reversal example, the POI is the imbalance plus the down-closed candles before the strong move up; price retraces into it and begins to move away — the cue to add long with the developing uptrend [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Lesson 2 frames the POI's stop logic in terms of order-block invalidation: "if the direction is not short, then the order blocks left behind will be taken out quickly with displacement up" — meaning a stop just past the OB is also a stop on the trade thesis [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The POI sits inside Lesson 2's broader checklist between "FIND your Order Blocks and Imbalances" and "Know your targets" [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
