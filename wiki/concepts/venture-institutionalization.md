# Venture Institutionalization

## Summary

The structural evolution of venture capital from a cottage industry into a sophisticated, PE-like institutional asset class; per SVB's H2 2025 "State of the Markets," VC funds now operate coast to coast, draw on global capital pools, utilize new fund structures to tap different LP pools, blur the lines between venture, PE, and private debt, and are increasingly dominated by megafund platforms — a transformation that Ian Sigalow characterizes as "somewhere in the first third of the evolution" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].

## Key claims

- SVB frames the evolution: "Venture has evolved since we started our careers. It has gone from a mere cottage industry to a pillar of private markets and technological innovation" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- In the 1980s and 1990s, a handful of venture funds — small by today's standards — located in Boston and on Sand Hill Road were the epicenter of venture; today VC funds operate coast to coast and draw on global pools of capital [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- Modern funds are "more sophisticated, utilize new structures to tap different pools of LP capital, and often blur the lines between venture, private equity (PE) and even private debt" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- Megafund platforms increasingly dominate the top end of the VC industry [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- Per Ian Sigalow (Co-founder and Managing Partner): "What you're seeing today is the start of the institutionalization of venture. The industry is kind of like Cro-Magnon on the evolutionary scale from ape to human. We're somewhere in the first third of the evolution. We will become an industry that looks more like PE given the number of companies and the global scale and ambition of these businesses" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
