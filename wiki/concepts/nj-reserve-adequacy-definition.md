# NJ Statutory Definition of Reserve-Fund "Adequacy"

## Summary

New Jersey's statutory definition of reserve-fund "adequacy" — added by S3992 in August 2025 to clarify the previously-undefined term in S2760 — anchors adequacy in the 30-year capital reserve funding plan: a reserve is "adequate" if and only if the fund is never projected to fall below zero dollars over the 30-year projection [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]]. This makes the zero-dollar plan the operational test of statutory adequacy.

## Key claims

- "Adequate" and "adequacy" mean a sum of money held by an association that is sufficient to balance the association's reserve fund [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- The sum must ensure that the association's reserve fund is never projected to fall below zero dollars in the association's 30-year capital reserve funding plan [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Before S3992, S2760 used the terms "adequate" and "adequacy" without defining them [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- The definition was added by S3992, which was signed into law on August 21, 2025 [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- The adequacy test is operationally identical to the zero-dollar plan requirement: a plan that violates the zero-floor is by definition not adequate [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
