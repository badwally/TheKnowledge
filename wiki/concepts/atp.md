# Adenosine Triphosphate (ATP)

## Summary

ATP (adenosine triphosphate) is the energy currency the body uses to power muscle contraction; on the bike, every watt of mechanical output traces back to ATP being broken down to ADP and resynthesised by one of three energy systems [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].

## Key claims

- ATP can be thought of as a charged battery: energy is released when it is broken down to ADP (adenosine diphosphate) [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
- ATP is held in very limited supply within the body, so continual resynthesis is required to keep muscles contracting [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
- Three systems regenerate ATP during exercise — the [[concepts/phosphocreatine-system|phosphocreatine system]], the [[concepts/glycolytic-system|anaerobic glycolytic system]], and the [[concepts/aerobic-energy-system|aerobic system]] — operating at different rates and capacities [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
- All three systems contribute to ATP supply at essentially every exercise intensity; no real-world cycling effort is fuelled by a single system in isolation [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
- Some research frames cycling fatigue not as pure acidity but as a combined signal from lactate, hydrogen ions, and ATP indicating the muscle environment is approaching damaging conditions (Pollak et al., 2014) [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
