# California HOA 90% Expenditure Test (R&TC §23701t)

## Summary

The California expenditure test under R&TC Section 23701t requires that 90% or more of a tax-exempt homeowners' association's annual expenses be for the acquisition, construction, management, maintenance, and care of association property (or, for time-share associations, for activities provided to or on behalf of members) [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]]. It is one of the six operational-test conditions an HOA must satisfy on an ongoing basis to maintain its California tax-exempt status, and constrains the deployment of the dues/fees/assessments collected under the parallel 60% source-of-income test [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].

## Key claims

- 90% or more of the expenses for the year must be for acquisition, construction, management, maintenance, and care of association property to satisfy the expenditure test under §23701t [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- For a time-share association, the 90% threshold may alternatively be met by expenses for activities provided to or on behalf of the members of the association [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- The expenditure test is enumerated as one of the six operational-test conditions under §23701t (alongside the 85% residential test, 60% source-of-income test, no-private-inurement rule, trust-fund requirement on unused dues, and minimum two-unit-different-owners membership) [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- The expenditure test is a continuing operational requirement, not a one-time qualification — failure to maintain it places the HOA's tax-exempt status at risk [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- The 90% threshold is an annual expense-percentage metric, computed against total expenses for the year [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
