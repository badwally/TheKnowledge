# GIC Laddering Strategy (Reserve Fund Application)

## Summary

A GIC laddering strategy is an investment pattern in which a condominium reserve fund's invested balance is divided into equal tranches that mature in successive years across a fixed horizon (commonly 1–3 or 1–5 years). Each maturing tranche either funds a scheduled capital expenditure or is reinvested at the longest rung of the ladder, producing a portfolio in which a predictable fraction matures each year — addressing liquidity for the upcoming year while capturing duration-sensitive yields on the remainder. Will MacKay (CIBC Wood Gundy) presents the strategy as the structural backbone of an Ontario §115(5)-compliant reserve-fund investment plan, paired with project-specific GICs or HISAs against named capital projects [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].

## Key claims

- A laddering strategy divides reserve-fund principal into equal-sized GIC tranches with staggered maturities (e.g., five tranches each maturing 1, 2, 3, 4, and 5 years forward) [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
- Maintaining a five-year ladder produces a portfolio in which roughly 20% of the reserve fund matures each year, providing predictable liquidity [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
- At each maturity, if the proceeds are not needed for a scheduled capital project, the tranche is reinvested at the longest rung (e.g., 5 years), preserving the ladder's shape indefinitely [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
- The strategy is designed to address the §115(5) Act-adherence + principal-protection + liquidity + return triad in a single mechanical pattern, rather than relying on subjective rate-timing judgments [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
- Funds earmarked for a specific upcoming capital project (e.g., a known $250,000 roof replacement next year) should be invested separately in a duration-matched GIC or held in a high-interest savings account, while the balance of reserve funds runs through the ladder [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
- An effective Ontario reserve-fund investment plan combines duration-matched project GICs, a HISA tranche, and a laddered GIC tranche — not a single strategy [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
- 3- and 5-year ladders are both effective duration choices; the differences between 3-, 4-, and 5-year GIC rates were characterized as minimal as of May 17, 2022, making the 3-year ladder a reasonable default for boards uncertain about future rate direction [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
- A 5-year ladder is preferable to a 3-year ladder if the board believes rates have peaked and will decline to support future economic growth [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
- Laddering provides a built-in hedge against rate changes: if rates rise, maturing rungs reinvest at higher levels; if rates fall, only the maturing fraction repriced lower while the balance remains at locked-in higher rates [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
- Reserve funds are typically better served by a cash-flow-driven plan than by attempting to time interest-rate cycles directly [[sources/web-2026-01-01-040]].
