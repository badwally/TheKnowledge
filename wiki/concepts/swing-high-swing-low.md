# Swing High and Swing Low Points

## Summary

Swing highs and swing lows are the elementary building blocks of market structure — three-candle patterns (with the middle candle creating the point) that mark areas where liquidity is created; retail traders use them as 'support' and 'resistance', while seasoned traders use them to enter or exit positions based on trend, and price-delivery algos treat them as targets above (buyside liquidity) and below (sellside liquidity) [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].

## Key claims

- Swing highs and swing lows are areas where liquidity is created [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- Retail traders will use swing highs and lows as 'support' and 'resistance' points [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- Seasoned traders will use swing highs and lows to enter or exit positions based on the trend [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- A valid swing point on any timeframe requires a minimum of 3 candles, with the middle candle creating the point [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- Uptrends create higher highs and higher lows; downtrends create lower highs and lower lows — the sequence of swing points is the diagnostic for trend direction [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- Sellside liquidity is found under daily candle lows or at swing lows; algos deliver price to these targets after a buyside push fails (e.g., a doji at a recent swing high signalling lack of buying interest) [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- Buyside liquidity sits above recent swing highs and is the natural first target for algorithmic delivery before price rotates to sellside [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
