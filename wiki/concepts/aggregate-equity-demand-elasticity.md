# Aggregate Equity Demand Elasticity

## Summary

The price elasticity of demand for the aggregate stock market — empirically estimated by Gabaix and Koijen (2021) at approximately 0.2, about 100 times smaller than rational or behavioral models predict, and the inverse of the market multiplier of approximately 5 [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].

## Key claims

- Empirical estimate: aggregate equity demand elasticity is approximately 0.2 — a 5% rise in equity market price reduces demand by only 1% [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- Most rational or behavioral models predict an elasticity about 100 times larger than 0.2 [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- The macro elasticity should arguably be lower than the micro elasticity for individual stocks because Ford and General Motors are closer substitutes than the equity-market index and a bond — micro-elasticity estimates are around 1 [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- A new literature exploring elasticities for U.S. factors such as size and value finds elasticities around 0.2, consistent with the macro estimate [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- Aggregation property: the total market elasticity is the size-weighted elasticity of market participants, with the correct measure of size being the share of equity each holds [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- The estimated demand elasticity of various institutions in the GIV analysis is also around 0.2 [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
