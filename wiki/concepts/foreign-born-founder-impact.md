# Foreign-Born Founder Impact

## Summary

The outsized contribution of foreign-born founders to US tech unicorn creation and the immigration-policy risk to that pipeline; per SVB's H2 2025 "State of the Markets," at least 59 of the top 100 highest-valued US unicorns have a foreign-born founder, collectively representing over $1.5T in aggregate value, while F-1 student visa applications — the primary pipeline for foreign-born tech founders — have dropped 20% from their 2015 peak with issuance rates falling 37 percentage points [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].

## Key claims

- At least 59 of the top 100 highest-valued US unicorns have a foreign-born founder, collectively accounting for more than $1.5T of aggregate value [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- 19 of the top 20 US unicorns have at least one foreign-born founder [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- The three highest-valued US unicorns — OpenAI, SpaceX, and Stripe — all have foreign-born founders [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- As many as 72% of foreign-born tech founders arrive in the US on student visas (F-1), per a Georgetown University study cited in the report [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- F-1 visa applications peaked in 2015 and have dropped 20% in the nine years since [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- The issuance rate for F-1 visas has been cut down by 37 percentage points from its peak due to added scrutiny on applications [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- Federal funding cuts to universities and increased vetting on student visa applicants could further squeeze the founder pipeline, potentially diverting would-be founders to other countries [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- SVB warns: "if the spigot is turned off, it may take years for the impact to register, or we may see others rise faster" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- The US generated more than half of newly created unicorns in the last five years, but that dominance hinges on a steady supply of global talent [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
