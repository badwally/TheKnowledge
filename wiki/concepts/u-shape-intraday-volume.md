# U-shape intraday volume pattern

## Summary

The U-shape intraday volume pattern is the empirical regularity that trading volume across major asset classes is high right after market open and again right before market close, with a trough in the middle of the day; Baltussen, Da, Lammers, and Martens (2020) document this U-shape across equities, bonds, commodities, and currencies and use it as the liquidity-based explanation for why hedging demand is concentrated in the last 30 minutes of the trading day [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].

## Key claims

- Figure 1 of the paper confirms a U-shape intraday volume pattern across the equity, bond, commodity, and currency markets — liquidity tends to be high right after open and before close [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
- Spreads are generally lower and market depth higher when trading toward the close, so investors who do not fully hedge their positions immediately after an intraday price jump will leave the bulk of the hedge to the last half hour, especially when trading larger quantities [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
- Both the late-informed-trading and infrequent-rebalancing alternative explanations of intraday momentum (Gao et al. 2018) hinge on the same U-shape volume pattern, since informed trading and rebalancing are expected to take place primarily at the start and end of the trading session when liquidity is high (Admati and Pfleiderer 1988; Bogousslavsky 2016) [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
- Baltussen et al. note that the fact that ROD predicts LH better than ONFH suggests that returns during the day also matter — pointing to hedging rather than start-of-day rebalancing or late informed trading as the dominant driver [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
