# Core Services as the Persistent Driver of Inflation

## Summary

The July 13, 2022 Q3-2022 IACFM presentation argues that CPI in mid-2022 was increasingly driven by inflation in core services — a category that tends to be more persistent and has a roughly 60% weighting in the overall index — with the contribution from shelter alone accounting for ~3% annualized run-rate inflation in Core CPI [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].

## Key claims

- CPI in recent months (mid-2022) has been increasingly driven by inflation in core services, which tends to be more persistent and has a ~60% weighting in the overall index [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- CPI category weightings cited in the presentation: Food 13%, Energy 8%, Core CPI 78%; within Core CPI, core goods 21% and core services 57% (32% shelter + 1% airline fares + 24% other core services) [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- The contribution from shelter alone to annualized monthly Core CPI inflation rose from 2.4% in November 2021 to 3.1% in May 2022 [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- The contribution from core services alone to annualized monthly Core CPI inflation moved from 3.4% (Nov 2021) → 3.1% (Dec 2021) → 3.9% (Jan 2022) → 4.7% (Feb 2022) → 5.3% (Mar 2022) → 6.5% (Apr 2022) → 5.5% (May 2022) [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- The percentage of Core CPI inflation from core services rose from 52% (Nov 2021) to 70% (May 2022), peaking at 134% in March 2022 — a reading above 100% means core services were generating more inflation than headline Core CPI as core goods deflated [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- May 2022 month-over-month inflation by core-services subcategory: Shelter 0.6%, Airline fares 12.6%, Other core services 0.3%, Core services aggregate 0.6% [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Core goods month-over-month inflation through this period was much more volatile: New cars & used cars went from 1.8% (Nov 2021) to (1.8%) (Mar 2022) to 1.4% (May 2022); Core goods aggregate moved from 0.9% (Nov 2021) to (0.4%) (Mar 2022) to 0.7% (May 2022) [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
