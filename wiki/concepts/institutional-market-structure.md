# Institutional Market Structure (ICT)

## Summary

The ICT framing of "institutional market structure" as the study of correlated assets — or the relation to inversely correlated assets — used to decide what smart money is accumulating or distributing; the canonical illustration in the Month 3 mentorship is comparing forex pair price swings against the U.S. Dollar Index (DXY) to read market symmetry [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].

## Key claims

- Institutional market structure is defined as the study of correlated assets or the relation to inversely correlated assets, used to decide what smart money is accumulating or distributing [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
- The framework is described as "easy to study with the DXY," and price should be studied to find market symmetry [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
- In forex, the operational test is to compare every price swing in the DXY with the pair traded — as DXY trades higher, expect lower in the pair, and the same logic applies symmetrically when DXY trades lower [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
- If DXY or the currency pair fails to move in tandem, smart money is actively trading, and an SMT divergence is in play [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
