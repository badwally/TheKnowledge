# Akash Staking Mechanism

## Summary

Provider anti-fraud, governance, and reputation system proposed by the 2018 Akash Network position paper: rather than gating new providers via a centralized approval process, the network requires providers to stake AKASH tokens at a meaningful value, with the stake fraction driving both governance voting weight and a reputation score that tenants may use as a deployment criterion [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].

## Key claims

- "The stability of the Akash network relies on a staking system that prevents bad actors from abusing our system" — the staking system is framed as creating "a prohibitive monetary disincentive for bad actors" [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- The risk of fraudulent behavior is highest when new, unknown providers join the network [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- Rather than a centralized or federated approval process, the Akash network allows anyone to join — but a new provider "must stake a meaningful value on the network in Akash tokens" [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- There is no minimum stake amount, but participation in Akash governance "is proportional to a provider's stake, taken as a fraction of the sum of all stakes" [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- Stake contribution is also factored into a provider's reputation score, which tenants may use as a deployment criterion [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
