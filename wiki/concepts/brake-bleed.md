# Brake Bleed

## Summary

Bleeding a [[concepts/hydraulic-disc-brake|hydraulic disc brake]] purges air from the sealed fluid system and refills it with fresh brake fluid; on SRAM HRD it follows one of two procedures depending on whether the caliper has a [[concepts/bleeding-edge|Bleeding Edge]] port [[sources/pdf-b51938faf250]].

## Key claims

- The bleed runs end-to-end as: prepare syringes, prepare the lever, prepare the caliper, bleed the system, then reinstall the brake pads [[sources/pdf-b51938faf250]].
- SRAM HRD documents two distinct bleed procedures — a Bleeding Edge procedure and a non-Bleeding Edge procedure — selected by inspecting the caliper to identify which fitting type is present [[sources/pdf-b51938faf250]].
- Bleeding requires syringes filled with brake fluid; SRAM publishes a syringe-storage step so the same syringes can be reused for future service [[sources/pdf-b51938faf250]].
- Disc-brake pad gap and (on [[concepts/monoblock-caliper|Monoblock]] calipers) pad retraction must be reset before pads are reinstalled, ensuring the bled system has correct piston starting positions [[sources/pdf-b51938faf250]].
- After bleeding and reinstalling pads, the brakes must be [[concepts/brake-bed-in|bedded in]] before normal use [[sources/pdf-b51938faf250]].
