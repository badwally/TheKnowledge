# Munger's Twenty-Five Psychological Tendencies

## Summary

A checklist of twenty-five standard causes of human misjudgment compiled by Charlie Munger, synthesized from three psychology textbooks, Robert Cialdini's *Influence*, and decades of cross-disciplinary observation — designed as a practical decision-making tool whose central insight is that psychological tendencies are "both numerous and inseparably intertwined" and must be assessed in combination, not isolation [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].

## Key claims

- The twenty-five tendencies are: (1) Reward and Punishment Superresponse, (2) Liking/Loving, (3) Disliking/Hating, (4) Doubt-Avoidance, (5) Inconsistency-Avoidance, (6) Curiosity, (7) Kantian Fairness, (8) Envy/Jealousy, (9) Reciprocation, (10) Influence-from-Mere-Association, (11) Simple Pain-Avoiding Psychological Denial, (12) Excessive Self-Regard, (13) Overoptimism, (14) Deprival-Superreaction, (15) Social-Proof, (16) Contrast-Misreaction, (17) Stress-Influence, (18) Availability-Misweighing, (19) Use-It-or-Lose-It, (20) Drug-Misinfluence, (21) Senescence-Misinfluence, (22) Authority-Misinfluence, (23) Twaddle, (24) Reason-Respecting, (25) Lollapalooza [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- The framework's central structural principle is that cognition is ordinarily situation-dependent — different situations cause different conclusions even when the same person is thinking in the same subject area — which Munger calls "the grand general principle of social psychology" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- The tendencies are acknowledged to be tautological to some extent, with overlaps, and could be laid out in a somewhat different way — but this matters only moderately because "a significant amount of messiness is unfixable in a soft science like psychology" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- The system is meant to be used as a checklist: "It is also essential for a thinking man to assemble his skills into a checklist that he routinely uses. Any other mode of operation will cause him to miss much that is important" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- The tendencies are "probably much more good than bad" — otherwise they would not exist as evolved adaptations — so they "can't be simply washed out automatically, and shouldn't be"; the goal is to recognize when they mislead [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- The system's paradox: "In social psychology, the more people learn about the system the less it is true, and this is what gives the system its great value" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Munger lists ten exemplary applications: Carl Braun's communication rules, pilot simulators, Alcoholics Anonymous, clinical training ("see one, do one, teach one"), the U.S. Constitutional Convention's secret-ballot rules, Granny's Rule for self-management, Harvard Business School's decision trees, Johnson & Johnson's acquisition autopsies, Darwin's anti-confirmation-bias method (morphed into FDA double-blind studies), and Warren Buffett's rule for open-outcry auctions ("Don't go") [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Munger critiques academic psychology for studying tendencies in isolation via repeatable single-variable experiments while ignoring compound effects — an approach he compares to physics ignoring astrophysics and all compound effects [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
