# Autonomous AI Systems

## Summary

AI systems whose defining property is the ability to operate with a level of autonomy sufficient to traverse changing settings, handle unexpected situations, and optimize performance over time without continual human oversight [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].

## Key claims

- Autonomy in this context is paired with adaptiveness: together they allow systems to cope with task-oriented processes whose specifics are not known in advance [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].
- Unlike rule-based classical AI that requires explicit instructions, autonomous systems are designed with rationalism that lets them reason and adapt to differing scenarios well enough to accomplish goals [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].
- Suitable application domains include autonomous devices, collaborative robots, and interactive decision-support systems in finance and healthcare — settings that require high levels of interaction [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].
- Demand for systems capable of autonomously handling intricate and dynamic processes is the principal driver of interest in Agentic AI [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].
- Autonomy operates within defined boundaries or structures, not as a generalized AGI claim — a distinction the survey treats as essential to scoping responsible deployment [[sources/pdf-83c3ccfff1f1]].
