# Market fragility

## Summary

Market fragility is Ambrus Capital's characterization of contemporary U.S. equity markets as superficially stable but structurally vulnerable to sudden, large dislocations, driven by reflexive feedback loops that mask accumulating risk during calm periods [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].

## Key claims

- It is no longer uncommon to see equity-market days with wild swings, yet dip buyers are continually rewarded — giving investors a false impression of stability [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- Illustrative example: on January 24, 2021, the SPX index sold off 3.8% and then went up more than 4.5% in a single trading session [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- Recent market behavior has produced extreme tail observations: 3 of 4 of the lowest-ever VIX prints occurred in 2017 and 4 of 5 of the highest-ever VIX prints occurred in 2020 [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- Ambrus Capital's metaphor for the current market is a forest where naturally occurring fires have been doused quickly each time, leaving "immense amounts of dry kindling just waiting to explode" [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- The drivers of market fragility identified are: speculative frenzy in a euphoric phase, changes to market microstructure, unprecedented stimulus, growth of option trading, concentration among options market makers, and the Dodd-Frank Act's effect on bank risk warehousing [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- Roughly 80% of all U.S. dollars in existence have been printed since January 2020, increasing capital-markets liquidity and the size of order flow, which in turn shrinks liquidity providers' margin for error [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
