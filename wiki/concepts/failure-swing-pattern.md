# Failure Swing (Double Top / Double Bottom)

## Summary

In daytradingrauf's DOL framework a "failure swing" is a pattern that retail traders read as a reversal — specifically a double top or double bottom — and that the contrarian DOL trader treats as a liquidity container: a structure that has trapped retail orders on the wrong side of the eventual move, suitable to fade only when higher-time-frame order flow supports it [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].

## Key claims

- Failure swings are explicitly defined as double tops and double bottoms: "failure swings (double tops and bottoms)" [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
- They are listed alongside bull/bear flags and trend line liquidity as one of three structural forms of market-structure liquidity to fade [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
- The pattern is interesting because retail traders "spool in orders without context of the market just by looking at these patterns," creating the order pool that DOL targets [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
- The fade is conditional: it requires HTF order flow to back up why the failure swing is being faded [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
