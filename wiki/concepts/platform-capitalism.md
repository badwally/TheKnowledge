# Platform Capitalism

## Summary

Platform capitalism, as theorised by Nick Srnicek (2017), describes an economic configuration in which a small number of large platform corporations — typically named as Amazon, Apple, Alphabet, Facebook (Meta), and Microsoft — accumulate market power by centralising user data and metadata and trading it [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].

## Key claims

- Centralisation places data and metadata in the hands of a few companies that buy, sell, link, and use it to manipulate, control, and advertise to users while subjugating other businesses [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].
- Data cooperatives are framed as a structural alternative to platform capitalism by redistributing data ownership and downstream economic gains to users or workers [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].
- The EU Data Governance Act's restrictions on reselling data for profit are framed in part as a check on platform-capitalist business models [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].
