# DXY SMT Divergence

## Summary

Smart Money Tool (SMT) divergence applied across the U.S. Dollar Index (DXY) and a foreign currency pair: a non-symmetrical condition — where the inversely correlated DXY makes a lower low while the foreign currency fails to make a higher high — is treated as a high-probability reversal signal, while a symmetrical condition is treated as trend continuation and a poor environment for stalking reversals [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].

## Key claims

- In symmetrical market conditions, when DXY makes a lower low, the foreign currency makes a higher high — confirming the trend is likely to continue, in which case stalking a reversal pattern is not high probability and should be avoided [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
- In non-symmetrical market conditions, when DXY makes a lower low and the foreign currency fails to trade higher, that is "DXY SMT" — confirming the trend is likely to reverse and stalking a reversal pattern is high probability [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
- The symmetric logic applies when DXY trades lower [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
- DXY SMT divergence is detected by comparing every price swing in the DXY with the pair the trader is trading; when DXY or the currency pair fails to move in tandem, smart money is actively trading [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
