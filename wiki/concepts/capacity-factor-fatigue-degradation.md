# Capacity factor as a fatigue-driven reliability covariate

## Summary

Capacity factor — the ratio of a wind turbine's actual energy output to its theoretical maximum — can serve as an environmental covariate in reliability models. In Bayesian hierarchical Accelerated Failure Time analyses of wind turbine gearboxes, capacity factor emerges as the most robust environmental driver of lifetime reduction, an effect interpreted as consistent with fatigue-based degradation mechanisms.

## Key claims

- In a Bayesian hierarchical AFT analysis of over 8,000 gearboxes and 64,000 cumulative operating years, capacity factor emerges as the most robust environmental driver of lifetime reduction [[sources/web-2026-05-01-6b7]].
- The authors interpret the capacity-factor effect on lifetime as consistent with fatigue-based degradation mechanisms in wind turbine gearboxes [[sources/web-2026-05-01-6b7]].
- Environmental covariates such as capacity factor are positioned in the framework as secondary contributors to gearbox failure relative to intrinsic manufacturer-specific design differences [[sources/web-2026-05-01-6b7]].
