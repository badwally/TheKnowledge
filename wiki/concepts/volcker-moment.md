# Volcker Moment

## Summary

Term introduced by Zoltan Pozsar in his February 16, 2022 Credit Suisse dispatch and elaborated in the May 13, 2022 follow-up "Ride of the 'Volkyries'" to describe a Fed posture of deliberate monetary shock — injecting volatility into markets to slay inflation through asset prices, financial conditions, and ultimately demand destruction [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].

## Key claims

- The "Volcker Moment" frames the Fed as needing to inject volatility into markets in order to control domestic services inflation (and demand for labor more broadly) through asset prices — stocks, housing, and crypto assets too [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- A former vice chair of the FOMC arguing that the Fed needs to shock markets — as Bill Dudley did in his April 6, 2022 column — is described by Pozsar as "as close as we will ever get to a former Fed policymaker endorsing the need for a 'Volcker Moment'" [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- A Fed that is writing a call option on the stock market needs to see a deeper correction, big enough to slow demand and push unemployment higher [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- The Fed leading the market — rather than following it — in this dance is itself "a 'Volcker Moment' in and of itself" [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Pozsar argues we have never achieved a soft landing, so the fastest pace of hikes in a generation combined with unprecedented balance-sheet shrinkage will not yield one either [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
