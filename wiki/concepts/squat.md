# Squat (Cycling-Strength Exercise)

## Summary

The squat is a foundational lower-body strength exercise for cyclists that targets the glutes, hamstrings, and quads to generate strength, power, and stability through the pedal stroke [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].

## Key claims

- Targets the glutes, hamstrings, and quads — the primary muscle groups responsible for cycling power production [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Performed with feet shoulder-width apart and pointing straight ahead, initiating the descent at the hips and dropping until thighs are parallel to the floor before driving back up through the hips [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Form cues: keep the chest up, back flat, and knees tracking outward (do not let knees collapse to the inside) [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Progressions include adding load (dumbbells, barbell) or moving to [[concepts/plyometrics-cycling|squat jumps]] for explosive power [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Equivalent or alternative variations: Smith machine squat, leg press, and goblet squat — all viable substitutes when cued correctly [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- The squat is the basis for several cycling-specific variants, including [[concepts/single-leg-cycling-strength|single-leg variants]] such as single-leg squat and Bulgarian squat [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
