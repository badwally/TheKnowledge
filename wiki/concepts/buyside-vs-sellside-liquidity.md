# Buyside vs Sellside Liquidity

## Summary

In Lesson 2: Market Structure, buyside liquidity sits at swing highs where short sellers' stops cluster, and sellside liquidity sits at swing lows where long-side stops cluster — and "taking liquidity" by running these stops is treated as a precondition for trend-defining displacement [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].

## Key claims

- Buyside liquidity is defined in Lesson 2 by reference to swing highs: "think where are the shorts positioned and where are their stops" [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The sellside-liquidity counterpart sits at swing lows — Lesson 2's bearish January-2022 example targets "the swing lows below (from the left side of the chart)" once price has taken buyside liquidity and is displacing down [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Lesson 2's checklist begins with "Liquidity (needs to be taken first)" — establishing liquidity-run as a precondition for the rest of the framework rather than an optional input [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- A failed breakout in one direction is interpreted as a stop-out of eager traders positioned that way; smart money sees this liquidity and uses those stops to position for the ensuing move [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Lesson 2's symmetry rule: if price fails to displace up and takes buyside liquidity, then by default price may be seeking to take out sellside liquidity below — and vice versa [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Lesson 2 ties liquidity to target-setting: once price has taken buyside liquidity and is displacing down, the targets become the swing lows visible on the left side of the chart [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The bullish-reversal example uses sellside liquidity ("new yearly lows") as the significant pre-condition — "$ES hits new yearly lows. A significant liquidity target," after which price refuses to displace down, signaling reversal [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
