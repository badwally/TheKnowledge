# Trendline Phantoms (False Trendlines)

## Summary

The ICT framing that diagonal "trendlines" are imaginary lines retail traders extend into the future to create support and resistance theories — and that market makers can capitalize on this fallacy by engineering moves that strip price away from those lines, leaving retail buys and sells "devoid of support" or "devoid of resistance" at the moment of execution [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].

## Key claims

- The market appears to have imaginary diagonal lines that support or resist price; retail traders extend these lines into the future and attribute support and resistance theories to them [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
- Retail will buy at trendline support and sell at trendline resistance respectively when price reaches them [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
- The use of trendline support by retail provides market makers liquidity; the chart may appear bullish but it is the opposite, and retail will buy at a moment when price will be devoid of support [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
- The use of trendline resistance by retail similarly provides market makers liquidity; the chart may appear bearish but it is the opposite, and retail will sell at a moment when price will be devoid of resistance [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
- The framework poses four diagnostic questions: does price have an awareness of trendlines, do banks associate "value" on the basis of trendline theory, is the nature of trendlines flawed, and how can market makers capitalize on this fallacy [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
