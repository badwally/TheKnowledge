# Bid-Offer Spread

## Summary

The difference between the price at which a market maker is willing to buy (bid) and the price at which they are willing to sell (offer) a currency pair; FX market-makers quote bid-offer spreads governed by the rule of "left bid, right offer" and adapt the rule for cross rates, swap points, and forward outrights [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].

## Key claims

- "The Rule of the Left Bid - Right Offer" is the foundational FX market-making convention: the lower number on a quote is the bid and the higher number is the offer [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- Bid-offer spreads on cross rates depend on whether the constituent currency pairs are quoted on the same terms or on different terms relative to USD [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- Bid-offer spreads on FX swaps are quoted in swap points, with the rule of thumb determined by the side of the market and the direction of the carry [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- "Trading conventions among market makers" govern how dealers communicate quotes and which side of the market is hit by a customer order [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- Reciprocal quotation terms (quoting EUR/USD vs USD/EUR) flip bid and offer to preserve the same economic transaction [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
