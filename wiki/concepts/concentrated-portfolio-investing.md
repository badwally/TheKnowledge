# Concentrated portfolio investing

## Summary

The practice of holding a small number of securities (typically 5-8) that the investor knows well, on the argument that knowing your companies is more protective than diversification; in Joel Greenblatt's Columbia class, concentration is both a strategy recommendation and the structural design of Gotham Capital [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].

## Key claims

- Greenblatt lists "Concentrate your investments" as one of the ways to beat the market in his first class [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Gotham Capital itself "is very concentrated. We invest in 5 to 8 securities. Know your companies very well" [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Greenblatt's framing of why concentration is more safe than diversifying: "You pick your spots" — when investing only in your highest-conviction ideas, your worst position is still vetted in a way a diversified position might not be [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- The math of low turnover and high concentration produces a structural workload reduction: "if your holding period is three to five years and you only have 4 to 6 securities, then you only need one or two ideas a year" — which Greenblatt offers as the reason he has time to teach his Columbia class [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Concentration combines with returning outside capital to keep the firm small and nimble, so it can invest "in as many situations as possible (not constrained by size)" [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
