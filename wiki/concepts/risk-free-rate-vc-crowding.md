# Risk-Free Rate VC Crowding

## Summary

The mechanism by which elevated risk-free bond yields crowd out LP allocations to venture capital; per SVB's H2 2025 "State of the Markets," high bond returns (approximately 5% risk-free) are luring some LPs away from venture, while the bond market's demand for a higher term premium — driven by persistent deficit concerns and inflation uncertainty — means that even if the Fed resumes cutting short-term rates, long-term capital is not getting meaningfully cheaper [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].

## Key claims

- "If you can make 5% risk free, why keep such high VC allocations?" — SVB frames this as the key question facing LPs considering venture allocations in the current rate environment [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- High bond returns are "luring some LPs away from venture," contributing to the decline in US VC fundraising [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- The bond market is "asserting itself, with investors demanding a higher term premium due to persistent deficit concerns and inflation uncertainty" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- Even if the Fed resumes cutting rates, "long-term capital is not getting meaningfully cheaper" because long-term yields are being driven by term premium rather than short-rate expectations alone [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- Rate cuts haven't lowered long-term borrowing costs; the report notes a flat 10-year yield alongside an increasing spread, with the bond market "flashing inflation persistence" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- US VC fundraising in H1 2025 totaled $28B against an $80B full-year outlook, continuing to trend down as "a volatile H1 filled with inflationary tariff policies led to investors waiting to see how the Fed might move rates and how the economy would respond" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- The June 2025 inflation reading ticked up to 2.7%, the highest in four months, as companies that had absorbed tariff costs after "Liberation Day" began passing higher prices on to consumers [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- The effective US tariff rate has oscillated between 10% and 20%+ in the last five months as shifting policies and trade agreements created uncertainty [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
