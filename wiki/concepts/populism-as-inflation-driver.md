# Populism as a structural force driving inflation

## Summary

Kai Volatility's Q2 2022 thesis that 40 years of monetary-policy dominance (1981-2021) drove a secular increase in inequality, which in turn produced the political populism — Tea Party, Occupy Wall Street, Trump, Sanders — that made the COVID-era ~$9 trillion fiscal response inevitable; the inflation that follows is therefore structural rather than psychological, and the Volcker playbook of demand destruction is the wrong tool for it [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].

## Key claims

- "Inequality, as measured by the Gini Coefficient and the top ten percent's share of domestic wealth … has been secularly increasing since monetary policy dominance took hold in the mid to late 1970s" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- "This is no coincidence. Inequality in the developed world has been overwhelmingly driven by the unconstrained monetary stimulus for the past forty years. Low interest rates represent cheap capital to the investment class. The overwhelming majority of the Fed's stimulus has ended up in the hands of the wealthy and corporations, which have used the capital to maximize profits" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Maximizing corporate profits entails lowering costs of production and spurring innovation and competition to capture market share — driving secular globalization and historic technological innovation, but at the cost of the domestic labor class [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- "Wages' share of the economy's total income has dropped by nearly 20% in the last 50 years and the middle class has been hollowed out in the U.S." [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- "This has, unsurprisingly, led to distrust in government and decreasing societal cohesion. Starting in 2009, it began to spur increasingly populist movements in the U.S. on both sides of the political spectrum. In 2009, it spawned the Tea Party movement on the Right; in 2011, it gave birth to Occupy Wall Street on the Left" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- "Donald Trump & Bernie Sanders are just the most recent incarnations of this movement in the U.S. Both politicians are simply populist vehicles created by the Federal Reserve & forty years of unencumbered monetary policy" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- "The structural transition of the political Right to the populist Left by Donald Trump in the U.S. was ultimately the catalyst that made for a revolution in wait. Once this occurred, the tidal wave of fiscal policy was all but inevitable. The COVID pandemic was simply the spark that led to its ultimate ignition" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- The total proposed COVID fiscal response of "nearly $9 Trillion and counting" dwarfs the Great Society in absolute size and is rivaled in adjusted size only by FDR's New Deal — anchoring the structural rather than psychological reading of 2020-2022 inflation [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
