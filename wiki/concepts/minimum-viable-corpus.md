# Minimum Viable Corpus

## Summary

The "minimum viable corpus" is the first inflection point along a startup's data journey, defined by Casado and Lauten as the smallest dataset sufficient to begin training a useful model — analogous to but distinct from the bootstrapping or cold-start problem in classical network-effects businesses [[sources/web-2019-05-09-487]]. In many enterprise data-scale-effect businesses, reaching the minimum viable corpus is comparatively low-investment and is explicitly not, by itself, a durable moat [[sources/web-2019-05-09-487]].

## Key claims

- A minimum viable corpus can be assembled from several sources: automating data capture from available sources (e.g. crawling the web); getting early users to trade their data for something in return; repurposing data from other domains through transfer learning; and synthetically generating data programmatically [[sources/web-2019-05-09-487]].
- Unlike the classical network-effects cold-start problem (which is hard to solve precisely because the network must already be live to attract volume), bootstrapping a minimum viable corpus in enterprise data-scale-effect domains is often achievable with relatively low investment [[sources/web-2019-05-09-487]].
- Reaching the minimum viable corpus is explicitly not a durable moat — but it can be used offensively against incumbents that have data but fail to apply it properly, e.g. a small enterprise-automation team using synthetic data to beat two global incumbents whose decades-old data corpuses were not well-suited to the problem at hand [[sources/web-2019-05-09-487]].
