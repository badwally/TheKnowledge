# Trend-following hit ratio

## Summary

The fraction of winning trades in a trend-following strategy; per Potters and Bouchaud (2006) and Bruder and Gaussel (2011) the hit ratio of trend-following is structurally below 50%, paired with an average gain larger than the average loss — the structural reason "trend followers lose more often than they gain" while still earning a positive expected return [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].

## Key claims

- Potters and Bouchaud (2006) focused on the hit ratio (the fraction of winning trades) of trend-following and showed that the best case is obtained when the asset volatility is low [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
- In the low-volatility limit the hit ratio is equal to 50%, but the hit ratio decreases rapidly when volatility increases [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
- Potters and Bouchaud concluded from this that "trend followers lose more often than they gain" [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
- Since the average P&L per trade is equal to zero in their model, Potters and Bouchaud also showed that the average gain is larger than the average loss — confirming the convex option profile of the momentum risk premium [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
- Bruder and Gaussel (2011) reproduce the hit-ratio-below-50% result within their option-profile / trading-impact decomposition [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
