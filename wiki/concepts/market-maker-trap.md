# Market maker trap

## Summary

A market maker trap is a deceptive price pattern engineered to lure retail traders into orders that are then reversed by institutional flow; ICT Month 2 names two canonical forms — false flags and false breakouts — and ties both to mature trends, higher-timeframe distribution, and consolidation/range conditions [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].

## Key claims

- Not all big price moves that move into short-term consolidations are bull or bear flags — in mature trends or HTF distribution levels, price posts false flags [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- Retail traders see a classic continuation pattern in a false flag, but the move resolves as a reversal [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- In false-breakout consolidations, the market moves into a trading range and breakout traders bracket the range with orders [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- Market makers send price into those bracketing orders, tripping retail traders into positions, and then reverse [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- Both traps are positioned in ICT Month 2 as the explanation for why naive pattern recognition ("classic continuation", "breakout") fails without the context of HTF bias and institutional order flow [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
