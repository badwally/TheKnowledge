# Ponzi flows

## Summary

Ponzi flows are the portion of fund flows that chase self-inflated returns rather than fundamental managerial skill; named and quantified by van der Beck, Bouchaud, and Villamaina (2024), who use them to characterize an endogenous capital-reallocation mechanism in actively managed funds [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].

## Key claims

- Ponzi flows arise because investors are unable to differentiate between self-inflated returns (price impact) and fundamental returns (stock-picking skill), so they chase both components of past fund returns with statistically indistinguishable coefficients [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- Around 2% of all daily fund flows can be attributed to Ponzi flows, and 8–12% of flows in the top decile of illiquid funds are Ponzi flows [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- Approximately $500 million of investor wealth is reallocated each day in ETFs alone because of the price impact of Ponzi flows [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- Funds with high Ponzi flows experience subsequent drawdowns of over 200% [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- Flows chasing self-inflated returns predict bubbles in ETFs and their subsequent crashes [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- The label "Ponzi flows" does not invoke the SEC's definition of a Ponzi scheme as investment fraud — the reallocation happens indirectly via observable market prices rather than direct capital transfers, but the wealth transfer "inevitably collapses" once the underlying price pressure reverts [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
