# FX Value Date

## Summary

The day on which the two parties to a foreign-exchange transaction actually exchange the two currencies; FX trading uses a fixed taxonomy of value dates — Cash, Tomorrow Next, Spot, and Forward Outright — each defined relative to the deal date, with the spot date (T+2) serving as the benchmark from which all other FX prices are derived [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].

## Key claims

- It is impractical, in most circumstances, for the value date and the trade date to be the same; the forward value date is required to allow both parties time to arrange for payments which often occur in different time zones [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- "Cash" value date is the deal date itself [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- "Tomorrow Next" (Tom Next) is one mutual business day after the deal date [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- "Spot" is two mutual business days after the deal date and is the market benchmark from which all other FX rates are calculated [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- "Forward Outright" is any value date three business days or more after the deal date — always longer than spot [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- The settlement date may not fall on a day that is a national holiday in either country [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- Exception: USD/CAD spot is T+1 rather than T+2 [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
