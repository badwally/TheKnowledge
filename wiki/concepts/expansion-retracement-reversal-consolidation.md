# Expansion, Retracement, Reversal, Consolidation

## Summary

In ICT (Inner Circle Trader) price-action analysis, "Expansion, Retracement, Reversal, Consolidation" are taught as the four defining elements of a trade setup — the discrete states price can be in at any moment — and each state is paired with a specific institutional order-flow reference point a trader is meant to look for [[sources/pdf-67b6fe78b28c]].

## Key claims

- The four defining elements of a trade setup are Expansion, Retracement, Reversal, and Consolidation [[sources/pdf-67b6fe78b28c]].
- Expansion is when price moves quickly from a level of Equilibrium; the speed shows a willingness of Market Makers to show their hand, and the trader looks for Orderblocks at or near Equilibrium [[sources/pdf-67b6fe78b28c]].
- Retracement is when price moves back inside the recently created price range, indicating Market Makers want to reprice to levels inefficiently traded; the trader looks for Fair Value Gaps and Liquidity Voids [[sources/pdf-67b6fe78b28c]].
- Reversal is when price moves in the opposite direction that IPDA has been allowing, indicating Smart Money has run liquidity and a large move should unfold in a new direction; the trader looks for Liquidity Pools above an old high or below an old low [[sources/pdf-67b6fe78b28c]].
- Consolidation is when price moves in a range and shows no movement higher or lower, indicating Market Makers want to hold the price and build orders on both sides of the market; the trader looks for the impulse price swing from Equilibrium [[sources/pdf-67b6fe78b28c]].
- These four elements pair with four reference points in institutional order flow: Orderblocks, Fair Value Gaps & Liquidity Voids, Liquidity Pools, and Equilibrium [[sources/pdf-67b6fe78b28c]].
