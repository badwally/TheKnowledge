# Stop Run

## Summary

In ICT-style price-action analysis, a stop run is one of the two forms of institutional swing point: the market makes a higher high, then fails and breaks down, causing a rejection at the highs that knocks out traders positioned with the prior trend and hands an immediate advantage to the opposite side [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].

## Key claims

- Stop runs happen when the market makes a higher high, then fails and breaks down, causing a rejection at the highs [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- The stop run is described as the most significant price pattern to learn conceptually [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- Stop runs can be anticipated when price is hovering just below a key institutional reference point [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- A stop run happens when the pattern is already stopped out, knocking out the traders, and it is an advantage to sellers [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- The dropping off after the rejection lulls traders into believing they are short, and it gives confidence to sell [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- The pattern is framed as the highest and most probable condition to be a short seller in the marketplace because it has a built-in advantage [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- When traders understand order blocks, they can avoid getting caught by the market [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
