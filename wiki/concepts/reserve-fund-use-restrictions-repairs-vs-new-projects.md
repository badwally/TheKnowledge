# Reserve Fund Use Restrictions (Repairs/Replacements vs New Projects)

## Summary

HOA reserve funds may be subject to restrictions on permissible use under the association's governing documents and applicable state law, which may limit reserve drawdowns to repairs and replacements of existing components rather than new projects [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]]. The distinction is salient to HOA boards considering whether to fund a capital improvement project from internal reserves or seek external financing: when use restrictions apply, reserve drawdown is unavailable as a funding option for a new-project scope and the board must choose between special assessment and bank financing [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].

## Key claims

- Governing documents and state law may restrict the use of reserve funds to repairs and replacements instead of new projects [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Using reserve funds also depletes liquidity that may have been earmarked for other projects, an additional consideration even where use restrictions do not strictly apply [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Boards may be tempted to use reserve funds to pay for a capital improvement; First Citizens's board-facing guide explicitly cautions against this without first considering financing as an alternative [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
