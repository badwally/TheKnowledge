# Server Underutilization

## Summary

Empirical claim — invoked as the supply-side rationale for a decentralized cloud-compute marketplace in the 2018 Akash Network position paper — that the vast majority of servers in production operate at low utilization, leaving recoverable capacity that a spot market priced in a native token could match against demand [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].

## Key claims

- "Analysts estimate that as many as 85% of servers in practice have underutilized capacity" — the position paper cites Glanz (2012), Kaplan, Forrest, and Kindler (2008), Liu (2011), and Koomey and Taylor (2015) as the supporting analyst literature [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- "Idle, underutilized servers prove to be costly and wasteful" — framed in the paper as the operational economics that should be recoverable by a marketplace [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- Even though running computing on-premise can offer better flexibility, performance, and security, organizations are abandoning their datacenter operations and migrating to cloud because "they are finding it increasingly hard to justify the operating costs due to lack of adequate automation along with low utilization footprint" [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- The difference in capital expenditure between running in the cloud and self-managing on-premise is described as marginal; the cloud's edge is in operating expenditure, where the providers have invested in automation with minimal human touch [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
- Providers use Akash because the marketplace "allows them to earn profits from either dedicated or temporarily-unused capacity" — the explicit monetization path for otherwise-idle servers [[sources/pdf-c9b8f466ea39]].
