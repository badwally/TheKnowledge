# Fixed income reset (2022)

## Summary

The asset-allocation thesis that 2022's record-breaking government-bond drawdown restored bonds' two core functions — income, and diversification against risky assets — by lifting yields out of the sub-1% "no income" zone where extended-credit substitutes had been forced into uncomfortably high correlations with equities [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].

## Key claims

- Before the 2022 reset, allocating to fixed income had been a never-ending source of headaches for multi-asset investors, because long-bull-market yields had fallen to a point where government bonds could deliver neither income nor diversification against risky assets [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- At one point a staggering 90% of the global government-bond universe was offering a yield of less than 1%, forcing investors into extended credit sectors with much higher correlations to equities [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- Low starting yields had also diminished government bonds' ability to deliver positive returns that could offset losses during equity bear markets [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- 2022 produced a record-breaking drawdown driven by surging inflation, central banks playing catch-up, and governments that had seemingly lost their fear of debt — combined with the fact that central banks were hiking by far more than markets expected [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- Looking forward, the income on offer from bonds is now far more enticing; the global government-bond benchmark has seen yields rise by roughly 200 basis points, and JPM AM is more excited about bonds than it has been in over a decade [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
