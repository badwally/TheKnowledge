# Intermediate Term High / Low (ITH / ITL)

## Summary

Intermediate Term High (ITH) and Intermediate Term Low (ITL) are ICT structural levels formed when price fills (rebalances) a fair value gap; ICT treats them as load-bearing levels that should not be violated while a directional move is intact, and they serve as both bias confirmation and trailing-stop reference points [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].

## Key claims

- Once price fills an FVG, an ITL is created — once price starts rallying it should not come back below the ITL [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
- ICT teaches internalizing rebalances as intermediate-term highs (ITHs) and intermediate-term lows (ITLs) [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
- If the trader has a bearish bias, they must anticipate intermediate-term highs as failures — bearish order blocks [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
- If the move has been bearish, up-closed candles should not be breached or broken through; they will act as resistance [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
- When an imbalance gets rebalanced, the high that forms during that rebalancing should not be violated if the trader is bearish [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
- All minor lower-timeframe swings are subordinate to daily candle structure, so ITHs/ITLs derive their authority from the daily timeframe [[sources/pdf-3a3f6480ad77]].
