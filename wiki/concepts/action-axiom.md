# Action Axiom

## Summary

The foundational starting point of Austrian economic theory as presented by Per Bylund: the irreducible fact that humans act purposefully — choosing means to attain ends, ranking alternatives, and bearing tradeoffs — from which all economic theorems are logically derived; Bylund devotes Chapter 2 of his primer to "The Starting Point," "Unpacking Human Action," and "The Genius of the Action Axiom" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].

## Key claims

- Bylund presents the action axiom as the "starting point" of economic theory, structuring Chapter 2 ("Economic Theory") around three sections: "The Starting Point," "Unpacking Human Action," and "The Genius of the Action Axiom" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- The economy is "about human actions and interactions. In fact, the economy is people acting and interacting. It is little or nothing else" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- "Our means are limited but our wants are not. We must figure out how to make as much as possible with the little we have" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- "Every choice we make and every action we take means that we forego what we did not choose" — every action involves a tradeoff [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- "By choosing one thing over another, by acting, we rank things' value to us — we economize. The economy is all of us economizing" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
