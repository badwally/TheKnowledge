# Timeframe-to-Trader Mapping (ICT)

## Summary

The ICT mentorship's framing that each chart timeframe maps to a specific trader-style: Monthly charts to position trading, Weekly charts to swing trading, Daily charts to short-term trading, and 4-hour or lower charts to day trading — with a complementary mapping of trader "models" (Trend, Swing, Short-Term, Day) onto the directional context defined by those higher-timeframe charts [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].

## Key claims

- The mentorship maps timeframes to trader styles: Monthly Charts for Position Trading, Weekly Charts for Swing Trading, Daily Charts for Short-Term Trading, and 4-Hour or less for Day Trading [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
- A Trend Trader trades in the direction of the Monthly and Weekly charts; a Swing Trader trades intermediate-term price action; a Short-Term Trader trades the weekly range for 1–5 days in duration; a Day Trader performs intraday swing trading [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
- Monthly-chart price swings are the biggest, take "lots of time to form," and unfold over many months; trading in this direction can yield low risk and high reward — several hundreds of pips over a long time period [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
- Weekly-chart moves unfold over many weeks and similarly yield several hundred pips of low-risk / high-reward setups over an intermediate-term time period [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
- Daily-chart moves are short-term price action; they take little time to unfold, trades last for 1–3 weeks, and swings can be 50–300 pips over a shorter time period [[sources/pdf-57b1d100694e]].
