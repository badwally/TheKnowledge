# California HOA 85% Residential Test (R&TC §23701t)

## Summary

The California 85% residential test under R&TC Section 23701t requires that the property owned and managed by a tax-exempt homeowners' association be at least 85 percent residential [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]]. The test is defined by reference to IRC Section 528 and Federal Treasury Regulations Section 1.528, the federal provisions on which §23701t is patterned, and is enumerated as one of the six operational-test conditions an HOA must satisfy to maintain California tax-exempt status [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].

## Key claims

- The property must be at least 85 percent residential to qualify under §23701t [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- The 85% residential test is explicitly defined by reference to IRC Section 528 and Federal Treasury Regulations Section 1.528 [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- The 85% residential test applies uniformly across all eligible §23701t organization types (condominium management associations, residential real estate management associations, cooperative housing corporations, time-share associations) [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- The test is enumerated as one of the six operational-test conditions under §23701t (alongside the 60% source-of-income test, 90% expenditure test, no-private-inurement rule, trust-fund requirement on unused dues, and minimum two-unit-different-owners membership) [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- The test is a continuing operational requirement, not a one-time qualification — failure to maintain it places the HOA's tax-exempt status at risk [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
