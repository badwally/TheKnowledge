# Dollar-topped debate

## Summary

The late-2022 FX-market question of whether the US dollar has peaked, with ING arguing that a Fed dovish turn is a necessary but not sufficient condition for a sustained dollar decline — investment destinations in Europe and Asia must also be attractive enough to pull funds out of dollar deposits yielding 4%+ [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].

## Key claims

- Softer US inflation data and some hints of softer Covid policy in China combined to knock the dollar some 8% off its late-September 2022 highs [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Those arguing for continued dollar decline are wholly focused on the Fed story and the extension of a Fed pivot into a full-blown easing cycle [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- ING agrees a dovish Fed turn — one that finally sees short-dated US yields start to fall — is a "necessary condition" for a drop in the dollar [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- The "sufficient condition" requires investment destinations in Europe and Asia being attractive enough to pull funds out of dollar deposits yielding 4%+ [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- ING remains sceptical that EUR/USD will be able to sustain gains above the 1.05 level in 2023 [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Sterling recovered after November 2022's fiscal U-turn, which ING reads as "a sign that policy credibility has a big role to play in FX markets" [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Japanese policymakers will be looking back at "incredibly effective FX intervention" to sell USD/JPY in September and October 2022 [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
