# Wealth Creation Distribution

## Summary

Stylized fact about long-horizon U.S. equity returns — wealth creation, defined as total shareholder return in excess of U.S. Treasury bills, is highly concentrated in a small number of stocks. Bessembinder's calculation, cited by Mauboussin and Callahan (2024), establishes that the all-time U.S. wealth-creation list overlaps heavily with the historical year-end top-3 by market capitalization, providing a long-run rationale for why equity-market concentration is the rule rather than an aberration [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].

## Key claims

- Total wealth creation for the stocks of all U.S. public companies from 1926 to 2022 was $55.1 trillion, with wealth creation defined as a return in excess of U.S. Treasury bills (Bessembinder calculation) [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Just 17 U.S. companies have appeared among the top 3 by year-end market capitalization at any point from 1950 to 2023, drawn from a population of about 28,000 stocks listed at any time over that period [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Only 11 of those 17 stocks held a top-3 spot for more than two years; 4 fleetingly reached number-one during a year but failed to hold the top position at year's end [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Companies appearing most frequently in a top-3 spot include ExxonMobil (47 years), AT&T (39), IBM (28), General Electric (23), and Microsoft (22); the average number of appearances is 13 and the median is 5 [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- 12 of those 17 historical top-3 companies also appear on Bessembinder's top-20 wealth-creator list, and all but Eastman Kodak are in the top 35 — establishing strong overlap between the rare "largest by market cap" set and the rare "largest by long-horizon wealth creation" set [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Nvidia — beneficiary of AI infrastructure spending — joined the top 3 in early 2024 [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
