# Florida Condominium Officer and Director Fiduciary Duties

## Summary

Florida Statute §718.111(1)(a) and (1)(d) together impose a two-layer fiduciary regime on the officers and directors of a Florida condominium association: a baseline fiduciary relationship to the unit owners, and a duty-of-care standard imported from Florida's general non-profit corporation law (§617.0830). Breach of duty exposes the individual to monetary damages under §617.0834 for criminal violations, improper personal benefit, recklessness, bad faith, malicious purpose, or wanton/willful disregard. The statute explicitly disclaims any equivalent fiduciary relationship between a manager employed by the association and the unit owners, leaving manager-side fiduciary status as an open question of common law or contract rather than statute [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].

## Key claims

- Officers and directors of a Florida condominium association have a fiduciary relationship to the unit owners by force of §718.111(1)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The Legislature explicitly declines to provide or remove a fiduciary relationship between any manager employed by the association and the unit owners, per §718.111(1)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- §718.111(1)(d) requires that an officer, director, or agent discharge his or her duties in good faith, with the care an ordinarily prudent person in a like position would exercise under similar circumstances, and in a manner he or she reasonably believes to be in the interests of the association — importing the duty-of-care standard from §617.0830 [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- An officer, director, or agent is liable for monetary damages under §617.0834 if the breach or failure constitutes a violation of criminal law, a transaction from which the individual derived an improper personal benefit (directly or indirectly), or recklessness/bad faith/malicious purpose/wanton-and-willful disregard of human rights, safety, or property, per §718.111(1)(d) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Forgery of a ballot envelope or voting certificate used in a condominium association election is punishable under §831.01, per §718.111(1)(d) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Theft or embezzlement of condominium association funds is punishable under §812.014, per §718.111(1)(d) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Destruction of, or refusal to allow inspection or copying of, an official record of a condominium association in furtherance of any crime is punishable as tampering with physical evidence under §918.13 or as obstruction of justice under Chapter 843, per §718.111(1)(d) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- An officer or director charged by information or indictment with any of the §718.111(1)(d) covered crimes must be removed from office, with the vacancy filled under §718.112(2)(d)2, until end of suspension or end of term, whichever occurs first [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- While criminal charges are pending, the individual may not be appointed or elected as an officer or director of any association and may not access the official records of any association except pursuant to court order, per §718.111(1)(d) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- If charges are resolved without a finding of guilt, the officer or director must be reinstated for the remainder of the term, if any, per §718.111(1)(d) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
