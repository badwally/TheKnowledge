# Ontario Condominium Annual Audit

## Summary

Ontario condominium corporations are subject to annual audit requirements under the Condominium Act, 1998. The CMRAO Financial Management Practice Guide assigns distinct roles to the manager, the board, and the auditor in carrying out the audit and the appointment/resignation/removal procedures for the auditor [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].

## Key claims

- Annual audit requirements are imposed on condominium corporations under the Condominium Act, 1998 [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- The Practice Guide assigns distinct roles to the manager, the board, and the auditor in conducting the audit [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- The Practice Guide provides Appendix D, a month-by-month process for the annual audit [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- The Practice Guide provides Appendix E, "List of Common Examples of Breaches to the Condo Act Included in Auditor's Reports," identifying recurring compliance failures surfaced through the audit [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Appointment of the auditor is governed by specific sections of the Condominium Act, 1998, documented in Appendix F of the Practice Guide [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Auditor resignation is governed by specific sections of the Condominium Act, 1998, documented in Appendix G of the Practice Guide [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Auditor removal is governed by specific sections of the Condominium Act, 1998, documented in Appendix H of the Practice Guide [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- The turnover meeting — when the developer or landowner turns the building over to a new owner-elected board — must be held within 42 days of the developer no longer owning a majority of units, and is the inflection point for document handover and the corporation's first audit cycle [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
