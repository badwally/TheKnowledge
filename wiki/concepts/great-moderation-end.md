# End of the Great Moderation

## Summary

The BlackRock Investment Institute's framing in its 2023 Global Outlook that the Great Moderation — the four-decade period of largely stable economic activity and inflation — is behind us, replaced by a new regime of greater macro and market volatility shaped by supply constraints rather than demand, where central banks cannot ride to the rescue when growth slows [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].

## Key claims

- "The Great Moderation, the four-decade period of largely stable activity and inflation, is behind us" [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- The new regime involves "greater macro and market volatility" that is "playing out" as of late 2022 — not a forecast but an observed condition [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- A key feature of the new regime is that "we are in a world shaped by supply" that involves "brutal trade-offs" — central bank policy rates are not the tool to resolve production constraints, they can only influence demand [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- The volatility of the new regime stands in "sharp contrast" to the Great Moderation — repeated inflation surprises sent bond yields soaring, crushing equities and fixed income simultaneously [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- Central bankers "won't ride to the rescue when growth slows in this new regime, contrary to what investors have come to expect" — they are deliberately causing recessions by overtightening policy to rein in inflation [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- BII expects central banks to eventually back off from rate hikes as the economic damage becomes reality, but expects inflation to cool and stay persistently higher than central bank targets of 2% [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- BII's bottom line on regime identity: "What worked in the past won't work now" [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
