# Single-Leg Strength Work for Cyclists

## Summary

Single-leg strength exercises — single-leg squat, Bulgarian squat, alternate lunge, reverse lunge, and step-up — train each leg independently to build cycling power, balance, and core control while exposing and correcting the left/right imbalances created by repetitive pedalling [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].

## Key claims

- **Single-leg squat** trains glutes, hips, hamstrings, calves, and quads simultaneously and improves balance; the pistol squat (raised leg held straight in front) is the bodyweight progression [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- **Bulgarian squat** (rear foot elevated to ~knee height) develops strength, balance, and core control and is performed with the leading leg about half a metre forward, lowering until the front thigh is near horizontal [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- **Alternate lunge** targets glutes and quads to increase strength, power, and stability for pedalling — performed by stepping forward into a lunge until both knees reach ~90° before pushing back through the front heel and switching legs (one rep = one rep per leg) [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- **Reverse lunge** targets glutes, hamstrings, calves, and quads with the explicit cycling rationale of staying smooth and powerful through the complete pedal stroke [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- **Step-up** builds quad and hamstring strength, power, flexibility, and cardiovascular capacity by stepping onto an elevated platform under control, switching legs each rep [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Common form cues across all variants: do not let the front knee travel past the toes, and keep the torso upright [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Standard progressions are added weight, increased reps, and reduced rest; for explosive variants, jump-switching adds [[concepts/plyometrics-cycling|plyometric]] load [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
