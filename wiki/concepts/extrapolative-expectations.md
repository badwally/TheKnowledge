# Extrapolative expectations

## Summary

Behavioral concept invoked by Lempérière et al. (2014) to explain the persistence of the trend-following anomaly across asset classes and centuries: investors extrapolate recent price moves into the future rather than mean-reverting toward fundamental value, producing a persistent universal bias in market behavior that allows momentum to compound before fundamentalist traders eventually push back [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].

## Key claims

- The paper attributes the universal trend anomaly to "an extremely persistent, universal bias in the behaviour of investors who appear to hold 'extrapolative expectations'" [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- This bias is treated as universal — both across epochs and across asset classes — which the paper takes as evidence the anomaly is behavioral rather than institutional [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- The behavioral mechanism is paired with the trend saturation effect: extrapolators push prices in the direction of the trend until the signal becomes large enough that fundamentalist traders step in to resist [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- The paper frames trends as "the exact opposite of the mechanisms that should ensure that markets are efficient, i.e., reversion forces that drive prices back to the purported fundamental value" — placing extrapolative expectations in direct tension with the Efficient Market Hypothesis [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
