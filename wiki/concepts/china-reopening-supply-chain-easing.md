# China reopening as supply-chain easing

## Summary

The thesis that a normalisation of the Chinese economy after Covid lockdowns would significantly ease the supply-chain disruptions that drove rapid goods inflation — judged by JPM AM, on net, as a driver of *lower* global inflation in 2023 even after accounting for any commodity-demand rebound [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].

## Key claims

- China entered 2023 with widespread lockdowns still in place; low vaccination rates among the elderly and a less comprehensive hospital network than in the west left the authorities reluctant to move toward a "living with Covid" policy [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- A prolonged period of lockdown also appears untenable; JPM AM expects China to experience an acceleration in activity as pent-up demand is released, with timing uncertain [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- Normalisation of the Chinese economy could significantly ease the supply-chain disruptions that have contributed to rapidly rising goods inflation [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- A rebound in Chinese growth could also boost demand for global commodities, but the firm's assessment is that on balance this is another driver of *lower* inflation in 2023 [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- Markets have shown how sensitive investors are to any signs of a shift in approach, with policy-change timing remaining the main investor-watched variable [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
