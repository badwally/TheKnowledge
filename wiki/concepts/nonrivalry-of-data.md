# Nonrivalry of Data

## Summary

Nonrivalry of data is the economic property that a given dataset — a person's location history, medical records, or driving data — can be used by many firms simultaneously without diminishing its availability to any one of them [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]]. This property distinguishes data from rival economic goods and underlies the argument that data is most efficiently used when shared broadly rather than hoarded by the firm that collected it [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].

## Key claims

- Data is nonrival: the same underlying records (e.g. location history, medical records, driving data) can be used by many firms simultaneously without preventing any other firm from using them [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
- Nonrivalry of data produces increasing returns in production: each additional firm that uses a dataset adds output without consuming the input [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
- Increasing returns from nonrivalry imply social gains to data being used broadly across firms, even when consumers also have privacy preferences that constrain how broadly data should circulate [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
- Nonrivalry frames unilateral data hoarding by firms as a market failure: firms may rationally restrict use of data they hold (fearing creative destruction by rivals who could use it more productively), producing an allocation that is inefficient relative to the broad-use benchmark [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
- The Jones and Tonetti (2020) model in the American Economic Review is the canonical formal treatment of the welfare economics of nonrival data [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
