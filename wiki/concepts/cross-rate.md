# Cross Rate

## Summary

An exchange rate between two currencies neither of which is the U.S. dollar, derived from the two currencies' rates against the dollar; cross rates require special bid-offer rules depending on whether the constituent rates are quoted on the same terms or on different terms relative to the dollar, and exchange-rate-movement interpretation must be revisited because direction-of-move depends on the base currency [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].

## Key claims

- Cross rates are exchange rates between two non-USD currencies derived from each currency's rate against USD [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- The bid-offer for a cross rate of currencies on the same terms (e.g., both quoted as units per USD) is calculated one way [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- The bid-offer for a cross rate of currencies on different terms (one direct, one indirect against USD) is calculated using a different rule [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- Exchange-rate movement interpretation must be revisited for crosses because direction-of-move depends on which currency is being quoted as the base versus the quote [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- Reciprocal quotation terms — quoting one currency in terms of the other and vice versa — connect to the same underlying exchange but flip bid and offer sides [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
