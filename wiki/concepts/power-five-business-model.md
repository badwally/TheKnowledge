# Power Five Business Model

## Summary

The self-sustaining commercial business model identified by Garthwaite, Keener, Notowidigdo, and Ozminkowski (2020) using k-means clustering of NCAA FBS athletic departments; the five "Power Five" athletic conferences — Big Ten, Pac-12, Big 12, SEC, and ACC — form a distinct cluster where athletic department revenue is generated primarily from athletic activities (ticket sales, apparel licensing, media rights) rather than from institutional transfers from the university, making them effectively commercial enterprises generating economic rents [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].

## Key claims

- Athletic departments have two primary revenue sources: revenue-generating activities (ticket sales, apparel licensing, media rights) and institutional support (transfers from the university) [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- A standard k-means clustering algorithm applied to FBS athletic departments identifies two distinct clusters [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- One cluster consists of low-revenue schools where athletic departments primarily rely on transfers from the university [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- The second cluster consists of high-revenue schools where the vast majority of athletic department revenue is generated directly by athletic activities [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- The second cluster corresponds exactly to the set of schools in the Power Five athletic conferences [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- The Power Five conferences are the Big Ten, Pac-12, Big 12, Southeastern Conference (SEC), and the Athletic Coast Conference (ACC) [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Power Five schools have traditionally operated successful athletic programs, participated in lucrative postseason activities, and negotiated valuable media rights packages [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Power Five athletic departments operate under a largely self-sustaining business model that closely represents a commercial enterprise generating economic rents [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- This model is distinct from other FBS schools, and therefore the Power Five schools serve as the main sample for the paper's rent-sharing analysis [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- The institutional support elasticity near zero confirms that Power Five schools are largely self-contained enterprises whose increased revenues do not simply reduce university transfers [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- The paper's rent-sharing estimates cannot speak to the economics of other intercollegiate athletic programs operating under very different business models [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
