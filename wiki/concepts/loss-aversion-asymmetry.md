# Loss aversion asymmetry

## Summary

Loss aversion asymmetry is the finding — invoked by Tom Hougaard with a 250% intensity figure attributed to neurobiology — that human beings experience a financial loss substantially more intensely than a financial gain of equal magnitude, with the further consequence that the loss does not even need to be real for the response to fire [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].

## Key claims

- Neurobiology has shown we experience a financial loss 250% more intensely than a financial gain [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
- The loss does not have to be real to produce the response — it can be in the trader's mind, yet just as powerful [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
- This asymmetry produces behaviour patterns that show up, and can be exploited, in any liquid market [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
- The intensity spike of a loss, real or imagined, is an emotional cocktail that causes an otherwise intelligent person to act impulsively [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
- People will do anything to end the pain — Hougaard's image is "a crack addict looking for an axe" — and the brain short-circuits and reacts [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
- This is the mechanistic reason Hougaard's "best loser wins" framing exists: the same neurobiology that makes loss painful also reliably converts otherwise intelligent traders into the losing 95% unless they can interrupt the response [[sources/pdf-1590d4650297]].
