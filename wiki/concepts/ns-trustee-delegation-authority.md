# Nova Scotia Trustee Delegation Authority (§3F)

## Summary

§3F of the Nova Scotia Trustee Act, introduced by the 2002 c. 10 s. 45 amendment, authorizes a trustee to delegate investment responsibility to an agent — including stockbrokers, investment dealers, and investment counsel — to the degree that a prudent investor might delegate in accordance with ordinary business practice [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]]. The section sets the trustee's monitoring obligations, the agent's duty of reasonable care, and a liability safe harbor for compliant delegation, while preserving the trustee's personal-action obligation where the trust requires it and excluding mutual-fund investment from the definition of delegated authority [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].

## Key claims

- §3F(1) defines "agent" to include a stockbroker, investment dealer, investment counsel, and any other person to whom investment responsibility is delegated by a trustee [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3F(2) permits a trustee to delegate to an agent the degree of authority with respect to the investment of trust property that a prudent investor might delegate in accordance with ordinary business practice [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3F(3) requires the delegating trustee to exercise prudence in establishing the terms of the authority delegated and in monitoring the performance of the agent to ensure compliance with the terms of the delegation [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3F(4) requires the agent, in performing a delegated function, to exercise reasonable care to comply with the terms of the delegation [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3F(5) provides that a trustee who complies with §3F(3) is not liable to the beneficiaries or to the trust for the decisions or actions of the agent to whom the function was delegated [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3F(6) does not authorize a trustee to delegate authority under circumstances in which the trust requires the trustee to act personally [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
- §3F(7) provides that investment in a security issued by a mutual fund as defined in the Securities Act or in a similar investment is not a delegation of authority with respect to the investment of trust property [[sources/web-2010-04-07-c59]].
