# 50 Day EMA as Intermediate-Trend Reference

## Summary

The 50 day exponential moving average is treated by Scott Redler as the intermediate-trend reference and the next layer of support to consult once a stock has lost the 8 & 21 day EMAs; in his framework it is not a primary entry signal but the next checkpoint in a layered MA stack that begins at 8/21 and ends at the 200 day [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].

## Key claims

- The 50 day moving average is labeled the "intermediate trend" reference, distinct from the very short-term (8 EMA), short-term (21 EMA), and long-term (200 EMA) layers [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- If a stock, index, or ETF loses the 8 & 21 day, Redler's next reference point is the 50 day; the layered stack is explicit — short-term first, then intermediate [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- SNAP is cited as a 50 day case study in Redler's eBook [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- The 50 day's role in Redler's Portfolio Approach is part of the trend-confirmation check ("SPY trending above the 8/21/50/200 day moving averages"), but the regime exit only requires a break of the 8 & 21 day — so the 50 day acts as a confirming filter, not a trigger [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
- Traditionally, the 50 day SMA is also the intermediate-trend reference for non-Redler technicians, so the choice of 50 day as the intermediate layer is a convention Redler inherits rather than an idiosyncratic preference [[sources/pdf-a0072c763cf8]].
