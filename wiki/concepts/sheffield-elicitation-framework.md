# Sheffield Elicitation Framework (SHELF)

## Summary

The Sheffield Elicitation Framework (SHELF) is a package of documents, templates, and software for eliciting probability distributions over uncertain quantities from a group of experts, designed to support decision-making in contexts where hard empirical data are sparse.

## Key claims

- SHELF is a package of documents, templates, and software developed to carry out elicitation of probability distributions for uncertain quantities from a group of experts [[sources/web-2026-05-13-b67]].
- SHELF is positioned as a response to a shortage of training and support for those wishing to conduct elicitations: by reading and carefully following the SHELF documentation, it is intended that an untrained facilitator can carry out a competent elicitation [[sources/web-2026-05-13-b67]].
- Although SHELF is designed primarily for group elicitation in order to synthesise the range of knowledge and opinions across an expert community, it can be used with a single expert with only trivial modification [[sources/web-2026-05-13-b67]].
- SHELF was developed by Tony O'Hagan and Jeremy Oakley, originally in the School of Mathematics and Statistics at the University of Sheffield, arising out of a long-standing commitment to research and practice in elicitation [[sources/web-2026-05-13-b67]].
- The principal spur for developing SHELF was discussions in the project 'Bayesian analysis in microbial risk assessment,' led by Helen Clough at the University of Liverpool and Marc Kennedy at the Food and Environment Research Agency (Fera), with particular encouragement from Andy Hart, formerly at Fera [[sources/web-2026-05-13-b67]].
- SHELF is distributed in part as an R package on CRAN, with version 1.13.0 published in April 2026 [[sources/web-2026-05-13-b67]].
