# Special situation investing

## Summary

A value-investing posture that targets corporate events and structures other investors avoid — "value investing with a catalyst" — explicitly framed by Joel Greenblatt as a corollary to small-cap investing on the principle that you go where other people aren't, into the more inefficient areas of the market [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].

## Key claims

- Greenblatt defines the category in his first class as: "Special situations: A corollary to small cap investing. You go where other people aren't. A more inefficient area of the market. Value investing with a catalyst" [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- The class is titled "Special Situation Investing" and is taught at Columbia Business School; Greenblatt frames it as the course "that I never had and that I wish I had" [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Greenblatt's own discovery of one of Gotham's best investments came from a special situation with a complicated capital structure — a stock trading at half cash value with a good business attached, which he and one other investor on Yahoo.com identified independently [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Greenblatt distinguishes special situation investing from activist investing: he won a proxy fight and eventually made money but found it "not worth the pain," calling it his first and last foray into activism [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
