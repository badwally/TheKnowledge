# BC Strata Deposit Insurance Partial-Eligibility Compliance Principle

## Summary

The partial-eligibility compliance principle is the regulatory rule, confirmed by BC Order in Council 101/2019 (March 6, 2019), that a strata corporation in British Columbia is compliant with Strata Property Regulation §6.11 in situations where only a portion of an account is eligible for deposit insurance [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]]. The principle eliminates the prior practice — observed by some strata corporations — of splitting CRF and special-levy money across multiple accounts to ensure each balance stayed under the $100,000 CDIC insurance limit [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]]. The principle applies symmetrically across the three deposit categories addressed by §6.11: savings accounts, chequing accounts, and term-deposit/GIC instruments [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].

## Key claims

- A BC strata corporation is compliant with Regulation 6.11 in situations where only a portion of an account is eligible for deposit insurance [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The compliance principle was explicitly codified by Order in Council 101/2019 through three parallel amendments: the §1.1 definition of 'insured account,' the §6.11(a) text governing savings/chequing accounts at financial institutions outside BC, and the §6.11(b) text governing term deposits and GICs [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- All three amendments use parallel 'all or part' language to ensure the partial-eligibility principle applies uniformly across deposit categories [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The amendment took effect immediately on March 6, 2019; strata corporations did not have to wait for a transition period or further implementing regulation [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The Ministry of Housing framed the principle as a clarification rather than a substantive change — the multi-account practice that the regulation had inadvertently encouraged was 'never intended' [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The principle removes the operative administrative friction of the CDIC $100,000 ceiling for strata corporations holding CRF or special-levy balances in CDIC-member institutions, while leaving CUDIC-member credit unions (which offer unlimited deposit insurance) unaffected by the issue [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
