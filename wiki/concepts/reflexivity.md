# Reflexivity

## Summary

Reflexivity is a term borrowed from epistemology and popularized by George Soros for financial markets, describing how imperfect investor views influence the situation to which they relate through the actions of participants; Ambrus Capital broadens the term to encompass both stabilizing (negative) and destabilizing (positive) feedback loops in markets [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].

## Key claims

- Soros explains reflexivity as the principle that imperfect views can influence the situation to which they relate through the actions of the participants — e.g., if investors believe markets are efficient, that belief changes the way they invest, which in turn changes the nature of the markets [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- Ambrus Capital uses "reflexivity" as a convenient umbrella term for both negative feedback loops that stabilize markets and positive feedback loops that destabilize them, broader than Soros's original meaning [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- Reflexive markets are fragile: they appear relatively stable but can suddenly veer out of control [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
- Ambrus Capital's thesis is that U.S. equity markets have become increasingly reflexive due to several structural factors: speculative frenzy, market-microstructure changes, unprecedented stimulus, growth of option trading, dealer concentration, and the Dodd-Frank Act [[sources/pdf-04854302f962]].
