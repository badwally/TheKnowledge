# Trapped-Positions Repricing

## Summary

In ICT-style price-action analysis, trapped-positions repricing is the mechanism that links institutional swing points to smart-money intent: market participants get trapped on the wrong side based on net long and net short orders, and price is then driven against them — higher to force trapped shorts to cover at a worse price, or lower to force trapped longs to capitulate before smart money buys back — so that the institutional side can scale off its position at a net profit [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].

## Key claims

- Market participants are trapped in positions based on net long and net short orders [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- If those trapped are short, the price will be repriced higher to force them to buy back at a higher price so they can scale off their position at a net profit [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- If those trapped are long, smart money will sell, and they will want to collapse their trade by selling at a lower price, avoiding selling at the point where smart money can buy it back [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- Stop runs realize this mechanic on the upside: the pattern is already stopped out, knocking out the traders, and it is an advantage to sellers [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- The drop after a stop-run rejection lulls traders into believing they are short and gives confidence to sell — at the moment most disadvantageous to them [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- It is essential to understand where the orders are relative to institutional order flow reference points, key support, and resistance on high time frames [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
