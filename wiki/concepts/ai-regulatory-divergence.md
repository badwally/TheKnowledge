# AI Regulatory Divergence

## Summary

The widening gap between Western (EU, US) risk-based AI legislation, Chinese prescriptive AI laws, and Japan's soft-law, non-binding framework — a divergence Japanese policy makers warn could isolate Japanese AI markets if not corrected [[sources/pdf-822d5467c24e]] [[sources/pdf-ai-2023-japan-s-ai]].

## Key claims

- The EU has been drafting AI legislation since around 2019 based on classifying AI risks into four categories — including human rights, health, and safety — with regulation calibrated to the degree of risk [[sources/pdf-822d5467c24e]].
- Since around 2019, the U.S. has pursued bipartisan legislative discussions focused on AI human-rights violations and national-security risks [[sources/pdf-822d5467c24e]].
- The U.S. and EU have begun cooperating to build legal systems based on shared values that are easy to interoperate [[sources/pdf-822d5467c24e]].
- China has enacted several specific laws and administrative regulations — including the Network Safety Law and Data Safety Law — that attempt to impose strict discipline on AI [[sources/pdf-822d5467c24e]].
- Japan in 2019 endorsed a non-regulatory, non-binding framework for AI to avoid stifling innovation, with policy tools formulated jointly by public and private sectors through soft law [[sources/pdf-822d5467c24e]].
- The whitepaper warns that the divergence between international regulatory directions and Japan's soft-law stance may lead to isolation of Japanese AI markets given increasing cross-border AI utilization [[sources/pdf-822d5467c24e]].
- The accelerated social implementation of GPT and other foundation models has challenged the assumptions underlying Japan's prior soft-law policy stance [[sources/pdf-822d5467c24e]].
- Japan currently has no hard-law regulation focused specifically on AI, even as other countries actively consider it — prompting reassessment of Japan's regulatory distance from Europe and the United States [[sources/pdf-ai-2023-japan-s-ai]].
- Japan is recommended to analyze AI regulations under consideration in the EU, the United States, and China and concretely consider where legal measures, including regulations, are necessary in the AI era [[sources/pdf-ai-2023-japan-s-ai]].
- Japan plans to actively and strategically engage in international AI rule-making — including via the G7 Summit it chairs in 2023 — to cooperate on global frameworks for AI utilization [[sources/pdf-ai-2023-japan-s-ai]].
- Japan also plans to discuss interpretation of intellectual-property laws related to generative AI and to consider guidelines that prevent abusive use while supporting Japan's content industry [[sources/pdf-ai-2023-japan-s-ai]].
