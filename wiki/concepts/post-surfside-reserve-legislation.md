# Post-Surfside Reserve Legislation

## Summary

Post-Surfside reserve legislation refers to state-law changes to condominium reserve requirements enacted in response to the June 2021 collapse of Champlain Towers South in Surfside, Florida. As of September 2024, three states have made such changes — Florida, Maryland, and Tennessee — and they are flagged distinctly in CAI's national summary [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].

## Key claims

- Three states changed their reserve laws in response to the June 2021 Champlain Towers South collapse: Florida, Maryland, and Tennessee [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- CAI's September 2024 summary distinguishes these post-Surfside-amended states by italicizing their state names in the document body [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Florida's post-Surfside regime introduces the Structural Integrity Reserve Study (SIRS), requires retention of SIRS records for at least 14 years per Section 718.501(1)(c), and removes the previously-available majority-vote waiver of reserve funding effective December 31, 2024 per Section 718.112(f)(2) [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- The Florida SIRS regime requires that for items in the SIRS the budgeted reserve amount be determined by the association's most recent SIRS; where an item is not yet covered by the SIRS, the budgeted reserve is computed using a formula based on estimated remaining useful life and estimated replacement cost or deferred maintenance expense [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- The Champlain Towers South collapse occurred in June 2021 in Surfside, FL [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
