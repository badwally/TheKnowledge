# Goods vs. Services Inflation Divergence (2022)

## Summary

The empirical pattern in late 2022 in which US goods-sector inflation fell sharply after spiking during Covid while service-sector inflation continued to rise — a divergence Apollo Chief Economist Torsten Sløk identifies as the central feature of the inflation outlook because services accounts for roughly 80% of GDP and the more interest-rate-sensitive components of GDP (housing, autos, capex) are concentrated on the goods side [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].

## Key claims

- After spiking during Covid, inflation of goods has fallen sharply in recent months, while inflation in the service sector remains on the rise [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- The decline in inflation in recent months has been driven mainly by the goods sector, while prices of services have proven stickier [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- The services sector accounts for roughly 80% of GDP, which makes services-side stickiness disproportionately important for the Fed's path back to 2% [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Spending on goods is slowing because growth in both goods production and sales were very high during Covid, and the goods sector consists of the more interest-rate-sensitive components of GDP — such as housing, autos, and capital expenditures [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- During quarantine, spending on goods briefly — and violently — swapped trajectories with services spending; per Apollo's PCE chart, spending on goods is now "poised to relinquish [the lead] once again" as services normalizes [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- The divergence is the operative reason the Fed's late-2022 communication keeps focusing on service-sector wage and price pressure rather than declaring victory on falling goods inflation [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
