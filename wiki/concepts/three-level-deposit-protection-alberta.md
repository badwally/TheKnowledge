# Three-Level Deposit Protection System (Alberta)

## Summary

The Alberta *Credit Union Act* establishes a three-level statutory deposit protection system for Alberta credit union depositors: (1) credit union capital, (2) the Credit Union Deposit Guarantee Fund, and (3) the Government of Alberta as ultimate backstop [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]]. The layered structure is the statutory mechanism by which CUDGC delivers its 100% unlimited deposit guarantee, with each level activating sequentially if the prior level proves insufficient to satisfy depositor claims [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].

## Key claims

- The Alberta *Credit Union Act* establishes the three-level system [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Level one is credit union capital: each credit union is required to build and maintain adequate levels of capital, comprised primarily of retained earnings, with common and investment shares owned by members also part of capital [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Level two is the Credit Union Deposit Guarantee Fund: Alberta credit unions fund the Fund through regular assessments; the Fund is managed by CUDGC and invested in high quality, liquid investments [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- Level three is the Government of Alberta: the Province ensures that the Corporation's obligation to Alberta credit union depositors is carried out [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- The Government-of-Alberta backstop is what enables CUDGC to extend its 100% guarantee "up to any dollar amount" — an unlimited coverage standard that is otherwise unsustainable from credit-union capital and Fund assessments alone [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
- The architecture is structurally parallel to BC's CUDIC three-tier system (member capital, deposit insurance fund, provincial backstop) and contrasts with CDIC's two-tier federal model (member capital + CDIC fund, with no explicit Government-of-Canada statutory backstop) [[sources/web-2000-01-01-611]].
