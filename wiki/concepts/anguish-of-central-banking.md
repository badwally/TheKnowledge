# The Anguish of Central Banking (Burns thesis)

## Summary

Thesis advanced by former Fed chair Arthur Burns in his September 30, 1979 IMF Belgrade speech "The Anguish of Central Banking," and revived by Kai Volatility's Q2 2022 commentary as the central reason the Powell-Volcker emulation will fail: when structural inflation is continually driven by political forces, the Federal Reserve's practical capacity to curb it is "very limited," even though the Fed remains capable of stabilizing actions [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].

## Key claims

- Burns gave the speech at the IMF meeting in Belgrade on September 30, 1979, after having left his job as Fed Chair in 1978 [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- The speech "spoke to the long-since forgotten painful experiences of the prior 15 years of Fed Governors from 1964-1979" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Burns's thesis, quoted directly: "My conclusion that it is illusory to expect central banks to put an end to the inflation that now afflicts the industrial democracies does not mean that central banks are incapable of stabilizing actions. It simply means that their practical capacity for curbing an inflation that is continually driven by political forces is very limited" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Per Kai Volatility, Volcker's vanquishing-of-inflation tale "is all too often conveniently circulated by the Fed" while the stories of William McChesney Martin, Arthur Burns, and William Miller "are all too conveniently sullied or brushed under the rug" — a pattern Kai Volatility calls "bordering on conspiracy theory" but defensible because public faith in the Fed is itself a monetary policy tool [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Kai Volatility argues this 1979 thesis is more relevant in 2022 than ever, because after 40 years of monetary policy dominance and secularly declining interest rates from 1981-2021, "the world likely faces its first secular inflationary period in two generations" — and like 1965-1980, it is "driven primarily by the political winds of populism" rather than psychology [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
