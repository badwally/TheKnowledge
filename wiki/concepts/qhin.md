# Qualified Health Information Network (QHIN)

## Summary

A Qualified Health Information Network (QHIN) is a designated US-based network that operates as a central connection point within TEFCA, using TEFCA's technical standards to exchange electronic health information; QHINs collectively form the network-of-networks backbone of TEFCA. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]

## Key claims

- QHINs are the central connection points within TEFCA and together create the network-of-networks that enables nationwide health information exchange. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- To become a QHIN, a network must be a US entity, complete the QHIN application, onboarding, and designation process, and sign the Common Agreement. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- The QHIN application and designation process typically takes about 12 months to complete. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- The first QHINs were designated in December 2023, and electronic health data began flowing among TEFCA QHINs within days of designation. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- QHINs share data with Participants — organizations such as hospitals, health systems, public health agencies, and regional health information exchanges (HIEs) — which may themselves have Subparticipants connected through them. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- QHIN-to-QHIN exchange is governed by the QHIN Technical Framework (QTF), which covers patient identity resolution, authentication, and performance measurement; QTF requirements flow down to participants and subparticipants as they exchange data across QHIN boundaries. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
- The QHIN Governing Council surfaces market needs that inform ONC and the Recognized Coordinating Entity's expansion of TEFCA's permissible Exchange Purposes over time. [[sources/web-2026-06-09-e4c]]
