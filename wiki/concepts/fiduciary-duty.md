# Fiduciary Duty

## Summary

Fiduciary duty is the obligation of one party (a trustee or fiduciary) to act in the interests of another (the beneficiary) when exercising rights or managing assets on the beneficiary's behalf; it is the legal mechanism at the core of data trusts in the Ada Lovelace Institute / AI Council framework [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].

## Key claims

- Fiduciary responsibilities sit at the core of trust law and are traceable to practices established in Roman law [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
- In a data trust, the trustee bears a fiduciary duty when exercising data rights (or the beneficial interest in those rights) on behalf of the beneficiaries, and the overseeing court provides additional safeguards [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
- Civil-law systems contain analogous obligations resembling trust-law fiduciary mechanisms, even where the common-law trust as such does not exist; the Ada Lovelace Institute / AI Council report flags this as a basis for cross-jurisdictional translation of data-trust models [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
