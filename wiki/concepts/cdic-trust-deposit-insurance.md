# CDIC Per-Beneficiary Trust Deposit Insurance

## Summary

CDIC per-beneficiary trust deposit insurance is the mechanism by which the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation extends $100,000 of coverage to each beneficiary of a trust deposit held at a CDIC member institution, rather than limiting coverage to a single $100,000 trustee-name limit aggregated across all beneficiaries. The mechanism is conditional on (a) the existence of a valid trust under provincial or territorial law and (b) the trustee disclosing required information about itself and the beneficiaries on the records of the member institution prior to the institution's failure. The mechanism is load-bearing for Canadian condominium reserve-fund deposit insurance: when a property manager or association acts as trustee holding reserve-fund deposits on behalf of unit-owner beneficiaries, the per-beneficiary structure determines whether the full reserve balance is insurable or whether coverage caps at a single $100,000 trustee-name limit [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].

## Key claims

- CDIC protects deposits held in trust separately from deposits in other deposit insurance categories; the trust-deposit category does not consume any other CDIC coverage category at the same member institution [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- The mechanism is conditional on two requirements: a valid trust established under provincial or territorial law (formal documentation not required) and disclosure of trustee/beneficiary information on the member institution's records prior to that institution's failure [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- Where the trustee meets CDIC's disclosure requirements, CDIC aggregates deposits placed by the trustee for the same beneficiary at the failed member institution and insures the total up to $100,000 per beneficiary [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- Where no beneficiary information is disclosed on the member institution's records, CDIC only protects up to $100,000 in the trustee's name as depositor — a single limit covering the entire trust deposit regardless of the number of beneficiaries [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- CDIC relies on the latest information disclosed on the records of the Member Institution to make its insurance determination, meaning disclosure currency at the moment of institutional failure is the controlling reference point [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- Worked example (Eileen and two grandchildren): a $250,000 trust deposit for two beneficiaries with equal 50% interests, where the trustee has met disclosure requirements, is insured for $200,000 ($100,000 per beneficiary); the remaining $50,000 is uninsured [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- Worked example (Joe, two children, and personal account): a $180,000 three-year GIC held in trust for two children with $90,000 assigned to each, plus a separate $50,000 chequing account in the trustee's own name, is fully insured for the entire $230,000 — the trust deposit yields $180,000 of per-beneficiary coverage and the chequing account yields $50,000 in a distinct deposit insurance category [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
- Three trustee categories with distinct disclosure requirements: a trustee, a professional trustee, and a nominee broker; nominee brokers face specific disclosure requirements separately enumerated under CDIC's "Changes to deposit insurance affecting Nominee Brokers" guidance [[sources/web-2024-04-01-048]].
