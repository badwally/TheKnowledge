# Narrative-beta portfolio

## Summary

A narrative-beta portfolio, in Bhargava, Lou, Ozik, Sadka, and Whitmore's (2022) framework, is a long-short equity portfolio constructed by sorting individual stocks on their estimated sensitivity to a target narrative — used either to gain exposure to the narrative or to hedge it; the COVID-19 recovery portfolio is the paper's case study, returning roughly 120.74% from November 2020 to December 2021 versus a flat case-count-beta benchmark [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].

## Key claims

- The methodology constructs a long-short portfolio by buying the 25 stocks with the lowest narrative-exposure measure and shorting the 25 stocks with the highest narrative-exposure measure for the chosen narrative [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
- For the COVID-19 narrative, individual stock sensitivities are estimated against negative coverage of COVID-19, so the long leg holds stocks whose prices rise when negative COVID coverage decreases and the short leg holds stocks whose prices decrease when negative COVID coverage decreases [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
- The resulting "COVID-19 recovery portfolio" had a negative return before Pfizer publicly announced on November 9, 2020 that its COVID-19 vaccine was more than 90% effective, then earned a return of 120.74% from November 2020 to December 2021 [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
- The narrative-beta portfolio tracks the COVID recovery much better than a similar long-short portfolio sorted on stock-return sensitivity to changes in actual COVID case counts; the case-count-beta sorted long-short portfolio does not show a similar upward trend after November 2020 [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
- The construction generalizes: the paper presents narrative-beta sorting as a methodology by which investors can select narrative-sensitive assets to achieve a desired narrative exposure for any target narrative [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
