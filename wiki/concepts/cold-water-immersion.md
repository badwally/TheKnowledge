# Cold Water Immersion (CWI)

## Summary

Practice of intentionally and fully immersing the body in cold water (≤16°c) to induce a hormetic stress response; per Brass Monkey, CWI is the most-researched of the three top cold-exposure modalities (CWI, cold showers, outside skin-to-air) because heat is transferred from the body roughly four times faster through cold water than through cold air [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].

## Key claims

- "Cold," per the studies Brass Monkey references, means anywhere below 16°c; a typical ice bath is 5–10°c, and Brass Monkey's own ice baths can be set between 0–10°c [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].
- Heat is transferred four times faster through cold water than cold air, which is why CWI dominates outside skin-to-air exposure for research-supported effect size [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].
- Cold showers are a combination of water and air exposure and serve as the next-best option when a body of cold water cannot be safely accessed [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].
- CWI reliably triggers a 530% (five-fold) increase in norepinephrine and triggers the sympathetic nervous system [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].
- CWI engages the nervous, immune, endocrine, and cardiovascular systems simultaneously — and the respiratory system as well when paired with breath-work such as the Wim Hof Method [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].
- Brass Monkey's reading of the literature is that the science out there does not meaningfully support cryotherapy or cold showers in great volume; CWI is the form with the strongest evidence base [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].
- The cardiovascular system — described as 60,000 miles of tubing (2.5 times around the globe) — is controlled by smooth vascular muscles that DCE can train to function more efficiently [[sources/pdf-291109666e00]].
