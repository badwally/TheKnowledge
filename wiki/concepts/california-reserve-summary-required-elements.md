# California Reserve Summary Required Elements (Civ. Code § 5565)

## Summary

The California reserve summary required elements are the four substantive content categories — plus three threshold constraints (basis, asset scope, formatting) — that Civil Code § 5565 imposes on the reserve summary that a Davis-Stirling common interest development association must distribute to members under Civil Code § 5300(b)(2). The regime defines reserve disclosure as a cash-equivalents-only, boldface-formatted snapshot tied to the most recent § 5550 reserve study, with per-component remaining-life data, fiscal-year-end cash-reserve estimates, a funding-percentage figure, and a per-unit deficiency figure [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]]. The per-unit deficiency element is calculated by default as (required cash − accumulated cash) ÷ number of separate interests, with a variation-reflecting calculation required when assessments vary by size or type of ownership interest [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].

## Key claims

### Threshold constraints on the summary

- The reserve summary required by § 5300(b)(2) must be based on the most recent reserve review or study conducted pursuant to Civil Code § 5550 [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- The summary must be based only on assets held in cash or cash equivalents; non-cash-equivalent assets are excluded from the disclosure basis [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- The summary must be printed in boldface type [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].

### Substantive required elements

- For each major component, the summary must disclose the current estimated replacement cost, the estimated remaining life, and the estimated useful life [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- As of the end of the fiscal year for which the study is prepared, the summary must disclose the current estimate of the amount of cash reserves necessary to repair, replace, restore, or maintain the major components (the "required cash" figure) [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- As of the same fiscal-year-end, the summary must disclose the current amount of accumulated cash reserves actually set aside (the "accumulated cash" figure) [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- If applicable, the summary must disclose construction-defect compensatory-damage award or settlement proceeds and the expenditure or disposition of those funds (including direct and indirect repair costs) as separate line items under cash reserves [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- An association obligated to issue a review of its financial statement under Civil Code § 5305 may include the construction-defect-proceeds disclosure in that review instead of in the reserve summary itself [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- The summary must disclose the percentage that the accumulated-cash figure equals the required-cash figure — i.e., the funding-percentage metric [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- The summary must disclose the current deficiency in reserve funding expressed on a per-unit basis [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].

### Per-unit deficiency calculation rule

- The default per-unit deficiency formula is: (required-cash amount) minus (accumulated-cash amount), with the result divided by the number of separate interests within the association [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
- The default divisor does not apply where assessments vary by size or type of ownership interest; in that case the association must calculate the current deficiency in a manner that reflects the variation [[sources/web-2000-09-30-c39]].
