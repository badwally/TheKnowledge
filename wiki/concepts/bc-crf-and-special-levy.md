# BC Contingency Reserve Fund and Special Levy

## Summary

In British Columbia, strata corporations are statutorily authorized to maintain two distinct pools of capital that fund non-operating expenses: the Contingency Reserve Fund (CRF), funded by annual budget contributions and used for common expenses that occur less than once a year or are unexpected; and Special Levies, raised by a 3/4-vote resolution of owners for a specific purpose. The Strata Property Act requires the two pools be accounted for separately, and the same investment universe under Strata Property Regulation §6.11 applies to both [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].

## Key claims

- A strata corporation may invest both its Contingency Reserve Fund (CRF) and special levies under the Strata Property Act [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- Contributions to the CRF are determined annually as part of the budget approval process at the annual general meeting [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- CRF expenditures are generally common expenses that usually occur less than once a year or are unexpected, such as major or capital repairs and emergencies [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- A special levy is money raised by the owners for any purpose established by a 3/4-vote resolution passed by the strata corporation at an annual or special general meeting [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- The Strata Property Act requires a strata corporation to account for the money collected for a special levy separately from other money of the strata corporation [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- CRF and special-levy investments must not be pooled funds; they could be in the same savings account, but for ease of accounting and allocation of interest, the best practice is separate bank accounts [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- Section 95 of the SPA requires that any interest or income earned on the money in the CRF become part of the fund and be reported as part of the strata corporation revenue; it must not be used for any other purpose [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- Interest earned on a special levy becomes part of the special levy [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- If money collected for a special levy (including interest earned) exceeds the amount required for the resolution and no owner is entitled to receive a refund from contributions made, the strata corporation may deposit the excess in the CRF [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- If a strata corporation is paying an investment fee to manage an investment instrument, the fee must be shown as a separate expense item on the annual financial report and must be either approved in the annual budget or by a 3/4-vote of the owners at an annual or special general meeting as either part of a special levy expense or a CRF expense [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- When evaluating the appropriate term for each investment, the strata corporation should first evaluate anticipated needs for CRF expenses, potential emergencies, or special-levy expense schedules — for example, a roof replacement scheduled in eighteen months argues against locking funds for three years [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
- Embracing depreciation reports tends to increase the reserve funds being collected and held by strata corporations, which in turn generates substantial interest income that must be considered by the professionals completing the three funding models in the depreciation report [[sources/pdf-dheide-2022-microsoft-word-600]].
