# European gas storage buffer (2022)

## Summary

The combination of summer 2022 gas-tank fills (largely with US LNG replacing Russian pipeline supply) and a mild autumn left European natural-gas inventories at near-full capacity entering winter 2022/23 — the buffer JPM AM cites for its view that Europe would make it through the winter without energy rationing [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].

## Key claims

- Russia, the former supplier of 40% of Europe's gas, stopped the bulk of its supplies in summer 2022 [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- Europe filled its gas tanks over the summer of 2022, largely replacing Russian gas with liquefied natural gas from the US [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- A very mild autumn meant the EU entered the three key winter months with storage tanks that were almost full — well above both the prior-10-year average and the prior-10-year range [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- JPM AM concludes that, unless temperatures turn bitterly cold in early 2023, Europe is increasingly likely to make it through winter without resorting to energy rationing [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- The gas in storage was obtained at a very high price; governments are largely shielding consumers from the bulk of higher energy prices, and whether that fiscal support continues into spring is an open question for the public purse [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
