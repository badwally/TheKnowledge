# Endogenous Growth Model (Jones-Romer Idea-Based)

## Summary

A class of macroeconomic growth models in which the long-run growth rate is determined within the model by deliberate R&D activity rather than by exogenous technical progress; Aschenbrenner (2020) builds on the Jones (1995) version of the Romer (1990) idea-based endogenous growth model, augmenting it with a safety sector to study the dynamics of existential risk [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].

## Key claims

- Aschenbrenner (2020) uses an endogenous idea-based growth model based on the Jones (1995) version of the Romer (1990) model [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- The technology production functions follow Jones (1995): Ȧ_t = S_at^λ A_t^φ for consumption technology and Ḃ_t = S_bt^λ B_t^φ for safety technology, with parameter restrictions φ < 1 and 0 < λ ≤ 1 [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Population grows exogenously at rate n, with Ṅ_t = n N_t; positive population growth is required for sustained exponential growth in the Jones (1995) flavor of endogenous growth models because the growing scale drives the long-run growth rate [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Resource constraints split the population: workers L_ct + L_ht = L_t, scientists S_at + S_ht = S_t, and L_t + S_t = N_t [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Under rule-of-thumb allocation along a balanced growth path, the growth rates are g_A* = g_B* = λn/(1−φ) for technology and g_c* = g_h* = αλn/(1−φ) for per-capita consumption and safety output [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- The asymptotic (impatient) optimal growth path is analyzed in Section 3 of the paper, with the scale effect of existential risk shown to govern long-run dynamics [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- The paper structure follows the standard endogenous-growth tradition by treating labor as the only factor of production and abstracting from intermediate goods for concision [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
