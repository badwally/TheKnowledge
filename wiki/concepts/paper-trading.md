# Paper trading

## Summary

Simulated trading using fake money against live market data — a way for traders to practice strategy mechanics without putting capital at risk. Nikonomics treats paper trading as a recommended onboarding step for traders new to options, but argues it has hard limits because it cannot reproduce the emotional tax of real money on the line [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].

## Key claims

- Paper trading is defined as trading where investors practice buying and selling stocks without using real money — a way to test investment strategies and learn the market without risking capital [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- It "emulates the real thing" by trading live, against real prices and real market data [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Webull is highlighted as offering paper trading for options specifically — a feature Nikonomics claims no other retail brokerage offered at the time of writing [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Nikonomics recommends new options traders start with paper trading and master the basics there before moving to real money [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Hard limit of paper trading: it cannot replicate "the emotions that come with having money at stake," so Nikonomics argues it's far better for traders to switch to real money as soon as they have a solid understanding of the mechanics [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Real-money exposure is positioned as the only way to learn risk management and decisions about when to enter and exit trades — i.e., emotional discipline is treated as an irreducible part of trader formation that simulation cannot teach [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
