# Trader-Style Confluence at FVGs

## Summary

Lesson 4 of the FVG series argues that Fair Value Gaps "work" as trading areas in part because different technical-trading methodologies — EMA systems, support and resistance, supply and demand zones, order flow, and order-size heatmaps — tend to identify entry levels that overlap with the same FVGs and order blocks marked by ICT-style price-action analysis [[sources/pdf-2698a38c6fa5]].

## Key claims

- An FVG is described as an area where orders have been left behind / left pending, which is offered as the structural reason different trader styles converge on the same zones for entries [[sources/pdf-2698a38c6fa5]].
- EMA-system traders add to a trend at specific moving-average levels that, in practice, tend to overlap with the FVG [[sources/pdf-2698a38c6fa5]].
- Support-and-resistance traders add near support, which is where the FVG and order-block structure usually set up the entry [[sources/pdf-2698a38c6fa5]].
- Supply-and-demand traders add in their zones, which is where order blocks and FVGs provide entries [[sources/pdf-2698a38c6fa5]].
- Order-flow traders look for bullish or bearish flows at specific areas to confirm the trend, and FVGs are most likely to be the location of those flows [[sources/pdf-2698a38c6fa5]].
- Other tools — for example heatmaps that illustrate order sizes at specific levels — also overlap with FVGs and order blocks [[sources/pdf-2698a38c6fa5]].
- The author of Lesson 4 personally combines volume price analysis with an ICT foundation to add positions inside FVGs and near order blocks, with direction derived from liquidity analysis across multiple assets [[sources/pdf-2698a38c6fa5]].
