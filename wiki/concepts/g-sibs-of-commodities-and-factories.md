# G-SIBs of Commodities and Factories

## Summary

Zoltan Pozsar's framing in his August 1, 2022 "War and Interest Rates" dispatch that Russia and China together functioned as the global economy's two systemically important counterparties — Russia as a "G-SIB of Commodities" and China as a "G-SIB of Factories" — supplying the cheap inputs that were the source of deflation fears in the West and that gave central banks the license for years of QE; in his framing they were jointly the world's "main 'guarantors of macro peace'" whose departure from that role drives the current inflation regime [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].

## Key claims

- Pozsar invites the reader to "think of Russia as a 'G-SIB of Commodities' and China as a 'G-SIB of Factories' that are the world's biggest producers of commodities and consumer goods, respectively" [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Russia and China together provided two of the three pillars of the low-inflation world (cheap commodities and cheap goods) [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- By extension, Russia and China have been the main "guarantors of macro peace," providing all the cheap stuff that was the source of deflation fears in the West [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- This deflationary supply, in turn, gave central banks the license for years of money printing (QE) [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Pozsar frames the East-West relationship as a fight between "the consumer-driven West, where the level of demand has been maximized, and the production-driven East, where the level of supply has been maximized to serve the needs of the West… until East-West relations soured, and supply snapped back" [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Sees the China-Russia "special relationship" in this context as an "alliance of resources" supplying the necessities the West needs to ensure social stability at lower ends of the income distribution [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
