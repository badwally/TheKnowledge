# Deprival-Superreaction Tendency

## Summary

Munger's fourteenth psychological tendency: the asymmetry whereby a ten-dollar loss hurts more than a ten-dollar gain helps, extended to include not only loss of a possessed reward but also the loss of an almost-possessed reward — a tendency that drives gambling compulsion, auction overbidding, sunk-cost escalation, and the irrational reluctance to sell appreciated assets [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].

## Key claims

- Loss aversion is asymmetric: "The quantity of man's pleasure from a ten-dollar gain does not exactly match the quantity of his displeasure from a ten-dollar loss. That is, the loss seems to hurt much more than the gain seems to help" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- The tendency encompasses both loss of the possessed and loss of the almost-possessed — a near-miss triggers reactions almost as intense as actual loss [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- In gambling: the tendency causes a passion to get even after a loss, growing with the loss; slot machines exploit this by producing many near-misses (bar-bar-lemon results) that trigger the tendency [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- In auctions: Social-Proof Tendency convinces a bidder the last price was reasonable, then Deprival-Superreaction prompts him to top the last bid; Munger recommends the "simple Buffett practice: Don't go to such auctions" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- In business: the tendency causes men to gradually use up all good assets in a fruitless attempt to rescue a failing venture — "one of the best antidotes to this folly is good poker skill learned young" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- In investing: Munger recounts declining to buy 1,500 additional shares of Belridge Oil at $115/share (which later sold to Shell at ~$3,700/share) because it would have required selling something or borrowing $173,000 — costing him approximately $5.4 million and demonstrating that "psychological ignorance can be very expensive" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- In stockholding: many Berkshire shareholders never sell or give away a single share after immense gains, driven primarily by Deprival-Superreaction Tendency — they "simply can't stand the idea of having their Berkshire Hathaway holdings smaller," fearing they will miss future gains [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- In ideology: the tendency protects ideological and religious views by triggering hatred toward vocal nonbelievers, especially former believers (perceived as betrayers), reinforcing groupthink; the combination of Inconsistency-Avoidance plus Deprival-Superreaction accounts for centuries of heresy-motivated killings [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- In labor relations: most pre-WWI labor strife deaths occurred when employers tried to reduce wages, and modern companies sometimes die because competition requires takeaways that labor will not consent to [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- A man with $10 million in his brokerage account will often be extremely irritated by the accidental loss of $100 from $300 in his wallet — misframing by comparing what is near rather than what really matters [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
