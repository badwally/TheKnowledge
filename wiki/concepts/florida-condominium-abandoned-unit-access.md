# Florida Condominium Abandoned-Unit Access

## Summary

Florida Statute §718.111(5)(b) grants a Florida condominium association the authority — at the sole discretion of the board and regardless of declaration silence — to enter an abandoned unit for inspection, repair, mold remediation, utility restoration, and preservation. The statute defines two presumption thresholds for abandonment (foreclosure-plus-4-weeks-vacancy or 2-months-vacancy-plus-unreachable-owner), imposes a 2-day notice requirement (except in emergencies), and makes any expenses incurred chargeable to the unit owner and enforceable as an assessment under §718.116 with full lien authority. The association may additionally petition for a court-appointed receiver to lease out the unit and offset the association's costs against rental income [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].

## Key claims

- The board, in its sole discretion, may enter an abandoned unit to inspect the unit and adjoining common elements, make repairs as needed, repair the unit if mold or deterioration is present, turn on utilities, or otherwise maintain, preserve, or protect the unit and adjoining common elements, per §718.111(5)(b)1 [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The board's abandoned-unit-access power applies regardless of whether authority is provided in the declaration or other recorded condominium documents, per §718.111(5)(b)1 [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- A unit is presumed abandoned if it is the subject of a foreclosure action and no tenant appears to have resided in the unit for at least 4 continuous weeks without prior written notice to the association, per §718.111(5)(b)1.a [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- A unit is also presumed abandoned if no tenant appears to have resided in the unit for 2 consecutive months without prior written notice and the association is unable to contact the owner or determine the owner's whereabouts after reasonable inquiry, per §718.111(5)(b)1.b [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Except in the case of an emergency, an association may not enter an abandoned unit until 2 days after notice of the association's intent to enter has been mailed or hand-delivered to the owner at the address reflected in the association's records, per §718.111(5)(b)2 [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Notice of intent to enter may be given by electronic transmission to unit owners who previously consented to receive notice by electronic transmission, per §718.111(5)(b)2 [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Any expense incurred by an association pursuant to the abandoned-unit-access provision is chargeable to the unit owner and enforceable as an assessment pursuant to §718.116, with the association's §718.116 lien authority available for collection, per §718.111(5)(b)3 [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The association may petition a court of competent jurisdiction to appoint a receiver to lease out the abandoned unit for the benefit of the association, offsetting against rental income the association's costs of maintaining/preserving/protecting the unit and adjoining common elements, including receivership costs, unpaid assessments, interest, administrative late fees, costs, and reasonable attorney fees, per §718.111(5)(b)4 [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
