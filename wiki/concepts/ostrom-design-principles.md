# Ostrom's Eight Design Principles for a Successfully Managed Commons

## Summary

Ostrom's eight design principles are a set of institutional-design rules identified by Elinor Ostrom that characterize successfully managed common-pool-resource (CPR) institutions; they were derived from a comparative study of long-lived commoning communities and published in her 1990 book *Governing the Commons: The Evolution of Institutions for Collective Action* [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]]. In the data-collectives domain, the principles function as the empirical baseline against which any proposed governance arrangement for pooled data must be evaluated.

## Key claims

- The principles were derived from a comparative review of long-lived commoning institutions, including irrigation communities in the Philippines, village commons in Japan, and the common pasture of the Swiss village of Torbel [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Principle 1: Clearly defined boundaries [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Principle 2: Regulations made by the commoners that correspond to the needs and conditions of the community and their environment [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Principle 3: An established system for decision-making that allows individuals affected by the regulations to change the regulations [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Principle 4: Monitors drawn from, or accountable to, the community of commoners who actively ensure the rules established by the commoners are being adhered to [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Principle 5: Graduated sanctions for members who violate regulations, determined by members of the community or by individuals accountable to the community [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Principle 6: Conflict-resolution mechanisms that are low-cost and easily accessible for members of the commons [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Principle 7: The ability to create regulations without the infringement of an outside authority [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Principle 8: Decentralized decision-making in the case of larger commons [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- At the time of the 1990 book, the youngest CPR institution Ostrom studied was over 100 years old and the oldest was over 1,000 years old, indicating that the principles describe empirically durable arrangements rather than theoretical constructs [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
