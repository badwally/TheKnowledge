# Top-down analysis

## Summary

Top-down analysis is the multi-timeframe trading workflow in which the higher timeframe sets bias and key levels and the lower timeframe is used to refine entries; ICT Month 2 prescribes it as the structural way to lower per-trade risk while preserving the higher-timeframe edge [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].

## Key claims

- The higher timeframe has more influence on price and is therefore the focus for setup framing; the lower timeframe is used to refine the trade [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- Higher-timeframe levels are transposed onto the lower timeframe so that refinement allows smaller stop placement [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- Banks analyze daily, weekly, and monthly charts; trades worth taking are ideally on the higher timeframe and aligned with institutional order flow [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- ICT Month 2 organizes analysis into three perspectives — Big Picture (macro, interest rates, intermarket, seasonal), Intermediate (top-down analysis, COT data, sentiment), and Short-Term (correlation, time & price, IPDA) — with top-down analysis sitting in the intermediate layer [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- The Month 2 entry recipe operationalizes top-down analysis by focusing on the daily chart to locate an orderblock, then refining the entry on lower timeframes [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
