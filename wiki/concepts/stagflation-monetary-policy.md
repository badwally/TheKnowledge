# Monetary Policy in a Stagflationary Environment

## Summary

The argument — central to the July 13, 2022 Q3-2022 IACFM presentation — that stagflation is fundamentally different from a typical recession and therefore requires restrictive rather than accommodative monetary policy: in a typical recession with low nominal growth and low inflation, easing stimulates demand; in a stagflationary environment with low real growth but high nominal growth, policymakers must adopt restrictive policy to reduce inflation, even if real GDP growth is negative [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].

## Key claims

- A stagflationary environment is fundamentally different from a typical recession and therefore requires a different monetary policy response [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- In a typical recession with low nominal growth and low inflation, policymakers can ease monetary policy to stimulate demand — examples cited include the easing cycles in the early 1990s, early 2000s, and 2008–2009 recessions [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- In a stagflationary environment with low real growth but high nominal growth, policymakers need to adopt restrictive monetary policy to reduce inflation [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Even if real GDP growth is negative, inflation can remain persistently high due to continuing supply-demand imbalances, which are exacerbated by easy monetary policy [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Q1 2022 fit the stagflationary template: nominal GDP growth of 6.6% annualized alongside negative real GDP growth of (1.6%) annualized and an implicit price deflator rising at 8.2% [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Q1 2022 real GDP growth was negatively impacted by approximately 320bps due to a historically large net export deficit; excluding the impact of net exports, Q1 2022 GDP would have grown by 1.6% on a QoQ annualized basis [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Inflationary pressures in the 1970s — as in the 2022 environment — were driven by energy price shocks resulting from geopolitical uncertainty, government budget deficits, and the growing bargaining power of labor [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Prematurely easing monetary policy in a stagflationary environment when real GDP growth slows with inflation remaining high "has been a serious policy mistake" per the IACFM presentation [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
