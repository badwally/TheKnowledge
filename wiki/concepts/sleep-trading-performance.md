# Sleep as Trading-Performance Lever

## Summary

Argument articulated by TraderEdge's Alex that prioritizing sleep is the single highest-leverage trader-performance intervention available — more important than any supplement, workout, pill, or drug — drawing on a one-month Whoop Band self-experiment and a set of population-level health statistics, then prescribing five science-backed sleep habits [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].

## Key claims

- TraderEdge's Alex argues that prioritizing sleep is "more important than any supplement, workout, pill, or drug" for improving trading and quality of life — and that it is free and accessible to anyone [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].
- Cognitive performance: sleep-deprived individuals have nearly a 2x higher risk of errors in tasks [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].
- Physical health: less than 7 hours of sleep increases the risk of heart disease by up to 48%, with a higher prevalence of diabetes and obesity also linked to insufficient sleep [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].
- Mortality: sleeping less than 6 hours per night is associated with a 12% greater risk of premature death [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].
- Mental health: approximately 90% of patients with depression report poor sleep quality [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].
- Immune function: individuals with less than 7 hours of sleep were almost 3 times more likely to develop a cold compared to those with 8 or more hours [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].
- The recommendation is anchored in a one-month Whoop Band self-experiment in which Alex took the time to optimize his sleep [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].
- Five science-backed sleep habits prescribed: establish a routine (consistent sleep schedule even on weekends to regulate the body's clock); create a restful environment (dark, quiet, cool bedroom — using blackout curtains, eye masks, earplugs, or white-noise machines); cut out alcohol entirely (which interferes with the sleep cycle and prevents deeper stages of sleep); limit screen time (avoid screens at least an hour before bed because blue light disrupts melatonin production); and mindful relaxation (reading, warm bath, meditation, or deep breathing to signal wind-down) [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].
