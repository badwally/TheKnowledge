# Momentum risk premium

## Summary

The excess return earned by systematically going long recent winners and short recent losers; one of the most important alternative risk premia, classified by Lempérière et al. (2014) as a market anomaly rather than a skewness risk premium because its performance is explained by behavioral theories rather than by bearing a systematic risk in bad times [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].

## Key claims

- Momentum is one of the oldest and most popular trading strategies in the investment industry, central to commodity trading advisors (CTAs) and managed futures (MFs) in the hedge fund industry, and the basic trading rules of the Turtle trading experiment held by Richard Dennis and William Eckhardt in the 1980s [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
- Trend-following is the main component of momentum strategies in practice, and trend-following investing is one of the more profitable styles, generating positive excess returns for over a century across asset classes per Lempérière et al. (2014b) and Hurst et al. (2014) [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
- Jegadeesh and Titman (1993) showed equity-market return predictability based on past returns: buying stocks that performed well over the past three to twelve months and selling stocks that performed poorly produces abnormal positive returns [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
- Subsequent academic work confirms equity momentum (Carhart 1997; Rouwenhorst 1998; Grundy and Martin 2001; Fama and French 2012) and identifies momentum across asset classes, including fixed-income and currency markets (Moskowitz et al. 2012; Asness et al. 2013) [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
- The nature of momentum strategies in commodity markets differs from equity markets because of backwardation and contango effects (Miffre and Rallis, 2007) [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
- Lempérière et al. (2014) classify carry and value as skewness risk premia (rewarded for taking systematic risk in bad times, per Ang 2014) and momentum as a market anomaly explained by behavioral theories rather than systematic risk [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
- Hedging demand from retail and institutional investors is an important factor explaining the momentum style, since the manufacturing of structured products is based on momentum strategies [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
- Grinblatt et al. (1995) found that 77% of 155 equity mutual funds analyzed between 1974 and 1984 were momentum investors [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
- The growth of factor investing has changed institutional investors' view of momentum: today many institutions build strategic asset allocation using a multi-factor portfolio exposed to size, value, momentum, low beta, and quality risk factors (Cazalet and Roncalli 2014); the framework has been extended to multi-asset classes including rates, credit, currencies, and commodities [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
- Three parameters govern the multi-asset momentum risk premium per the Amundi multivariate model: the vector of Sharpe ratios, the covariance matrix of asset returns, and the frequency matrix of the moving-average estimator [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
- Mixing concave and convex strategies is crucial for managing the skewness risk of diversified portfolios, since the correlation-diversification (Markowitz) approach is inadequate for managing a universe of traditional and alternative risk premia whose relationships are non-linear [[sources/pdf-5a6062a63a4b]].
