# Naked Restraints (Competition Law)

## Summary

A "naked restraint" on competition is an agreement between competitors — to fix prices, allocate markets, or restrict output — that is not integrated with, or implemented in furtherance of, a legitimate collaboration, strategic alliance, or joint venture [[sources/web-2024-06-27-57a]] [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]]. Under Canadian competition law, naked restraints are the subset of competitor agreements that fall within the section 45 criminal conspiracy prohibition [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].

## Key claims

- The naked-restraint concept is the doctrinal boundary the 2009 amendments to the Competition Act drew around section 45: criminal sanctions apply to naked price-fixing, market-allocation, and output-restriction agreements, while integrated collaborations are routed to civil review under section 90.1 [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Naked restraints are often referred to as "hard core" cartels [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- The naked-restraint test is the Bureau's mechanism for protecting pro-competitive joint ventures and strategic alliances from criminal exposure even when those collaborations include price, output, or territorial provisions ancillary to the alliance [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- The Bureau's Competitor Collaboration Guidelines operationalize the naked-restraint test through worked examples (e.g., price-fixing, market-allocation, output-restriction, information-sharing) showing how integration with a legitimate collaboration moves an arrangement off the criminal track [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
