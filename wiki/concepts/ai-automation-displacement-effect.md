# AI Automation Displacement vs. Productivity Effect

## Summary

The two opposing effects of extensive-margin automation in Acemoglu's (2024) task-based framework: automation always produces a positive productivity effect (reducing costs increases overall output) but simultaneously displaces workers from the tasks they previously performed; the net effect on wages is ambiguous and depends on how much the capital stock adjusts [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].

## Key claims

- The productivity effect is always positive: automation reduces costs and increases total output, which tends to raise wages [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- The displacement effect is always negative: workers are removed from the tasks AI now performs, reducing labor demand [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- When the required rate of return on capital R(K) is constant, automation always increases wages because the productivity effect dominates [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- When R(K) is increasing (fixed capital stock), the displacement effect can exceed the productivity gain, reducing wages — as shown in Acemoglu and Restrepo (2018) [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- The three proof-of-concept studies (Peng et al. 2023, Noy and Zhang 2023, Brynjolfsson et al. 2023) only measure the productivity effect because subjects in the treatment groups are always assigned the task — displacement cannot be observed in these experimental designs [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Peng et al. (2023) found 55.8% faster task completion for GitHub Copilot users in JavaScript HTTP server implementation — but this task is "very well-defined" and the gains are "less likely to be broadly applicable" [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Noy and Zhang (2023) found 40% faster completion of simple white-collar writing tasks with ChatGPT-3.5, plus an 18% quality improvement [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Brynjolfsson et al. (2023) found 14% faster customer service resolution in a real business setting — the only study using a genuine workplace deployment — but with a slight, statistically insignificant decline in quality; the top quintile of associates experienced no improvement at all [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
