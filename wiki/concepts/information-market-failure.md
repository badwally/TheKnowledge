# Information Market Failure

## Summary

"Information market failure" is the term Vincent, Prewitt and Li (2025) apply to the predicted outcome of unmediated AI-builder ↔ information-producer transactions: producer compensation falls below sustainable levels, capital concentrates with model builders, and the informational commons that AI depends on degrades [[sources/arxiv-2506.10272]].

## Key claims

- AI without producer-side market coordination is predicted to exacerbate a "large-scale information market failure" — capital concentration combined with a potential "ecological collapse" of the informational commons [[sources/arxiv-2506.10272]].
- The failure is framed as a power-concentration problem: AI-driven scale gives buyers (model builders) leverage that atomized primary producers (journalists, researchers, creatives) cannot match in one-off negotiations [[sources/arxiv-2506.10272]].
- Collective bargaining is proposed as the corrective — deliberately introduced market frictions plus aligned incentives intended to sustain producer livelihoods and the commons AI consumes [[sources/arxiv-2506.10272]].
