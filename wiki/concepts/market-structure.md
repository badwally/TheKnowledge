# Market Structure

## Summary

Market structure (MS) is the topology of swing highs and swing lows that defines whether price is in an uptrend, downtrend, or consolidation; the Lesson 3 framing is that Higher Time Frame (HTF) charts give a better representation and understanding of the trend, so MS analysis is read top-down from HTF to LTF rather than bottom-up [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].

## Key claims

- When referring to market structure (MS), Higher Time Frame (HTF) charts give a better representation and understanding of the trend [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- Market structure is read off swing highs and swing lows, which on any timeframe require a minimum of 3 candles with the middle candle creating the point [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- Uptrends create higher highs and higher lows, whereas downtrends create lower highs and lower lows — the swing-point sequence is the diagnostic for trend direction [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- A market structure shift (mss) occurs when price changes sentiment and breaks through previous price structures, invalidating the current trend and starting a new one [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- After an mss, traders use the market structure created by subsequent price action to determine what price will do next — consolidate then continue, or reverse [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
