# Condominium Bank Reconciliation as Financial Control

## Summary

Per the CAO Guide on Condo Finances, a bank reconciliation should be included for each bank account in the monthly financial package; the reconciliation compares the balance in the condominium corporation's bank account against the bank balance in its accounting records at the end of each month and details any differences between the two [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]]. The Guide frames the reconciliation as serving two purposes simultaneously — ensuring all bank transactions are recorded AND providing an early warning for potential fraudulent activity — and directs directors to specifically review the outstanding cheque section, then trace those outstanding cheques against the subsequent bank statement to confirm they actually clear [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].

## Key claims

- A bank reconciliation should be included for each bank account in the monthly financial package [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- A bank reconciliation is a document that compares the balance in a condominium corporation's bank account with the bank balance in its accounting records at the end of each month [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- The reconciliation details any differences between the two balances [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Bank reconciliation is an important process to ensure that all bank transactions are recorded [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Bank reconciliation can be an early warning for potential fraudulent activity [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Directors should review monthly bank reconciliations, and should specifically look at the outstanding cheque section [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Directors should look at the subsequent bank statement to confirm whether the outstanding cheques are actually clearing [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- If a director has not seen an outstanding cheque clear on a subsequent bank statement, an inquiry should be made [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
