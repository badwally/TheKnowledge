# General Mobilization (Pozsar)

## Summary

Zoltan Pozsar's August 1, 2022 stress test of his war-economy thesis: if the world is genuinely engaged in a major global conflict, one should expect "general mobilization" of national resources — and Pozsar argues this is exactly what is happening across Russia, France, China, and (in monetary form) the U.S., with Powell's anti-inflation effort framed as a "general mobilization" of Paul Volcker's legacy [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].

## Key claims

- Major wars involve "general mobilization," and Pozsar argues "the world is indeed mobilizing to fight wars, shortages, and the pandemic" [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Russia has passed laws requiring industries to step up their contribution to the war effort — "a general mobilization of industry" [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- In France, President Macron has called for "general mobilization" as Europe tries to adjust to life without gas — "a general mobilization to save resources" [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- China is aiming to eliminate Covid — "a general mobilization through lockdowns" [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Only the U.S. does not engage in general mobilization in the conventional sense; instead it uses diplomacy to extract more for less: more OPEC production, Russian production for less, Chinese goods without tariffs [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Powell's effort to establish his credibility as an inflation fighter is itself "a 'general mobilization' of Paul Volcker's legacy," per Pozsar [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
