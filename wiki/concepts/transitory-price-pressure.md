# Transitory price pressure

## Summary

Transitory price pressure is a price impact that arises from non-fundamental flow (such as hedging or rebalancing) and that reverts in the days following the flow, in contrast to permanent price impact from informed trading on fundamental information; Baltussen, Da, Lammers, and Martens (2020) use mean-reversion tests as the key diagnostic that distinguishes the hedging-demand explanation of market intraday momentum from an informed-trading alternative [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].

## Key claims

- Under the hedging explanation, the predictability of the last-half-hour return reflects transitory price pressure and should therefore reverse in the future; under an informed-trading explanation, predictability would reflect permanent price impact from fundamental-information arrival and should not reverse [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
- Empirically, in all four asset classes (equities, bonds, commodities, currencies), returns over the next three days are opposite in sign to those of the predicted last-half-hour return [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
- For equities, bonds, and commodities specifically, this next-three-day mean-reversion is highly statistically significant — consistent with transitory price pressure arising from hedging [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
- A second piece of supporting evidence: the predictive power of rest-of-day returns does not extend to S&P 500 futures returns beyond 4pm ET (after most options and LETFs settle), even though futures continue to trade actively at substantial volume until 4:15pm — hard to reconcile with the informed-trading channel, which would still operate during the post-close window [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
