# Active Inference

## Summary

Active inference is the extension of hierarchical predictive coding from perception to action — originally developed by Friston and colleagues — on which perception and action work together to reduce prediction error by *sculpting and selecting sensory inputs* as well as by updating internal models [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]]. Clark's 2013 BBS article identifies the action-extended formulation (Friston & Stephan 2007; Friston et al. 2009; Friston 2010; Brown et al. 2011) as the main focus for its treatment of "hierarchical prediction machine" approaches [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].

## Key claims

- The same predictive-coding strategy that explains perception is extended to action, so that perception and action become "intimately related" and jointly reduce prediction error [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].
- The unifying move offers what Clark calls "an attractive, unifying perspective on the brain's capacities for learning, inference, and the control of plasticity" [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].
