# China–US basic research competition

## Summary

The trend of Chinese basic-research investment, scientific output, and AI talent development closing on and in some metrics surpassing the United States — argued by the Stanford Emerging Technology Review 2026 to be a defining feature of the current geopolitical and innovation landscape [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].

## Key claims

- The United States still funds more basic research than China does, but China is copying the U.S. innovation playbook by investing more and more in basic research and concentrating talent in research universities [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- China's basic research investment is rising six times faster than that of the United States, and on current trends China is poised to overtake the U.S. by the end of the decade [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- In 2024, for the first time, the number of Chinese contributions surpassed those from the United States in the Nature Index, which tracks 82 of the world's premier science journals [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- A 2025 Hoover Institution study found that more than half of China's leading AI researchers behind DeepSeek's breakthrough large language model were educated and trained entirely in China [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- The world's best and brightest are no longer automatically coming to the United States to be educated and possibly stay; global talent has far more educational and training options now than it did ten or twenty years ago [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- The SETR 2026 frames reversing the downward slide of American K–12 education and recruiting and retaining the brightest minds from abroad as central to American technological competitiveness and national security [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
