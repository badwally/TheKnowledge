# Reciprocal Access (Open Banking)

## Summary

Reciprocal access is the open-banking design principle that all participating entities — incumbent banks, fintech entrants, credit unions, and any other accredited participant — are equally subject to consumer data-sharing requests, to promote competition and innovation and prevent asymmetric access [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]]. Under Canada's framework, in-scope data must be shared free of charge in a standardized machine-readable format whenever a consumer makes a request through an accredited participant [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].

## Key claims

- Reciprocal access means every participating entity is equally a data sender (when a consumer asks for their data to be shared) and a data recipient (when a consumer directs another participant to share data) [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The stated purpose of the reciprocity requirement is to promote competition and innovation in Canada's financial sector [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- In-scope data must be shared free of charge in a standardized, machine-readable format [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Accredited third-party service providers handling consent management, authentication, or data movement on behalf of a participating entity are not themselves participating entities; only entities accredited as participants can engage in consumer-driven banking on their own behalf [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Participating entities that outsource consent management, authentication management, or data movement to an accredited third-party service provider remain liable for their responsibilities under the Consumer-Driven Banking Act [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
