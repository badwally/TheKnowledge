# FDIC Deposit Insurance Limit ($250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category)

## Summary

The standard FDIC-insured deposit limit is $250,000 per depositor, per FDIC-insured bank, per ownership category [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]]. The limit applies to almost all deposit accounts and certain bank items — including savings, checking, CDs, money market deposit accounts, NOW accounts, and money orders/cashier's checks — but explicitly excludes mutual funds, bond/stock investments, annuities, life insurance, cryptocurrency, municipal securities, safe deposit boxes, and U.S. Treasury securities [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]]. The per-bank component of the limit aggregates across the same bank's branches; an HOA cannot circumvent the per-bank cap by spreading deposits across multiple branches of the same institution [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]]. The per-ownership-category component is the dimension that enables coverage-maximization strategies through IntraFi-network products such as CDARS and ICS, where funds are distributed across separate FDIC-insured member banks while preserving the single-banking-relationship benefit to the HOA [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].

## Key claims

- The standard FDIC-insured limit is $250,000 per depositor, per bank, and for every account ownership category [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- The limit applies across multiple deposit accounts at the same bank, including the same bank's branches [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Covered deposit products include savings accounts, checking accounts, time deposits (CDs), money market deposit accounts (MMDAs), Negotiable Order of Withdrawal (NOW) accounts, and money orders/cashier's checks/other official bank-issued items [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Non-covered products include mutual funds, bond investments, stock investments, annuities, life insurance policies, cryptocurrency assets, municipal securities, safe deposit boxes and their contents, and U.S. Treasury notes/bills/bonds [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- If a bank fails, the depositor can claim insured funds up to the FDIC's coverage limit; any uninsured excess is lost [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- Since the FDIC was established in 1933, no bank or savings association depositor has lost any FDIC-insured funds [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
- The per-ownership-category structure is the dimension that enables IntraFi-network coverage-maximization products (CDARS, ICS) to extend effective FDIC coverage beyond the $250,000 single-bank cap [[sources/web-2024-03-06-aca]].
