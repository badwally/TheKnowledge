# Tragedy of the Commons

## Summary

"Tragedy of the Commons" is the framework, associated with Garrett Hardin, holding that common-pool resources will inevitably be depleted by rational, self-interested actors absent privatization or centralized control [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]]. Elinor Ostrom argued that this model was too abstract and was empirically refuted by long-lived commoning institutions [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]]. The framework remains the standard foil against which CPR governance arguments — including arguments for data commons — are positioned.

## Key claims

- Hardin's model held that commons would inevitably — and tragically — be depleted by rational, self-interested actors [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
- Ostrom argued the model failed to account for real CPR institutions that had successfully regulated community access for centuries or even millennia, citing cases such as Torbel, Philippines irrigation communities, and Japanese village commons [[sources/web-2022-09-01-460]].
