# Warrant-stock diagram

## Summary

A two-dimensional plot of warrant price against the price of the associated common stock, used by Thorp and Kassouf in "Beat the Market" (1967) as the central tool for spotting overpriced and underpriced warrants and for setting up basic-system hedges (long stock, short warrant) and reverse hedges [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].

## Key claims

- Introduced in chapter 2 ("Warrants: Options on the Future") as the visual frame for "the two basic rules relating warrant prices to stock prices" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Chapter 2 also introduces "adjusted warrants and adjusted exercise price" so that warrants with different terms can be compared on a common footing in the diagram [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- The book frames the existence of stable price relationships between warrants and stocks as "the warrant-stock law: predictability in the stock market," listed as a topic of chapter 2 [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Chapter 6, "How to Use the Basic System," lists "Using the warrant-stock diagram" as a step in the workflow, sandwiched between "Picking short-sale candidates" and "Choosing the mix" between common stock and short warrant [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- The book's introduction states the authors "predict and analyze the price relationships which exist between convertible securities (warrants, convertible bonds, convertible preferreds, puts, and calls) and their common stock," using these to "forecast future price relationships and profits" without needing to predict individual security prices [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Appendix D, "The prediction of warrant prices," provides the technical foundation behind the diagram [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- The introduction also notes that "the tables and charts in the book make our strategy easier to use" while the appendices supply the technical foundation that "need not be read to successfully employ our winning method" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
