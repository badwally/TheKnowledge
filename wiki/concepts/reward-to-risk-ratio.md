# Reward-to-risk ratio

## Summary

The reward-to-risk (RR) ratio is the multiple of risk a trade is structured to capture; ICT Month 2 frames it as a substitute for win rate, arguing that trades with 3:1 RR or higher allow durable profitability at win rates well below the levels demanded by 1:1 setups [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].

## Key claims

- A primary aim of ICT trade selection is favorable RR with explicit respect for the risk side over the reward, targeting trades with 3:1 RR or higher [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- ICT Month 2 presents a table relating each RR ratio to the win rate required for profitability — the implication being that higher RR substantially lowers the win-rate threshold [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- 3:1 RR trades are described as giving the account a foundation, while 5:1 RR trades efficiently cover prior losses [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- The "Optimal Trading Goal" prescribed in Month 2 is 50% accuracy, 1% risk per trade, and 5:1 RR — generating a 20% return on equity over 10 trades [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- For initial trading on small accounts, a degraded target is recommended: 20 pips per week, 1.5% risk per trade, 1:1 RR — yielding roughly 6% compounded per month [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
- The supporting psychological frame is that losses are a cost of doing business and that risk management combined with high-probability setups produces large percentage returns even when individual trades fail [[sources/pdf-13d51286c4cc]].
