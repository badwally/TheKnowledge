# Inflation Bust

## Summary

Andrew McCaffery's term in Fidelity's 2023 Investment Outlook for the scenario in which central banks overtighten in response to high inflation and trigger a sharp recession — the headline framing "Rates overshoot risks inflation bust" used to argue against the market's prevailing soft-landing pivot expectation [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].

## Key claims

- McCaffery uses "inflation bust" as the headline framing for Fidelity's Overview chapter: "Rates overshoot risks inflation bust" [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Defines the risk as overtightening by central banks triggering a sharp recession in the course of trying to slay inflation [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues markets want to believe central banks will blink and change direction toward a soft landing, but Fidelity's view is that a hard landing remains the most likely outcome in 2023 [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Predicts sharp rallies on expected Fed action will reverse when the action doesn't materialise as the market expects [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Argues rates should eventually plateau, but if inflation remains sticky above 2 per cent rates are unlikely to reduce quickly — even if central banks take other measures to maintain liquidity and manage debt piles [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Salman Ahmed's accompanying Macro chapter argues that if the Fed stays true to its current word and doesn't stop until inflation is back near 2 per cent, "a 'standard' recession could turn into something worse" — making explicit the inflation-bust mechanism [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Salman Ahmed argues the financial system cannot take positive real rates for any material length of time (due to high levels of debt) before financial stability becomes an issue, an argument that grounds the inflation-bust risk in real-rate dynamics [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Salman Ahmed warns the Fed is too focused on backward-looking data, especially the labour market — by the time labour-market weakness shows up it may already be too late, increasing the probability of overshoot [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
