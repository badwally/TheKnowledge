# Volatility by Design

## Summary

Zoltan Pozsar's framing that the volatility and illiquidity in U.S. risk markets in May 2022 is not collateral damage but the Fed's intended policy output — a desired outcome that tightens financial conditions and slows demand, from which the Fed will not be deterred but rather emboldened [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].

## Key claims

- The volatility on traders' screens is "a volatility by design" that is a desired outcome for the Fed [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- "Volatility is the best policeman of risk assets" — quoted by Pozsar from an astute market participant [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Liquidity in Eurodollar futures is "shockingly poor" and described by one market participant as "non-existent," exacerbating moves in rates markets [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- The market does not know what to price — whether the peak for fed funds is in, or whether the peak is at 4% or 5% — and this uncertainty is feeding volatility and a sell-off in equities and crypto assets [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- From the perspective of a Fed that wants to see financial conditions tighten, the equity and crypto sell-off is "a good thing" [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Volatility is "napalm for risk assets" — the Fed will not be deterred by extreme volatility and lack of liquidity but will be emboldened by it in its singular pursuit of slower demand [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Per President Daly's screen-tape comment "WANT TO SEE MORE TIGHTENING OF FINANCIAL CONDITIONS," the Fed is not yet satisfied with the volatility-driven tightening achieved as of May 13, 2022 [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
