# Sticky vs. Flexible CPI

## Summary

A split of the Consumer Price Index basket into a "sticky-price" cut (a weighted basket of items whose prices change relatively slowly) and a "flexible" cut (a weighted basket of items whose prices change relatively frequently), maintained by the Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta; cited by Apollo in its 2023 outlook as the diagnostic showing that even as headline pressures moderated, the "sticky" components of the CPI were continuing to rise — which explains why the Fed continued to step on the brakes through year-end 2022 [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].

## Key claims

- The sticky-price CPI is described as "a weighted basket of items that change price relatively slowly," and the flexible cut is "a weighted basket of items that change price relatively frequently" [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Apollo presents Atlanta Fed flexible CPI 12-month and Atlanta Fed sticky CPI 12-month series side-by-side to show that, through October 31, 2022, the two cuts had diverged: the flexible series rolling over while the sticky series continued to rise [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- The persistence of the sticky cut is part of why "four-out-of-every-five components of the inflation basket" were still showing price growth of 5% or more at year-end 2022, broadening US inflation rather than concentrating it in a few categories [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Apollo uses the sticky cut as a justification for continued Fed tightening — "that explains why the Federal Reserve continues to step on the brakes" [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
