# Internal vs External Liquidity

## Summary

Classification of liquidity relative to a defined Price Delivery (PD) Array: internal liquidity consists of the resting orders and swing points inside the dealing range (between the defining swing high and swing low), while external liquidity consists of swing highs and lows outside the range — with internal liquidity typically taken before the directional move and external liquidity serving as the move's target [[sources/pdf-f2565310670c]].

## Key claims

- Internal liquidity is liquidity inside a PD Array — the resting orders between the swing low and swing high that define the dealing range [[sources/pdf-f2565310670c]].
- External liquidity refers to liquidity outside the defined PD Array — swing highs and lows beyond the range [[sources/pdf-f2565310670c]].
- In a Bullish PD Array, price takes internal liquidity by dropping into the discount zone (into order blocks) before beginning the move higher [[sources/pdf-f2565310670c]].
- In a Bearish PD Array, price enters the premium zone to take internal liquidity (e.g., into a bearish FVG) before displacing down and out of the range [[sources/pdf-f2565310670c]].
- Typical directional targets are the most recent swing point that defines the PD Array boundary and then older swing points outside the range (external liquidity) [[sources/pdf-f2565310670c]].
- The source illustrates internal liquidity being swept in a Bullish PD Array: every time price dropped into the discount range it attempted to push back up, eventually displacing up and away after taking internal liquidity from order blocks [[sources/pdf-f2565310670c]].
