# Production Constraints as Inflation Driver

## Summary

The BlackRock Investment Institute's supply-side argument in its 2023 Global Outlook that production constraints — not excess demand — are the primary driver of the post-2020 inflation regime, and that these constraints leave central banks with a brutal trade-off: either crush demand down to what the economy can produce without inflation (requiring a deep recession) or live with more inflation [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].

## Key claims

- "Production constraints are driving this new regime: The pandemic shift in consumer spending from services to goods caused shortages and bottlenecks. Aging populations led to worker shortages" [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- The result is that developed markets "can't produce as much as before without creating inflation pressure" — explaining why inflation is high even though economic activity is below its pre-Covid trend [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- Central bank policy rates "are not the tool to resolve production constraints; they can only influence demand in their economies" — leaving them with a brutal trade-off between engineering a deep recession to close the output gap or accepting above-target inflation [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- BII illustrates the trade-off with a chart showing that getting inflation all the way back to the 2% target would require the Fed to deal a significant blow to the economy — an estimated 2% fall in GDP between Q3 2022 and Q3 2023 [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- Three long-term trends will keep production capacity constrained and cement the new regime: (1) aging populations causing continued worker shortages in many major economies, (2) persistent geopolitical tensions rewiring globalization and supply chains, and (3) the transition to net-zero carbon emissions causing energy supply and demand mismatches [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- Some production constraints could ease as spending normalizes, but the three structural trends ensure the constraint regime persists [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- "Production constraints are fueling inflation and macro volatility. Central banks cannot solve these constraints. That leaves them raising rates and engineering recessions to fight inflation" [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
