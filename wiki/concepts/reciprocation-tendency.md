# Reciprocation Tendency

## Summary

Munger's ninth psychological tendency: the automatic, often subconscious tendency to reciprocate both favors and disfavors with extreme intensity — a tendency that facilitates commercial trade and marriage but also enables compliance practitioners to manipulate targets through small concessions, and that in its disfavor form can escalate reciprocal hostility to extremes of cruelty in wars and "road rage" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].

## Key claims

- The tendency is extreme and operates substantially at a subconscious level in both humans and many animals — "apes, monkeys, dogs, and many less cognitively gifted animals" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- An automobile salesman giving a cup of coffee is likely tricking the buyer, by small courtesy alone, into parting with an extra five hundred dollars; the effect is worse when the buyer is a purchasing agent spending someone else's money [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Sam Walton's antidote: an absolute prohibition on purchasing agents accepting any favors from vendors — not even a hot dog; Munger endorses this as "profoundly correct" given the subconscious level at which the tendency operates [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Cialdini's experiment demonstrated reciprocal concession: after requesting two years of weekly juvenile-delinquent supervision (100% rejection), a follow-up request for one zoo trip tripled acceptance from 1/6 to 1/2 [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Cialdini's theory explains the Watergate burglary: after an extreme whores-and-yacht proposal was rejected, the attorney general conceded to the smaller burglary request — a subconscious reciprocation that Munger believes contributed to a president's resignation [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- In war, the tendency to reciprocate disfavors increases hatred to levels causing extremely brutal conduct — for long stretches, no prisoners were taken; Genghis Khan insisted on corpses being hacked into pieces [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Paradoxically, minor courtesies have sometimes caused weird pauses in fighting, even in the middle of WWI trench warfare, as small favors from one side triggered favor-reciprocation from the other, much to the dismay of generals [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- The standard antidote to overactive hostility: Tom Murphy's rule — "You can always tell the man off tomorrow, if it is such a good idea" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- The tendency may be the evolutionary basis of guilt: a mental conflict between reciprocate-favor tendency in one direction and reward-superresponse tendency pushing enjoyment of 100% of some good thing in the other direction [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
