# Citizen-Directed Data Administration

## Summary

Citizen-directed data administration is a model in which individuals retain sovereignty over their personal data while a trustee institution provides storage, consent management, and brokered access on their behalf [[sources/web-2021-05-05-125]]. The data-cooperative literature operationalises this model through three user roles — personal data storers (cooperative members), queriers, and the cooperative itself — and through revocable, granular consent that contrasts with the conventional point-of-collection consent model [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].

## Key claims

- The trustee model frames the cooperative as a fiduciary for member data, with duties of management, care, loyalty, and obedience to members [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].
- Members exercise sovereignty by adding, modifying, or destroying data and data streams in a personal data store, by managing sharing at variously granular levels, by electing cooperative leadership, and by receiving benefits including financial rewards [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].
- Queriers are external parties trading for access to data — running queries and algorithmic analyses — and paying for services rendered, with their algorithms vetted to ensure member safety, consent, and absence of bias [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].
- Individuals join data cooperatives both for greater power and control over data sharing and for data altruism — the ability to consensually share personal data toward altruistic causes like pharmaceutical research [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].
- Granular control over data sharing increases member consent while simultaneously lowering querier barriers to combining data across institutions that would otherwise require separate consent agreements [[sources/web-2024-04-04-ad6]].
