# The Great Reflation

## Summary

"The Great Reflation" is Zoltan Pozsar's August 24, 2022 Credit Suisse dispatch label for the inflationary regime that, on his theory-of-trade-expectations argument, is downstream of de-globalization — the natural successor to "The Great Moderation" once Chimerica and Eurussia have both collapsed and the lowflation pillars are gone [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]]; the BlackRock Investment Institute's 2023 Global Outlook arrives at a convergent conclusion from a supply-side rather than geopolitical lens, arguing that the Great Moderation is over and persistent production constraints (aging workforces, geopolitical supply-chain rewiring, net-zero energy transition) will keep inflation structurally above central bank targets [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].

## Key claims

- Defined by the chain: "if trust drove globalization, and globalization drove 'The Great Moderation,' distrust will drive de-globalization, and de-globalization 'The Great Reflation'" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Pozsar argues that even after generating a big "L"-shaped recession to slow inflation, inflation is "still above target as we're dealing with the costs of the split of Chimerica and Eurussia" — and that "these costs include the Fed's inability to cut policy rates because the ramp-up in demand hits supply constraints immediately — inflation seems ever present; almost persistent" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Recovery path: getting from "L" to "L/" cannot come from consumption (because the old "spouses" to import from are gone) and instead must come from "investments, with help from the government" — the "war means industry" thesis [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- The macro-supply argument behind persistence: "Consumption is unlikely to generate the '/', as we don't have the old 'spouses' to import from. It follows that we will have to produce stuff on our own" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Pozsar argues forecasts of a rapid deceleration of inflation are "naively optimistic" because the TRICKs are poking holes in the Pax Americana that underwrote lowflation [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- The dispatch frames three concrete "moments of reckoning" that argue against rapid disinflation: depleted U.S. artillery stockpiles in Ukraine (annual U.S. 155mm production would last about two weeks), multi-year delays from Raytheon (Stinger replacements) and ASML (lithography), and the message of Taiwan's blockade that chip supply chains can be cut [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- BII's convergent argument: "The Great Moderation, the four-decade period of largely stable activity and inflation, is behind us" — the new regime of greater macro and market volatility is shaped by supply constraints that central banks cannot resolve [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- BII identifies the same structural persistence Pozsar does but attributes it to three production-constraint trends — aging workforces, geopolitical supply-chain rewiring, and net-zero energy transition — rather than to the collapse of specific bilateral relationships (Chimerica, Eurussia) [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- BII expects inflation to cool but stay "persistently higher than central bank targets of 2%," converging with Pozsar's view that inflation will remain above target even after central banks back off from rate hikes [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
