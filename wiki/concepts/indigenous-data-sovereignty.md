# Indigenous Data Sovereignty

## Summary

Indigenous data sovereignty is the principle that Indigenous Nations should own, control, and govern data about their communities, lands, and members; in the Canadian First Nations context, FNIGC's First Nations Data Governance Strategy operationalizes the principle through a national network of First Nations–led Regional Information Governance Centres (RIGCs) [[sources/web-2025-11-28-252]].

## Key claims

- In the FNIGC framing, First Nations data sovereignty is to be achieved through an incremental plan rather than a single legislative or technical intervention, with the explicit design constraint that "no First Nation is left behind" [[sources/web-2025-11-28-252]].
- The FNDGS treats data sovereignty as plural rather than uniform: each First Nation realizes sovereignty in alignment with its "distinct world view," not via a single national model imposed top-down [[sources/web-2025-11-28-252]].
- The operational unit of First Nations data sovereignty in this model is a regional, First Nations–led data and statistical service centre (RIGC) rather than a single national repository [[sources/web-2025-11-28-252]].
- The FNIGC vision ties data sovereignty to concrete outcomes — informing decisions, improving outcomes, and enabling collaboration on common priorities — rather than treating ownership as an end in itself [[sources/web-2025-11-28-252]].
- The strategy's legitimacy basis is collective articulation by First Nations leadership, rights holders, and data sovereignty experts through multi-year engagements, positioning sovereignty as grounded in rights-holder consultation rather than government grant [[sources/web-2025-11-28-252]].
