# Lollapalooza Effect

## Summary

Munger's twenty-fifth and culminating psychological tendency: the tendency to get extreme consequences from confluences of multiple psychological tendencies acting in favor of a particular outcome — a concept Munger argues "dominates life" yet was not treated coherently in any psychology textbook he examined [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].

## Key claims

- The lollapalooza effect accounts for the extreme result in the Milgram obedience experiment, where at least six tendencies — including Authority-Misinfluence, Social-Proof (from inactive bystanders), Inconsistency-Avoidance, and others — acted in confluence to produce behavior far more extreme than any single tendency would predict [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- It also accounts for the extreme success of destructive cults that have "stumbled through practice evolution" into bringing pressure from many psychological tendencies to bear simultaneously on conversion targets — with susceptibility varying among individuals, as Pavlov's dogs varied in ease of breakdown, but some targeted minds simply "snap into zombiedom" [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Munger uses the McDonnell Douglas airliner evacuation test as a non-laboratory case study: Reward-Superresponse Tendency (couldn't sell the plane without passing), Doubt-Avoidance Tendency (rush to decide), Authority-Misinfluence (government's directive to make the test "realistic"), Inconsistency-Avoidance (preserving the plan once decided), Social-Proof (employees seeing others not objecting), and Deprival-Superreaction (the gambler's impulse to repeat after the first test failed) combined to produce two rounds of serious injuries including one permanent paralysis [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- Munger critiques academic psychology for studying tendencies one at a time in controlled experiments while ignoring compound effects — comparing this to physics ignoring astrophysics and all compound effects [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
- The practical antidote is a checklist approach: an intelligent person with Munger's tendency list, used as a checklist, would by simply going down it diagnose the confluence immediately — as demonstrated by the Milgram case, where over a thousand papers preceded adequate understanding that a checklist user would have reached at once [[sources/pdf-munger-2021-revised-psychology-of]].
