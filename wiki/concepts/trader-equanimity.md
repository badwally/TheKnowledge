# Trader Equanimity

## Summary

The emotional posture Ari Kiev's "Trading to Win" (1998) demands of master traders: maintaining calm in the face of both loss and excessive profit, since neither despair nor euphoria — both of which can be learned — should cloud the trader's judgment, especially as improving performance means committing to bigger numbers and more complex dimensions of the game [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].

## Key claims

- Per Kiev, mastering the inevitable feeling of vulnerability and the desire to vacate the field requires an enormous ability to abandon pride and to maintain equanimity in the face of loss or excessive profit [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].
- The master trader knows — and the trader can learn — that neither despair nor euphoria should cloud one's judgment [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].
- As performance improves, the market becomes even more challenging, requiring the trader to commit to bigger numbers or more complex dimensions of the game [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].
- Willingness to do this can lead the trader to give up more old habits and to become more at one with the universe [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].
- For master traders, the monetary result is secondary to the gratification of making the right market call; they get their primary satisfaction from having an idea about a stock and implementing it in a profitable trade — a frame that supports equanimity by decoupling self-worth from P&L [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].
