# Gamma Process

## Summary

The gamma process is a stochastic process with monotonic sample paths and independent increments that has been widely adopted as a deterioration model for infrastructure assets, because its mathematical tractability and monotonic structure align with the irreversible nature of physical degradation.

## Key claims

- The gamma process has been widely employed as an infrastructure performance deterioration model owing to its appealing monotonic sample paths, independent increments, and mathematical tractability [[sources/arxiv-2508.13359]].
- The traditional gamma process has more recently been enhanced by introducing an upper bound to satisfy the practical modelling need that many infrastructure performance deterioration processes are constrained by physical or managerial limits [[sources/arxiv-2508.13359]].
- Bounded variants of the gamma process — bounded transformed gamma processes (BTGPs) and bounded nonstationary gamma processes (BNGPs) — are the principal model families used to represent constrained infrastructure deterioration in modern asset management studies [[sources/arxiv-2508.13359]].
