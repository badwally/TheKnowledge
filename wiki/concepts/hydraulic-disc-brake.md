# Hydraulic Disc Brake

## Summary

A road-bike hydraulic disc brake transmits force from a handlebar lever to the wheel via fluid in a sealed hose, pushing pistons in a caliper that clamp pads against a rotor — a design exemplified by SRAM's [[entities/sram-hrd|Hydraulic Road Disc (HRD)]] system [[sources/pdf-b51938faf250]].

## Key claims

- The brake comprises a lever, a hydraulic hose, a caliper containing pistons and pads, and a rotor, all served by a sealed fluid path [[sources/pdf-b51938faf250]].
- The fluid path must be evacuated of air and refilled with fresh fluid to function correctly, which is the purpose of the [[concepts/brake-bleed|bleed procedure]] [[sources/pdf-b51938faf250]].
- The hose connects to the lever and caliper via threaded fittings — on SRAM HRD, a compression nut combined with a [[concepts/stealth-a-majig|Stealth-a-majig]] hose barb [[sources/pdf-b51938faf250]].
- Routine service includes pad-gap reset, pad advancement, and (where applicable) pad-retraction reset on [[concepts/monoblock-caliper|Monoblock]] calipers [[sources/pdf-b51938faf250]].
- New pads must be [[concepts/brake-bed-in|bedded in]] after installation before the brake delivers normal stopping performance [[sources/pdf-b51938faf250]].
