# Macro vs Micro Elasticity Puzzle

## Summary

The observation in Gabaix and Koijen (2021) that the macro (aggregate-market) elasticity of demand for equities (~0.2) is much lower than the micro (individual-stock) elasticity (~1), and that — counterintuitively — the macro elasticity should arguably be lower than the micro because the equity index and a bond are weaker substitutes than two individual stocks [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].

## Key claims

- A large literature estimates demand elasticities for individual stocks using a variety of methodologies, with the latest "micro" demand elasticity estimates approximately equal to 1 [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- The macro elasticity should arguably be lower than the micro elasticity because, for example, Ford and General Motors are closer substitutes than the equity-market index and a bond — suggesting a macro elasticity less than 1, consistent with the estimated ~0.2 [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- A new literature exploring elasticities for U.S. "factors" such as size and value finds elasticities of around 0.2, consistent with the low macro elasticity estimate [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- The transfer of equity risk across investor sectors is small (about 0.6% of the aggregate value of the equity market per quarter for the average pair of investor sectors), implying that the demand elasticity of most investors is small — or that investors experience nearly identical demand shocks, which would be implausible since disagreement in elastic markets would generate large flows [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- In light of this existing micro and factor evidence, Gabaix and Koijen argue that their low macro elasticity estimate "may be less surprising" [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
