# Helicopter Drops of Money

## Summary

Milton Friedman's metaphor for money creation distributed directly to the public, used in Zoltan Pozsar's May 2022 dispatch to describe the pandemic-era "fiscal QE" that combined traditional asset-purchase QE with direct fiscal transfers and pushed the level of demand higher [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].

## Key claims

- Milton Friedman played with the helicopter theme in his "helicopter drops of money" notion [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Chair Ben Bernanke earned the helicopter-themed moniker "Helicopter Ben" because of the parallel between central banks and helicopters [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- During the most recent round of QE, Pozsar argues, the Fed combined QE with fiscal policy and the government implemented Friedman's notion of "helicopter drops of money" [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Asset price inflation on the back of traditional QE, and consumption growth on the back of fiscal QE (helicopter money), pushed the level of demand higher while the pandemic and geopolitics pushed the level of supply lower [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Pozsar concludes that fiscal QE was too much and traditional QE is no longer appropriate [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- In helicopter analogies it is not the vehicle but the payloads that matter, and payloads can be either money or volatility — Pozsar's central rhetorical move in re-purposing the metaphor for the May 2022 Fed [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
