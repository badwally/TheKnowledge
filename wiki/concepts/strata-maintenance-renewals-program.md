# Strata Maintenance and Renewals Program (BC)

## Summary

The Strata Maintenance and Renewals Program is the BC Housing-defined framework — published in the Maintenance Matters bulletin series — that operationalizes the Strata Property Act's repair-and-maintain obligations into a structured plan, funded program, role assignment, maturity assessment, and cyclical improvement loop, with the depreciation report acting as the 30-year financial anchor [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]]. For the condo-capital-infra engagement, it supplies the Canadian counterpart to CAI's reserve-study framing and a ready-made four-tier maturity scale for segmenting prospective strata customers in the Canada-first GTM.

## Key claims

- BC Housing distinguishes three artifacts: a Maintenance and Renewals Plan (itemizes maintenance and renewals tasks with suggested implementation timeframes, often as tables or checklists), a Maintenance and Renewals Program (commits the funding and resources required to achieve a specific set of maintenance and renewals activities), and a Depreciation Report (provides strata lot owners and prospective purchasers information about the strata corporation's buildings and shared assets, outlining the maintenance and renewals work over a 30-year period and the projected costs) [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
- The Strata Property Act requires stratas to repair and maintain the common property and assets, establish an operating fund for common expenses that usually occur either once a year or more often, and establish a contingency reserve fund for common expenses that usually occur less often than once a year or that do not usually occur [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
- BC Housing characterizes strata maintenance-and-renewals maturity along a four-tier scale. Reactive Management: no maintenance manual or inventories, no depreciation report commissioned, inadequate maintenance budget, reserve fund will not replace most asset projects without special levies, maintenance service contracts not reviewed annually, no resources dedicated to overseeing the required maintenance [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
- Managed Care tier: maintenance manual covers some building systems and has not been updated for a few years, some understanding of asset renewal requirements but no formal depreciation report, maintenance budget can only support some requirements, reserve preparation inconsistent with some projects funded through special levies, maintenance service agreements exist for regulated assets (e.g., elevators, fire safety equipment), no consistent maintenance resource [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
- Comprehensive Management tier: maintenance manual covers majority of building systems but has not been updated for a few years, depreciation report prepared by a qualified professional a few years ago, maintenance budget generally adequate, reserve fund will replace most assets without special levies and is generating good interest, maintenance service agreements exist for most assets, maintenance team requires further guidance once policy is updated [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
- Showpiece Facility tier: maintenance manual covers all building systems and is up to date, depreciation report prepared and routinely updated every three years, maintenance budget supports the maintenance plan, reserve account adequate to meet all upcoming renewal projects without any special levies and generating interest, maintenance service agreements exist for all assets, strata council providing the necessary oversight to ensure the program is on track [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
- Role assignments: the strata council performs the duties of the strata corporation (subject to applicable regulations and by-laws) and retains an ongoing role in reviewing the plan and program with the strata manager, overseeing decisions regarding alternative courses of action, selecting contractors, and other tasks; summaries of annual service agreements, maintenance schedules, and depreciation reports may be provided to each council member as part of monthly meeting agendas [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
- The strata manager's role varies with the manager's skills and resources; when an external program administrator is hired, the strata manager and/or a consultant may be required to supervise the administrator and provide background information such as construction drawings, existing service agreements, and the annual operating budget [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
- The program administrator safeguards and updates the maintenance and renewals plan, owns overall coordination and management of activities, gathers information and updates the plan periodically, develops long-term and annual task lists (the latter informed by an appropriate building envelope specialist), provides budgets and tracks costs, acts as primary liaison for the maintenance and renewals team, tracks progress by documenting completion of maintenance tasks and inspection results, reports findings and recommendations (including next-year budget and updated project schedule) to council, and maintains the central reference database [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
- Homeowners are required to provide access to their homes so maintenance personnel can carry out tasks without undue interference (with reasonable notice required), and are expected to promptly notify the council and/or program administrator of any problems observed within the confines of their strata property [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
- The strata council retains a consultant when the council, owners, and program administrator lack the expertise and time to assess certain program elements — typical triggers include the need for more comprehensive knowledge and planning, work that requires more than one trade contract, the need for technical drawings and specifications, and inspection-driven plan updates [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
- Before hiring a contractor, the strata council should review the contractor's financial situation, insurance coverage for work in a condominium environment, bonding capacity, and the appropriate experience of both the company and on-site personnel [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
- BC Housing frames the program as a Plan-Do-Check-Adjust cycle: as the program is implemented, the strata learns more about the building and its maintenance and renewals needs, the plan is refined and updated, and over time this cyclical pattern produces an efficient and reliable maintenance and renewals program [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
- The bulletin notes that owners with home warranty insurance could risk limiting coverage as a result of negligent or improper maintenance, and that the Homeowner Protection Act regulations do not specify the contents, standards, or organization of maintenance manuals — leaving the design and construction team to define them, often requiring the strata to extend the manual to ensure proper asset protection [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
- Maintenance Matters #11 (envelope) sits in a BC Housing bulletin series that includes #2 (roof) and #7 (planning for maintenance and renewals of physical components), with #11 focused specifically on building envelope — one of the engagement's six probabilistic components [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-11]].
