# Dot-Com AI Parallel

## Summary

The explicit comparison drawn by SVB and venture practitioners between the current generative AI investment cycle and the dot-com era, centered on the observation that early adopters of platform shifts are often not the market winners and that stretched valuations are part of the hypercycle dynamic; per SVB's H2 2025 "State of the Markets," generative AI is "likely the most powerful tool to come from the innovation economy in the last two decades" but "inventing a technology is not the same as commercializing it," with valuations starting to "stretch rationality" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].

## Key claims

- SVB frames generative AI as "likely the most powerful tool to come from the innovation economy in the last two decades" but immediately qualifies: "inventing a technology is not the same as commercializing it" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- "As we saw with the dot-com era, early adopters of platform shifts are often not the market winners" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- "Today's valuations are starting to stretch rationality. It is likely many companies won't achieve high returns, but the best companies could get so big they make up for the failures" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- SVB characterizes hypercycles as "part and parcel of venture" — each new invention builds on itself, failures spin out into new successes, and "the flywheel of innovation keeps turning" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
- Per Mercedes Bent (Venture Partner): "My advice is one, don't over-raise. Two, if you did, pretend you don't have that money. Send it to a bank account and forget you have it. And then, do not hire relative to the amount you raised, hire relative to the product milestones that you need to achieve in six months, 12 months, 18 months, 24 months, and really only unlock the budget as you hit the milestones" [[sources/pdf-silicon-valley-2025-state-of-the]].
