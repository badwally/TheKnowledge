# Remaining Useful Life (RUL)

## Summary

Remaining Useful Life (RUL) is the estimated time (or operational cycles) remaining before an industrial system or component reaches its end of life. Accurate RUL estimation, together with quantified uncertainty around it, is treated as a critical component of predictive maintenance strategies.

## Key claims

- Accurate estimation of the remaining useful life (RUL) of industrial systems is a critical component of predictive maintenance strategies [[sources/web-2025-11-10-fd9]].
- RUL prediction should be paired with uncertainty quantification: the Diaz-Gonzalez et al. (2025) method is presented as a data-driven RUL approach that also quantifies uncertainty, drawing inspiration from model-based particle filtering techniques [[sources/web-2025-11-10-fd9]].
- An RUL prediction problem can be reformulated as direct estimation of an end-of-life distribution from observed performance data, bypassing explicit state-space simulation [[sources/web-2025-11-10-fd9]].
- In the system-level prognostics setting, RUL predictions are validated against ground-truth system performance metrics from high-fidelity simulation datasets such as the unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) dataset used in Diaz-Gonzalez et al. (2025) [[sources/web-2025-11-10-fd9]].
