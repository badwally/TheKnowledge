# Data as Property

## Summary

"Data as property" is the contested legal-theoretic claim that data — the content of information, as distinct from its physical embodiment or any underlying copyrightable expression — should be subject to formal property rights vested in individuals, firms, or some other rights-holder [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].

## Key claims

- Materialist accounts of data commercialisation, exemplified by Ritter and Mayer (2018), argue for formalising property rights in data on the grounds that 'information in any digital or electronic medium is and always has been, physical, tangible matter' [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- In many jurisdictions, data does not have formal status as property, and in the absence of clear property rights, material possession and control of data often amount to de facto ownership (Scassa, 2018) [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Economists tend to approach privacy as a form of constrained decision-making aimed at optimising data's value as an intangible commodity, leading to proposals to strengthen individual control through formal property rights (Jones & Tonetti, 2020), reduce information asymmetries (Acquisti et al., 2016), and mitigate privacy externalities (Acemoglu et al., 2019) [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Privacy rights are intended to constrain a Lockean vision of data ownership by recognising the moral claims of individuals described in data, against the surveillance-capitalist treatment of human experience as 'free raw material for translation into behavioral data' (Zuboff, 2019) [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Data retains an ambiguous legal status as both a fungible commodity and an object of rights-claims (Daly, 2022) [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
