# Ontario Condominium Fund Account Types

## Summary

The CAO Guide on Condo Finances identifies four types of fund or net asset accounts typically presented on an Ontario condominium corporation's balance sheet: the Operating Fund, the Reserve Fund, the Contingency Fund, and the Capital Asset Fund [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]]. Each fund is reported on its own schedule within the monthly financial statement package, and the segregation principle is operationally load-bearing: combining the net Capital Asset Fund balance with operating funds would mislead owners into believing there is more cash in their surplus than reality, because the Capital Asset Fund balance is not a cash fund [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].

## Key claims

- A condominium corporation's balance sheet has three main sections: Assets, Liabilities, and Fund or Net Asset accounts [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Typical asset items include operating and reserve cash in bank accounts, investments, common expenses fees receivable, prepaid expenses, and capital assets such as a superintendent suite or guest suite [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Typical liability items include accounts payable, accrued liabilities, and mortgage payable [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- The basic accounting formula is Assets – Liabilities = Equity (Net Assets); the fund or net asset accounts theoretically represent the amount of money that would be returned to owners if the corporation liquidated all assets and repaid all debt [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Four types of fund or net asset accounts are typically presented on an Ontario condominium corporation's balance sheet: Operating Fund, Reserve Fund, Contingency Fund, and Capital Asset Fund [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- A separate Reserve Fund schedule should be included in the financial statement package, detailing contributions to the reserve fund and the expenses (major repairs and replacements) charged against it [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Directors should review reserve expenses monthly to ensure the expenses qualify as eligible reserve fund expenditures; if unsure, inquiries should be made with the manager or auditor [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- A Contingency Fund can be created by directors who want to separate some operating funds for emergencies; a separate schedule should detail any contributions or withdrawals from the contingency fund [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- A Capital Asset Fund schedule records capital assets such as a superintendent's suite, guest suite, vehicle, or other assets purchased by the corporation; any mortgage or loan against the asset is recorded as a liability on the statement of financial position, with the net amount of asset cost less corresponding liability recorded in the Capital Asset Fund [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- The Capital Asset Fund schedule details any purchases of capital assets during the year, amounts transferred into the fund to pay the mortgage or loan and the corresponding interest paid in that year, and amortization if the asset is amortized [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Capital assets are segregated in the Capital Asset Fund so that they are separated from operating funds; combining the net Capital Asset Fund balance with operating funds would mislead owners to believe there is more cash in their surplus than in reality, because the net Capital Asset Fund balance is not a cash fund [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
