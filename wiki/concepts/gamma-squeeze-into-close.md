# Gamma squeeze into close

## Summary

A specific end-of-day price-action setup in which dealer hedge unwinds at peak intraday gamma force dealers to buy back the underlying — producing self-reinforcing upside momentum into settlement and often targeting the upper call strike [[sources/pdf-ea646c451aae]].

## Key claims

- With 15–20 minutes left to the close, gamma is at its highest intraday, making dealer hedging most aggressive [[sources/pdf-ea646c451aae]].
- If a spike in call volume at the upper strike represents the closing of short calls, dealers must unwind their hedges — which were previously shorting futures to offset the calls — meaning they must buy back SPX futures and create strong upside momentum [[sources/pdf-ea646c451aae]].
- If SPX starts moving up toward the upper call strike, dealers must keep buying to maintain neutrality, which can create a fast-moving rally into the close — "often called a gamma squeeze" [[sources/pdf-ea646c451aae]].
- Most likely outcome of the setup: a rapid upward move toward the upper call strike, with momentum acceleration as dealer hedging fuels the rally and potential for a blow-off top or pinning at the upper strike into the close [[sources/pdf-ea646c451aae]].
- Key trading implication if already long: ride the move but be prepared to take profits near the strike; if flat, entering long at the breakout can work but requires tight risk management [[sources/pdf-ea646c451aae]].
