# Ontario Types of Condominium Corporations

## Summary

The Ontario Condominium Act, 1998 distinguishes between multiple types of condominium corporations — freehold (the default unit-ownership form), common elements (unit-less corporations where each owner owns a parcel of land to which a common interest in shared elements is attached), leasehold (where units and common interests are subject to leasehold interests held by owners), phased corporations, and vacant land corporations — each carrying its own definitional rules, declaration/description requirements, and downstream procedural regimes under the Act and Ontario Regulation 48/01 [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]] [[sources/web-2023-10-01-d55]].

## Key claims

- A "freehold condominium corporation" is a corporation in which all the units and their appurtenant common interests are held in fee simple by the owners [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- A "leasehold condominium corporation" is a corporation in which all the units and their appurtenant common interests are subject to leasehold interests held by the owners [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- A "common elements condominium corporation" is the corporation type described in subsection 138(2) of the Act [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- The "common interest" definition splits along the freehold/common-elements axis: for all corporations except a common elements condominium corporation, the common interest is appurtenant to a unit; for a common elements condominium corporation, it is appurtenant to the owner's parcel of land described in the declaration [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- The "lessor" of a leasehold condominium corporation is the person who owns the freehold estate in the land described in the description [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- The Table of Contents enumerates separate Parts of the Act dedicated to phased condominium corporations, common elements condominium corporations, vacant land condominium corporations, and leasehold condominium corporations — each with its own creation, declaration-content, description-content, disclosure-statement, status-certificate, and turn-over provisions, confirming that each is treated as a distinct legal-procedural regime [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- A "standard condominium corporation" is operationalized in Ontario Regulation 48/01 as a freehold condominium corporation that is not a common elements condominium corporation or a vacant land condominium corporation [[sources/web-2023-10-01-d55]].
- Ontario Regulation 48/01 classifies corporations created before Part II of the Condominium Act, 1998 came into force, and continued as corporations under subsection 178(1) of the Act, as standard condominium corporations [[sources/web-2023-10-01-d55]].
