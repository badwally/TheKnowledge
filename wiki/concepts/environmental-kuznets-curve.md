# Environmental Kuznets Curve

## Summary

The empirical pattern that environmental degradation rises with economic development at low income levels and falls at high income levels, formalized by Stokey (1998); Aschenbrenner (2020) draws an analogy from this curve to argue that existential risk may follow a similar inverted U-shape over the path of economic growth [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].

## Key claims

- Stokey (1998), as cited in Aschenbrenner (2020), demonstrates that if the marginal utility of consumption diminishes rapidly, there is an inverted U-shape relationship between economic development and pollution, often called the "environmental Kuznets curve" [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Aschenbrenner finds that the level of existential risk may follow a similar inverted U-shape under moderate scale-effect parameters, providing a formal analog of the environmental Kuznets curve for x-risk [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- A key disanalogy: Stokey (1998) considers environmental degradation that additively reduces utility, whereas existential risk imperils the survival of human civilization itself — "a quite different concern" in Aschenbrenner's words [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- The paper situates itself within the broader literature on the relationship between economic growth and environmental degradation, with Brock and Taylor (2005) cited as an overview [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
