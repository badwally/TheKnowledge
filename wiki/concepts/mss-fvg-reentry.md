# MSS-FVG Re-entry

## Summary

The MSS-FVG re-entry is the trade-execution rule in Lesson 3: after an imbalance creates a market structure shift (mss) by taking out a previous swing high or swing low, the Fair Value Gap responsible for that mss becomes the actionable area to add to a position in the direction of the new trend when price retraces back into it [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].

## Key claims

- The imbalance responsible for an mss creates a Fair Value Gap (FVG) that is the area where Lesson 3 wants traders to add with the new trend that is forming [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- When the FVG and a prior order block overlap, the setup gains additional confluence to add in the trend direction — for the long example in Lesson 3, the giant down-candle preceding the move up represents buyers loading up before the move and is expected to hold for a bullish bias [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- The trader wants price to enter the FVG and begin to move out of it; the more aggressive the move back out, the stronger the push toward the target [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- When price re-enters the FVG that produced the mss, the bias is to find the entry in the direction of the mss — for example, after a yearly sellside-liquidity sweep and an mss to the upside, retracement into the FVG should be treated as an opportunity to go long [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- After an upside mss runs major sellside liquidity, the next targets for long adds become recent swing highs, where smart-money longs take profit at obvious liquidity [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- Stops go under the order block swing low and can be moved up as price displaces away from the adds [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
- An mss occurs when a recent swing high or swing low gets taken out AFTER taking out key liquidity, with displacement creating the imbalance/FVG that becomes the add-to-trend zone [[sources/pdf-4e80c02052f4]].
