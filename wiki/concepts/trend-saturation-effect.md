# Trend saturation effect

## Summary

Empirical finding from Lempérière et al. (2014) that the predictive power of a trend-following signal saturates for large signal values: the relationship between past returns and future returns is approximately linear at small signals but flattens at large signals, which the authors attribute to fundamentalist traders stepping in when their conviction signal becomes strong enough [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].

## Key claims

- The trend predictability "in fact saturates for large values of the signal," rather than continuing to scale linearly [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- The saturation is "needed for the long term stability of markets" — without it, trend-following would generate unbounded returns and prices would not mean-revert [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- Behavioral mechanism: "fundamentalist traders do not attempt to resist 'weak trends', but step in when their own signal becomes strong enough" [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- The saturation effect is documented as a structural feature of the data alongside the long-horizon empirical anomaly itself [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
