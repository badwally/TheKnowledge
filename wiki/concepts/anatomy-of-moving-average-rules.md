# Anatomy of moving-average trading rules

## Summary

A methodology, introduced by Valeriy Zakamulin's 2016 paper, that examines how the value of every moving-average market-timing trading indicator is computed and shows that — despite appearing different at first sight — all such indicators are computed in the same general manner: as a weighted moving average of price changes, with the only real difference between rules lying in the shape of that weighting function [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].

## Key claims

- Despite being computed seemingly differently at first sight, all technical trading indicators considered in the paper are computed in the same general manner [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].
- The computation of every technical trading indicator can equivalently be interpreted as the computation of a weighted moving average of price changes [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].
- The only real difference between diverse market-timing rules coupled with various types of moving averages lies in the shape of the weighting function used to compute the moving average of price changes [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].
- Every trading rule can also be presented as a weighted average of the momentum rules computed using different averaging periods, so the momentum rule can be considered an elementary trading rule on the basis of which more elaborate rules are constructed [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].
- A one-to-one equivalence holds between a price-minus-moving-average rule and a corresponding moving-average-change-of-direction rule [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].
- The methodology offers "a new and very insightful re-interpretation of the existing market timing rules," reframing what looked like a zoo of disparate techniques as variants of a single weighting-function family [[sources/pdf-ec9fdef2193c]].
