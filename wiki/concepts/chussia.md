# Chussia

## Summary

"Chussia" is Zoltan Pozsar's term in the August 24, 2022 Credit Suisse dispatch for the post-divorce special relationship between China and Russia — "a marriage of commodities and industry, uniting the largest commodity producer (Russia) and the factory of the world (China), potentially in control of Eurasia"; Pozsar frames it as a new "heavenly match" forged from the divorces of Chimerica and Eurussia [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].

## Key claims

- Defined by Pozsar as the special relationship between China and Russia — "a marriage of commodities and industry, uniting the largest commodity producer (Russia) and the factory of the world (China), potentially in control of Eurasia" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Origin story: "one disenfranchised spouse (China from the Chimerica union) found another (Russia from the Eurussia union) to form an economic union out of revenge: one gets commodities it does not have, and the other chips and other stuff it can no longer get from the West owing to Western sanctions" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Operates as a substitute supply chain: "Russian piped natural gas was replaced with U.S. LNG for continental Europe, Taiwanese, South Korean, and Japanese chips with Chinese chips for Russia" — chip flows were re-drawn, not blocked [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Pozsar argues that "(most) chips are chips whether they were made by TSMC or SMIC, and Russian missiles can fly perfectly fine with either of them" — a soft-drink analogy meant to argue that sanctions on Western chip supply do not stop Russian missile production [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Distinguished by Pozsar from the broader "BRICS" framing — Chussia is the core "king and queen on the Eurasian chessboard," while the broader "alliance of the sanctioned" is the TRICKs (Turkey, Russia, Iran, China, North Korea) [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
