# Condominium Fund Accounting

## Summary

Condominium Fund Accounting is the application of the Canadian not-for-profit accounting principle of "Fund Accounting" to condominium corporations: once a fund is established, it may only be used for the purpose for which it was intended. In Ontario, every condominium corporation must establish at minimum an Operating Fund and a Reserve Fund [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]]. The CAO Best Practices Guide reinforces this segregation by characterizing the reserve fund as a "distinct fund maintained by the condominium corporation that can only be used for the purpose of major repair and replacement of the common elements and assets of the condominium corporation" [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]].

## Key claims

- In Ontario, condominium corporations are considered not-for-profit organizations and must follow the Canadian accounting standards for not-for-profit organizations [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Under Fund Accounting, once a fund is established, it may only be used for the purpose for which it was intended [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Every condominium corporation must establish two separate funds at minimum: an Operating Fund and a Reserve Fund [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]]; Section 115(2) of the Condominium Act, 1998 codifies this two-account requirement, and a corporation may maintain one or more accounts of each type [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]].
- Additional funds may be established as needed, such as a Contingency Fund or Capital Assets Fund; the corporation's auditor can advise on whether such additional funds may be required [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- The Operating Fund is used to cover the day-to-day costs required to operate the condominium corporation; the specific expenses the corporation has the right to incur on behalf of the owners are defined by the corporation's Declaration [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Reserve Funds may only be used for the uses defined in the Condominium Act, 1998 — specifically major repair and replacement of common elements and assets per Section 93(2), with no other use permitted per Section 95(1) [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Reserve Funds cannot be used for changes to common elements, to acquire new assets, or to repair individual units (unless those repairs relate to a Reserve Fund eligible project) [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]]; the CAO Guide reinforces that the reserve fund "cannot be used for any alterations or improvements to the condominium corporation's common elements" [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]].
- Reserve fund contributions are mandatory under Section 93(4) of the Condominium Act, 1998, which requires the corporation to collect contributions from owners as part of their common expenses fees [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]].
- The CAO Guide analogizes the reserve fund to a household savings account for emergencies and planned upkeep, but with the key distinction that contributions are mandatory rather than discretionary [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]].
- Even owners in newly-built condominium corporations that are unlikely to need major repairs for some time must still contribute to the reserve fund for eventual repair or replacement of the common elements [[sources/pdf-c974b5dbd563]].
