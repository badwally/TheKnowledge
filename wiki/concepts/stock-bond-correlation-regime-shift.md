# Stock-Bond Correlation Regime Shift

## Summary

The BlackRock Investment Institute's argument in its 2023 Global Outlook that the negative correlation between stock and bond returns — a cornerstone of portfolio construction for recent decades, in which bond prices would go up when stocks sold off — has flipped positive in the new regime, meaning stocks and bonds can both go down at the same time, and that this breakdown is structural rather than temporary [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].

## Key claims

- "The negative correlation between stock and bond returns has already flipped" — a one-year rolling correlation chart of U.S. equity and government bond returns shows the correlation turning positive after decades of negative readings [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- The flip means stocks and bonds "can both go down at the same time" — breaking the diversification assumption underlying the 60/40 portfolio and similar constructions [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- The causal mechanism: central banks are unlikely to come to the rescue with rapid rate cuts in recessions they engineered to bring down inflation, and if anything policy rates may stay higher for longer than the market expects [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- Investors will increasingly demand more compensation (term premium) for holding long-term government bonds amid high debt levels, rising supply, and higher inflation [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- Central banks are shrinking their bond holdings, Japan may stop purchases, and governments are still running deficits — meaning the private sector needs to absorb more bonds [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- "So-called bond vigilantes are back, as seen when market forces sparked a yield surge to punish profligate UK policies" — referencing the September 2022 UK gilt crisis [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- BII characterizes the underweight on long-term government bonds as their "strongest conviction in any scenario" within their tactical matrix [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
- BII still favors fixed income for income — short-term government bonds, mortgage securities, and investment-grade credit — but argues long-dated bonds will not play their traditional portfolio-diversifier role [[sources/pdf-won-2022-bii-global-outlook]].
