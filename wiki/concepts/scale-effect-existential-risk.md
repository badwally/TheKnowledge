# Scale Effect of Existential Risk

## Summary

The parameter combination in Aschenbrenner's (2020) model — captured by the elasticities ε (risk-with-consumption) and β (risk-with-safety) — that governs how proportionally growing both consumption and safety affects existential risk; the sign of (ε − β) determines whether risk falls to zero, asymptotes at a positive level, or grows without bound, and is the single most consequential modeling choice in the paper [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].

## Key claims

- The endogenous hazard rate is δ_t = δ̄ C_t^ε H_t^(−β), so risk depends on the total size of the economy rather than per-capita variables — what Aschenbrenner calls the "scale effect" [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Under rule-of-thumb allocation along a balanced growth path, the growth rate of risk is g_δ* = (ε − β)(g + n), so δ_t → 0 if ε < β, δ_t → δ* > 0 if ε = β, and δ_t → ∞ if ε > β [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- If existential risk decreases with scale (ε < β), no special concern for safety is required for risk to fall to zero exponentially and for risk to decrease with growth [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- If existential risk increases with scale moderately, the level of existential risk may follow an inverted U-shape — the time-of-perils case [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Modeling existential risk as depending on total instead of per-capita variables is what allows for a scale effect; previous work by Martin and Pindyck (2015, 2019) and Aurland-Bredesen (2019) imposed a knife-edge zero-scale-effect assumption by positing a fixed set of catastrophes averted by a constant tax on consumption [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Aschenbrenner argues this knife-edge assumption is unsatisfactory: holding safety spending constant as a fraction of output only holds risk constant when the scale effect is exactly zero or when population doesn't grow [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Concrete justifications for total-not-per-capita scale dependence: catastrophic climate-change risk depends on total emissions, pandemic risk on total opportunities for zoonosis, nuclear-winter risk on total nuclear weapons, and WMD-terrorism risk on what the most dangerous person can do — not the average person [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Existential-risk mitigation similarly depends on total spending on carbon-emissions abatement, biosecurity, AI safety, etc., paralleling the way technological development depends on scale in endogenous idea-based growth models [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
