# NJ Zero-Dollar Balance Plan Requirement

## Summary

Under S3992 (August 2025), every NJ capital reserve funding plan must include a zero-dollar balance plan — a 30-year funding projection that permits the reserve fund to reach zero dollars but never fall below it. The zero-dollar plan is the operational instantiation of the statutory adequacy definition: a plan that allows the fund to be projected below zero at any point in the 30-year window is, by definition, not adequate [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].

## Key claims

- All capital reserve funding plans must include a zero-dollar plan [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- The zero-dollar plan is a 30-year funding plan that permits the reserve fund to reach zero dollars [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- The plan is not allowed to project the reserve fund to fall below zero [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- There are no limitations on how often the plan may hit the zero-dollar mark [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Associations are not limited to one plan; additional capital funding plans may be produced that provide balances greater than zero [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Before S3992, the law required only a 30-year funding plan; the zero-dollar plan inclusion requirement was added by S3992 [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- The zero-dollar plan requirement is the operational anchor for the statutory "adequacy" definition added by the same legislation [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
