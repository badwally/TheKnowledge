# Options premium trading (no-exercise model)

## Summary

A framing of retail options trading in which the trader buys and sells option contracts purely to capture changes in the contract's premium, with no intent to ever exercise the option — Nikonomics treats this as the default mental model for retail day traders and argues that confusion in beginner options education comes from assuming exercise [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].

## Key claims

- Nikonomics defines retail options trading as "similar to buying shares of a stock": you purchase option contracts on a stock at a certain price, and if the value increases you can sell them for a profit [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Unlike normal shares, options also let you make money if the stock price goes down — by buying puts — which the source identifies as one of the key differences between options and shares [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Options allow traders to control a large amount of stock for a smaller investment, leading to potentially much larger profits compared to outright share purchase, while exposure on the buy-side is capped at the premium paid (loss has the same percentage downside as the position but a strictly bounded absolute downside — see "you can only lose what you put in" below) [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- The source is explicit: "We are solely trading the options premiums, we have no intent to 'exercise' the options" [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Nikonomics blames the prevalence of exercise-centric content for retail confusion: "Most of the information about options you find online doesn't pertain to what we're doing here. ... This is why from first glance options can be so confusing" [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- The premium itself is described as "the fee that the holder pays to purchase the option contract," determined by supply and demand and a function of underlying price, time to expiration, and volatility [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
- Risk-bounded property highlighted by the source: with options premium trading, "you can only lose what you put in" — i.e., loss is capped at the premium paid [[sources/pdf-5a5936689043]].
