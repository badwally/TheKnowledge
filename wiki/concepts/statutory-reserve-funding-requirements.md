# Statutory Reserve Funding Requirements (US)

## Summary

Statutory reserve funding requirements are state-law mandates that condominium associations actually fund their reserve accounts, as distinct from the separate question of whether a reserve study must be conducted. As of September 2024, twelve US states require reserve funding for condominium associations [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].

## Key claims

- Twelve states require reserve funding for condominium associations: Connecticut, Delaware, Florida, Hawaii, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, Nevada, Ohio, and Oregon [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- California is notable as a state that mandates reserve studies but does not statutorily require reserves to be funded [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Colorado similarly does not statutorily require reserve funding [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Florida's funding mandate is the most prescriptive: annual budgets must include reserve accounts for roof replacement, pavement, painting, and other items with a replacement cost exceeding $10,000; the amount to be reserved for each item is determined by the association's most recent Structural Integrity Reserve Study, and effective December 31, 2024 members of a unit-owner-controlled association may not vote to provide no reserves or less than the required reserves, per Section 718.112(f)(2) [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Connecticut requires that proposed condominium budgets provide adequate reserves for capital expenditures, with the basis on which reserves are calculated and funded disclosed annually to all unit owners within 30 days of adoption, per Sections 47-88e and 47-261e [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Illinois requires the board to adopt a budget providing reasonable reserves for capital expenditures and deferred maintenance; under the Condominium Act, an association without a reserve requirement in its instruments may waive the reserve requirement in whole or in part by a 2/3 vote, per 760 ILCS 605/9 [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Hawaii's funding requirement is computed by formula based on the estimated life and the estimated capital expenditure or major maintenance required for each part of the property, with separate designated reserves for each part of the property whose capital expenditure or major maintenance will exceed $10,000, per HRS §514B-148 [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Indiana requires all sums assessed by the association of co-owners to be established using generally accepted accounting principles applied on a consistent basis and to include the establishment and maintenance of a replacement reserve fund, per Section 32-25-4-4 [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Florida homeowner associations (as distinct from condominium associations) may adopt budgets that include reserve accounts but are not required to; reserves may be waived with proper notification, per Section 720.303(6) [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
