# Byzantine Generals Problem

## Summary

The coordination quandary of maintaining a permanent shared public ledger among participants who do not trust one another and have no custodian; Bitcoin's adversarial-collaboration design between miners is presented by Taleb as a game-theoretic approach that attempts to "attenuate or circumvent" this problem [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].

## Key claims

- Described in the paper as "the coordination quandary known as the 'Byzantine generals problem'" — the maintenance of a permanent shared public ledger in the absence of a custodian and trust between participants [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Bitcoin "provides a game theoretic approach to mitigate the effects of the absence of custodian and lack of trust between participants in the maintenance of a permanent shared public ledger — attenuating or circumventing the coordination quandary known as the 'Byzantine generals problem'" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- The mitigation operates through an adversarial collaboration between miners who validate transactions on a public ledger; as a reward they receive coins plus a fee from the underlying transactions [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Taleb notes that "the entire ideological basis behind bitcoin is complete distrust of other operators — there are no partial custodians; the system is fully distributed, though prone to concentration" — the latter caveat anchored in the powerlaw distribution of holdings with tail index ≈ 5 [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
