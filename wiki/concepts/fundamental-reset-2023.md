# Fundamental Reset (2023)

## Summary

Credit Suisse's framing of the 2023 macro outlook: a fundamental and lasting reset of macroeconomics and geopolitics driven by the COVID-19 pandemic, shifting demographics, climate change, and weakening business investment in the wake of geopolitical ruptures, with the implication that the global economy will grow at a much slower pace than in the 2010-2019 period [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].

## Key claims

- The global economy has undergone a fundamental and lasting reset due to the COVID-19 pandemic, shifting demographics, climate change, and weakening business investment in the wake of geopolitical ruptures [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- The reset implies the global economy will grow at a much slower pace than in the 2010-2019 period [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- Inflation will remain elevated in 2023, though it is expected to eventually peak and start to decline [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- The transition out of negative rates is 'behind us' — framed by CS as a structural shift requiring investors to adapt their process [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- 2023 is likely to be challenging because financial conditions are all but certain to remain tight and the fundamental reset is continuing [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
- Reset prescription: a robust investment process and broad diversification across asset classes, including alternatives [[sources/pdf-8f021296560c]].
