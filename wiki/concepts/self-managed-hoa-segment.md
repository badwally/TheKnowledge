# Self-Managed HOA Segment

## Summary

The self-managed HOA segment refers to homeowner associations whose communities are managed directly by volunteer boards of homeowners rather than by professional property management companies — accounting for 30% to 40% of community associations and comprising 2.5 million volunteer board members per the framing in PayHOA's May 15, 2024 Series A announcement [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]]. The segment is structurally distinct from the professionally-managed-HOA market served by community association management (CAM) SaaS platforms (CINC Systems, Vantaca, Yardi Breeze Premier, FirstService Residential, Associa) — vendors that "catered to professional property managers" historically left self-managed HOAs to "cobble together solutions with unconnected tools or generic software not designed for their specific needs," per founder Mike Bollinger's public framing [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]]. The segment is the load-bearing ICP-exclusion anchor for Longspan's condo-capital-infra reserve-study go-to-market under ADR-0004: Longspan's target ICP is professionally-managed condominium and HOA corporations with a property-management-company or licensed-reserve-analyst counterparty in the loop, not volunteer-board-operated self-managed HOAs whose all-in-one-software needs are addressed by category-specific vendors like PayHOA [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]].

## Key claims

### Segment definition

- Self-managed HOAs are homeowner associations whose communities are managed directly by volunteer board members rather than by professional property management companies [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]].
- Self-managed HOAs are framed by PayHOA founder Mike Bollinger as having structurally distinct needs from professionally-managed HOAs, with "larger companies catered to professional property managers" leaving the self-managed segment under-served [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]].

### Segment scale (per PayHOA framing, May 2024)

- Self-managed HOAs account for 30% to 40% of community associations [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]].
- The segment is made up of 2.5 million volunteer board members [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]].

### Segment-specific software needs (per Bollinger framing)

- Before purpose-built self-managed-HOA software like PayHOA emerged, self-managed HOAs were "forced to cobble together solutions with unconnected tools or generic software not designed for their specific needs — some even came to us with shoe boxes of paper receipts" [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]].
- Functional scope of segment-specific software (per PayHOA's product framing): a "central hub" for association board members handling finances, maintenance requests, and communication with their communities — an all-in-one bookkeeping-plus-maintenance-plus-communications stack rather than an unbundled vertical stack [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]].

### Segment-specific monetization model (per PayHOA's pricing)

- Monthly subscription pricing based on the number of units in the community, starting at $49 per month for HOAs with 25 units or less — a per-unit-light SMB pricing posture rather than the per-management-company-account enterprise pricing of CAM SaaS incumbents [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]].

### Institutional-capital validation (per Elephant Ventures framing)

- Peter Fallon, general partner at Elephant Ventures, framed the segment's underaddressed status as the investment thesis behind the May 15, 2024 PayHOA $27.5M Series A: "Many HOAs manage their communities themselves, and for too long, their needs haven't been fully addressed" — confirming that institutional growth capital is validating the self-managed-HOA segment as a fundable category structurally separate from the professionally-managed-HOA market [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]].
- PayHOA's positive EBITDA, 70%+ YoY revenue growth, and 652,000+ users at the time of the $27.5M Series A confirm the self-managed segment is large enough and economically viable enough to support a venture-scale category-specific vendor [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]].

### Boundary blurring at the margins

- Bollinger has noticed an increased number of property management companies signing up for the PayHOA platform, framing this as opening up the company's total addressable market beyond the self-managed segment proper — surfacing a partial boundary-blur at the small-property-manager end of the professionally-managed segment, where lightweight tools designed for self-managed boards become attractive to small CAM operators [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]].

### Implication for condo-capital-infra ICP framing

- The self-managed-HOA segment is the load-bearing ICP-exclusion anchor for Longspan's condo-capital-infra reserve-study go-to-market under ADR-0004: Longspan's target ICP is professionally-managed condominium and HOA corporations with a property-management-company or licensed-reserve-analyst counterparty in the loop, not volunteer-board-operated self-managed HOAs whose software needs are structurally addressed by all-in-one volunteer-board-operated tools like PayHOA rather than by reserve-study-and-capital-planning specialists [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]].
- The PayHOA Series A's $27.5M scale, 652,000-user footprint, and 70%+ YoY revenue growth confirm the self-managed-HOA segment is meaningfully distinct from the professionally-managed market targeted by the CAM SaaS exit-thesis acquirer set (CINC Systems, Vantaca, Yardi, FirstService Residential) and therefore not an addressable beachhead for the reserve-study GTM [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]].
- The segment-exclusion framing is operationally testable through partner-discovery and customer-segmentation criteria: any Longspan prospect identifying as "no property manager, board operates the building itself" or "we use PayHOA / Buildium HOA / similar all-in-one volunteer-tool" should be deprioritized regardless of unit count, because the reserve-study workflow has no purchase-decision lever in that organizational structure [[sources/web-2024-05-15-1b2]].
