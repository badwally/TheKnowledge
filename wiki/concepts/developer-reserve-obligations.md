# Developer Reserve Obligations (US)

## Summary

Developer reserve obligations are state-law requirements imposed on the developer (declarant) of a condominium or planned community — separate from the ongoing requirements imposed on the association — to conduct an initial reserve study, fund initial reserves, or establish a statutory reserve account at the time the community is created [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].

## Key claims

- Reserve studies are required of developers in five states: California, Delaware, Florida, Nevada, and Oregon [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- Reserve funding is required of developers in six states: Arizona, Delaware, Florida, Nevada, Oregon, and Wisconsin [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- In Oregon, the declarant, on behalf of a homeowners association, must conduct an initial reserve study, prepare an initial maintenance plan, and establish a reserve account [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- In Wisconsin, the developer of a condominium created on or after November 1, 2004, must establish a statutory reserve account when the condominium is created and execute a statutory reserve account statement; the declarant determines the annual amount to be assessed unit owners for reserve funds [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
- The developer-side and association-side reserve regimes are tracked as separate statutory categories: a state may impose either, both, or neither, and the state lists do not perfectly overlap (e.g., Arizona requires developer funding but has no statutory study or funding requirement on the association itself) [[sources/pdf-fc88aa6c2822]].
