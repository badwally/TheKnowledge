# Theory of Trade Expectations

## Summary

Dale Copeland's argument, advanced in the book "Economic Interdependence and War" and adopted by Zoltan Pozsar in the August 24, 2022 Credit Suisse dispatch, that great-power decisions to fight or stay at peace turn on whether expectations of the future trade environment are positive or negative — a frame Pozsar uses to bridge geopolitics and the inflation outlook [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].

## Key claims

- Pozsar quotes Copeland directly: "when great powers have positive expectations of the future trade environment, they want to remain at peace in order to secure the economic benefits that enhance long-term economic power. When, however, these expectations turn negative, leaders are likely to fear a loss of access to raw materials and markets, giving them an incentive to initiate crises to protect their commercial interests" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- The theory is based on a 200-year history covering, among others, the Napoleonic and Crimean wars [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Pozsar pairs the theory with the gnomic summary: "if there is trust, trade works. If trust is gone, it doesn't" — and treats it as the logical bridge from broken trust to de-globalization [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- Pozsar applies the theory to two outcomes simultaneously: the U.S./Russia/China conflict and the inflation outlook — "This 'theory of trade expectations' holds lessons for understanding not only today's conflict between the U.S. on the one hand, and Russia and China on the other, but also the outlook for inflation" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
- The corollary Pozsar draws: "if trust drove globalization, and globalization drove 'The Great Moderation,' distrust will drive de-globalization, and de-globalization 'The Great Reflation'" [[sources/pdf-503a04772afb]].
