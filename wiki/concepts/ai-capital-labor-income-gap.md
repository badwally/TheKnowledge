# AI-Driven Capital-Labor Income Gap

## Summary

The theoretical and empirical prediction from Acemoglu (2024) that AI advances — whether through automation or task complementarity — will widen the gap between capital and labor income, because both channels reduce the labor share when the elasticity of substitution between tasks is less than one (the empirically relevant case) [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].

## Key claims

- Both automation (expanding the set of capital-produced tasks) and task complementarities (raising labor productivity in existing tasks) reduce the labor share when sigma < 1, which is the empirically plausible case with sigma approximately 0.5 [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Only the creation of new labor-intensive tasks raises the labor share; all other AI channels increase inequality between capital and labor [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Even when AI improves the productivity of low-skill workers in certain tasks without creating new tasks, this may increase rather than reduce labor income inequality through ripple effects — displaced workers compete for tasks previously performed by other groups [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Acemoglu proves by example in a task-based model that a reduction in the productivity gap between low-skill and high-skill workers in some tasks can lead to greater inequality, because when sigma < 1 and task prices fall sharply, high-skill workers reallocate to tasks where their comparative advantage yields a higher relative wage [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- AI exposure is more equally distributed across demographic groups than pre-AI automation (robotics, software), so wage inequality effects are less adverse than previous automation waves [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Nevertheless, there is no evidence AI will reduce inequality; the between-group standard deviation of log wages increases slightly from 0.35 to 0.36 [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Low-education women, especially white native-born low-education women, are the demographic group most likely to experience real wage declines from AI [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- The capital share of national income is estimated to increase by about 0.31 percentage points as a result of AI rollout, confirming that capital-labor inequality will rise [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- GDP increases above TFP gains (from capital deepening) overstate welfare improvements, because extra investment comes out of consumption [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
