# Endogenous price spiral

## Summary

An endogenous price spiral is a self-reinforcing feedback loop between a fund's price impact and investor flows: price impact lifts realized returns, return-chasing investors allocate more capital to the fund, that capital is reinvested in the same positions, and the cycle repeats until the underlying price pressure reverts; van der Beck, Bouchaud, and Villamaina (2024) build their model on this mechanism in the spirit of Brunnermeier and Pedersen (2009) [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].

## Key claims

- The paper builds on Lou (2012) — that funds trading on flows exert price pressure on their portfolio securities — and shows that the flow-performance relationship causes endogenous price spirals in the spirit of Brunnermeier and Pedersen (2009) when future fund flows chase price pressure [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- The combination of price impact and return chasing causes an endogenous feedback loop and a reallocation of wealth to early fund investors, which unravels once the price pressure reverts [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- Funds with concentrated portfolios in illiquid basket securities have a high potential impact on the underlying; flows into these funds push up the prices of the underlying, leading to high realized fund returns, which pull in further flows ("Ponzi flows") that produce continued price impact [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- For ETFs specifically, the cycle takes the form of endogenous boom-and-bust cycles in both the ETF price and the underlying, propagated through the ETF arbitrage mechanism [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
