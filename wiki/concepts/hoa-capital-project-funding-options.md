# HOA Capital Project Funding Options (Reserves vs Special Assessment vs Financing)

## Summary

An HOA board funding a capital improvement project has three principal options: (i) draw from the association's cash reserve funds, (ii) charge homeowners a special assessment (a one-time fee for a specific expense), or (iii) finance through a bank that specializes in loans designed for HOAs [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]]. First Citizens Bank's April 14, 2026 board-facing guide frames the decision as a long-term-impact comparison, with each option carrying distinct tradeoffs on reserve liquidity preservation, resident financial strain, project-completion phasing, and repayment timing [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].

## Key claims

- Before drawing from reserves or issuing a special assessment, HOA board members should consider whether financing could offer greater flexibility and stability [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- When deciding between internal and external funding, HOA board members should consider the long-term impact of each approach [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Using reserve funds depletes liquidity that may have been earmarked for other projects [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Governing documents and state law may restrict the use of reserve funds to repairs and replacements rather than new projects [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- A special assessment is a one-time fee charged to homeowners to cover a specific expense [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Special assessments can seem straightforward but may strain residents financially or create tension with the community [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Special assessments can slow project progress if collections run short [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Bank financing allows the association to complete all phases of a project at once, maintaining a consistent community appearance while spreading repayment over time [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Bank financing avoids the pitfalls of phased construction, per First Citizens's Jeff Barnett [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Bank financing helps avoid large, upfront payments while allowing the association to continue contributing to its reserve funds [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- HOA boards should choose the financing structure and loan terms that best fit their community's project scope and cash flow [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
