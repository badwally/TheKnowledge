# Canadian Condo Corporation — Agent for Unit Owners Doctrine

## Summary

The "agent for unit owners" doctrine is CRA's analytical framework, articulated in External Technical Interpretation 2014-0528171E5, for re-characterizing income earned by a Canadian condominium corporation from the lease of common elements as income of the unit owners (rather than the Corporation), where the relevant provincial law supports the agency characterization [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]]. The doctrine functions as a safe harbour for the Corporation's paragraph 149(1)(l) tax-exempt status when the corporation enters into a lease agreement that would otherwise generate non-incidental income: if the income is properly the unit owners' (via agency), the Corporation does not have income that could be made available for the personal benefit of members, and the exemption survives — albeit with the consequence that the unit owners must include the allocated profit in their income for tax purposes [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].

## Key claims

- The space being rented may not belong to the Corporation; rather, the Corporation may be better viewed as acting as an agent for the unit owners in entering into the lease agreement [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- If the agency characterization holds, the lease agreement generally would not jeopardize the tax-exempt status of the Corporation [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Under the agency characterization, the related profit must be allocated appropriately to the unit owners and included in their income for tax purposes [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- The agency characterization is conditioned on the relevant provincial law: it applies only "if this is appropriate under the relevant provincial law" [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Subsection 11(2) of the *Condominium Act* of Ontario (S.O. 1998, c.19) — which provides that "the owners are tenants in common of the common elements and an undivided interest in the common elements is appurtenant to each owner's unit" — is cited as the operative provincial-law authority supporting the agency characterization in Ontario [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Where provincial law indicates the income is the income of the Corporation (i.e., the agency characterization does not apply), the Corporation may not be tax-exempt under paragraph 149(1)(l) [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
