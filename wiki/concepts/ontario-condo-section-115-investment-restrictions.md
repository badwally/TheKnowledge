# Ontario Condo §115 Reserve Fund Investment Restrictions

## Summary

Section 115 of the Ontario Condominium Act, 1998 defines both the permitted-investment universe and the prohibited-investment categories for condominium corporation reserve funds in Ontario [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]]. The regime is enumerated permissively (a closed list of eligible securities) plus negatively (explicit prohibitions on equities, derivatives, and speculative instruments), and is anchored on three operating principles: preservation of capital is paramount, liquidity must be maintained for planned expenditures, and investment income belongs to the reserve fund [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]]. The §115 regime sits structurally alongside Alberta's permissive Authorized Corporation Investments regime (which allows up to 15% dividend-paying common shares) and BC Strata Property Regulation §6.11's eligible-investment definition (insured savings/term deposits/GICs plus A-rated bonds and BBB-rated fixed-income ETFs) — making Ontario the most restrictive of the three documented Canadian eligible-investment regimes [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].

## Key claims

- Section 115 of the Condominium Act, 1998 governs reserve fund investment requirements for Ontario condominium corporations [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- Permitted investments: guaranteed investment certificates (GICs); bonds issued by Canadian governments; deposits in credit unions; other investments permitted under the regulations [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- Investment restrictions: no equity investments (stocks); no derivatives; no speculative instruments [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- Operating principles: preservation of capital is paramount; liquidity must be maintained for planned expenditures [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- All investment income earned on reserve funds must be allocated to the reserve fund, not the operating fund [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- Proper financial statement presentation requires reserve fund investment income shown separately, investment gains/losses allocated to reserve fund, and disclosure of investment holdings in notes [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- For large condominium corporations with substantial reserve funds, practitioner guidance recommends working with a CPA experienced in not-for-profit accounting to ensure investment income is properly reported and allocated [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
