# Alberta Reserve Fund Plan (§23(4))

## Summary

The Alberta reserve fund plan is the §23(4) board-approved artifact that translates the qualified person's §23(3) reserve fund report into a funding mechanism: it must establish the reserve fund (if one does not yet exist) and set forth the method and amounts needed for funding and maintaining the reserve fund [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]]. Per §23(5), the plan must demonstrate that — based on the underlying reserve fund report — sufficient funds will be available, through owners' contributions or any other reasonable method, to repair or replace the depreciating property in accordance with the report [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].

## Key claims

- The board must approve a reserve fund plan after reviewing the §23(3) reserve fund report [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- The plan must (i) establish a reserve fund, if one has not already been established, and (ii) set forth the method of and amounts needed for funding and maintaining the reserve fund [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- The plan must provide that, based on the reserve fund report, sufficient funds will be available — by means of owners' contributions or any other method that is reasonable in the circumstances — to repair or replace, as the case may be, the depreciating property in accordance with the reserve fund report [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- The corporation must provide owners with copies of the approved reserve fund plan prior to collecting any funds for the matters dealt with in the underlying reserve fund report [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- Until a corporation has approved a plan under §23(4) and met the §23(6) owner-disclosure requirement so as to be eligible to collect funds for the reserve fund, the corporation may continue to collect or otherwise receive funds for a fund similar in nature to a reserve fund, and may make expenditures from and generally continue to operate that fund [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
- The Alberta §23(4) plan-approval mechanism is structurally analogous to — but procedurally distinct from — the Ontario §94(8) board-proposed plan, which the Ontario corporation discloses to owners via the Notice of Future Funding (NOFF) [[sources/web-2011-06-02-7ff]].
