# Bullish Order Block

## Summary

In Lesson 2: Market Structure, a bullish order block is the set of red (down-closed) candles immediately preceding a displacement move up — marked with a box and treated as the demand zone where buyers are going long, where price is expected to retrace before continuing higher [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].

## Key claims

- Bullish order blocks are formed by down-closed (red) candles when combined with displacement up on any time frame [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- These zones are equivalent to a demand zone for supply-and-demand traders — the area of "support" [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Whether there is a series of red candles or only one red candle before the displacement up, it does not matter — the trader marks the pre-displacement red candles with a box [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Bullish order blocks showcase where buyers are going long [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The simplifying rule from Lesson 2: "RED candles before displacement UP are BULLISH order blocks" [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- In the Lesson-2 bullish reversal example, after the failure to displace down from a fresh-yearly-low consolidation, price finds support at the order block left behind by an earlier weak move up — the down-closed candle before that move — before producing energetic displacement up [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The point of interest in a bullish setup is the imbalance and order-block zone (purple-box region in Lesson 2's diagram); the trader wants price to retrace into it and then move away from it back up [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The protective stop in the bullish setup goes at the order-block swing low (just under the order-block box), and can be moved up as price moves up and away from the adds [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
