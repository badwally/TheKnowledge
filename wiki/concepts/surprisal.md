# Surprisal

## Summary

Surprisal (Tribus 1961) is the formal information-theoretic measure of the "surprise" induced by a mismatch between the sensory signals a system encounters and those it predicted — explicitly distinguished from surprise in the normal, experientially loaded sense [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]]. In predictive-processing accounts, prediction error reports surprisal, making it the central currency on which the brain operates [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].

## Key claims

- Prediction error is treated as a proxy (Feldman & Friston 2010) for sensory information itself; viewed through information theory and entropy, that proxy is surprisal [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].
- The distinction between surprisal and ordinary surprise matters because the predictive-coding framework's "surprise minimization" claims are formal, not phenomenological [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].
