# Deglobalization and Re-shoring

## Summary

Structural macro theme Fidelity International identifies as a multi-year force in its 2023 Investment Outlook — the gradual bifurcation of China and the West, driven by the pandemic and US-China tensions, leading to reconfiguration of supply chains and creating opportunities in countries like Mexico, Canada, parts of Latin America, Thailand, and Vietnam [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].

## Key claims

- Andrew McCaffery argues "the deglobalisation that has arisen from the pandemic and tensions with the US will take time to work its way through but is a theme that will grow" [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Henk-Jan Rikkerink expects the structural theme of deglobalisation and re-shoring to continue in 2023, with the gradual bifurcation of China and the West continuing [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Rikkerink argues that while bifurcation may be a drag on China, the reconfiguring of supply chains will provide opportunities for other countries — especially Mexico and Canada, but also some Latin American economies and Thailand and Vietnam [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Salman Ahmed identifies deglobalisation as one of three structural trends — alongside decarbonisation and dealing with high debt levels — that are likely to keep inflationary pressure elevated over the coming years [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Anne Richards identifies "a gradual decoupling of large economies from globalised supply and cooperation networks" as a risk that may exacerbate the challenges facing asset markets in 2023 [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
