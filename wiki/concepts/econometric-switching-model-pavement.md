# Structured econometric switching model for pavement M&R effectiveness

## Summary

A structured econometric switching model for pavement maintenance and rehabilitation (M&R) effectiveness pairs a discrete model of the highway agency's choice of M&R activity with a set of continuous pavement-response equations — one equation per M&R activity. Jointly estimating the discrete-choice and activity-specific response components corrects for the self-selection of pavement sections into M&R activities, recovering consistent parameter estimates for activity-specific effectiveness from observational data.

## Key claims

- Madanat and Mishalani (1998) present a structured econometric approach for estimating the effectiveness of pavement M&R activities consisting of (i) a discrete model describing the choice of M&R activity by the highway agency, and (ii) a set of continuous models representing pavement response, with one equation for each activity [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- This joint model system accounts for the self-selected nature of the sample and consequently obtains consistent model parameter estimates for the activity-specific pavement-response equations [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- The approach is positioned within the econometric literature on selectivity bias in models combining discrete and continuous choices; Mannering's 1987 *Transportation Research Record* paper on selectivity bias in discrete and continuous choice is cited as antecedent empirical work [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- The paper's case study, using a data set provided by the Indiana Department of Transportation, demonstrates the applicability and value of the methodology for pavement M&R effectiveness estimation [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
