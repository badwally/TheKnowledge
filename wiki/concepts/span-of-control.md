# Span of Control

## Summary

A concept from human organizational theory (Ouchi and Dowling, 1974) denoting the limits of hierarchical authority a single manager can exercise — and, by extension, the manager-to-worker ratio that an organization can sustain — invoked by Tomašev et al. (2026) as central to both orchestration and oversight in intelligent AI delegation [[sources/pdf-nenad-toma-2026-intelligent-ai-delegation]].

## Key claims

- Span of control informs how many orchestrator nodes are required relative to worker nodes in an AI agent network, and how many AI agents a single human expert can reliably oversee without excessive fatigue and with an acceptably low error rate [[sources/pdf-nenad-toma-2026-intelligent-ai-delegation]].
- Span of control is goal-dependent (Theobald and Nicholson-Crotty, 2005) and domain-dependent, and its impact on outcomes is most pronounced in tasks of higher complexity (Bohte and Meier, 2001) [[sources/pdf-nenad-toma-2026-intelligent-ai-delegation]].
- The optimal span of control depends on the relative importance of cost versus performance and reliability (Keren and Levhari, 1979): more sensitive and critical tasks may require highly accurate oversight at higher cost, while these costs may be relaxed at the expense of granularity for less consequential routine tasks [[sources/pdf-nenad-toma-2026-intelligent-ai-delegation]].
- The optimal choice necessarily depends on the relative capabilities and reliability of the involved delegators, delegatees, and overseers [[sources/pdf-nenad-toma-2026-intelligent-ai-delegation]].
