# Fund illiquidity

## Summary

Fund illiquidity is a portfolio-weighted measure of how much of a security's daily trading volume a fund's ownership represents; introduced by van der Beck, Bouchaud, and Villamaina (2024) as a regulatory reporting measure that captures a fund's potential for generating self-inflated returns [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].

## Key claims

- Fund illiquidity is defined as the portfolio-weighted average of the fund's ownership in the underlying securities relative to their daily volume [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- Interacted with fund flows, fund illiquidity measures how much of daily volume a fund on average buys when it reinvests flows into its existing positions [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- Fund illiquidity decomposes into the product of portfolio concentration and fund size [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- Most funds are either concentrated (small specialized funds) or large (diversified index trackers); when concentrated funds become large, fund illiquidity spikes and self-inflated returns become significant [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- The paper proposes fund illiquidity as a simple regulatory reporting measure that captures a fund's potential for self-inflated returns [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- Fund illiquidity is the conditioning variable in the paper's difference-in-difference estimator that separates flow-induced price impact from price discovery [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
