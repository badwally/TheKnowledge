# Circle of competence

## Summary

The principle that an investor should restrict serious analysis to companies and industries they actually understand; in Joel Greenblatt's Columbia class, the circle of competence is the practical safeguard that allows non-genius investors to outperform — "You don't have to be the best in the world at figuring stuff out" — and is operationalized as "you have to know what you know" [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].

## Key claims

- Greenblatt names the concept in his first class: "You have to know what you know—Your Circle of Competence" [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Greenblatt's framing of the principle is that the difficult investments are not the obstacle — "if it is hard for me to figure it out, I go onto the next one" — and the discipline is to pass on what falls outside your circle [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Greenblatt cites a student observation that other practitioners use industry depth as their edge — "Linda Greenblatt focuses in retail" — as a concrete example of working within a defined circle [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- The circle of competence is what makes Greenblatt's claim that "the fact that you are here means you can do the analysis" non-trivial: the analysis is doable, but only inside the bounds where the investor already has knowledge [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
