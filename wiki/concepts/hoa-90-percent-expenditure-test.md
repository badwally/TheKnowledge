# HOA 90% Expenditure Test (§528 Qualification)

## Summary

The 90% expenditure test is the second of two threshold tests an association must satisfy to qualify as a homeowners association under §528 [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]]. The test requires that at least 90% of the association's expenses for the tax year consist of expenses to acquire, build, manage, maintain, and care for its property — with timeshare associations additionally allowed to count expenses for activities provided to or on behalf of members of the timeshare association [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]]. The test is administered as Item C on Form 1120-H and operates in tandem with the 60% gross income test (Item B) [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]]. Capital reserve outlays and routine common-element maintenance expenditures both fall squarely within the qualifying expenditure categories, making compliance with the 90% test generally straightforward for associations whose spending is dominated by capital-asset upkeep and routine operations [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].

## Key claims

- To qualify as a homeowners association under §528, at least 90% of the association's expenses for the tax year must consist of expenses to acquire, build, manage, maintain, and care for its property [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- In the case of a timeshare association, the 90% expenditure test also counts expenses for activities provided to, or on behalf of, members of the timeshare association [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- The 90% test is administered as Item C on Form 1120-H [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- Treas. Reg. §1.528-10 governs the cost-allocation rule when facilities are used (or personnel are employed) for both exempt and nonexempt purposes [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
- The 90% expenditure test operates jointly with the 60% gross income test under §528: failure of either test disqualifies the association from §528 status for that tax year [[sources/web-2025-12-31-dae]].
