# Easy-to-Learn vs. Hard-to-Learn Tasks

## Summary

A distinction introduced in Acemoglu (2024) between tasks where AI can readily achieve expert-level performance (easy-to-learn) and tasks where context dependence, lack of objective outcome metrics, and high-dimensional action spaces limit AI to average-human performance at best (hard-to-learn), arguing that existing productivity estimates from easy tasks cannot be extrapolated to the economy as a whole [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].

## Key claims

- Easy-to-learn tasks are defined by two characteristics: a reliable, observable outcome metric and a simple (low-dimensional) mapping between action and outcome [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Examples of easy tasks: boiling an egg, verifying system identity, composing well-known programming subroutines, writing press releases, simple customer service — tasks where AI can identify expert performance and replicate it [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Hard tasks typically lack a simple action-to-outcome mapping, have vast relevant contexts, require new problem-solving, and have no large curated dataset of successful outcomes; e.g., diagnosing a persistent cough [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- In hard tasks, AI training data mostly comes from observing how humans act rather than from objective outcome measures, so AI performance converges to average human performance rather than exceeding it [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- This distinction is different from the routine vs. non-routine classification of Autor et al. (2003): AI's promise is performing non-routine tasks, but some non-routine tasks are easy-to-learn (e.g., programming subroutines) while others are hard-to-learn (e.g., tasks requiring judgment and intuition) [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- "Easy tasks" need not be easy for humans — in fact, cost savings are most pronounced when tasks are expensive for humans but easy for AI to learn; conversely, some hard tasks are straightforward for humans precisely because humans learn them through experience and intuition rather than outside observation [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- All three proof-of-concept experimental studies (Peng et al. 2023, Noy and Zhang 2023, Brynjolfsson et al. 2023) measured productivity gains in easy-to-learn tasks [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Acemoglu estimates hard-task cost savings at 7% of labor costs — approximately one quarter of the 27% baseline for easy tasks — based on extrapolation from the Brynjolfsson et al. customer service results, which already represent somewhat harder tasks [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Using a four-step classification (verb classification, IWA classification, LDA topic modeling, gradient-boosted tree), 72.6% of wage-bill-weighted exposed tasks are classified as easy-to-learn [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Incorporating the easy/hard distinction reduces the 10-year TFP estimate from 0.66% to 0.53% [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
