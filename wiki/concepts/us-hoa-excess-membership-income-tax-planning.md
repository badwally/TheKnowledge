# US HOA Excess Membership Income Tax Planning

## Summary

US HOA excess membership income tax planning is the set of four named techniques Gary Porter, CPA identifies for managing a homeowners association's exposure to federal income tax on net membership income under IRC §277, in light of the IRS's increasingly restrictive interpretation of Revenue Ruling 70-604 [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]]. The four techniques are: (1) file Form 1120-H to avoid the §277 / 70-604 regime entirely; (2) allocate excess net membership income to capital reserves before year end under IRC §118; (3) modify the budget for the subsequent year to purposely generate the membership loss required to absorb a rollover; (4) qualify for exemption under IRC §501(c)(4) to escape taxation on both interest income and net membership income [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].

## Key claims

- Four planning techniques are available to avoid IRS audit exposure on excess net membership income for an HOA otherwise subject to IRC §277 and Rev. Rul. 70-604 [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Technique 1 — file Form 1120-H: avoids the entire issue of excess net membership income; for most associations, this approximately doubles the tax compared to Form 1120 / §277 / 70-604, but with less audit risk [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Technique 2 — IRC §118 allocation: if an association appears to have large excess net membership income, allocate the excess to capital reserves before year end under the provisions of IRC §118, thus avoiding Rev. Rul. 70-604 altogether [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Technique 3 — budget modification to force absorption: if an association has a large carryover from year one to year two, modify the year-two budget to purposely create a membership loss equal to the rollover; the association does not have to modify its assessment structure — it can simply allocate more money into the "capital" components of its replacement (reserve) fund [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Technique 4 — IRC §501(c)(4) exemption: qualify the association as a social welfare organization to avoid taxation on net membership income (and interest income); most associations will not qualify, but the path should be reconsidered now that the IRS interpretation of Rev. Rul. 70-604 is restrictive [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- Other tax planning techniques exist beyond the four named, but were not discussed in the source — which intentionally focused on the major planning areas only [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- The article's overall practitioner recommendation is for proper long-term tax planning by a qualified professional to navigate the §277 / 70-604 / 1120-H / 501(c)(4) decision matrix [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
- The IRS-audit-risk failure mode under §277 / 70-604 — where a disallowed election produces tax greater than 1120-H would have produced — is the primary driver behind the planning hierarchy [[sources/web-2026-01-01-a9a]].
