# Reserve Funding Methods

## Summary

Reserve funding methods are the named target-setting strategies that determine how much an association contributes to its reserve fund and how it judges adequacy. The CommunityPay platform's reserve-studies module enumerates five funding methods — Full Funding, Baseline Funding, Threshold Funding, Statutory Minimum, and Custom — and supports multi-method calculations so an association can evaluate the same component schedule under each strategy [[sources/web-2024-11-01-fc2]]. Each method produces a distinct annual contribution requirement and a distinct percent-funded target, which the platform projects across a 30-year horizon and compares against deficit years and special-assessment scenarios [[sources/web-2024-11-01-fc2]].

## Key claims

- Full Funding is defined as targeting 100% of future obligations [[sources/web-2024-11-01-fc2]].
- Baseline Funding is identified by the cash flow method [[sources/web-2024-11-01-fc2]].
- Threshold Funding is defined by a minimum balance target [[sources/web-2024-11-01-fc2]].
- Statutory Minimum funding is defined by state requirements [[sources/web-2024-11-01-fc2]].
- Custom funding targets are supported as a fifth option alongside the four standard methods [[sources/web-2024-11-01-fc2]].
- The CommunityPay reserve-studies module performs multi-method funding calculations that show "exactly where you stand and what it takes to reach your target funding level" for each method [[sources/web-2024-11-01-fc2]].
- The 30-year cash flow projection generated by the platform includes "required annual funding to meet target by method (full, baseline, threshold)" as one of its named projection elements [[sources/web-2024-11-01-fc2]].
- Board-ready reports generated by the platform include "funding analysis by method" as a named report section [[sources/web-2024-11-01-fc2]].
