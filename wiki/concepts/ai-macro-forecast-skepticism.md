# AI Macro Forecast Skepticism

## Summary

The argument from Acemoglu (2024) that prominent AI macro forecasts — Goldman Sachs (7% global GDP / 1.5% annual US productivity growth), McKinsey (1.5–3.4% annual GDP growth), and Korinek and Suh (100–300% GDP growth from AGI) — are implausibly large when disciplined by Hulten's theorem and realistic estimates of task exposure and cost savings [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].

## Key claims

- Goldman Sachs (2023) predicted a 7% increase in global GDP ($7 trillion) and a 1.5% per annum increase in US productivity growth over 10 years [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- McKinsey Global Institute (2023) forecast generative AI could boost the global economy by $17.1 to $25.6 trillion, with an overall AI impact of 1.5–3.4 percentage points of annual GDP growth in advanced economies [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Korinek and Suh (2024) predict a "baseline" GDP growth of 100% over 10 years from AGI, with an "aggressive" scenario of 300% [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Acemoglu's framework, using the same underlying evidence on AI capabilities, produces estimates an order of magnitude smaller: 0.53–0.66% TFP and 0.93–1.56% GDP over 10 years [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- The gap is explained by three factors the bullish forecasts overlook: (1) only 23% of exposed tasks can be profitably automated within 10 years, (2) cost savings must be measured in overall (not just labor) terms, and (3) hard-to-learn tasks will yield much lower productivity gains than the easy tasks studied so far [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Additional reasons the Hulten-derived estimates may still be too high: less than 1.5% of US businesses had any AI investment as of 2019, J-curve adjustment costs may delay productivity gains for 20+ years (per Greenwood and Yorukoglu 1997), and AI energy requirements create measured GDP gains without welfare improvement [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- The bullish forecasts also ignore the possibility that new AI tasks with negative social value could offset genuine productivity gains [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
