# Skill-Signal Alpha Erosion

## Summary

The finding from Lu, Mortal, and Ray (2020) that fund performance declines significantly after poker tournament cashes — 40–70 bp per month relative to matched peers — with the decline concentrated where net inflows are highest, suggesting that a valid skill signal becomes self-defeating when investor flows responding to it trigger decreasing returns to scale or associated distractions [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].

## Key claims

- Performance measured as either raw returns or seven-factor alphas decreases significantly after poker tournament cashes, with funds underperforming their matched peers by 40–70 bp per month [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].
- The decrease in performance occurs alongside the documented increase in net inflows to these funds, suggesting the mechanism is decreasing returns to scale as highlighted in Berk and Green (2004) [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].
- Given the magnitude of the decline, the authors also entertain the possibility that distraction associated with poker playing may contribute to some of the relative performance decline [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].
- The paradox: poker tournament success is a valid signal of fund management skill (approximately 10 bp/month outperformance), but the investor response to that signal (increased inflows of 1.2–1.8%/month) erodes the very alpha the signal predicted [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].
- Given this dynamic, hedge fund investors would be better off investing in an otherwise similar manager without poker tournament success [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].
