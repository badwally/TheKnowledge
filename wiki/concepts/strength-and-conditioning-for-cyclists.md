# Strength and Conditioning for Cyclists

## Summary

Strength and conditioning for cyclists is a structured supplementary training practice — typically one or two sessions per week — that targets cycling-specific power, range of movement, coordination, flexibility, agility, strength, and stability rather than general gym goals [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].

## Key claims

- The point of cycling-specific S&C is to provide a 'solid internal scaffold' supporting all other training, with the dual goals of improved efficiency and boosted performance [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- A second goal is [[concepts/cycling-injury-prevention|injury prevention]] — improved strength and stability, plus correcting imbalances created by repetitive overuse on the bike [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Programs target seven specific qualities: power, range of movement, coordination, flexibility, agility, strength, and stability [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Sessions can be done at home with a mat, dumbbells, and/or a band, or in a gym using machines and free weights — equipment is not a barrier to entry [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- When a strength day collides with a ride, the ride takes priority: cyclists should ride first, then do the strength session afterwards [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Strength prescription evolves across the [[concepts/cycling-periodization-phases|four training phases]] (Prep / Base / Build / Peak), with intensity and volume rising into Build and tapering through Peak [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Cyclists new to strength work are advised to book a personal-trainer session or two to validate technique before training independently [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
