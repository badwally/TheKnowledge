# Volcker vs. Burns Stagflation Policy Comparison

## Summary

The historical comparison the July 13, 2022 Q3-2022 IACFM presentation uses to motivate restrictive policy: under Arthur Burns (1970–1978), the Fed was insufficiently restrictive in light of high inflation and lost its inflation-fighting credibility as expectations became unanchored; under Paul Volcker (1979–1987), the Fed maintained the Fed Funds rate above inflation even as real GDP slowed, restoring inflation-fighting credibility [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].

## Key claims

- Under Arthur Burns' tenure as Chair of the Federal Reserve from 1970 to 1978, monetary policy was insufficiently restrictive in light of extremely high inflation, resulting in inflationary expectations becoming unanchored [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- The Burns-era Fed prematurely lowered the Fed Funds rate as real GDP growth slowed; the Fed Funds rate remained lower than inflation [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Burns-era Fed Funds rate at year-end (1970–1978): 3.0%, 3.5%, 5.5%, 9.0%, 8.0%, 4.9%, 5.9%, 6.5%, 10.0% [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Burns-era Core CPI YoY (1970–1978): 6.6%, 3.1%, 3.0%, 4.7%, 11.1%, 6.7%, 6.1%, 6.5%, 8.5% — i.e., the Fed Funds rate was below Core CPI in multiple years [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Burns-era nominal GDP growth ran 5.5%–13.0% annually while real GDP growth was as low as (0.5%) and (0.2%) — the canonical "high nominal, low real" stagflation pattern [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- S&P 500 annual total return during the Burns era (1970–1978): 3.9%, 14.3%, 19.0%, (14.7%), (29.7%), 37.2%, 23.9%, (7.2%), 6.6% [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Result: Federal Reserve lost its inflation-fighting credibility under Burns [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Under Paul Volcker's tenure as Chair of the Federal Reserve from 1979 to 1987, monetary policy was successful in moderating inflation by maintaining a high Fed Funds rate even in the face of slowing real GDP growth [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Volcker maintained the Fed Funds rate in excess of inflation even as inflation declined [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Volcker-era Fed Funds rate at year-end (1979–1987): 14.0%, 18.0%, 12.0%, 8.5%, 9.5%, 8.3%, 7.8%, 6.0%, 6.9% — consistently above contemporaneous Core CPI [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Volcker-era Core CPI YoY (1979–1987): 11.3%, 12.2%, 9.5%, 4.5%, 4.8%, 4.7%, 4.3%, 3.8%, 4.2% — declining as restrictive policy was sustained [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- S&P 500 annual total return during the Volcker era (1979–1987): 18.49%, 32.40%, (4.9%), 21.6%, 22.6%, 6.2%, 31.7%, 18.7%, 5.3% [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Result: under the Volcker-era tightening cycle of the early 1980s, inflation expectations stabilized and the Federal Reserve maintained its inflation-fighting credibility [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
