# AMD Cycle (Accumulation, Manipulation, Distribution)

## Summary

ICT's name for a three-phase intraday price-action template — Accumulation, Manipulation, Distribution — also called the Power of 3 (PO3); pitched as a way to identify market maker models and anticipate where price is likely to go without forecasting, by pairing overnight trading ranges with points of liquidity [[sources/pdf-a3789262265a]]; Ali Khan's "The ICT Bible" (2023) adds mechanical detail: IPDA holds price in a range to build buystops and sellstops (accumulation), reprices above or below the range to trigger those stops (manipulation / Judas swing), then smart money pairs the triggered liquidity with positions in the opposite direction (distribution) [[sources/pdf-ali-khan-2023-the-ict-bible]].

## Key claims

- "The power of 3 (or what is known as Accumulation, Manipulation, Distribution → AMD), is a very simple strategy to use and identify market maker models, and where price is likely to go" [[sources/pdf-a3789262265a]].
- The framework is built for futures markets — specifically $ES, $NQ, $YM — although the concept can be applied to anything [[sources/pdf-a3789262265a]].
- The lesson claims AMD doesn't need "any magical forecasting, just a confirmation of market structure and market structure shifts after price has taken out liquidity" [[sources/pdf-a3789262265a]].
- AMD helps visualize price by using overnight trading ranges and pairing them with points of liquidity [[sources/pdf-a3789262265a]].
- Intraday timing prescription: accumulation between 6:00 PM EST and 5:00 AM EST; manipulation and distribution between 2:00 AM EST and 10:30 AM EST [[sources/pdf-a3789262265a]].
- The lesson states these timing windows are the same as ICT killzones, and frames itself as a beginner-simplified statement for those not yet familiar with killzones [[sources/pdf-a3789262265a]].
- Entry rule: take entries once liquidity has been taken and there is a market structure shift in the direction of interest for price distribution [[sources/pdf-a3789262265a]].
- Per Ali Khan, IPDA holds price in a range during accumulation, allowing liquidity to build up above the range in the form of buystops and below the range in the form of sellstops [[sources/pdf-ali-khan-2023-the-ict-bible]].
- Per Ali Khan, IPDA then reprices above the consolidation range, triggering the buystops into active market orders — this is the manipulation phase, "often referred to as the Judas swing" [[sources/pdf-ali-khan-2023-the-ict-bible]].
- Per Ali Khan, smart money pairs liquidity from the willing buyers (buystops) with short positions and offloads these positions to willing sellers below the consolidation range (sellstops) — this is the distribution phase [[sources/pdf-ali-khan-2023-the-ict-bible]].
- Per Ali Khan, this process is part of IPDA's market efficiency paradigm logic, whereby smart money will sell to the buyers and buy back from the sellers (and vice versa) [[sources/pdf-ali-khan-2023-the-ict-bible]].
- Per Ali Khan, the speed of the manipulation leg also causes FOMO for buyers who chase the market, thus entering longs in the expansion leg higher — another example of how IPDA engineers liquidity by the manipulation of price [[sources/pdf-ali-khan-2023-the-ict-bible]].
- Per Ali Khan, the same AMD concept can be seen with retail patterns such as trendlines, support and resistance, head and shoulder patterns, bull and bear flags — each designed to draw retail money into the marketplace [[sources/pdf-ali-khan-2023-the-ict-bible]].
- Per Ali Khan, when trading one should consider "Where are the retail stops?" when undertaking trade analysis [[sources/pdf-ali-khan-2023-the-ict-bible]].
