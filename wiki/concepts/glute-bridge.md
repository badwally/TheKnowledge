# Glute Bridge

## Summary

The glute bridge is a floor-based posterior-chain exercise for cyclists that simultaneously builds power, hip flexibility, and injury resistance by working the glutes, hamstrings, erector spinae, rectus abdominis, and adductors in a single movement [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].

## Key claims

- Targets the glutes, hamstrings, erector spinae, rectus abdominis, and adductors in a single movement [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Cited rationales: increase power, improve hip flexibility, and prevent injury [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Performed lying face up with knees bent and feet shoulder-width on the floor, lifting the hips until knees, hips, and shoulders form a straight line, holding briefly while squeezing the glutes [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Form cue: avoid over-extending and arching the lower back — focus on the glute squeeze, not lumbar movement [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Progression is the single-leg bridge with one leg raised and held straight [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Substitutes include squats, donkey kicks, banded standing glute kicks, the glute machine, and the adductor machine [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
