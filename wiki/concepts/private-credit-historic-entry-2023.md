# Historic Private-Credit Entry Point (2023)

## Summary

Apollo's December 2022 thesis — articulated by Chief Economist Torsten Sløk — that 2023 represents "a historic entry point in private credit and attractive opportunities in private equity for investors able to be providers of capital in a time of stressed and distressed markets," with the high-yield primary market virtually shut down and capital scarce and expensive [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].

## Key claims

- Apollo sees "a historic entry point in private credit" for investors able to provide capital in stressed and distressed markets in 2023 [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Apollo also sees "attractive opportunities in private equity" alongside private credit, framing both as the destination for capital rotating away from public 60/40 portfolios [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- The macro context Apollo cites for the entry point: capital markets in 2023 "will likely remain vulnerable" and volatility will likely persist because "capital remains scarce and expensive," and high-yield primary credit markets "will likely stay virtually shut down for the time being" [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- The selection discipline Apollo prescribes alongside the thesis: "selectivity in asset selection, valuations, and entry points will be paramount" — i.e., the entry point is not a beta call on private markets but a stock-picking call on stressed deals [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Apollo's investor-flow expectation: "many investors—weary and battered after a disastrous performance of 60/40 portfolios of public equities and bonds in 2022—are likely to turn to private markets as they adjust their holdings in 2023" [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- A less aggressive Fed — or a potential Fed "pivot" in 2023 — should be bullish for asset prices (public and private) ranging from rates to credit to equities, supporting the entry-point thesis [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
