# Numeraire

## Summary

In Taleb's framing, a true numeraire must be of minimum variance with respect to an arbitrary basket of goods and services — a property cryptocurrencies, by virtue of their high price volatility, fail to satisfy [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].

## Key claims

- "A true numeraire must be one of minimum variance with respect to an arbitrary basket of goods and services" — the standard Taleb applies to evaluate whether bitcoin can serve as a unit of account [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Gold and silver lost their inflation-hedge status during the Hunt brothers' silver squeeze in the late 1970s, and Taleb uses this episode to discuss "what would be required from a true inflation-hedged store of value" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Bitcoin's empirical 3-month annualized return volatility "does not seem to drop over time," and at higher levels of capitalization "return volatility compounds," with Taleb reporting a half-trillion-dollar swing in BTC market capitalization in 2021 [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Taleb distinguishes between success for a "digital currency," which requires some stability and usability, and speculative price appreciation; he argues the two have been mistakenly conflated in the bitcoin discourse [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Bitcoin's apparent success has, in Taleb's account, been "masked by the inflation of the currency value, generating (paper) profits for a large enough number of people to enter the discourse well ahead of its utility" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
