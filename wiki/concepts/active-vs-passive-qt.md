# Active vs Passive Quantitative Tightening

## Summary

Distinction drawn by Zoltan Pozsar between two modes of Fed balance-sheet shrinkage: passive QT, in which the duration delivered into the market each month is determined by Treasury refunding decisions, and active QT, in which the Fed itself controls the amount of duration each dollar of balance-sheet shrinkage delivers into the market [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].

## Key claims

- The Fed has two types of buttons to push to release its volatility payloads: talking tough (a string of rate hikes, a string of 50 bps hikes, and maybe even 75 bps) and shifting from passive QT to active QT [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Active QT is defined as a regime in which the Fed controls the amount of duration each dollar of balance-sheet shrinkage delivers into the market, as opposed to leaving that decision to the refunding decisions of the Treasury [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- In his earlier "Volcker Moment" piece, Pozsar argued for the Fed to do active QT [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- As of May 13, 2022, Pozsar concedes that the verbal-tightening (talking-tough) strategy is working wonderfully for the moment — yields and mortgage rates are higher, stocks are lower [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Pozsar argues the best thing the Fed could do to achieve its goals is to "stop talking" — hike 50 bps and sell $50 billion of 10-year notes the day after, and put an end to press conferences — to keep the market on its toes and keep them guessing [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
