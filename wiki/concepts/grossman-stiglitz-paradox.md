# Grossman-Stiglitz Paradox

## Summary

The 1980 result cited by Cliff Asness as the "formal proof" that perfectly informationally efficient markets are impossible — the formal counterpart to Eugene Fama's classroom argument that perfect efficiency is "an extreme and unrealistic hypothesis" [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].

## Key claims

- The Grossman-Stiglitz (1980) paradox is a formal proof of the impossibility of perfectly efficient markets [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Per Asness, it is the formal counterpart to Eugene Fama's classroom claim that "perfect efficiency is an extreme and unrealistic hypothesis" [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
- Once one accepts that markets are not perfectly efficient, "the question of how efficient it actually is becomes legitimate to ask," and the further notion that the level of efficiency is constant through time becomes unlikely — making "how efficient?" and "Has this changed through time?" legitimate empirical questions [[sources/pdf-c25a6984aba0]].
