# Interest rate triad

## Summary

The interest rate triad is an ICT methodology construct that overlays three Treasury maturities — the 30-Year Bond (long-term), 10-Year Note (intermediate-term), and 5-Year Note (short-term) — to identify smart-money accumulation and distribution and to confirm equity trade ideas at focus points such as orderblocks, liquidity pools, and fair value gaps [[sources/pdf-08bcb2d549ba]].

## Key claims

- Interest rates are characterized as the biggest force that moves the markets, and understanding interest rate shifts can assist in trade selection [[sources/pdf-08bcb2d549ba]].
- The triad consists of the 30-Year Bond representing the long-term rate, the 10-Year Note representing the intermediate-term rate, and the 5-Year Note representing the short-term rate [[sources/pdf-08bcb2d549ba]].
- Comparing these three rates is described as unlocking price action by revealing where institutional positioning is occurring in the interest market [[sources/pdf-08bcb2d549ba]].
- Overlaying the three markets shows where accumulation and distribution is taking place; the three rates should confirm each higher high or lower low [[sources/pdf-08bcb2d549ba]].
- A failure swing across the three rates reveals smart-money participation in the markets and validates trade setups [[sources/pdf-08bcb2d549ba]].
- The ICT Action Plan instructs the trader, when price trades to a focus point (orderblock, liquidity pool, or fair value gap), to refer to the interest rate triad and the DXY to confirm smart money is behind the trade idea; if there is no obvious indication that large funds are moving, the trade idea should be passed [[sources/pdf-08bcb2d549ba]].
