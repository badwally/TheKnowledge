# Capital Scarce & Expensive Regime (2023)

## Summary

Apollo's December 2022 framing of the 2023 capital-markets environment: capital remains scarce and expensive, volatility will likely persist, high-yield primary credit markets will likely stay virtually shut down, and selectivity in asset selection, valuations, and entry points will be paramount — a regime in which providers of private capital have outsized leverage [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].

## Key claims

- Apollo argues that capital markets will likely remain vulnerable in 2023 and volatility will likely persist because "capital remains scarce and expensive" [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Apollo argues high-yield primary credit markets "will likely stay virtually shut down for the time being," meaning issuers cannot easily access term funding through traditional public credit channels [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- Apollo's prescription for investors operating in this regime: "selectivity in asset selection, valuations, and entry points will be paramount" [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- The investor-flow consequence: capital rotates from public 60/40 portfolios — battered by 2022 — into private markets, where Apollo specifically sees a "historic entry point in private credit and attractive opportunities in private equity for investors able to be providers of capital in a time of stressed and distressed markets" [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
- The macro hinge: a less aggressive Fed — or a potential Fed "pivot" in 2023 — should be bullish for asset prices (public and private) ranging from rates to credit to equities, but the bullish path runs through a regime that is still capital-scarce and selectivity-driven, not a cyclical risk-on rotation [[sources/pdf-d196e853ebc0]].
