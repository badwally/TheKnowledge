# Divergence between prices and values

## Summary

The observation that stock prices fluctuate dramatically over short windows while the underlying values of the businesses they represent do not; in Joel Greenblatt's Columbia class, this asymmetry is the foundational opportunity that the rest of the value-investing course is built around — "prices fluctuate more than values—so therein lies opportunity" [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].

## Key claims

- Greenblatt names the principle directly in his first class: "Divergence between Prices and Values: Prices fluctuate more than values—so therein lies opportunity" [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Empirical anchor: large-cap stocks like IBM, Cisco, EBay, KKD, and Google show wide 52-week high/low spreads — "a business that hasn't changed much but the price has gone from $35 to $70. $7 to $30 and right now to $20" [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Greenblatt argues this divergence persists even in calm regimes: "this has been the least volatile period in many years, and there have been 100% moves over one to two year periods" [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Underlying business values for established firms (Disney, Boeing, Wal-Mart) are "not fluctuating nearly as widely as the prices," so "there should be great opportunity, yet there are not many winners in the market" [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Greenblatt declines to provide a causal theory — "I don't know and I don't care" — and instead anchors the strategy on exploiting the gap rather than explaining it [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- The divergence is exploitable because Mr. Market is rational over two-to-three-year horizons even though irrational over one-to-two-year horizons [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
