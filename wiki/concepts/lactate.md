# Lactate

## Summary

Lactate is a by-product of [[concepts/glycolytic-system|anaerobic glycolysis]] that, together with hydrogen ions and [[concepts/atp|ATP]], signals that muscle conditions are approaching the limits of sustainable work — it is a fatigue *signal*, not simply a waste product or the cause of "the burn" [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].

## Key claims

- Lactate is formed when pyruvate from glycolysis is converted to lactate alongside positively charged H⁺ and NAD⁺ ions [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
- The NAD⁺ produced is reusable in further metabolic processes, while accumulating H⁺ ions push the muscles and blood toward acidic conditions [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
- Older models attributed the "burning" sensation of hard riding to acidity from H⁺ accumulation; current evidence (Pollak et al., 2014) frames the sensation as a combined signal from lactate, H⁺, and ATP indicating that muscle conditions are nearing damaging levels and intensity must drop [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
- Even when aerobic capacity is sufficient to handle pyruvate production, some pyruvate is still converted to lactate and H⁺; training fat oxidation slows glycolysis and reduces lactate/H⁺ output (Bassett & Howley, 2000) [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
- Whether pyruvate becomes lactate or enters the aerobic system depends on muscle oxygen availability and the muscle's aerobic capacity (Grey et al., 2014) [[sources/pdf-21a07e2e3e63]].
