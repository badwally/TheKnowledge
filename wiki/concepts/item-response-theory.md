# Item response theory (IRT)

## Summary

Item response theory (IRT) is a hierarchical Bayesian modelling framework for analysing categorical or graded responses to a set of items, in which person-specific latent traits and item parameters are estimated jointly.

## Key claims

- Item response theory is one of the canonical examples of hierarchical Bayesian models for clustered data, alongside mixed/multilevel models and structural equation models [[sources/yt-WJ1BvfOm-94]].
- In IRT the cluster corresponds to a person and the units correspond to items, with y a multivariate vector of responses to different items per person [[sources/yt-WJ1BvfOm-94]].
- In Bayesian IRT the person-level latent traits ζ_j are typically treated as parameters, although in non-Bayesian formulations they would be treated as random variables [[sources/yt-WJ1BvfOm-94]].
- The distinction between marginal and conditional versions of predictive information criteria is illustrated in the talk with an IRT example to show that the distinction matters substantively in practice [[sources/yt-WJ1BvfOm-94]].
