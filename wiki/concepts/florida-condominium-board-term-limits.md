# Florida Condominium Board Member Term Limits

## Summary

Florida Statute §718.112(2)(d) imposes a statutory cap on consecutive board service for members of Florida residential condominium boards. A board member may not serve more than 8 consecutive years, with two exceptions: (1) an affirmative vote of unit owners representing two-thirds of all votes cast in the election, or (2) when there are not enough eligible candidates to fill the vacancies on the board at the time of the vacancy. The clock starts at July 1, 2018 — only service on or after that date counts toward the limit — and the limit does not apply to boards of nonresidential or timeshare condominiums [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].

## Key claims

- A board member may not serve more than 8 consecutive years unless approved by an affirmative vote of unit owners representing two-thirds of all votes cast in the election or unless there are not enough eligible candidates to fill the vacancies on the board at the time of the vacancy [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Only board service that occurs on or after July 1, 2018 may be used when calculating a board member's term limit [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- The term-limit provision of §718.112(2)(d)2 does not limit the term of a member of the board of a nonresidential or timeshare condominium [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Board members may serve terms longer than 1 year if permitted by the bylaws or articles of incorporation [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- Except in a timeshare or nonresidential condominium (or if the staggered term of a board member does not expire until a later annual meeting, or if all members' terms would otherwise expire but there are no candidates), the terms of all board members expire at the annual meeting, and such members may stand for reelection unless prohibited by the bylaws [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- A person delinquent in the payment of any assessment due to the association is not eligible to be a candidate for board membership and may not be listed on the ballot [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- A person convicted of any felony in Florida (or any offense in another jurisdiction that would be a felony in Florida) is ineligible for board membership unless the felon's civil rights have been restored for at least 5 years as of the date such person seeks election [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
- The validity of an action by the board is not affected if it is later determined that a board member was ineligible due to a felony conviction [[sources/web-2018-07-01-694]].
