# Reverse hedging

## Summary

The mirror image of the Thorp-Kassouf basic system: short the common stock and long an underpriced warrant or convertible, applied in "Beat the Market" (1967) when the warrant-stock diagram identifies warrants priced below their fair-value relationship to the underlying common [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].

## Key claims

- Introduced in chapter 8 ("More on Warrants and Hedging") under the heading "Reverse hedging," alongside discussion of "Spotting candidates for reverse hedging" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Extended in chapter 10 ("The General System") to convertible bonds — including a worked example called "Reverse hedging with Collins Radio 'warrants'" — and a section on "Best candidates for reverse hedging" among convertible-bond situations [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Sits alongside "What determines warrant prices?" and "What is a warrant worth?" in chapter 8, framing reverse hedging as the response when fair-value analysis identifies a warrant as cheap rather than rich [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Treated as part of the same convertible-arbitrage toolkit as the basic system, applied symmetrically depending on whether the warrant or convertible is overpriced (basic system) or underpriced (reverse hedging) [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
