# Value investing cycle

## Summary

The observation that value-investing strategies generate strong long-run excess returns precisely because they undergo multi-year periods of underperformance that drive less-disciplined practitioners out of the strategy; in Joel Greenblatt's Columbia class, the cycle is summarized as "value investing works because it doesn't work all the time" and grounded in the Pzena benchmark and the O'Shaughnessy cautionary tale [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].

## Key claims

- Greenblatt frames the principle directly: "Value investing works, but it tends to work in cycles" and "value investing works because it doesn't work all the time" [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Long-run benchmark cited from Richard Pzena: 1960-2005 returns of 16.3% per year for a Low P/E, Low P/Sales benchmark versus 11% for the S&P 500, a 5.3-percentage-point spread — but with five-year-or-more periods of underperformance [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Worst-cycle example: in 1995-2000, the same value benchmark returned 71% versus the S&P 500's 163%, an underperformance of 91% [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Behavioral consequence: Pzena lost 70% of his investors during the 1995-2000 drawdown; only four of his original investors remained from his $14 billion in AUM at the time of the class [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Cautionary tale: James P. O'Shaughnessy, author of "What Works on Wall Street," started a fund in 1996-1997 based on his book, underperformed the market by 25% over three years, then sold his company to Hennessy Funds at the end of 1999 — "the bottom of the cycle" — just before his strategy would have rewarded patience [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Vindication of patience: Hennessy's HFCGX (Cornerstone Growth) and HFCVX (Cornerstone Value) funds, which patented O'Shaughnessy's strategies, returned 13.44% and 6.47% per year respectively versus -2.01% per year for the Vanguard 500 over 6/1/00 through 5/31/05 [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Greenblatt's own cycle: down 5% in 1998-1999 worried about a bubble breaking, kept doing what he was doing, then up 130% in 2000 when the markets came back [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
- Greenblatt's pedagogical conclusion: understanding why value investing has to work over time is "the only way you can stick it out" through the underperformance cycles [[sources/pdf-d0b9b7e97b5c]].
