# FX Spot Transaction

## Summary

The exchange of one currency for another for immediate delivery — by market convention settling two mutual business days (T+2) after the trade date — at the spot exchange rate, which serves as the benchmark price for the underlying value of the currency and the reference from which all other FX value-date prices are computed [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].

## Key claims

- An "outright" is a transaction in which two parties simply exchange one currency for another; if exchange is for immediate delivery the outright is a spot transaction, if for forward delivery it is a forward [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- The two parties to an outright must agree on the two currencies, the amount of one currency, the settlement date, and the exchange rate; the amount of the second currency is derived from the first amount and the exchange rate [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- By market convention, FX trades settle T+2 (two mutual business days after trade date) unless otherwise specified; this is "value for spot" [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- The settlement date may not fall on a day that is a national holiday in either country [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- Exception: spot for the Canadian Dollar against the USD is one business day later (T+1) rather than T+2 [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- Rates for value dates other than spot are always calculated relative to the spot rate [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
- The spot exchange rate is the benchmark price the market uses to express the underlying value of the currency [[sources/pdf-4b87a8059b38]].
