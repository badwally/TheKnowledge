# Pavement maintenance and rehabilitation (M&R) effectiveness

## Summary

Pavement maintenance and rehabilitation (M&R) effectiveness is the magnitude of improvement in pavement condition and/or slowing of pavement deterioration that follows the application of a given M&R activity to a highway pavement section. Effectiveness estimates feed directly into highway-agency decisions about which M&R activity to apply, but quantifying effectiveness from observational data is complicated by the self-selected nature of the sample of treated sections, requiring structured econometric approaches to obtain consistent estimates.

## Key claims

- In selecting the type of M&R activity to apply to highway pavement sections, engineers rely on several items of information, foremost among which is the effectiveness of the various M&R activities in slowing pavement deterioration and improving its condition [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- A common analytic approach is to develop separate models of pavement deterioration for each type of M&R activity in order to quantify activity-specific effectiveness [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- Such per-activity deterioration models have often been unsatisfactory in practice, producing poor fits to data or counterintuitive signs of important variable coefficients [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- Madanat and Mishalani (1998) propose recovering consistent M&R effectiveness estimates by combining a discrete agency-choice model with a continuous pavement-response model per activity, jointly estimated to correct for the self-selected nature of the sample [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
- A case study applying this approach to data from the Indiana Department of Transportation demonstrated the value and applicability of the methodology for M&R effectiveness estimation [[sources/web-1998-09-20-413]].
