# Bearish Order Block

## Summary

In Lesson 2: Market Structure, a bearish order block is the set of green (up-closed) candles immediately preceding a displacement move down — marked with a box and treated as the supply zone where sellers are going short, where price is expected to retrace before continuing lower [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].

## Key claims

- Bearish order blocks are formed by up-closed (green) candles when combined with displacement down on any time frame [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- These zones are equivalent to a supply zone for supply-and-demand traders — the area of "resistance" [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Whether there is a series of green candles or only one green candle before the displacement down, it does not matter — the trader marks the pre-displacement green candles with a box [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Bearish order blocks showcase where sellers are going short [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The simplifying rule from Lesson 2: "GREEN candles before displacement DOWN are BEARISH order blocks" [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- In the Lesson-2 January 2022 worked example, price ran short stops above a swing high on low volume, consolidated for 6–8 days, then made a one-day energetic move down whose magnitude equalled the two-day move up that preceded the consolidation — the green up-closed candles inside that consolidation are the bearish order block [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- After the bearish-OB-creating displacement, the lesson sets the playbook as: wait for price to retrace back to the daily bearish imbalance and then go short, with the protective stop at the most recent swing high (which often coincides with the order-block boundary itself) [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Lesson 2 takes the daily hammer candle that forms after price reaches the prior expansion zone as the trigger to "get excited" and look for the retrace-to-OB short entry [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
