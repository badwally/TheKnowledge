# Portfolio concentration

## Summary

Portfolio concentration is one of the two factors — together with fund size — into which van der Beck, Bouchaud, and Villamaina (2024) decompose fund illiquidity; concentrated portfolios are a precondition for self-inflated returns to become economically large [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].

## Key claims

- Fund illiquidity decomposes into the product of portfolio concentration and fund size [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- Most funds are either concentrated (small specialized funds) or large (diversified index trackers); when concentrated funds become large, fund illiquidity spikes and self-inflated returns become significant [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- The importance of self-inflated returns in explaining funds' overall returns increases monotonically in both portfolio concentration and fund size [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- The collapse of Archegos Capital Management is presented as the high-profile illustration that when investment funds trade concentrated positions, portfolio returns can be driven by the funds' own price impact [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- For ETFs, holding concentrated positions in individual stocks causes the fund to accumulate substantial ownership in those securities as it grows, which the ETF arbitrage mechanism then translates into price impact in the underlying [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
