# Reciprocal Deposits

## Summary

Reciprocal deposits are a feature of network deposit-placement services in which member banks exchange their own large customer deposits for a matching amount of deposits from other network member banks in insurable (<$250,000) increments, allowing the originating bank to provide customers with multi-bank FDIC coverage while keeping the equivalent funded total on balance sheet [[sources/web-2008-01-01-0ad]].

## Key claims

- The reciprocal-deposits feature enables network banks to exchange their own customers' large deposits for a matching amount of deposits from other member banks in insurable increments under $250,000 [[sources/web-2008-01-01-0ad]].
- Reciprocal exchange allows banks to attract and retain large, safety-conscious customers with or without keeping the deposits on balance sheet [[sources/web-2008-01-01-0ad]].
- Network members can also buy deposits placed in ICS and CDARS from other network members that are flush with cash, providing a way to diversify the balance sheet with funding that counts as deposits rather than borrowings [[sources/web-2008-01-01-0ad]].
- The full amount of customer funds placed through the service can stay local to support lending opportunities at the originating community bank [[sources/web-2008-01-01-0ad]].
