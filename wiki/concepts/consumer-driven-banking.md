# Consumer-Driven Banking (Open Banking)

## Summary

Consumer-driven banking — internationally known as open banking — is a regulated framework that lets individuals and businesses share their financial data with approved service providers of their choice through secure APIs, rather than via screen scraping [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]]. Canada's framework is built around three public-policy objectives: (1) competition, innovation, and economic growth; (2) consumer financial well-being and protection; (3) safety and soundness of the financial sector [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].

## Key claims

- Approximately nine million Canadians currently share their financial data via screen scraping, exposing them to security, liability, and privacy risks; the framework is designed to replace this practice with API-mediated access [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The framework is intended to enable new financial products — including using timely rental-payment data to demonstrate creditworthiness, broader loan adjudication with better pricing, and integration between banking data and accounting/tax/payroll software for SMBs [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The Department of Finance asserts that regulated open-banking frameworks in other countries have been shown to empower consumers, enhance data accessibility, and support new financial service providers and business models [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Phase 1 (read access) covers banks above a retail-volume threshold with opt-in for other financial institutions; Phase 2 will consider "write access" — the ability to initiate actions such as payments or product enrollment across participating entities [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The UK's Data (Use and Access) Bill — a forward-looking "open data" extension of UK open banking — is projected to deliver a £10 billion economic boost over 10 years, cited by the Canadian framework as evidence for the broader economic case [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- The framework includes safeguards to protect national security and the integrity of the financial sector; the Minister of Finance holds national-security authorities supported by security and intelligence agencies [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
- Implementation and oversight are delegated to the Bank of Canada under a cost-recovery model, leveraging the Bank's existing supervisory role under the Retail Payment Activities Act [[sources/web-2025-11-06-ff1]].
