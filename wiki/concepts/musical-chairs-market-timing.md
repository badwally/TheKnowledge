# Musical Chairs Market Timing

## Summary

A childhood-game metaphor Ari Kiev uses in "Trading to Win" (1998) to describe trade-timing discipline: the trader must time their entry so that they don't jump for a chair before the music has stopped, but also must not linger too long after the music stops; transposed onto stocks, the trader must wait through more rises and falls than is comfortable to maximize profit, but must exit before the rationalizing-and-hoping trap of the downturn locks them in [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].

## Key claims

- Reading the market's direction and the directions of specific stocks is essential to trading success — Kiev compares it to the childhood game of musical chairs [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].
- In musical chairs, the player must time the move so that they do not jump for a chair before the music has stopped, and also do not linger too long after the music stops [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].
- Transposed onto stocks: the trader must stay in longer — through more rises and falls — than they may have the patience for in order to maximize profit, but must not stay in so long that, by trying to catch the "tops," they end up stuck on the downturn [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].
- Kiev frames the discipline as one of cutting losses by acknowledging a failed situation, rather than rationalizing, hoping, and convincing oneself that a stock will eventually turn around — a behavior pattern that generates losses instead of cutting them [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].
