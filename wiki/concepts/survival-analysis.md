# Survival analysis

## Summary

Survival analysis is a class of statistical methods for estimating survival curves and hazard rates that characterize time-dependent failure probabilities, applied in predictive maintenance to model the prognostics of machine components.

## Key claims

- Survival analysis is used in predictive maintenance to estimate survival curves and hazard rates that characterize time-dependent failure probabilities of machine components [[sources/pubmed-35595821]].
- Both parametric and non-parametric models can be used in survival analysis to estimate survival curves and hazard rates [[sources/pubmed-35595821]].
- In the context of mobile work equipment, hazard functions for different machine components are complex and versatile and are best modeled via non-parametric survival estimators, though Generalized-gamma and Weibull models can be appropriate for individual machine components [[sources/pubmed-35595821]].
- Different types of censoring can mask the presence of different populations in survival data, causing severe problems for statistical estimators and the interpretations of results [[sources/pubmed-35595821]].
