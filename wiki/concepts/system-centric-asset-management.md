# System-Centric Asset Management

## Summary

System-centric asset management treats infrastructure as an interconnected system whose performance, risk, and financial effort must be jointly optimised, in contrast to component-centric approaches that prioritise interventions on an individual asset basis (e.g., like-for-like replacement). It is the methodological frame underpinning AWARE-P and other modern utility planning platforms.

## Key claims

- A system-centric approach is positioned against component-centric asset management — specifically, approaches such as like-for-like prioritization and replacement — on the grounds that the complexity of urban water infrastructure planning cannot be addressed by component-by-component decisions [[sources/web-2012-01-01-57d]].
- System-centric methods aim to ensure the best possible compromise between three competing objectives — performance (level-of-service), risk, and financial effort — rather than optimising any one objective in isolation [[sources/web-2012-01-01-57d]].
- The motivation for system-centric methods is the combination of high levels of deferred maintenance and rehabilitation and overwhelming investment needs in urban water services infrastructure, which together demand wise spending and innovative, efficient planning [[sources/web-2012-01-01-57d]].
- System-centric asset management is operationalised in AWARE-P through a software platform that hosts methods and models for assessing water systems over multiple planning horizons [[sources/web-2012-01-01-57d]].
