# Fed Call vs Fed Put

## Summary

Framing introduced by Zoltan Pozsar in May 2022 to describe a regime change in the FOMC's posture toward risk assets — from writing a put option on stocks (the "Fed put," supportive when prices fall) to writing a call option (capping upside and demanding lower prices) in service of slaying inflation [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].

## Key claims

- Pozsar argues the Fed is now in the business of writing a call option on risk assets — not just stocks, but housing and crypto as well [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Whether one thinks of the FOMC's target level for the stock market and financial conditions as a call option, or as a put option just with a lower strike price, is semantics — the operative point is that the Fed wants lower asset prices [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Pozsar declines to provide a target for the S&P 500 but reasons through three reference levels: at 4,000 the Fed appears unsatisfied, at 3,500 the post-pandemic gains would be erased (still feels like a put with a lower strike), and at 2,500 some pre-pandemic gains would also be eaten into [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Stocks are not the only egg in the wealth basket — housing and crypto wealth declines also determine how much damage will need to be done to stocks for financial conditions to tighten enough [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- A Fed writing a call option on the stock market needs to see a deeper correction big enough to slow demand and push unemployment higher [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
