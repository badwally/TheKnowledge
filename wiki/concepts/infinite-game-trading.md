# Infinite-Game Mindset Applied to Trading

## Summary

Application of Simon Sinek's finite-vs-infinite-game framework — from his book "The Infinite Game" — to trading, where the trader rejects per-trade or short-term performance metrics in favor of long-term sustainability, adaptability, and resilience as a participant in a market that has no defined endpoint [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].

## Key claims

- In a finite game (football, chess) players are known, rules are fixed, and the endpoint is clear — the objective is to win; in an infinite game (business, politics) players come and go, the rules are changeable, and there is no defined endpoint, so there are no winners or losers, only "ahead and behind" [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].
- A finite-game approach to trading focuses on individual trades or short-term performance metrics, where traders aim to "win" by making a profit on each transaction or beating the market in a given time frame; the infinite-game approach instead focuses on sustainability and adaptability over the long term [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].
- An "infinite trader" does not obsess over the outcome of each trade and is more concerned with developing a robust trading strategy that can withstand market volatility and evolve over time; success is measured by maintaining a resilient portfolio and achieving personal financial goals over many years rather than outperforming others in the short term [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].
- Infinite traders prioritize five behaviors: continuous learning and adaptability, risk management (managing risk effectively so losses can be absorbed and the trader can continue participating), long-term goals (financial objectives over short-term excitement), emotional discipline (not letting wins or losses dictate self-worth), and strategic vision (a clear understanding of why they trade and what they hope to achieve beyond making money) [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].
- Infinite traders are less likely to chase trends or react emotionally to short-term market movements because they understand the market is a complex, ever-changing system with multitudes of participants and no true "end" to the game [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].
- Playing the infinite game in trading is offered as the antidote to burnout and the emotional rollercoaster associated with trying to win in a finite sense, especially for those who do this professionally to make a living [[sources/pdf-b72c5d424466]].
