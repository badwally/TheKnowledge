# Expert Elicitation

## Summary

Expert elicitation is the structured process of quantifying expert knowledge as probability distributions over uncertain quantities, used to support inference and decision-making when hard empirical data are sparse.

## Key claims

- Expert elicitation is increasingly important for quantifying expert knowledge in situations where hard data are sparse, which is often the context in which difficult policy decisions must be made [[sources/web-2026-05-13-b67]].
- Eliciting from a group of experts, rather than a single expert, is generally treated as important so that the range of knowledge and opinions across the expert community is synthesised [[sources/web-2026-05-13-b67]].
- Despite a growing role for elicitation, there has historically been little in the way of training and support available to those who wish to conduct elicitations, motivating the development of structured packages such as SHELF [[sources/web-2026-05-13-b67]].
