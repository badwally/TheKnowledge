# William McChesney Martin Moment (1967)

## Summary

Kai Volatility's Q2 2022 framing for a less-discussed pre-Volcker Federal Reserve tightening episode: from June 1967 to June 1969, William McChesney Martin's Fed hiked the funds rate "a gut-wrenching 7%" from 3.5% to 10.5%, the recession that followed lasted 11 months (December 1969 to November 1970), and despite mounting unemployment "inflation was barely affected" — the empirical counterexample Kai Volatility uses to argue that demand-side Fed tightening has structural limits when inflation is driven by political forces [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].

## Key claims

- "Before the heralded 'Volcker Moment' in 1979 was the less discussed 'William McChesney Martin Moment' in 1967" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- From June 1967 to June 1969 the Federal Reserve ratcheted the fed funds rate up "a gut-wrenching 7%" from 3.5% to 10.5% [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- The recession that followed lasted for 11 months, beginning in December 1969 and ending in November 1970 [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- "Despite mounting unemployment, unlike in the case of Volcker's heralded dual recession, inflation was barely affected" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- The episode foreshadowed the 1971 Nixon price-and-wage freeze: "faith was lost in the Federal Reserve, as the economy declined into stagflation. In 1971 critical economists and politicians called for the government to intervene directly and freeze prices and wages" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Per Kai Volatility, the lessons of this period have been "broadly whitewashed, largely ignored by the Fed and history" — the lesson being Burns's: when structural inflation is driven by underlying political forces, the Federal Reserve's capacity to curb it is "very limited" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Kai Volatility uses the Martin Moment to undercut the modern Volcker-emulation playbook by showing that the Fed had already tried the same tool (large rapid hikes) earlier in the 1965-1980 inflation and the tool failed to work [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
