# Rail Surface Defects

## Summary

A family of structural deteriorations that develop on the running surface of railway tracks under traffic loading — including squats, rolling contact fatigue, gauge corner cracking, wheel burn, and corrugation — whose unattended progression can result in safety risks and rail breaks.

## Key claims

- Rail surfaces deteriorate and develop structural defects such as cracks and squats over time; if left unattended, these defects can result in everything from poor ride quality to a safety risk [[sources/web-2026-03-04-157]].
- Loads applied to the rail profile drive degradation of the rail surface, leading to Rolling Contact Fatigue (RCF), squats, and corrugation [[sources/web-2026-03-04-157]].
- Surface-initiated defects mainly appear due to traffic density and axle load increment, while subsurface-initiated defects result from metallurgical faults [[sources/web-2026-03-04-157]].
- Unattended defects may result in a rail break and subsequent profound safety and operational consequences [[sources/web-2026-03-04-157]].
- Upon identification, two main maintenance options are available — surface grinding and replacement; grinding may be conducted to mitigate some types of defects such as corrugation but is ineffective for others such as severe squats, where replacement of the affected rail must be conducted [[sources/web-2026-03-04-157]].
- The surface defect types considered in the Khalaj et al. study, verified by metallurgists, are squats, rolling contact fatigue, gauge corner cracking, wheel burn, and corrugation; squats were the most frequently recorded surface defect [[sources/web-2026-03-04-157]].
