# Bayesian Brain

## Summary

The "Bayesian brain" is the family of accounts on which neural processing implements probabilistic inference over the hidden causes of sensory input, computing and updating probability distributions rather than reading the world off bottom-up feature maps [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]]. Clark's 2013 BBS target article lists "Bayesian brain" as one of its keywords and treats it as the umbrella under which hierarchical predictive coding and predictive processing sit [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].

## Key claims

- A single sensory effect is consistent with many sets of causes distinguished only by their relative, context-dependent probability of occurrence, so the brain must compute multiple probability distributions [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].
- The bidirectional cortical hierarchy implements a version of "empirical Bayes" (Robbins 1956), in which higher-level models supply priors for lower-level inference and the priors themselves are progressively tuned by sensory input [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].
- The Bayesian conception traces back through Helmholtz (1860) and was carried into cognitive psychology by MacKay (1956), Neisser (1967), and Gregory (1980) under the heading of "analysis-by-synthesis" [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].
