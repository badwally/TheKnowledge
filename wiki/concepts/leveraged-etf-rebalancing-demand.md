# Leveraged ETF rebalancing demand

## Summary

Leveraged ETFs (LETFs) seek to deliver a multiple of the daily market return of their underlying, which forces them to rebalance their hedge in the direction of the day's price move — a structural source of end-of-day, price-insensitive flow that Baltussen, Da, Lammers, and Martens (2020) use as their second piece of identification for the hedging-demand explanation of market intraday momentum [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].

## Key claims

- LETFs seek to deliver a multiple of the daily market return of their underlying, which fixes the rebalance to the day's close and makes the hedging demand a function of the LETF's market capitalization and stated leverage [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
- As of the end of February 2009, Cheng and Madhavan (2010) estimated that LETF rebalancing made up 16.8% (50.2%) of market-on-close volume on a day when the market moved 1% (5%) [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
- Shum et al. (2015) argue that market-on-close orders carry fill risk, so LETF hedging can begin as early as 30 minutes before the close — which fits the empirical fact that rest-of-day returns predict last-half-hour returns [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
- Hedging demand from LETFs varies considerably in the cross-section and over time; Baltussen et al. find strong cross-sectional and time-series evidence that LETF hedging demand on a particular index drives the magnitude of that index's market-intraday-momentum pattern [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
- LETF market makers "have little discretion but to hedge at the end of the day," which is one of the five reasons the paper offers for why intraday hedging is concentrated in the last 30 minutes [[sources/pdf-2742492120a8]].
