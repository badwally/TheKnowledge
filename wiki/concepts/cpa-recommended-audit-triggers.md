# CPA-Recommended Audit/Review Triggers for HOAs

## Summary

HOACPA, a CPA firm specializing in homeowners-association financial work, publishes an enumerated list of ten circumstances under which it strongly suggests an HOA commission a CPA Review or Audit even when not required by the Davis-Stirling Act's §5305 $75,000 gross-income threshold. The list operationalizes practitioner judgment about elevated risk conditions — unusual cash events, weak internal controls, governance transitions, financial distress, and assessment-volume scale — that warrant independent CPA assurance beyond the statutory floor [[sources/web-2018-08-01-f87]].

## Key claims

- HOACPA identifies ten particular circumstances under which a Review or Audit is recommended even when §5305 does not require one [[sources/web-2018-08-01-f87]].
- Trigger 1 — large amounts of money received for unusual circumstances, with construction-defect settlements and large insurance proceeds named as examples [[sources/web-2018-08-01-f87]].
- Trigger 2 — unusual replacement-fund/reserve activity, including large reserve projects, severe underfunding, or reserve not meeting budgeted expectations [[sources/web-2018-08-01-f87]].
- Trigger 3 — change in management companies, to verify correct balances are carried over and properly set up on the new books [[sources/web-2018-08-01-f87]].
- Trigger 4 — little or no internal control (e.g., self-managed association where the treasurer writes checks, signs checks, and prepares bank reconciliations) [[sources/web-2018-08-01-f87]].
- Trigger 5 — board not receiving financial statements on at least a quarterly basis, especially where no standard accounting system is in place [[sources/web-2018-08-01-f87]].
- Trigger 6 — suspected fraud or embezzlement; HOACPA advises disclosing the suspicion to the CPA before engagement, as a forensic accountant referral may replace a standard GAAP/GAAS audit [[sources/web-2018-08-01-f87]].
- Trigger 7 — significant political infighting among board members or unpleasant board transitions (e.g., recall) [[sources/web-2018-08-01-f87]].
- Trigger 8 — developer turnover of association control to homeowners, recommended even when not required by state law [[sources/web-2018-08-01-f87]].
- Trigger 9 — association in financial difficulty, where added professional scrutiny is most needed despite cost pressure [[sources/web-2018-08-01-f87]].
- Trigger 10 — associations with over $500,000 in annual assessments, regardless of whether §5305's gross-income trigger is met [[sources/web-2018-08-01-f87]].
- The $500,000 annual-assessments trigger (Trigger 10) is a CPA-firm recommendation, not a Davis-Stirling Act statutory threshold; the only statutory CA threshold is the $75,000 gross-income Review trigger at §5305 [[sources/web-2018-08-01-f87]].
- The list applies to both Reviews and Audits — HOACPA does not separate the ten triggers into Review-only versus Audit-only categories, leaving engagement-type selection to practitioner-board judgment based on the specific circumstance [[sources/web-2018-08-01-f87]].
