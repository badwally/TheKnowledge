# Ontario Common Elements Definition

## Summary

The Ontario Condominium Act, 1998 defines "common elements" by exclusion — as all the property except the units — establishing a residual-category architecture that drives downstream rules for maintenance, repair, reserve-fund obligations, insurance, and access [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]]. The companion definition of "common expenses" is deliberately non-exhaustive: it covers expenses related to the performance of the corporation's objects and duties plus anything specified as a common expense by the Act, the regulations, or the declaration — giving the declaration a co-equal role with the statute and regulations in defining what is chargeable to owners [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].

## Key claims

- "Common elements" means all the property except the units [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Common expenses" means the expenses related to the performance of the objects and duties of a corporation and all expenses specified as common expenses in the Act, in the regulations, or in a declaration [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Common surplus" means the excess of all receipts of the corporation over the expenses of the corporation [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- "Common interest" is the interest in the common elements appurtenant to a unit (in standard freehold corporations) or to the owner's parcel of land described in the declaration (in common elements condominium corporations) [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- The Table of Contents enumerates a separate Part dedicated to common elements condominium corporations — including its own creation, declaration-content, description-content, by-laws, disclosure-statement, and substantial-damage provisions — reflecting that common elements can also constitute the entirety of the property held by a unit-less corporation [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
- The Table of Contents enumerates discrete sections for Repair after damage, Repair, Maintenance, Property insurance, Proceeds, Double coverage, Other insurance, and Deductible within the financial-obligations cluster — all of which operate on the common-elements category as defined by exclusion [[sources/web-2014-07-24-031]].
