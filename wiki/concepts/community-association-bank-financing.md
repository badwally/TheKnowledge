# Community Association Bank Financing

## Summary

Community association bank financing is the mechanism by which a chartered depository extends a loan directly to a homeowners association (HOA) — not to individual unit owners — with the association repaying the loan using funds collected from homeowner assessments over multiple years [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]]. The structure is positioned as a third option for HOA boards funding a capital improvement, alongside (i) drawing from cash reserves and (ii) charging homeowners a special assessment [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]]. Banks that specialize in this segment market themselves as offering more than capital, bringing HOA-specific industry knowledge to guide boards and management companies through loan selection, homeowner communication, and operational coordination with HOA accountants and attorneys [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].

## Key claims

- The loan is made directly to the association, not to individual unit owners [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- The association repays the loan using funds collected from homeowner assessments over multiple years [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- The approach helps avoid large, upfront payments while allowing the association to continue contributing to its reserve funds [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Financing allows the association to complete all phases of a project at once, maintaining a consistent community appearance while spreading repayment over time [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Banks specializing in community association lending bring industry knowledge to guide HOAs and management companies through the entire financing process, from loan selection to homeowner communication [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- Experienced HOA bankers can attend HOA meetings to explain loan terms and answer resident questions [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- HOA banking specialists can help management companies with questions related to best practices for financial operations around HOA financing, working closely with accountants and attorneys [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
- When evaluating lenders, HOA boards should consider financial strength as well as experience; banks with long histories serving HOAs and strong balance sheets are positioned as better able to fund projects on favorable terms and provide dependable ongoing support [[sources/web-2026-04-14-940]].
