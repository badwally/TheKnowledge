# Cyclotourism

## Summary

Cyclotourism is a non-competitive form of cycling that strings together multi-day, scenic stages so riders can cover a region's geography and culture rather than race a clock — exemplified by the [[entities/gt-20|GT 20]], pitched as a cyclo-tourist itinerary across [[entities/corsica|Corsica]] designed for all levels of cyclist [[sources/pdf-432fe168a2d1]].

## Key claims

- A cyclo-tourist itinerary is presented as a multi-stage route accessible to 'all types of cyclists' — experts and enthusiasts alike — rather than only competitive racers [[sources/pdf-432fe168a2d1]].
- The format pairs the riding with non-cycling experiences such as tasting locally sourced products, meeting producers, and exploring scenic mountain and ocean landscapes [[sources/pdf-432fe168a2d1]].
- Pacing is flexible and matched to ability: the GT 20 is suggested at 5–8 days for intermediate riders, with beginners taking a slower, longer trip [[sources/pdf-432fe168a2d1]].
- The format is bicycle-agnostic — the GT 20 brochure explicitly invites both standard-bike and electric-bike riders [[sources/pdf-432fe168a2d1]].
- Commercial operators sell guided, vehicle-supported variants of cyclotourism as packaged holidays — e.g., [[entities/marmot-tours|Marmot Tours]]'s Classic Cols range layers two guides, support vans, hotel-to-hotel logistics, and a [[concepts/flexible-route-cycling-tour|tiered Easier/Classic/Challenge]] route menu over the same scenic-multi-day cycling format [[sources/pdf-f03d709c7ff7]].
