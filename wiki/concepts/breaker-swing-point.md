# Breaker Swing Point

## Summary

In ICT-style price-action analysis, the breaker swing point is the optimal institutional trade entry pattern: a false break above an old high (or below an old low) followed by a rejection and quick move away, which produces the deepest-discount buy and the most-premium sell available in market structure — psychologically scary, but the pattern that most reliably anticipates institutional sponsorship [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].

## Key claims

- The breaker pattern is a swing point that offers outstanding entry points, but it can be scary due to the fear of reversal [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- The breaker swing point is a false break above an old high rejection that quickly moves away, and it is described as the best pattern in market structure to anticipate institutional evidences [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- The bearish swing point with a bearish breaker is given as an example of a trigger point, and when the high is broken, that becomes the trigger point [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- The breaker swing point and the failure swing are described as the two conceptual ways to view the marketplace in terms of swing points [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- Breaker is characterized as the optimal trade entry pattern, as it is the deepest discount buy and the most premium sell — scary but encourages confidence building [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- In a demo account, traders can practice stepping in front of the marketplace to see what happens to the price [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
- Once the pattern breaks down, it becomes a trigger to sell or buy [[sources/pdf-734e612fdf31]].
