# Canadian Condominium Corporation — Non-Income Treatment of Fee Surplus

## Summary

Any excess of a Canadian condominium corporation's member fees and contributions over the corporation's expenditures for the year is NOT considered income of the corporation for federal income-tax purposes — this is CRA's administrative position in paragraph 3 of Interpretation Bulletin IT-304R2 [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]]. This treatment is structurally distinct from the treatment of reserve-fund interest, rental income, and other 'incidental' income, all of which are treated as income of the corporation (though typically exempt from Part I tax under the NPO regime) [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]]. The non-income characterization of fee surplus is independent of NPO status: it would apply even to a condominium corporation that lost its NPO exemption by carrying on a business.

## Key claims

- Generally, the expenditures of a condominium corporation are met by its members on a proportionate basis [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- Any excess of the members' condominium fees and contributions over the corporation's expenditures for the year is not considered to be income of the corporation, per IT-304R2 ¶ 3 [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- This non-income treatment is administratively distinct from — and operates in parallel with — the tax treatment of reserve-fund interest, rental income, and other incidental income, all of which IS income of the corporation [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
- The non-income characterization of fee surplus arises from the nature of the members' contributions, not from the corporation's NPO status; the income-character analysis is independent of paragraph 149(1)(*l*) qualification [[sources/web-2002-09-05-ce9]].
