# Convertible securities arbitrage

## Summary

The general framework articulated in chapter 10 of Thorp and Kassouf's "Beat the Market" (1967) for trading warrants, convertible bonds, convertible preferreds, puts, and calls jointly with their underlying common stocks based on predictable price relationships — the umbrella under which the "basic system" hedge and "reverse hedging" sit [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].

## Key claims

- The book's introduction frames the strategy as predicting and analyzing "the price relationships which exist between convertible securities (warrants, convertible bonds, convertible preferreds, puts, and calls) and their common stock" — and notes "we do not need to predict prices of individual securities in order to win" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- More than 300 of the 3,500 securities traded on the New York and American stock exchanges at the time of writing are convertibles, with more than 200 associated common stocks [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- The total of over 500 securities is about 15% of all the securities listed and has a market value the authors estimate at "perhaps $50 billion" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Convertible securities at the time were held in the portfolios of "several million investors" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Chapter 10 — "The General System: The Evaluation of Convertible Securities" — covers the scope of convertibles, anatomy of a convertible bond, picking convertible-bond situations, the basic system applied to convertible bonds (with a worked example using Dresser Industries "warrants"), convertible preferred stocks, call options, and "Puts, calls, and the basic system" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Chapter 8 ("More on Warrants and Hedging") extends the warrant universe to over-the-counter, regional, and Canadian warrants — covered alongside "What determines warrant prices?" and "What is a warrant worth?" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- The framework's authorial credentials cited on the title page include Kassouf's prior books "Evaluation of Convertible Securities" and "A Theory and an Econometric Model for Common Stock Purchase Warrants" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- The book's "scientific proof" of the framework rests on four pillars: a 25%/year backtest 1946-1966 (chapter 7), a statistical analysis of basic-system opportunities (Appendix E), the authors' five-year cash record (chapter 5), and a theoretical argument (Appendix C) [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
