# Index Committee Construction

## Summary

Methodology by which an index — most prominently the S&P 500 — is assembled and maintained by a human committee using soft eligibility criteria rather than mechanical rules. Mauboussin and Callahan (2024) emphasize that what the committee does *not* do (no macro forecasts, no sector limits, no factor analyses, no performance expectations) is more notable than what it does, and that this restraint is what produces the index's low turnover and long-term orientation [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].

## Key claims

- The S&P 500 is constructed by an index committee that tends to follow some criteria but has no hard and fast rules [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Eligibility criteria for inclusion include a market capitalization of at least $18 billion (with a sufficient amount that trades freely), profitability in the most recent quarter, cumulative profitability over the past four quarters, and sector representation consistent with the total market [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- The committee performs no macroeconomic forecasts, no sector or industry limits, no position weightings, no factor analyses, and no performance expectations — "more notable than what the index committee does is what it does not do" [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- The index has low turnover and invests for the long term [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- On average there are about 25 changes in the S&P 500 annually, or about 5% of the total number of stocks; one removal and one addition counts as a single change [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
