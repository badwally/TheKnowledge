# BC Strata Property Regulation §1.1 "Insured Account" Definition

## Summary

The 'insured account' definition added to §1.1 of the BC Strata Property Regulation by Order in Council 101/2019 (March 6, 2019) is the statutory anchor that resolved a multi-year interpretive dispute over whether a BC strata corporation could hold contingency reserve fund or special-levy money in any single account whose balance exceeded the $100,000 CDIC insurance limit [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]]. The definition's 'all or part' language is the operative element that made this scoping disambiguation possible — without it, the §6.11 reference to 'insured' accounts continued to admit the narrow reading that the entire deposit must be insured [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].

## Key claims

- §1.1 of the Strata Property Regulation now defines 'insured account' for the purposes of the Strata Property Act to mean 'an account with a deposit, all or part of which is eligible to be insured by the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation or guaranteed by the Credit Union Deposit Insurance Corporation of British Columbia' [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The definition was introduced by Order in Council 101/2019, announced March 6, 2019 [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The 'all or part' phrase is the central operative element: it explicitly admits accounts whose balances exceed the CDIC $100,000 limit, so long as some portion of the deposit is eligible for CDIC or CUDIC coverage [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The definition was added at the same time §6.11(a) and §6.11(b) were amended to substitute parallel 'all or part' language for the prior text, ensuring consistency across the definitions section and the investment-eligibility regime [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The Ministry's strata-housing webpage characterized the prior interpretive ambiguity as creating a 'perceived administrative burden' — strata corporations that read §6.11 narrowly believed they had to divide investments across many accounts to keep balances under the deposit insurance limit [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
- The definition operates symmetrically across the two named insurers despite their structurally different coverage regimes: CDIC offers $100,000-per-deposit insurance while CUDIC offers unlimited deposit insurance protection [[sources/web-2019-03-07-e42]].
