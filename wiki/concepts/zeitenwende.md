# Zeitenwende

## Summary

German term meaning "game changer," used by Carsten Brzeski in ING's December 2022 outlook to characterize 2022 for Europe — a year of war in the EU's backyard, an end to cheap energy, and an end to globalization as Europeans knew it, layered on top of pre-existing structural challenges like population ageing and weak international competitiveness [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].

## Key claims

- Brzeski applies the German word "Zeitenwende," which he glosses as "game changer," to capture the structural break of 2022 for Europe specifically [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- The three Zeitenwende components Brzeski names: a war in the EU's backyard which is still ongoing with no end in sight, an end to cheap energy, and an end to globalisation as Europeans knew it [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Combined with the longer-term challenges of population ageing, lack of international competitiveness, and the never-ending debate on further European integration, Europe's "to-do list is long" [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- Brzeski concludes the chances are very high that the continent will have a hard time returning to a pre-growth trajectory any time soon [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
- The Zeitenwende framing is the conceptual underpinning for ING's 2023 eurozone forecast — a mild recession followed by only very subdued growth with double-dip risk, as the region shoulders structural challenges and transitions [[sources/pdf-87087198976b]].
