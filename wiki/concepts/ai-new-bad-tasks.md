# AI-Generated New Bad Tasks

## Summary

The concept from Acemoglu (2024) that some new tasks created by AI — such as deepfakes, manipulative digital advertising, addictive social media algorithms, and AI-powered cyberattacks — have negative social value, causing GDP to overstate welfare gains; using estimates from Bursztyn et al. (2023) on social media, Acemoglu calculates that AI could appear to increase GDP by 2% while reducing welfare by −0.72% [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].

## Key claims

- The welfare function includes an externality term: W = C − E, where E captures harms from misinformation, manipulation, and addiction; new bad tasks increase revenue (and measured GDP) but reduce consumer utility [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Bursztyn et al. (2023) find that TikTok and Instagram users would pay an average of $53/month to keep using social media, but would pay an average of $19/month to get everyone off it — implying that for every $53 of revenue, there is a net negative welfare effect of $19 [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- This yields a proportionate damage ratio of −19/53 ≈ −0.36: for every dollar of revenue from manipulative new tasks, welfare declines by 36 cents [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Total 2022 revenues from Meta, Alphabet, Snapchat, TikTok, and Twitter (X) reached $460 billion, or about 1.64% of US GDP; IT security spending adds at least 0.28% of GDP; combined with estimated malicious IT attack spending, the bad-task sector totals roughly 2% of GDP [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- Applying the −0.36 ratio to 2% of GDP yields a negative welfare effect of 0.72% of GDP in consumption-equivalent units, which could more than offset the modest TFP gains [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- If manipulative uses of generative AI become more widespread, the negative welfare effects could be larger [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
- This analysis suggests caution in interpreting all GDP increases from AI as positive welfare effects [[sources/pdf-fb3e28ab676a]].
