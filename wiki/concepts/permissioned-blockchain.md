# Permissioned vs Public Blockchain

## Summary

Blockchain networks operate in one of two governance models: public chains with decentralized governance open to anyone, or private/permissioned chains operated by an administrator who controls access [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].

## Key claims

- A public blockchain relies on decentralized governance and is typically open to the public; Bitcoin is the canonical example of a public, anonymous blockchain [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- A private blockchain is operated by an administrator who performs or delegates the task of granting access to the network [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- The permissioning model directly determines antitrust exposure: identifying perpetrators is particularly difficult on public anonymous blockchains, while regulator entry points may be contentious in permissioned blockchains [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- If a permissioned blockchain becomes critical infrastructure for competing in a market, incumbents holding access credentials may have an incentive to exclude new entrants [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
