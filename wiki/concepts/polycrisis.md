# Polycrisis

## Summary

Framing concept Fidelity International uses as the headline of its 2023 Investment Outlook — the simultaneous, mutually-reinforcing crises of high inflation, aggressive monetary tightening, the Russia-Ukraine war, an energy crisis, weak consumer confidence, and political disruption — which Fidelity argues require a fundamentally cautious investor posture rather than the soft-landing optimism the market is pricing [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].

## Key claims

- Fidelity titles its Investment Outlook 2023 "Navigating the polycrisis," structuring the framing as a confluence of challenges rather than a single shock [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- The crises Fidelity catalogues include persistently high inflation, aggressive global policy tightening (led by the Federal Reserve), the continued fallout of the Russia-Ukraine war, the energy crisis, weak consumer confidence, and political disruptions [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- The polycrisis framing supports Fidelity's house view that a hard landing remains the most likely outcome in 2023 [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- The framing also drives Fidelity's call for defensive positioning — cash, uncorrelated assets, and government bonds as core multi-asset components until volatility subsides [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Fidelity argues many of the polycrisis factors are pointing to increased downside risk, while acknowledging that markets are forward-looking and sentiment will rise before data shows the economy to be on an improving track [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
- Anne Richards in her foreword identifies geopolitical uncertainty and a gradual decoupling of large economies from globalised supply and cooperation networks as polycrisis factors that may exacerbate the challenges facing asset markets in 2023 [[sources/pdf-90e7f9350657]].
