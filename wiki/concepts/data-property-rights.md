# Consumer Data Property Rights

## Summary

Consumer data property rights are a policy mechanism in which legal ownership of personal data is vested in the consumer (the data subject) rather than in the firms that collect it [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]]. In the Jones and Tonetti (2020) model of nonrival data with privacy preferences, allocating property rights to consumers can generate equilibrium data allocations that are close to the social optimum, because consumers internalize both the privacy cost of disclosure and the economic gains from selling their data to multiple firms [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].

## Key claims

- Giving data property rights to consumers can generate allocations of data use across firms that are close to the social optimum in a model with nonrival data and consumer privacy preferences [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
- Under consumer property rights, individuals balance their concerns for privacy against the economic gains that come from selling their data broadly to multiple buyers [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
- Consumer property rights are proposed as an alternative to the firm-property-rights baseline, in which firms that hold data may hoard it to deter rivals from using it productively (a creative-destruction motive), producing an inefficient allocation [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
- The Jones and Tonetti (2020) optimality result is theoretical — derived from a general-equilibrium model of nonrival data and privacy preferences — and is not an analysis of any named data collective, statute, or governance structure [[sources/web-2020-09-10-e49]].
