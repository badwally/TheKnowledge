# Warrantable vs Non-Warrantable Condominium Loans

## Summary

"Warrantable" is the industry label for a condominium-unit loan that meets the project-level eligibility standards Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac impose on the underlying condominium association — distinct from the borrower-level underwriting that governs a single-family-detached mortgage. A non-warrantable loan is one that fails those standards. The distinction is operationally consequential because non-warrantable loans typically carry larger down payments, higher interest rates, and harder qualification requirements, and because GSE eligibility depends on association-level finances, reserves, and insurance — meaning a single non-compliant association can suppress financing access (and therefore property values) for every owner in the building [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].

## Key claims

- A loan on a property that meets Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac eligibility standards is called "warrantable" [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- A loan on a property that does not meet those standards is called "non-warrantable" [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Non-warrantable loans typically require larger down payments than warrantable loans [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Non-warrantable loans typically carry higher interest rates than warrantable loans [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Non-warrantable loans typically have harder qualification requirements than warrantable loans [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac do not just evaluate the borrower — they evaluate the entire condominium project, including the association's finances, reserves, and insurance [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- If an association's finances, reserves, or insurance fail GSE standards, buyers in that community cannot obtain affordable financing, sellers cannot sell at competitive prices, and existing owners see property values undermined [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- Per the CAI Foundation for Community Association Research's survey of 700+ board members, managers, and business partners, 64% of respondents at communities already deemed ineligible reported that the denial had negatively impacted home sales or property values [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- The mechanism by which an association becomes non-warrantable is the GSE project-review process: Full Review evaluates the association's budget, reserve funding, insurance, delinquency rates, pending litigation, special assessments, and inspection reports [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
- The March 18, 2026 Fannie Mae LL-2026-03 / Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-C update tightens the project-level criteria that determine warrantability — raising the reserve-funding floor, eliminating Limited Review for >10-unit projects, and replacing the prior 50% investor concentration cap with a more flexible regime [[sources/web-2026-04-15-f75]].
