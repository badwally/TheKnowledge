# Flow-performance relationship

## Summary

The flow-performance relationship is the empirical regularity that fund flows respond to past fund returns; van der Beck, Bouchaud, and Villamaina (2024) document it at a daily frequency for ETFs and show that investors weight self-inflated and fundamental return components nearly identically, implying they cannot distinguish managerial skill from price impact [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].

## Key claims

- The paper documents a strong relationship between fund flows and fund returns at a daily frequency, with the weight investors place on more distant fund returns decaying exponentially [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- After decomposing past fund returns into a self-inflated component and a fundamental component, the flow-performance relationship is not confined to the fundamental component: investors also chase the price-impact component, with coefficients on both return components almost identical and both highly statistically significant [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- The implication is that investors are unable to differentiate between self-inflated returns (price impact) and fundamental returns (stock-picking skill) — a relatively strong constraint on the rationality of ETF investors [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- Pure return chasing may indicate Bayesian learning about the fund manager's skill in the Berk and Green (2004) sense, but the paper shows investors chase their own price impact and are hence unable to distinguish realized from expected returns [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
- The literature on flow-performance sensitivity originates with Ippolito (1992), Chevalier and Ellison (1997), and Sirri and Tufano (1998); this paper extends it to a daily frequency and to a decomposition of returns by source [[sources/pdf-806192f007eb]].
