# Directed Technical Change

## Summary

A class of endogenous growth models, developed by Acemoglu (2002) and Acemoglu et al. (2012), in which the direction of innovation between sectors is itself an economic choice; Aschenbrenner (2020) uses this framework with a consumption sector and a safety sector to model the tradeoff between economic growth and existential risk [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].

## Key claims

- Aschenbrenner builds an endogenous idea-based growth model based on the Jones (1995) version of the Romer (1990) model, with directed technical change between a consumption sector (output C_t) and a safety sector (output H_t) [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- The directed-technical-change framework draws on Acemoglu (2002), Acemoglu et al. (2012), and Jones (2016), the latter of which features a consumption and safety sector with directed technical change [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- In the model, consumption output C_t = A_t^α L_ct uses consumption technology A_t while safety output H_t = B_t^α L_ht uses safety technology B_t, with α > 0; total population N_t splits into workers L_t and scientists S_t, each further split between sectors [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Three allocative decisions arise endogenously: the fraction of total scientists working on consumption (s_t), the fraction of total workers working on consumption (ℓ_t), and the fraction of the population that is a scientist (σ_t) [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- A "rule of thumb" allocation, analogous to Solow's (1956) fixed saving rate in the neoclassical growth model, holds the fractions of scientists and workers in safety fixed; the optimal allocation lets these shares evolve in response to risk and consumption [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
- Following the standard endogenous-growth tradition, the model takes labor as the only factor of production, and intermediate goods are micro-founded with a continuum but omitted for concision [[sources/pdf-e7ff11e4ad9b]].
