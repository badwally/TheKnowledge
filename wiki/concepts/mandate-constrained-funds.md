# Mandate-Constrained Funds

## Summary

The model component in Gabaix and Koijen (2021) where institutional funds operate under tight mandates — for instance, a mixed fund required to hold 80% of assets in equities — making them unable to absorb large flows elastically and providing the microfoundation for aggregate-market inelasticity [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].

## Key claims

- In the basic model, a representative consumer can invest in two funds: a pure bond fund, and a mixed fund that invests in stocks and bonds according to a given mandate — for instance, that 80% of the fund's assets should be invested in equities [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- When the consumer sells $1 of the pure bond fund and invests this $1 in the mixed fund, the mixed fund must invest the inflow into stocks and bonds; that pushes up stock prices, which makes the mixed fund want to invest more in stocks, which pushes prices up further, and so on — in equilibrium the total value of the equity market increases by $5 [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- The core economics survives, suitably modified, if the fund is more actively contrarian — its policy is to buy more equities when the expected excess return on equities is high [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- The model aggregates well: if different investors have different elasticities, the total market elasticity is the size-weighted elasticity of market participants, with the correct measure of size being the share of equity they hold [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- Many institutions empirically have tight mandates, which Gabaix and Koijen confirm empirically — if one wants to buy $1 worth of equities, many funds cannot supply that, since for instance a fund that invests entirely in equities cannot exchange them for bonds [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- The model extends readily to an infinite horizon: in that case, the price today is influenced by the cumulative inflows to date and the present value of future expected flows — divided by the market elasticity [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- The model also clarifies how to measure net flows into the aggregate stock market even though for every buyer there is a seller, which guides the empirical analysis [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
