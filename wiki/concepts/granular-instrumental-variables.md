# Granular Instrumental Variables (GIV)

## Summary

The instrumental-variables methodology developed by Gabaix and Koijen (2020) and used in their 2021 inelastic-markets paper to identify the aggregate equity demand elasticity and the price impact of flows: the size-weighted sum of idiosyncratic demand shocks of large institutions or sectors is used as an exogenous instrument [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].

## Key claims

- Conceived for the 2021 inelastic-markets paper and worked out as a stand-alone methodology paper, Gabaix and Koijen (2020), which the inelastic-markets paper cites as the source of the GIV approach [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- Idiosyncratic demand shocks of large institutions or sectors are extracted from factor models estimated on the changes in their holdings [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- The size-weighted sum of these idiosyncratic shocks (the GIV) is used as a primitive instrument to identify how demand shocks affect aggregate prices and the demand of other investors [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- Applied in the inelastic-markets paper to estimate both the aggregate sensitivity of equity prices to demand shocks (the multiplier of approximately 5) and the demand elasticity of various institutions (around 0.2) [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- Robustness checks across data sets, including the Flow of Funds and 13F filings, yield consistent multipliers around 5 [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
