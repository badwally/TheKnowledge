# Bottom-Up AI Regulation

## Summary

Bottom-up AI regulation refers to policies, guidelines, and best practices for AI development and use created not by national governments but by professional associations, unions, and community organizations within their own workplaces and communities; the framing is positioned in the source as the practical fallback for Canadians in the absence of comprehensive national AI legislation after AIDA's death [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].

## Key claims

- AI regulation can and does exist outside of government, through professional associations, unions, and community organizations [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].
- Following the 2023 WGA and SAG-AFTRA strikes, both U.S. unions established new regulations governing the training of AI models on union-protected creative works and the use of generative AI applications in the workplace [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].
- Canadian professional associations and unions including the Canadian Bar Association, the College of Physicians & Surgeons of Manitoba, and the Elementary Teachers' Federation of Ontario have already created guidelines and rules for regulating how AI tools can be used within their professions and workplaces [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].
- The author frames her own essay on "AI countergovernance," Partnership on AI's Guidelines for Participatory and Inclusive AI, NIST's AI Risk Management Framework Playbook, and TechTarget's guide to acceptable-use AI policy as resources for creating such bottom-up policies [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].
- The source argues these bottom-up approaches will play an important role as Canada's political upheaval continues and new regulatory norms for AI emerge [[sources/web-2025-01-17-451]].
