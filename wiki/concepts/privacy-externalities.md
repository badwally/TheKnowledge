# Privacy Externalities

## Summary

Privacy externalities are the collective impacts of data collection — described by Acemoglu et al. (2019) — that fall on people other than those whose data are directly collected, because inferences about non-participants can be drawn from the data of those who do participate [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].

## Key claims

- Acemoglu et al. (2019) use 'privacy externalities' to refer to the collective impacts of data collection, in which the choices of individual data subjects affect others through inference and aggregation [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Even if an individual opts out of data collection, their preferences and behaviours can be inferred by comparing them to people that share similar features (Acemoglu et al., 2019), undermining the assumption that individual data rights are sufficient for individual control [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Mitigating privacy externalities is one of three economist-favoured proposals — alongside strengthening individual control through property rights (Jones & Tonetti, 2020) and reducing information asymmetries (Acquisti et al., 2016) — for empowering citizen-consumers to make more rational choices about data describing them [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- The existence of privacy externalities helps motivate collective data-governance mechanisms, which intervene at the scale at which data collection actually produces effects rather than at the individual scale at which rights are exercised [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
