# U.S. HOA Annual Tax-Form Election (1120-H vs. 1120)

## Summary

U.S. homeowners associations have a unique tax situation found in no other area of U.S. tax law: they may choose annually how to file their federal tax returns and what tax rate to pay [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]]. An association may file as a homeowners' association using IRS Form 1120-H or as a regular corporation using Form 1120, and this election can change from one tax year to the next [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]]. Form 1120-H is simpler and most common; Form 1120 is more complex but offers a more favorable rate on the first $50,000 of taxable income [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].

## Key claims

- U.S. associations have the choice of how to file their tax returns and what tax rate to pay — a unique tax situation found in no other area of U.S. tax law [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- The two main filing options are Form 1120-H (homeowners' association) and Form 1120 (regular corporation) [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- The filing-form decision can change annually [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Form 1120-H is the form specifically designed for HOAs, is the easiest to prepare, and is the most common tax form used by associations [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Form 1120 is the regular corporation tax form, is much more difficult to prepare, and can potentially cause membership net income to be taxable [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Form 1120 offers a 15% rate on the first $50,000 of taxable income, more favorable than Form 1120-H's 30% rate on non-exempt function income [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Filing Form 1120 carries inherent risks including more restrictive accounting procedures and what appears to be additional IRS audit risk [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- More restrictive Form 1120 accounting procedures include segregation of operating and reserve cash, adoption and adherence to a budget that agrees with a reserve study, and adequate accounting for various reserve items by capital and non-capital categories [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- The board must weigh whether the association qualifies to file Form 1120 and whether the tax advantages outweigh the audit risk [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- Form 1120 is the "simple way to go" framing is inverted in the source itself: Form 1120-H is the simple way; the source's reference to Form 1120 as commonly used appears to be a typographical anomaly within an otherwise consistent narrative recommending 1120-H as easier [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
- An additional, rarer option is Form 990 for associations that qualify as exempt organizations [[sources/web-2026-05-15-040]].
