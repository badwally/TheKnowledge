# Shelter Inflation Persistence

## Summary

The argument from the July 13, 2022 Q3-2022 IACFM presentation that shelter inflation — about a third of CPI — is likely to remain elevated because (a) CPI/PCE shelter measures lag observed market trends, (b) higher mortgage rates push demand into rentals, and (c) NIMBY-ism, environmental policy, and rent control restrict new affordable supply [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].

## Key claims

- Shelter inflation accounts for approximately a third of overall CPI [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- CPI and PCE shelter inflation measures understate observed market trends: CPI and PCE reported approximately 5%–6% year-over-year increases in shelter costs while CoreLogic and Zillow market indices showed approximately 20% year-over-year increases in home prices and mid- to high-teens year-over-year increases in asking rents (as of mid-2022) [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Higher mortgage rates and a higher cost of home ownership drive rental demand, putting additional pricing pressure on rental markets as households increasingly view renting as a more affordable alternative to homeownership [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- New housing will remain supply-constrained because rent control and rent stabilization policies, environmental concerns, and NIMBY-ism have discouraged the construction and increased the cost of new affordable housing [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- The contribution from shelter alone accounted for ~3% annualized run-rate inflation in Core CPI as of May 2022 [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Shelter is 33% of CPI but only 16% of PCE — one of the principal reasons PCE inflation lags CPI inflation when shelter prices are rising [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
