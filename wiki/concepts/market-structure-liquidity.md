# Market Structure Liquidity

## Summary

Market Structure Liquidity, in daytradingrauf's DOL framework, is the second layer of draws on liquidity: chart structures — bull and bear flags, failure swings (double tops and bottoms), and trend line liquidity — are treated as containers for retail orders, and the trader's edge is to take the opposite side of the crowd that is spooling orders into these patterns, but only when higher-time-frame order flow supports the fade [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].

## Key claims

- The framework's posture is explicitly contrarian: "This type of liquidity I look to do the opposite of what everyone is looking at such as harmonic patterns" [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
- Patterns are useful precisely because so many traders trade them naively: harmonic patterns are described as a "good way to identify liquidity because many traders spool in orders without context of the market just by looking at these patterns" [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
- Three structural forms of market-structure liquidity are enumerated by the source: bull and bear flags, failure swings (double tops and double bottoms), and trend line liquidity [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
- The contrarian posture is conditional, not unconditional: "You must have HTF order flow to back up why you're 'fading' these patterns" [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
- These three forms are described as among the author's "favorite to look at and do the opposite to" [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
