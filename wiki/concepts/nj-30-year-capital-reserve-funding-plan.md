# NJ 30-Year Capital Reserve Funding Plan

## Summary

New Jersey requires associations of planned real estate developments to maintain a 30-year capital reserve funding plan; S3992 (August 2025) clarified that the 30-year horizon is the test bed for the statutory adequacy definition, and that every capital reserve funding plan must now include a zero-dollar variant projecting balances across the 30-year window [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].

## Key claims

- NJ associations of planned real estate developments must maintain a 30-year funding plan [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Under S3992, all capital reserve funding plans must include a zero-dollar plan showing a 30-year funding projection [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- The plan must not allow the reserve fund to be projected to fall below zero dollars over the 30-year horizon [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- The plan may permit the reserve fund to reach zero dollars [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- There is no statutory limit on how often the plan may hit the zero-dollar mark within the 30-year projection [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- Associations may produce multiple capital funding plans, including additional plans showing balances greater than zero [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
- The 30-year plan is the operational anchor of the statutory adequacy definition added by S3992 [[sources/web-2025-06-10-ddd]].
