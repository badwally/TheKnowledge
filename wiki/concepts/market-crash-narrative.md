# Market Crash narrative

## Summary

The Market Crash narrative is Bhargava, Lou, Ozik, Sadka, and Whitmore's (2022) media-coverage-based indicator measuring how often "markets" terms (SPY, NYSE, and other variants) appear textually close to "crash"-family terms (variants include bear or meltdown); they identify it as the narrative with the highest explanatory power for US equity markets and as a predictor of future SPY returns even after controlling for past returns and the VIX [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].

## Key claims

- The Market Crash narrative is defined as a measure of how often "markets" (including SPY, NYSE, and other variants) appear textually close to "crash" (variants include bear or meltdown) [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
- Among the authors' 73 narratives, Market Crash has the highest explanatory power for US equity markets in their daily rolling three-month univariate regressions ranked by time-series average R² [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
- The intensity of digital-media coverage of the Market Crash narrative closely tracks the VIX index, with increases in Market Crash coverage often leading to increases in VIX [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
- In a predictive regression of future SPY returns on past market returns, VIX, and media coverage on the Market Crash narrative, the coefficient on the Market Crash narrative is negative and statistically significant — evidence that media narratives contain information for future market returns beyond traditional macroeconomic indicators [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
- This predictive result is the empirical hook the authors use to motivate a narrative-based dynamic asset-allocation strategy [[sources/pdf-19445750a197]].
