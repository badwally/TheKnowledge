# Datafication

## Summary

Datafication is the process — described by Mejias and Couldry (2019) — by which data-driven technologies and pervasive algorithmic decision-making come to mediate people's everyday lives, rendering more and more aspects of human experience as digital data for collection, analysis, and circulation [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].

## Key claims

- The data-rights paradigm of personal data protection expanded globally alongside datafication, the growing role of data-driven technologies and pervasive algorithmic decision-making in people's everyday lives (Mejias & Couldry, 2019) [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- As the massive effects of datafication have become clearer, so have the limitations of personal data protection based on informed consent and the assumption that individuals can reasonably predict how data about them will be used and shared (Custers, 2016) [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Datafication has collective dimensions that individual data rights cannot fully address: data from individuals are valuable to collectors because they inform insights about groups, and aggregated data inform algorithmic analyses used to govern people at the collective scale [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Collective data governance proposals respond to datafication by intervening in 'data production' — the processes of creation, aggregation, management, storage, analysis, and commercialisation that turn observations into value [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
