# Federal R&D funding decline (United States)

## Summary

Multi-decade decline in U.S. federal research-and-development spending as a share of GDP, treated by the Stanford Emerging Technology Review 2026 as a structural risk to the United States' role as the world's innovation leader [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].

## Key claims

- Federal R&D funding in the United States has plummeted in percentage terms since the 1960s, falling from 1.86 percent of GDP in 1964 to just 0.66 percent of GDP in 2016 [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- The United States used to spend more of its GDP on science and research than any nation in the world; as of SETR 2026, it ranks eighth [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- The Creating Helpful Incentives to Produce Semiconductors (CHIPS) and Science Act of 2022 was supposed to begin reversing this decline by dramatically increasing federal funding for basic research, but those increases were subsequently scrapped [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- Current budget proposals call for further reductions in the National Science Foundation budget, which funds all fields of fundamental science and engineering outside of medicine, and the National Institutes of Health budget, which funds medical research [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
- The report frames the decline as harvesting today the research seeds planted decades ago while not planting for the future like the United States once did [[sources/pdf-stanford-2026-the-stanford-emerging]].
