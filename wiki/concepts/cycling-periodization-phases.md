# Cycling Periodization Phases (Prep / Base / Build / Peak)

## Summary

Cycling training plans are commonly divided into four sequential phases — Prep, Base, Build, and Peak — each with a different role for [[concepts/strength-and-conditioning-for-cyclists|strength and conditioning]] work, ranging from movement-quality work in Prep to a deliberately reduced strength load in Peak [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].

## Key claims

- **Prep phase**: develop neuromuscular efficiency, stability, and functional strength — i.e., establish movement quality before chasing load [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- **Base phase**: build a strength foundation that can be used for power development, with the explicit goal of translating strength gains into sport-specific (cycling) work [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- **Build phase**: develop power, balance, and neuromuscular control; form is the gating constraint — riders are told to reduce intensity rather than break form [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- **Peak phase**: shift focus to sport-specific (on-bike) training, decreasing the stress of strength sessions by reducing both intensity and volume [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Across phases, S&C sits at one or two sessions per week, scheduled around (and never prioritised over) bike sessions on shared days [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
