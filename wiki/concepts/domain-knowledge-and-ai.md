# Domain Knowledge and AI

## Summary

Evidence that, contrary to predictions that big data and machine learning would render domain expertise obsolete, only researchers with sufficient domain training can effectively harness AI's predictive power in scientific discovery [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].

## Key claims

- Survey responses from materials scientists identify training and experience with similar materials as key to their evaluation process [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
- Researchers in the top quartile of evaluation ability are 3.4× more likely to have published an academic article on their material of focus [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
- Scientists who struggle to judge AI-suggested compounds report that their background offers little assistance [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
- Findings contradict the view (Anderson 2008; Gennatas et al. 2020) that big data renders domain knowledge obsolete [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
- Researcher discretion boosts discovery rates for two-thirds of scientists, indicating experts perceive features of the design problem not captured by the algorithm [[sources/pdf-22a560609b73]].
