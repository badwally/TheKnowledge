# Quantitative tightening

## Summary

The central-bank policy of reducing the size of the balance sheet built up under quantitative easing — operating, JPM AM argues, under the same principle of leverage over long-term yields, and therefore expected to be modest in scale and undisruptive given that bond supply remains meaningful in 2023 and borrowing costs have already risen materially [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].

## Key claims

- Quantitative easing was designed to give central banks extra control and leverage over long-term interest rates, helping the market absorb large-scale government issuance [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- JPM AM expects quantitative tightening to operate under the same principle as QE [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- Because bond supply is still expected to be meaningful in size in 2023 and borrowing costs have already risen meaningfully, the firm expects central banks to be modest in their balance-sheet-reduction ambitions [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
- The firm does not expect a particularly concerted effort, nor any significant disruption, from QT [[sources/pdf-2b2d34f2b0ff]].
