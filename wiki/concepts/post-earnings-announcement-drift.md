# Post-Earnings Announcement Drift (PEAD)

## Summary

Post-Earnings Announcement Drift is the well-documented anomaly that stock prices continue to drift in the direction of an earnings surprise for some time following the announcement, indicating that the market does not fully incorporate the new information immediately; Zarattini and Stamatoudis (2024) flag PEAD as directly relevant to overnight gap research because earnings announcements are a common gap catalyst [[sources/pdf-1662a4368954]]. Augenblick, Lazarus, and Thaler (2024) offer a theoretical account consistent with PEAD: because earnings announcements are typically strong signals (high diagnosticity), the signal-strength-dependent updating framework predicts underreaction — and experimental evidence confirms that underreaction is more severe the larger the signal diagnosticity [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].

## Key claims

- PEAD is a well-documented anomaly in financial markets in which stock prices continue to drift in the direction of an earnings surprise for some time following the earnings announcement [[sources/pdf-1662a4368954]].
- Ball and Brown (the source bibliography lists the year as 2013, which appears to be a citation-formatting error for the original 1968 paper "An Empirical Evaluation of Accounting Income Numbers" that documents this finding decades before Bernard & Thomas 1989) are among the first to document the PEAD phenomenon, finding that stocks with positive earnings surprises tend to experience a prolonged period of abnormal returns following the announcement [[sources/pdf-1662a4368954]].
- The drift can last from several weeks to several months, indicating that the market does not fully incorporate the new information immediately [[sources/pdf-1662a4368954]].
- Bernard and Thomas (1989) reinforce these findings, suggesting the delayed market reaction can be attributed to investor underreaction to earnings news; positive-surprise stocks continue to outperform and negative-surprise stocks continue to underperform even after the initial announcement period [[sources/pdf-1662a4368954]].
- PEAD is particularly relevant to the study of stock price gaps because earnings announcements are a common catalyst for such gaps; stocks that gap up significantly on positive earnings news may exhibit similar post-announcement drift, leading to continued price appreciation or plateauing over time [[sources/pdf-1662a4368954]].
- Augenblick, Lazarus, and Thaler (2024) find that underreaction is the expected behavioral response to strong signals — and that "underinference...is more severe the larger is the diagnosticity" per Benjamin's (2019) survey of the experimental literature — providing a theoretical mechanism consistent with PEAD: earnings surprises are high-diagnosticity signals and therefore subject to systematic underinference [[sources/pdf-ned-augenblick-2025-overinference-from-weak]].
