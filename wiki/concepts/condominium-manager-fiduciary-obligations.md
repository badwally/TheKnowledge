# Condominium Manager Fiduciary Obligations (Ontario)

## Summary

Condominium manager fiduciary obligations in Ontario flow from Section 115 of the Condominium Act, 1998 — which requires that money received on behalf of or for the benefit of the corporation be held in trust (115(1)), restricts the universe of reserve-fund-eligible investment securities (115(5)), and requires the Board to develop an investment plan before investing any reserve-fund money (115(8)) — and from the CMRAO's licensing regime under the Condominium Management Services Act, 2015, which defines the substantive scope of those obligations through the published Competency Profile and practice guides [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]] [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].

## Key claims

- Section 115(1) of the Condominium Act, 1998 requires that "a person who receives money on behalf of or for the benefit of the corporation, including money received from owners as contributions to the common expenses or the reserve fund, shall hold the money, together with interest and other proceeds earned from investing it, in trust for the performance by the corporation of its duties and obligations" [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Section 115(5) of the Condominium Act, 1998 restricts the universe of reserve-fund-eligible investment securities to bonds, debentures, GICs, deposit receipts/notes, or similar instruments issued or guaranteed by Canada or a province, or issued by an Ontario institution insured by CDIC or DICO [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- Section 115(8) of the Condominium Act, 1998 requires the Board to develop an investment plan based on the anticipated cash requirements of the reserve fund as set out in the most recent reserve fund study before investing any part of the reserve fund; this is the statutory grounding for the licensee's investment-process support obligation [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- The CMRAO Competency Profile (2019) and the February 2025 Financial Management Practice Guide together define the substantive scope of licensee financial-management obligations [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Licensee obligations include preparing, presenting, and implementing the annual budget [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Licensee obligations include collecting the common expenses [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Licensee obligations include reviewing the general ledger to ensure accurate expense reporting [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Licensee obligations include interpreting financial statements and explaining variances to the Board of Directors [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Licensee obligations include monitoring the corporation's financial status and reporting concerns to the Board [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Licensee obligations include supporting the Board through the annual financial audit process [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Licensee obligations include monitoring reserve fund spending and balance relative to the RFS [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- Licensee obligations include supporting the Board in the reserve fund investment process [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]]; the investment-process support obligation is statutorily anchored in Section 115(8)'s investment-plan requirement, and operationally requires coordination with a licensed investment professional distinct from the reserve fund study provider [[sources/web-2026-01-01-3bf]].
- Licensee obligations include implementing the necessary financial controls to protect the corporation's financial assets [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
- The CMRAO Practice Guide explicitly states that it is for information and reference only, is not a substitute for legal advice, and that the legislation prevails over the guide where the two conflict [[sources/pdf-aa9367a8a4a9]].
