# Surveillance Capitalism

## Summary

Surveillance capitalism is Shoshana Zuboff's (2019) term for an economic logic that 'unilaterally claims human experience as free raw material for translation into behavioral data,' intensifying the contradictions between democratic ideals and capitalist imperatives in western liberal citizenship [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].

## Key claims

- Zuboff (2019) characterises surveillance capitalism as a system that unilaterally claims human experience as free raw material for translation into behavioural data [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Surveillance capitalism produces a Lockean vision of data ownership in which many collectors act as if data are 'just there' for the taking, ignoring the humans in the data (Couldry & Mejias, 2019) [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Under surveillance capitalism, fragmented and partial observations render people legible to state and market actors for sorting and stratification (Austin, 2022; Fourcade & Healy, 2016), including targeted marketing, credit scoring, and risk assessment (Zuboff, 2019) [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Privacy rights are intended to constrain the Lockean vision of data ownership by recognising the moral claims of individuals described in data [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Some collective data governance proposals seek to build or even seize 'surveillance capitalism's means of production' — the infrastructures for data collection and storage — to assert greater control over how data are accessed and used to produce economic and social value [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
