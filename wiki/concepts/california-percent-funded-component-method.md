# California Percent-Funded Component Method (§5570(b)(4))

## Summary

The California percent-funded component method is the statutory formula prescribed at Civil Code §5570(b)(4) for computing the amount of reserves needed to be accumulated for a component at a given time: the current cost of replacement or repair multiplied by the number of years the component has been in service divided by the useful life of the component [[sources/web-2025-01-01-946]]. The percent-funded figure that appears on items (6) and (7) of the Assessment and Reserve Funding Disclosure Summary is derived by comparing the projected reserve fund cash balance against this computed required amount [[sources/web-2025-01-01-946]].

## Key claims

- Statutory formula: required reserves for a component at a given time = current cost of replacement or repair × (years in service ÷ useful life of the component), per Civil Code §5570(b)(4) [[sources/web-2025-01-01-946]].
- Disclosure-not-funding clause: the calculation "shall not be construed to require the board to fund reserves in accordance with this calculation" — funding decisions remain a board prerogative [[sources/web-2025-01-01-946]].
- Alternate-method permission: item (6) of the form explicitly supports a parallel "alternate, but generally accepted, method of calculation" with the required reserve amount under that method disclosed and an explanation attached [[sources/web-2025-01-01-946]].
- Per-component derivation: the method is component-by-component; the association-level percent-funded figure is implicitly an aggregation across all major components included in the reserve study [[sources/web-2025-01-01-946]].
- Cost basis: the calculation uses current cost of replacement or repair, not historical or inflated cost [[sources/web-2025-01-01-946]].
- Time-in-service basis: the numerator multiplier is years the component has been in service (not years remaining), implying a straight-line accumulation expectation across the useful life [[sources/web-2025-01-01-946]].
- Form presentation: item (6) reports the required amount at the end of the current fiscal year alongside the projected cash balance and percent funded; item (7) projects the required amount, cash balance, and percent funded for each of the next five budget years both before and after the approved funding plan is implemented [[sources/web-2025-01-01-946]].
