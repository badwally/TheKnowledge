# Inflationary psychology theory

## Summary

Economic theory — codified into a "zeitgeist" among the modern policy cognoscente per Kai Volatility's Q2 2022 commentary — that the primary cause of secular inflation in the modern economy is psychological rather than structural: once an inflationary mindset takes hold, consumers pull demand forward, firms pass through higher costs and offer higher wages, and the loop becomes self-fulfilling unless central banks short-circuit it preemptively [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].

## Key claims

- The theory holds that "once an inflationary mindset takes hold, for whatever short-term initial reason, consumers pull forward demand in order to beat anticipated future price increases" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Firms readily pass along higher costs to consumers — "including the future cost increases that they anticipate" — and become less resistant to offering higher wages [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Left unchecked, this naturally plays out as a self-perpetuating wage-price spiral, and once long-term inflation expectations take hold they become a self-fulfilling prophecy [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- The implication is that secular inflation can be relegated to rare, short, cyclical phenomena if the self-fulfilling loop can be short-circuited — leading to the policy prescription "circumvent a potential wage-price spiral before it starts, at all costs" / "Go big, early, and do whatever it takes" [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Per Kai Volatility, the theory underlies the modern Fed's contention that previous chairs (William McChesney Martin, Arthur Burns, William Miller) were derelict in their duty and could have bypassed 10-15 painful years of inflation with foresight and gumption [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
- Kai Volatility's critique: this prevailing narrative "fails to contemplate that not all moments in history are created equal" — Volcker did the right thing in 1979-1981, but "1965 was not the same time as 1980," and the 1965-1980 inflation was structurally driven by political forces rather than psychology [[sources/pdf-ed56b074fb11]].
