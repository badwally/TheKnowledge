# Basic system (Thorp-Kassouf warrant hedge)

## Summary

The hedge presented as "the basic system" in Thorp and Kassouf's 1967 "Beat the Market" — long the common stock and short the associated overpriced warrant in a calculated mix designed to deliver "high profit with low risk," with the authors reporting an averaged 25%/year return over a seventeen-year (1946-1966) backtest and a 25%/year five-year cash record from 1961-1966 [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].

## Key claims

- Introduced in chapter 4 ("The Basic System") under the framing "Hedging: high profit with low risk," with explicit attention to "changing the mix" between long-stock and short-warrant components [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Chapter 5 ("$100,000 Doubles") illustrates the system with investments made by one of the authors over a five-year period, including the Molybdenum, Bunker-Ramo (Teleregister), and Sperry Rand trades [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Chapter 6 ("How to Use the Basic System") lays out the operational steps: "Identifying the listed warrants. Picking short-sale candidates. Using the warrant-stock diagram. Which are best? Choosing the mix. How much protection: Dividing your capital among the candidates" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Chapter 7 ("Further Proof: The Historical Record") presents "a simplified mechanical strategy" and reports it "averaged more than 25% a year over a seventeen-year period" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- The introduction reports that "when stocks fell from September 1929 to 1930, the basic system could have doubled an investment" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- The authors' five-year cash record (1961-1966) using the system is reported as "no losses and an average return of 25% per year" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- The introduction reports that "one of the authors more than doubled $100,000 in just four years (Chapter 5)" using the system [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- The minimum capital to operate the system is given as "the amount required to open a margin account," stated as $2,000 at the time of writing, with the caveat that the threshold is "subject to change" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Chapter 10 extends the basic system from listed warrants to convertible bonds (with a worked example using Dresser Industries "warrants"), convertible preferreds, puts, and calls — branded "the general system: the evaluation of convertible securities" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Appendix C presents "Scientific proof that hedging can offer high expected return" — a theoretical argument the authors describe as having "convinced colleagues in whom we confided" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Chapter 9 ("Can Anything Go Wrong?") catalogues the failure modes the authors consider — short squeezes, a 1929-style crash, volatile price movements, extension of warrant privileges, banning of short sales, and "extensive use of the basic system" by other investors [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
