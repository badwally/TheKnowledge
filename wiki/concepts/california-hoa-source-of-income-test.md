# California HOA 60% Source-of-Income Test (R&TC §23701t)

## Summary

The California source-of-income test under R&TC Section 23701t requires that 60% or more of a tax-exempt homeowners' association's gross income come from membership dues, fees, and assessments [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]]. It is one of the six operational-test conditions an HOA must satisfy on an ongoing basis to maintain its California tax-exempt status, and operates in parallel with the 90% expenditure test that constrains the use of those funds [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].

## Key claims

- 60% or more of gross income must come from membership dues, fees, and assessments to satisfy the source-of-income test under §23701t [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- For cooperative housing corporations, payments from stockholder members to pay acquisition indebtedness or other fees are treated as fees and assessments for source-of-income test purposes [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- The source-of-income test is enumerated as one of the six operational-test conditions under §23701t (alongside the 85% residential test, 90% expenditure test, no-private-inurement rule, trust-fund requirement on unused dues, and minimum two-unit-different-owners membership) [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- The source-of-income test is a continuing operational requirement, not a one-time qualification — failure to maintain it places the HOA's tax-exempt status at risk [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- The 60% threshold is a gross-income percentage, not a net-income or fund-balance metric [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
