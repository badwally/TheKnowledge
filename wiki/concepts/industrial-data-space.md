# Industrial Data Space

## Summary

An industrial data space is a governance and technical framework that lets companies share data securely and sovereignly across organisational boundaries, presented as a key building block for a resilient, AI-enabled, and sovereign European data economy [[sources/web-2026-06-08-406]]. The International Data Spaces Association (IDSA) reference architecture is the standards layer underpinning Catena-X (automotive) and other industrial data ecosystems worldwide [[sources/web-2026-06-08-406]].

## Key claims

- Industrial data spaces enable secure, trusted, sovereign data exchange across the value chain, supporting regulatory compliance, supply-chain resilience, and competitiveness [[sources/web-2026-06-08-406]].
- The IDSA reference architecture is the open standards layer on which Catena-X and other industrial data ecosystems are built [[sources/web-2026-06-08-406]].
- Per IDSA CEO Lars Nagel, the value of industrial data spaces has historically been established mainly through theoretical models and isolated pilot projects — the Data Space Accelerator is positioned as a shift toward empirical evidence at scale [[sources/web-2026-06-08-406]].
- Realising the full value requires participation across all tiers of the supply chain, not just OEMs and Tier-1 suppliers [[sources/web-2026-06-08-406]].
- Documented use cases in the Catena-X data space include Certificate Management, Product Carbon Footprint, Digital Product Passport, traceability, quality management, demand and capacity management, short-term supply management, and circular economy [[sources/web-2026-06-08-406]].
- The German Manufacturing-X framework treats Catena-X (automotive) as a template for replicating industrial data spaces in chemicals, semiconductors, and other sectors [[sources/web-2026-06-08-406]].
