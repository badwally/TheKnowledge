# Product Differentiation and Data-Sharing Collaboration Incentives

## Summary

In game-theoretic models of competitor data sharing, the degree of product differentiation between firms is a primary determinant of whether they will collaborate: the more differentiated the products (the less they are close substitutes), the stronger the incentive to pool training data, because the gains from a better model are less offset by helping a direct rival [[sources/arxiv-2305.16052]]. Tsoy and Konstantinov (NeurIPS 2023) derive this result from a conventional market model and pair it with a second driver — the difficulty of the underlying learning task — that operates in the same direction [[sources/arxiv-2305.16052]].

## Key claims

- Reduced competition between firms — operationalized as greater dissimilarity (differentiation) between the firms' products — fosters data-sharing collaboration [[sources/arxiv-2305.16052]].
- Harder learning tasks also foster collaboration [[sources/arxiv-2305.16052]].
- Both findings are derived from an instantiation of a general data-sharing framework on a conventional market model from economic theory [[sources/arxiv-2305.16052]].
- The market-conditions effect on data-sharing incentives is profound rather than incremental [[sources/arxiv-2305.16052]].
