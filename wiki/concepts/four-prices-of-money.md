# Four Prices of Money

## Summary

Zoltan Pozsar's framework dividing money into four prices — par, interest, foreign exchange, and the price level (with inflation as its derivative) — used in his August 1, 2022 "War and Interest Rates" dispatch to argue that post-Cold War macro investors traded the first three in a peacetime setting where central banks were the counterparty, while today's economic war between empires drives the fourth, against which central banks are not fighting markets but rather "cleaning up" inflationary consequences [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].

## Key claims

- The four prices of money are: par, interest, foreign exchange, and the price level [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- George Soros, Stanley Druckenmiller, Paul Tudor Jones, and Louis Bacon traded the first three prices of money during the post-Cold War "golden age" of macro investing, in a peaceful world punctuated only by relatively small military conflicts — these were "nominal conflicts" involving markets and central banks [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Today's conflict — "a complex economic war between 'empires'" — drives the fourth price of money: the price level of goods and services and its derivative, inflation [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
- Central banks aren't fighting markets in this regime; they are "cleaning up" the inflationary consequences of the economic war — "the besieged prices are not par, FX pegs, and bases between various rates, but the price level of goods and services, which is inflating faster than target" [[sources/pdf-6ae97b9c27c7]].
