# Speculator-Hedger Momentum Transfer

## Summary

The empirical finding from Moskowitz, Ooi, and Pedersen (2011) that speculators profit from time series momentum at the expense of hedgers: using weekly position data from the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), the authors find that speculators trade in the direction of past return trends, positioned to capture the positive trend for the first 12 months, and reduce their positions as the trend begins to reverse — while hedgers take the opposite side of these trades throughout [[sources/pdf-lasse-pedersen-2011-title-how-to]].

## Key claims

- Speculators trade with time series momentum, being positioned on average to take advantage of the positive trend in returns for the first 12 months [[sources/pdf-lasse-pedersen-2011-title-how-to]].
- Speculators reduce their positions at the time when the trend begins to reverse [[sources/pdf-lasse-pedersen-2011-title-how-to]].
- Consequently, speculators appear to be profiting from time series momentum, and its eventual reversal, at the expense of hedgers [[sources/pdf-lasse-pedersen-2011-title-how-to]].
- Using a vector auto-regression (VAR), the authors confirm that speculators trade in the same direction as a return shock and reduce their positions as the shock dissipates, whereas hedgers take the opposite side of these trades [[sources/pdf-lasse-pedersen-2011-title-how-to]].
- Very different types of investors in different asset markets produce the same patterns at the same time [[sources/pdf-lasse-pedersen-2011-title-how-to]].
- The roll yield component of time series momentum — driven by liquidity and price pressure effects in futures markets — is associated with hedging pressure and is not affected by over-reaction, unlike spot price changes which show long-term reversals [[sources/pdf-lasse-pedersen-2011-title-how-to]].
