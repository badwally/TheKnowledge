# Amateurism Regressive Transfer

## Summary

The distributional finding by Garthwaite, Keener, Notowidigdo, and Ozminkowski (2020) that rent-sharing in NCAA college athletics effectively transfers resources away from students who are more likely to be Black and more likely to come from poor neighborhoods toward students who are more likely to be white and come from higher-income neighborhoods, documented using complete roster data for every student-athlete playing sports at FBS schools in 2018 matched to neighborhood socioeconomic characteristics [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].

## Key claims

- The paper uses complete roster data for every student-athlete playing sports at FBS schools in 2018, combined with a newly assembled dataset matching athletes to neighborhood socioeconomic characteristics [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- The rent-sharing from football and men's basketball to other sports and athletic department functions effectively transfers resources away from students who are more likely to be Black and more likely to come from poor neighborhoods [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Those transferred resources flow toward students who are more likely to be white and come from higher-income neighborhoods [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- The transfer is a structural consequence of the NCAA amateurism rules: rents are generated primarily by football and men's basketball, whose athletes are the population from which resources are transferred, and the rents are redistributed to fund other sports and athletic department spending benefiting a demographically different population [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
