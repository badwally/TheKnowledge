# California HOA Tax-Exempt Status (R&TC §23701t)

## Summary

California homeowners' associations — including condominium management associations, residential real estate management associations, cooperative housing corporations, time-share associations, and mutual water companies — may obtain California state tax-exempt status under R&TC Section 23701t, which is patterned after IRC Section 528 and Federal Treasury Regulations Section 1.528 [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]]. Unlike the federal regime (annual election by filing IRS Form 1120H), California requires affirmative application via FTB Form 3500 and the issuance of an Exempt Determination Letter by the Franchise Tax Board; without one, the HOA is subject to the California minimum franchise or income tax [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].

## Key claims

- California HOAs apply for tax-exempt status under R&TC Section 23701t (patterned after IRC §528 and Treas. Reg. §1.528) [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Eligible organization types: condominium management associations, residential real estate management associations, cooperative housing corporations, time-share associations, and mutual water companies [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- The property must be at least 85 percent residential to qualify under §23701t [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Membership in the association must be a condition of owning a unit, home, or lot in the development (except mutual water companies, which must provide water to residential members) [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- California requires affirmative application via Form 3500 and issuance of an Exempt Determination Letter; the federal regime by contrast allows annual exemption election via IRS Form 1120H [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- FTB recommends submission of Form 3500 at least 90 calendar days before tax-exempt status is needed [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Each applicant must pass two general tests: an organizational test (corporate articles or unincorporated-association documents meeting §23701t requirements) and an operational test (active operation per §23701t, including the source-of-income test, expenditure test, no private inurement, trust requirement on unused dues, and minimum two-unit-different-owners membership) [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- HOAs that do not receive California tax-exempt status remain subject to the minimum franchise or income tax [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- California law does not provide a counterpart to IRC §501(c)(12); mutual water companies exempt federally under §501(c)(12) must independently qualify under §23701t to gain California exemption [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
- Even after receiving §23701t exemption, an HOA must file Form 100 to report taxable income exceeding $100 (non-exempt-function income — interest, dividends, nonmember receipts, gains from sale of property, etc.) [[sources/web-2020-12-01-877]].
