# Director Monthly Review of Unaudited Financial Statements

## Summary

The CAO Guide on Condo Finances establishes that directors of an Ontario condominium corporation must review monthly unaudited financial statements in depth — not merely receive them — and must challenge unexplainable variances or potential errors with the preparer (manager or bookkeeper), because the monthly statements are the basis for in-year financial decisions and uncorrected errors carry forward to future months and to the next budget cycle, ultimately degrading the corporation's reported financial position [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]]. The Guide enumerates three specific monthly review procedures directors should perform on the Statement of Operations alone: reconciling monthly common-expenses-fee revenue to budget (anchored to the 100%-of-ownership-percentages structure of Schedule D of the declaration), investigating large expense discrepancies versus the prior month / year / budget, and watching for credit (negative) balances in expense accounts as an early warning of issues such as utility-meter malfunction [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].

## Key claims

- Unaudited financial statements are prepared by managers or by directors / bookkeepers (if self-managed); however, they should always be reviewed in depth by the directors [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- It is important that records are kept monthly by the corporation's managers or directors (if self-managed) and that they are accurate, as they will be the basis for financial decisions made by directors [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Directors should review statements monthly, identify any errors or omissions, and ensure they are corrected so that they do not carry forward to future months [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Diligent directors should review the statements in detail and ask questions of the preparer when they become aware of unexplainable variances or other potential errors [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Best practice: directors should ensure inaccuracies or errors within unaudited financial statements are remedied as soon as possible; queries to the accounting department (if managed) or bookkeeper (if self-managed) should receive a response in a timely manner, certainly within the next month [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- If errors are not addressed and not corrected before the next year's budget has to be prepared, the financial position of the condominium corporation will be worse than it appears [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Procedure 1 on the Statement of Operations: check that the common expenses fees for the month agree to the budget; the budget is based on the ownership percentages in Schedule D of the declaration which always totals 100%, so the total common expenses fees recorded each month should agree to the budget [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Procedure 1 corollary: if monthly common expenses fees do not agree to budget, the differences should be investigated, as the discrepancy could suggest that the amounts being charged monthly to unit owners are incorrect [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Procedure 2 on the Statement of Operations: directors should look for any large discrepancies in expense accounts from the prior month, the prior year, or in comparison to the budget; if there are large discrepancies, inquiries should be made, as there may be understated or overstated expenses requiring adjustment [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Procedure 3 on the Statement of Operations: ensure there are no credit (negative) balances in expense accounts; an expense account would normally have a positive balance, indicating the total expense incurred year-to-date [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Credit-balance edge case: in utility accounts where the condominium pays a bulk bill for common areas and amounts used by owners in their own units and then recovers amounts from owners, if recoveries exceed bulk expenses for the period the entire account becomes negative [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- A negative utility account balance is an early warning sign that there may be problems with the meters and will require testing of meter functioning, because the recoveries exceeding the entire bulk expense (common areas plus all owners' consumption) cannot be correct [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
- Directors should also review reserve expenses monthly to ensure the expenses qualify as eligible reserve fund expenditures, with inquiries directed to the manager or auditor when in doubt [[sources/pdf-994c881efb49]].
