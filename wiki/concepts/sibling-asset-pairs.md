# Sibling asset pairs

## Summary

In ICT-style price-action analysis, "sibling" asset pairs are correlated instruments that for the most part like to move together — for example $SPY with $QQQ on the equity-ETF side, and $ES with $NQ (and $YM) on the index-futures side — making any drift apart between them a tradable signal that ICT terms SMT divergence [[sources/pdf-19ce7da9ecfc]].

## Key claims

- Asset classes can be thought of as siblings: when you think of $SPY, you associate it with $QQQ; for futures the same holds true for $ES with $NQ (and $YM) [[sources/pdf-19ce7da9ecfc]].
- These siblings, for the most part, like to move together, but there will be times when they drift apart [[sources/pdf-19ce7da9ecfc]].
- The drifting-apart phenomenon is what ICT-style traders call divergence, also termed Smart Money Technique (SMT) or Smart Money Reversal (SMR) [[sources/pdf-19ce7da9ecfc]].
- Divergence highlights the relationship between a pair or groups of pairs and is an easy way of spotting possible strength or weakness among similar asset classes — or different ones when running a macro analysis [[sources/pdf-19ce7da9ecfc]].
