# GSE Condominium Project Review Process

## Summary

The GSE Condominium Project Review Process is the underwriting workflow by which Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac evaluate whether a condominium project is eligible to back a loan in the conventional secondary mortgage market. Under the March 18, 2026 update (Fannie Mae Lender Letter LL-2026-03 and Freddie Mac Bulletin 2026-C), lenders must rely on either full review or waiver of project review — the limited review track is retired for loan applications on or after August 3, 2026, and waiver eligibility is expanded to projects with up to 10 units [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].

## Key claims

- After August 3, 2026, lenders rely on either full review or waiver of project review; limited review is retired [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Waiver of project review eligibility is expanded to include projects with up to 10 units, providing greater flexibility for smaller communities [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- The full review process requires substantially more documentation and formal lender questionnaires for nearly all condominium sales [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- The shift increases administrative burden on community associations, managers, and volunteer leaders [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- The changes shift greater responsibility to lenders and associations to demonstrate that condominium projects are financially sound based on Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac guidelines [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Florida-specific review requirements for new, attached condominium projects are retired under the same March 18, 2026 update [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Condominium communities are encouraged to proactively confirm their eligibility status by reviewing the GSEs' current ineligible project lists [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
- Communities that now meet the new insurance coverage standards can begin the process to restore conventional-financing access via the GSEs' published channels [[sources/web-2026-03-18-a4e]].
