# Canadian Condo Corporation — Tax Treatment of Leasing Common Elements

## Summary

When a Canadian condominium corporation enters into a contract to lease common elements (rooftops, parking, walls, etc.) to a third party for revenue, the federal income-tax treatment turns on a two-step analysis articulated by CRA in External Technical Interpretation 2014-0528171E5: first, whether the leasing income is incidental within the meaning of paragraph 149(1)(l); second, whether under the relevant provincial law the lease income is properly characterized as income of the Corporation or income of the unit owners through the Corporation's agency role [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]]. The T.I. examined a fact pattern involving a solar-panel leasing contract on common-element roofs and identified two CRA concerns: (i) that lease income flowing to a freeze or reduction in monthly maintenance fees would be available for the personal benefit of members, and (ii) whether the lease agreement is connected to the corporation's not-for-profit objectives [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].

## Key claims

- Incidental income from the rental of common areas may be treated as income of the Corporation and generally will not affect the tax-exempt status of the Corporation [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Income that is not incidental will usually be considered to be income of the unit owners, if this is appropriate under the relevant provincial law [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- The space being rented may not belong to the Corporation; rather, the Corporation may be better viewed as acting as an agent for the unit owners in entering into the lease agreement [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- Where the relevant provincial law indicates the income is the income of the Corporation (rather than the unit owners), the Corporation may not be tax-exempt under paragraph 149(1)(l) [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- CRA has expressed concern that lease-agreement income flowing to a freeze or reduction in members' condominium fees would likely be available for the personal benefit of members of the Corporation [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- CRA has also expressed concern as to whether a third-party lease agreement is connected to the Corporation's not-for-profit objectives [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- In the underlying solar-panel fact pattern, lease income was to be contributed to the Corporation's reserve fund, potentially offsetting or freezing annual reserve fund increases, reducing annual reserve fund contributions, and/or reducing property owners' monthly maintenance fees [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
- The 2014 T.I. bears an explicit disclaimer that it may not represent the current position of the CRA [[sources/web-2014-08-18-d79]].
