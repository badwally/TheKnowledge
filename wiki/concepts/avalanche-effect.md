# Avalanche effect (warrant)

## Summary

Thorp and Kassouf's term in "Beat the Market" (1967) for the accelerating decline of an overpriced warrant when the underlying common stock falls — an asymmetry that makes shorting overpriced warrants the high-leverage leg of the basic-system hedge [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].

## Key claims

- Introduced in chapter 3 ("Short Selling: Profits in Bad Times") under the heading "Molybdenum warrants and the avalanche effect," placed alongside the basic mechanics of "Selling warrants short" [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- The book's appendix A is dedicated to the "Mathematics of the avalanche effect," indicating the concept is given a formal treatment alongside the narrative one [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- The "Molybdenum story" returns in chapter 5 ("$100,000 Doubles") — including a "Moly coda" — as a worked example of monetizing the avalanche effect through the basic system [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
- Chapter 3 frames the broader claim that short selling can deliver "profits in bad times," with the avalanche effect as the structural mechanism that makes overpriced warrants particularly profitable to short into a falling stock [[sources/pdf-9da6062f0265]].
