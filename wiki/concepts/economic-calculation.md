# Economic Calculation

## Summary

The process by which entrepreneurs and managers in a market economy use money prices to evaluate whether a particular combination of inputs creates value — the mechanism Per Bylund treats as the capstone of Part II ("Market") in his primer, devoting the longest chapter (Chapter 7, pages 83–101) to the nature of a productive economy, the driving force of entrepreneurship, the production of value, and the relationship between entrepreneurship and management [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].

## Key claims

- Bylund devotes Chapter 7, the longest chapter of his primer (pages 83–101), to economic calculation, structured in four sections: "The Nature of a Productive Economy," "The Driving Force," "The Production of Value," and "Entrepreneurship and Management" [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- The chapter builds on Chapter 6's treatment of "Money Prices" as the enabling mechanism for economic calculation — without money prices, rational allocation of resources across complex production processes cannot occur [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
- Economic calculation is the capstone of Part II ("Market"), following chapters on the market as process (Chapter 4), production and entrepreneurship (Chapter 5), and value, money, and price (Chapter 6) [[sources/pdf-judy-2022-primer-adjusted-a]].
