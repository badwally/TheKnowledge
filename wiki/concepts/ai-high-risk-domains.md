# High-Risk AI Domains

## Summary

The three categories of significant AI risk — human-rights/health/safety, national security, and undue interference with democratic processes — that Japan's April 2023 AI Whitepaper identifies as the appropriate scoping lens for hard-law AI regulation [[sources/pdf-ai-2023-japan-s-ai]].

## Key claims

- The whitepaper enumerates three types of significant AI risk: risks that infringe on human rights, health, and safety; risks to national security caused by AI; and risks of undue interference with democratic processes by AI [[sources/pdf-ai-2023-japan-s-ai]].
- These three categories are presented as the concrete domains where legal measures, including regulations, may be necessary in the new era of AI [[sources/pdf-ai-2023-japan-s-ai]].
- The framing is offered against the observation that, while there is no hard-law AI regulation in Japan, such regulation is being considered in other countries [[sources/pdf-ai-2023-japan-s-ai]].
- Japan is recommended to analyze AI regulations under consideration in the EU, the United States, and China before specifying where domestic legal measures should land [[sources/pdf-ai-2023-japan-s-ai]].
- The whitepaper pairs the high-risk-domain framing with a call for active and strategic Japanese engagement in international AI rule-making, including the G7 Summit Japan chairs in 2023 [[sources/pdf-ai-2023-japan-s-ai]].
