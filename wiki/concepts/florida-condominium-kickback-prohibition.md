# Florida Condominium Kickback Prohibition

## Summary

Florida Statute §718.111(1)(a) criminalizes the solicitation, offer to accept, or acceptance of a kickback by an officer, director, or manager of a Florida condominium association. Violation is a third-degree felony, triggers civil penalty under §718.501(1)(e), and requires mandatory removal from office with vacancy declared. The provision carves out an explicit exception for services or items received in connection with trade fairs or education programs, recognizing the legitimate role of vendor-sponsored continuing education in the property-management industry [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].

## Key claims

- An officer, director, or manager of a Florida condominium association may not solicit, offer to accept, or accept a kickback, per §718.111(1)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Knowingly soliciting, offering to accept, or accepting a kickback is a third-degree felony punishable as provided in §§775.082, 775.083, or 775.084, per §718.111(1)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- Violation also subjects the individual to a civil penalty pursuant to §718.501(1)(e), per §718.111(1)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- A violator must be removed from office and a vacancy declared, per §718.111(1)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The prohibition does not bar an officer, director, or manager from accepting services or items received in connection with trade fairs or education programs, per §718.111(1)(a) [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
- The statute explicitly extends the kickback prohibition to managers, even though §718.111(1)(a) elsewhere disclaims a statutory fiduciary relationship between managers and unit owners — making the kickback prohibition the primary statutory manager-conduct restriction in Chapter 718 [[sources/web-2024-01-01-4d7]].
