# Regime-Switching Cost Escalation

## Summary

Regime-switching cost escalation is a cost-modeling technique that replaces the flat 2-4% inflation assumption used by every deterministic reserve study with a stochastic process that captures regime shifts — sudden multi-year jumps in construction costs and insurance premiums — calibrated to historical shocks like 2022-2025 [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]]. The condo-capital-infra synthesis includes it in the planned engine method stack as a direct response to the cost-shock variance that hit Florida condos after Surfside [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].

## Key claims

- All deterministic reserve models use a flat inflation assumption of 2-4% [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- Construction cost ran 6-9% in 2022-2025, materially exceeding deterministic plan assumptions [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- Florida condo insurance premiums ran 30-100% over the same window [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- No vendor today handles climate or cost-escalation risk explicitly [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- Regime-switching models appear in the planned engine method stack alongside autoregressive cost forecasts and ENR-index-driven escalation, under the "Cost / financial" method family [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- The variance these models capture (cost shock plus insurance shock) is exactly the variance underlying the $50-100K special assessments hitting boards that had nominally "fully funded" their reserves [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
