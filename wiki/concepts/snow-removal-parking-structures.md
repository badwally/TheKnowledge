# Snow and Ice Removal for Precast Parking Structures (PCI MNL-136-04)

## Summary

PCI MNL-136-04 treats snow and ice removal as a distinct maintenance discipline for parking structures in winter-weather regions, organized around five basic parameters: planning, proper equipment, chemical deicers, written procedures, and how-to instruction for snow-removal personnel [[sources/pdf-6b0d49e91108]].

## Key claims

- Snow and ice removal is necessary, if not paramount, for functional performance, public safety, and the long-term durability of parking structures in winter-weather regions [[sources/pdf-6b0d49e91108]].
- Basic planning and specifications for snow removal should begin during the initial design phase of the parking structure, with the owner, architect, engineer, contractor, and precast/prestress concrete manufacturer all involved in determining how snow and ice will be removed [[sources/pdf-6b0d49e91108]].
- Snow and ice removal operations should be planned around local climate conditions, with anticipated maximum snowfall and frequency influencing planning strategies [[sources/pdf-6b0d49e91108]].
- Operations such as removal of snow and ice from the deck surface, storage of snow and ice, and use of certain types of equipment can cause major functional and performance problems to the structure if not properly addressed during the initial design phase [[sources/pdf-6b0d49e91108]].
- Removal of snow is typically handled by moving the snow to a snow chute or snow-melting equipment, or by moving it to and through a gate opening in exterior spandrels [[sources/pdf-6b0d49e91108]].
- Storage of snow requires strict operating procedures and protected dumping zones to ensure the safety of workers, pedestrians, and vehicles; the number of dumping locations depends on the parking structure size, anticipated snowfall rate, and adjacent property locations [[sources/pdf-6b0d49e91108]].
- Multi-level parking structures require special procedures to prevent the penetration of falling snow onto lower structure levels [[sources/pdf-6b0d49e91108]].
- When snow is stored on the top level of a parking structure, equipment operators must be provided with detailed storage guidelines before each clearing operation; if guidelines are not provided in the original design, a structural engineer must generate guidelines for maximum storage-pile size, location, and height [[sources/pdf-6b0d49e91108]].
- Top-level snow storage planning must also account for obstruction of sight lines, loss of parking spaces, and the deleterious effects of concentrated salts and deicers [[sources/pdf-6b0d49e91108]].
- Before snow-melting equipment is chosen, the structure must be checked to ensure that structural components can safely support the equipment's maximum working weight [[sources/pdf-6b0d49e91108]].
- A structural engineer must verify the structural adequacy of the parking deck if wheel loads from snow-melting equipment exceed 3,000 pounds or the total distributed equipment weight exceeds 50 pounds per square foot [[sources/pdf-6b0d49e91108]].
