# Path Dependence of Stores of Value

## Summary

Taleb's argument that an asset which requires active maintenance by interested and incentivized people to keep its physical (or operational) presence cannot serve as a long-term store of value, in contrast to assets like gold and silver whose physical persistence is independent of human attention [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].

## Key claims

- "We cannot expect a book entry on a ledger that requires active maintenance by interested and incentivized people to keep its physical presence, a condition for monetary value, for any period of time — and of course we are not sure of the interests, mindsets, and preferences of future generations" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Once bitcoin drops below a certain threshold, "it may hit an absorbing barrier and stays at 0 — gold on the other hand is not path dependent in its physical properties" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Following the technology-extinction argument cited at reference [7] in the paper, "technologies tend to be supplanted by other technologies with a vulnerability in proportion to their past survival duration (>99% of the new is replaced by something newer), whereas items such as gold and silver have proved resistant to extinction" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Bitcoin "is supposed to be hacker-proof and is based on total infallibility in the future, not just at present"; it is "based on perfect immortality; unlike conventional assets, the slightest mortality rate puts its value at 0" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Gold and other dividend-free precious items "have held some financial status for more than 6,000 years, and their physical status for several orders of magnitude longer (i.e., they did not degrade or mutate into some other alloy or mineral)"; about half of current gold production goes to jewelry, one-tenth to industry, and a quarter to central-bank reserves [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
- Taleb separately notes a reasoning error in claiming "that an innovation, bitcoin, can become the 'new gold' ab ovo, when gold wasn't decided to be so by fiat thanks to a white paper; it organically became a reserve asset ex post, through centuries of competitive selection against other modes of storage, payment, and collectibles" [[sources/pdf-752c8824b750]].
