# Trader intuition

## Summary

Trader intuition is the ability to make decisions based on experience and instinct — encompassing pattern recognition, market-sentiment assessment, and rapid decisions under uncertainty; Zarattini and Stamatoudis (2024) treat it as a less quantifiable but potentially significant performance factor and design an anonymized-chart experiment to isolate its contribution to overnight-gap trading [[sources/pdf-1662a4368954]].

## Key claims

- Trader intuition is the ability to make decisions based on experience and instinct, crucial in discretionary trading; unlike algorithmic trading which relies on predefined rules and mathematical models, discretionary trading leverages the trader's judgment and perception of market conditions [[sources/pdf-1662a4368954]].
- Intuition in trading encompasses recognizing patterns, assessing market sentiment, and making rapid decisions amid uncertainty; experienced traders often develop a "gut feeling" that can enhance their trading performance [[sources/pdf-1662a4368954]].
- Kahneman and Klein (2009) explore the conditions under which intuition can be reliable, emphasizing the importance of experience and pattern recognition; their work suggests that intuition, when grounded in extensive experience and expertise, can be a powerful tool in trading [[sources/pdf-1662a4368954]].
- Sadler-Smith and Shefy (2004) assert that intuitive decision-making is a critical component of expertise, especially in complex and dynamic environments like financial markets, and argue that intuitive judgment can complement analytical approaches by providing a more holistic perspective on market conditions [[sources/pdf-1662a4368954]].
- Dane and Pratt (2007) describe intuitive expertise as developing through repeated exposure to specific patterns and outcomes — pattern recognition where traders, through experience, become adept at quickly identifying market signals and making rapid decisions [[sources/pdf-1662a4368954]].
- Most prior gap-related research has focused on quantitative approaches and algorithmic trading systems, leaving the role of discretionary trading and intuition less explored; Zarattini and Stamatoudis (2024) explicitly bridge that gap by providing empirical evidence on a discretionary trader's performance in an anonymized, unbiased setting [[sources/pdf-1662a4368954]].
