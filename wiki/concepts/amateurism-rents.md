# Amateurism Rents

## Summary

The economic rents created at NCAA FBS schools by rules that bar student-athletes from sharing in the profits generated by their participation; Garthwaite, Keener, Notowidigdo, and Ozminkowski (2020) estimate that less than 7 percent of football and men's basketball revenue flows to athletes through scholarships and stipends, compared to approximately 50 percent of revenue paid as salary in professional leagues, leaving a surplus that is distributed to other sports, facilities, coaches' salaries, and athletic department personnel rather than to the athletes who generate it [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].

## Key claims

- Intercollegiate amateur athletics in the US largely bars student-athletes from sharing in any of the profits generated by their participation, creating substantial economic rents for universities [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Athlete compensation is strictly limited to academic scholarships covering the cost of attendance and a modest stipend for living expenses [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Less than 7 percent of football and men's basketball revenue is paid to athletes through these two forms of compensation [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Even 7 percent likely overstates actual compensation because scholarship values are based on list price of tuition rather than the net price paid by the average student — which averages 40–45 percent of list price at public and private non-profit universities [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Professional football and men's basketball players receive approximately 50 percent of revenue as salary under their respective collective bargaining agreements [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- The rents are primarily generated by men's football and men's basketball, which account for 58 percent of total FBS athletic department revenue [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- The potential for rent-sharing is exacerbated by the fact that athletic departments are legal non-profit enterprises that may find it undesirable or unseemly to show large and persistent excess revenue on their balance sheets [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- The authors calculate a hypothetical wage structure for college athletes using the collective bargaining agreements in professional sports leagues as a benchmark to understand the magnitude of available rents [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
- Recent threats to the rents have arisen from litigation, legislation, and the global coronavirus pandemic [[sources/pdf-matthew-notowidigdo-2020-gkno-manuscript-1025]].
