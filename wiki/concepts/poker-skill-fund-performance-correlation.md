# Poker-Skill/Fund-Performance Correlation

## Summary

The empirical finding from Lu, Mortal, and Ray (2020) that hedge fund managers who cash in poker tournaments significantly outperform non-poker-playing peers by approximately 10 basis points per month in return and alpha measures, with the effect strengthening for more competitive tournaments — suggesting poker skills (incomplete-information decision-making, patience, risk assessment) are correlated with fund management skills [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].

## Key claims

- Hedge fund managers who have cashed in at least one poker tournament significantly outperform managers with no cashes in the Hendon Mob database, with monthly return and alpha measures approximately 10 bp higher [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].
- The outperformance effect is stronger for tournaments with more entrants, larger buy-ins, and ultimately those winning higher cash prizes [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].
- Managers who cash in positions better than the median cashing position (top 5.9th percentile in the sample) perform significantly better than managers who cash in worse positions, suggesting the effect reflects skill rather than luck from more frequent play [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].
- Fund managers who won poker tournaments prior to starting their funds also outperform other similar types of funds started at the same time, addressing the reverse-causality concern that good fund performance funds more tournament entries [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].
- Results are robust to four alternative skill measures proposed by Berk and van Binsbergen (2015), Kacperczyk, Sialm, and Zheng (2008), Sun, Wang, and Zheng (2012), and Kacperczyk and Seru (2007) [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].
- Poker is singled out over other games because it is a "game of incomplete information" — more akin to markets than chess (complete information) or bridge (where much uncertainty resolves through logical deduction) [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].
- Two transferable skills identified via holdings-level analysis: poker winner managers exhibit significantly lower turnover (greater patience) and less susceptibility to the disposition effect than non-poker winner managers [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].
- Despite the valid skill correlation, hedge fund investors would be better off investing in an otherwise similar manager without poker tournament success, because the inflows generated by the poker win signal erode post-tournament alpha by 40–70 bp per month [[sources/pdf-sugsu-2020-microsoft-word-hfholdem]].
