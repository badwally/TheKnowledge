# Rent-sharing between firms and workers

## Summary

The practice — common among non-business CEOs but rarely observed among CEOs with business degrees — of passing some portion of firm-level rents (e.g., quasi-rents from positive export shocks) through to workers as higher wages; Acemoglu, He, and le Maire (2022) identify a differential rent-sharing response to export shocks as the proximate mechanism for the wage and labor-share decline observed when business managers take over [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].

## Key claims

- The proximate cause of the (relative) wage effects of business-manager appointments is changes in rent-sharing practices following the appointment of business managers, identified using firm-specific export demand shocks in Denmark [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
- For non-business managers, rent-sharing elasticity estimates suggest that a 10% increase in value added per worker is associated with a 1.9% increase in wages, and a 10% increase in profit per worker is associated with a 1% increase in wages [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
- For business managers, there is a precise zero impact on wages following the same kind of export-driven increase in firm performance — they do not share these export-driven rents with their employees [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
- This differential rent-sharing response explains the entirety of the event-study estimates on wages following a business-manager appointment [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
- The effect is almost completely driven by positive export shocks: following an exogenous decline in exports, business and non-business managers behave similarly, presumably because cutting wages is challenging even for business managers who prioritize cost reductions [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
- Business and non-business managers show no major differences in productivity, sales, employment, or investment responses to export demand shocks — there is no compelling evidence that business managers are more productive or adaptable in this context either [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
