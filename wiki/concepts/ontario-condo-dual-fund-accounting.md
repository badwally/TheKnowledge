# Ontario Condo Dual-Fund Accounting (Operating + Reserve)

## Summary

Ontario condominium corporations must maintain two distinct funds with separate accounting under the Condominium Act, 1998: an Operating Fund (day-to-day expenses) and a Reserve Fund (long-term capital repairs and replacements) [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]]. The dual-fund structure is the foundational accounting architecture for Ontario condominium financial reporting, and proper cost allocation between the two funds is a load-bearing CPA-judgment exercise — mixing the funds (using reserve fund money for operating deficits or vice versa) is enumerated as the #1 common financial-reporting mistake in CPA practitioner guidance [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]]. The CMRAO Practice Guide and CAO Guidelines reinforce strict separation, requiring interfund transfers to be tracked with proper board authorization [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].

## Key claims

- Ontario condominium corporations are required to maintain two distinct funds with separate accounting: an Operating Fund and a Reserve Fund [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- Operating Fund covers day-to-day operating expenses: salaries, utilities, maintenance, regular repairs, snow removal, landscaping, management fees, insurance premiums, professional fees, and reserve fund contributions [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- Reserve Fund covers long-term capital repairs and replacements: roof, elevators, parking garage, HVAC systems, exterior cladding and windows, building envelope restoration [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- Operating Fund is funded by monthly common-expense fees; Reserve Fund is funded by designated contributions [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- The Operating Fund can have a deficit subject to board approval; the Reserve Fund (per the source's framing) cannot fall below a 10% threshold — though this 10% claim warrants statutory verification against §94 contribution-rate language [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- Reserve funds must be reported separately from the operating fund in financial statements via a Statement of Changes in Reserve Fund [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- All investment income earned on reserve funds must be allocated to the reserve fund, not the operating fund — investment gains/losses allocated to reserve fund; disclosure of investment holdings in notes [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- The #1 common financial-reporting mistake enumerated by CPA-practitioner guidance is mixing operating and reserve funds — using reserve fund money to cover operating deficits or vice versa [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- The recommended mitigation is strict separation with all interfund transfers tracked under proper board authorization [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
- Proper cost allocation is a CPA-judgment exercise: "determining whether an expense belongs in the operating fund or reserve fund is critical for accurate reporting," with allocation principles distinguishing minor repairs (under useful-life threshold → operating) from major capital projects and end-of-useful-life component replacement (→ reserve) [[sources/web-2026-03-05-dc5]].
