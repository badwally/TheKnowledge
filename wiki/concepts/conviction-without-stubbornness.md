# Conviction Without Stubbornness

## Summary

The dual posture Steve Cohen identifies in master traders in his foreword to "Trading to Win" (1998): demonstrating complete conviction when trading — enough to take large financial risks — while simultaneously retaining the ability to admit mistakes and minimize their consequences when wrong; these are paired traits, not a contradiction [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].

## Key claims

- Per Cohen, super-traders are totally committed to their own particular style and demonstrate complete conviction when trading [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].
- Because of this conviction, they are able to take large financial risks and have the confidence and belief that probability is on their side [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].
- At the same time, they have the ability to admit their mistakes and minimize their consequences — losses — when they are wrong [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].
- Per Cohen, most traders fail at both halves at once: they are indecisive, lack conviction, are afraid of taking risks and making mistakes, and are unaware of personality flaws holding them back [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].
- Per Kiev, the dual posture connects to the musical-chairs timing problem: a trader stuck holding a declining stock — convinced it will turn around — has confused conviction with rationalization rather than admitting the failed setup and cutting the loss [[sources/pdf-869163b2f118]].
