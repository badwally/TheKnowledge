# Flood-induced pavement deterioration

## Summary

Flood-induced pavement deterioration is the phenomenon by which flooding events accelerate the loss of pavement structural and functional condition, observable as faster roughness growth (e.g., higher IRI deterioration rates) in flood-exposed pavement sections relative to non-flooded sections.

## Key claims

- Flooding can damage pavement infrastructure significantly, causing both immediate and long-term structural and functional issues [[sources/arxiv-2507.01056]].
- Flood-affected pavements experience a more rapid increase in roughness, as measured by the International Roughness Index, compared to non-flooded sections [[sources/arxiv-2507.01056]].
- Flood exposure characteristics relevant to pavement deterioration include flood duration and spatial extent [[sources/arxiv-2507.01056]].
- Proactive flood mitigation strategies argued to enhance pavement resilience in vulnerable regions include improved drainage systems, flood-resistant materials, and preventative maintenance [[sources/arxiv-2507.01056]].
