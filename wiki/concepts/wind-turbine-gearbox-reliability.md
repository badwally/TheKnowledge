# Wind turbine gearbox reliability

## Summary

Wind turbine gearbox reliability concerns the time-to-failure behaviour of gearbox subsystems in wind turbines, which are major components whose failures dominate wind farm operation and maintenance costs. Inference on gearbox reliability is complicated by confounding between manufacturer-specific design variability and the environmental conditions to which turbines are exposed.

## Key claims

- Wind turbine major components failures represent a disproportionate share of wind farm operation and maintenance costs [[sources/web-2026-05-01-6b7]].
- Robust statistical inference on wind turbine reliability is challenging because large-scale datasets aggregate turbines exposed to heterogeneous environmental conditions, creating confounding between intrinsic design variability and wind-driven effects [[sources/web-2026-05-01-6b7]].
- In a study of over 8,000 gearboxes and 64,000 cumulative operating years, intrinsic design differences dominate the failure signal relative to environmental covariates [[sources/web-2026-05-01-6b7]].
- Capacity factor emerges as the most robust environmental driver of gearbox lifetime reduction in the same study [[sources/web-2026-05-01-6b7]].
