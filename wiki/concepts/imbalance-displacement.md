# Imbalance / Displacement

## Summary

In the market-structure framework taught in Lesson 2: Market Structure, an imbalance is a candle gap formed by the middle candle of a three-candle set — created by a sudden change in the appetite for price (displacement) that leaves an incomplete auction with orders unfilled, marking a zone price is likely to revisit before continuing the trend [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].

## Key claims

- An imbalance occurs when the appetite for price suddenly increases or decreases, an event the lesson labels displacement [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The lesson's more technical definition of displacement is "the change of state in delivery of price," usually coupled with order blocks [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Displacement-style explosive moves are seen near breakouts, reversal breakouts, or during trends, and occur during the expansion phase of price [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- An imbalance is recognized as a candle gap on any timeframe formed by the middle candle of a set of three [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- A bullish imbalance is the gap between the high of the preceding candle and the low of the ensuing candle [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- A bearish imbalance is the gap between the low of the preceding candle and the high of the ensuing candle [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- The economic significance attributed to imbalances is that the algo has created an incomplete auction of price — buyers or sellers have been left behind before their orders could be completed [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Price will then either continue with the trend or retrace and complete the auction, allowing traders to get in before price continues again with the trend [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
- Imbalances matter because they are where a trader may add positions to join the trend the algos are setting — capturing a big bulk of the move rather than the exact bottom or top [[sources/pdf-81305d181382]].
