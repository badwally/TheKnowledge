# Data Trust

## Summary

A data trust is a legal structure that provides independent stewardship of data, intended to increase access to data while retaining the trust of both individuals and organisations contributing it [[sources/web-2019-04-13-653]]. The construct was originated and operationalised by the Open Data Institute (ODI), which describes data trusts as one approach within a broader family of data-stewardship and data-access models [[sources/web-2019-04-13-653]].

## Key claims

- Data trusts are framed by the ODI as a response to the question of who should have access to data, for what purposes, and to whose benefit [[sources/web-2019-04-13-653]].
- In 2018, the ODI assessed data trusts in partnership with the UK Office for Artificial Intelligence and Innovate UK [[sources/web-2019-04-13-653]].
- The ODI piloted three real-world applications of the data-trust method: a food-waste pilot, an illegal-wildlife-trade pilot, and a city-data pilot with the Greater London Authority and the Royal Borough of Greenwich [[sources/web-2019-04-13-653]].
- The ODI's research programme commissioned external work on the legal requirements, legal landscape, decision-making process, technology, certification, and economic function of data trusts [[sources/web-2019-04-13-653]].
- Data trusts are one of multiple approaches to making data accessible; the ODI maintains a Data Access Map to navigate the variety of terms and methods [[sources/web-2019-04-13-653]].
