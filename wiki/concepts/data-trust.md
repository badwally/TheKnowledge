# Data Trust

## Summary

A data trust is a proposed collective data-governance mechanism in which a fiduciary third party holds and administers rights over personal data on behalf of beneficiaries (data subjects), positioned as one of four commonly proposed collective intermediaries alongside decentralised autonomous organisations, data cooperatives, and data unions [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].

## Key claims

- Data trusts build on individualist data rights by treating data as a form of collective value and redistributing benefits of data production back to members [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Like the other intermediary forms studied, data trusts aim to better align the social and economic value of aggregated data with the normative interests of the individuals described in it, rather than to replace individual data rights [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Some trust proposals operate as brokers — contractually constraining how members' data may be used without ever controlling it — while others propose to control or build the infrastructures of data collection and storage [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
- Demand for trust-based collective data governance spans many jurisdictions and a wide range of otherwise divergent ideological positions [[sources/web-2023-09-05-c31]].
