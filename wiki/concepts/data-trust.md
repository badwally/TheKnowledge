# Data Trust

## Summary

A data trust is a legal structure that provides independent stewardship of data, intended to increase access to data while retaining the trust of both individuals and organisations [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]]. In the Ada Lovelace Institute / AI Council formulation, a data trust creates a vehicle for individuals to state their aspirations for data use and mandate a trustee to pursue those aspirations, with the trustee bearing a fiduciary duty in exercising data rights on behalf of the beneficiaries [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].

## Key claims

- The distinctive elements of the data-trust model are the role of the trustee (who bears a fiduciary duty in exercising data rights on behalf of the beneficiaries) and the role of the overseeing court in providing additional safeguards [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
- Data trusts work best in contexts where individuals and groups wish to define the terms of data use by creating a new institution (a trust) to steward data on their behalf and to represent them in negotiations about data use [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
- A trust is a legal relationship between at least two parties, in which one party (the trustee) manages the rights associated with an asset for the benefit of another (the beneficiary) [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
- Almost any right can be held in trust so long as three conditions are met: a clear intention to establish a trust, a defined subject matter/property of the trust, and specified beneficiaries (including as a conceptual category rather than nominally) [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
- Trust law has ancient roots, with the fiduciary responsibilities at its core traceable to Roman law; the UK conception of a trust as an entity originates in medieval England, where landowners leaving for the Crusades transferred estate-management rights to caretakers, and disputes were resolved in the Courts of Chancery [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
- Data trusts can be built with a highly participatory structure that requires systematic input from the individuals who set up the trust, or alternatively can delegate to the data trustee the responsibility to determine what processing is in the beneficiaries' interest [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
- The Ada Lovelace Institute / AI Council report positions data trusts as one of three legal mechanisms (alongside data cooperatives and corporate/contractual mechanisms) in the data-governance toolbox, with no one-size-fits-all answer [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
