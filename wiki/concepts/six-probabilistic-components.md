# Six Probabilistic Components

## Summary

The "six probabilistic components" is the minimum capital-infrastructure scope adopted by the condo-capital-infra engagement: probabilistic depth on six high-variance, high-cost components — roof, building envelope, plumbing risers, HVAC central plant, elevators, and parking deck / podium structure — with the long tail of remaining components covered deterministically using CAI-compliant methodology [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]]. It is a project scope lock per the engagement synthesis [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].

## Key claims

- The six components are roof, building envelope, plumbing risers, HVAC central plant, elevators, and parking deck / podium structure [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- Probabilistic methods (component-level RUL distributions, Monte Carlo aggregation, regime-switching cost escalation) are concentrated on these six components [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- The remaining long-tail components are matched to incumbents via deterministic CAI-compliant coverage [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- This split is the minimum capital-infrastructure scope deemed sufficient to compete credibly against incumbents while keeping methodology depth defensible [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- The six-component scope is one of the project's explicit scope locks — not to be revisited without explicit request [[sources/docx-bf4965d0d33a]].
- Building envelope priors for deck and balcony surfaces are anchored by BC Housing's Maintenance Matters No. 6 bulletin: liquid-applied urethane membranes have an expected service life of approximately 10 years and sheet-applied vinyl (PVC) membranes 10 to 15 years, with longer life possible under low traffic and reduced exposure when regularly maintained [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-06]].
- BC Housing identifies 15+ years of membrane age as a step-up-inspection trigger — risk of damage to deck/balcony structure and exterior walls materially increases at this point, warranting more frequent inspections rather than an automatic replacement decision [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-06]].
- Parking deck / podium structure shares waterproofing-membrane, drainage, and venting failure modes with balcony decks; BC Housing MM-06's failure-mode taxonomy (membrane water ingress at holes/cracks/joints, guardrail-base leaks, saddle leaks at guardwall corners, internal floor-drain leaks, scupper degradation, structural movement in the first 18 months for wood-frame buildings) is the prior calibration basis for these envelope/podium components [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-06]].
- Inspection cadence for envelope and podium components per BC Housing MM-06: biennial inspection of membranes, guardrails, and guardwalls; annual scupper attachment checks; twice-yearly surface cleaning to keep drainage paths free; for new wood-frame buildings, additional inspections before the end of year one and year two to detect problems from wood shrinkage and structural movement [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-06]].
- New building-code requirements (as referenced in MM-06) mandate more durable membranes on deck surfaces, similar to those used on roof surfaces — relevant to replacement-cost forecasting because deck membrane replacements increasingly trigger upgrade rather than like-for-like replacement [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-06]].
- Roof component priors are anchored by BC Housing's Maintenance Matters No. 2 bulletin, which defines a two-system taxonomy — low-slope (dominant in multi-unit residential buildings) and steep-slope (dominant in townhouses and single detached houses) — with some buildings combining both types [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-02]].
- Roof system service lives range from 10 years to over 30 years depending on design, exposure, construction, and materials per BC Housing MM-02 — wide enough to require an RUL distribution rather than a single point estimate, and a direct prior input for the roof component's survival model [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-02]].
- Inspection cadence for the roof component per BC Housing MM-02: twice yearly (spring and fall), plus post-event inspection after high winds, extreme rain, hail, or rooftop construction; inspections must be performed by a qualified roofing inspector or contractor familiar with the building's roof system [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-02]].
- Roof failure-mode taxonomy per BC Housing MM-02 (the prior calibration basis for roof survival models): membrane splitting/ridging/blistering on low-slope roofs; missing gravel ballast (UV and weather exposure); missing granules on asphalt shingles from foot traffic, wind scouring, or tree-branch contact; curled/broken/cracked/missing shingles, shakes, or tiles; excessive moss or algae growth (premature failure on shakes, shingles, and certain low-slope membranes); standing water (ponding) from blocked drains; flashing/eavestrough/downpipe damage; overflowing scuppers indicating main-drain blockage; attic black-staining indicating ventilation or air-leak problems [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-02]].
- BC Housing MM-02 frames roof renewal as a renewals plan covering frequency and approximate cost — aligned with BC Strata Property Act contingency-reserve-fund obligations for less-than-annual common expenses such as roof replacement [[sources/pdf-bc-housing-2020-maintenance-matters-02]].
