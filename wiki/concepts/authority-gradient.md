# Authority Gradient

## Summary

A concept originally coined in aviation (Alkov et al., 1992) describing scenarios in which significant disparities in capability, experience, and authority between participants impede communication and lead to errors; subsequently studied in medicine and now invoked by Tomašev et al. (2026) as a relevant frame for AI delegation, particularly for reasoning about liability firebreaks and oversight when irreversible real-world actions are at stake [[sources/pdf-nenad-toma-2026-intelligent-ai-delegation]].

## Key claims

- In medical settings, a significant percentage of errors is attributed to the manner in which senior practitioners conduct supervision (Cosby and Croskerry, 2004; Stucky et al., 2022) — a more experienced person may make erroneous assumptions about the knowledge of a less experienced worker, resulting in under-specified requests [[sources/pdf-nenad-toma-2026-intelligent-ai-delegation]].
- In the AI delegation framework, irreversible tasks with real-world side effects (executing a financial trade, deleting a database, sending an external email) require stricter liability firebreaks and steeper authority gradients than reversible tasks such as drafting an email or flagging a database entry [[sources/pdf-nenad-toma-2026-intelligent-ai-delegation]].
