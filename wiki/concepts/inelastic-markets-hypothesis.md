# Inelastic Markets Hypothesis

## Summary

The thesis of Gabaix and Koijen (2021) that the aggregate price elasticity of demand for stocks is small (approximately 0.2) so that flows in and out of the equity market have large impacts on prices — empirically, $1 of net flow into equities raises aggregate market value by about $5 [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].

## Key claims

- The simplest "efficient markets" model says the price of equities is the present value of future dividends, so the valuation of the aggregate market should not change when an investor sells $1 of bonds and buys $1 of stocks; Gabaix and Koijen find both theoretically and empirically that the market's aggregate value rises by about $5 [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- The aggregate equity demand elasticity is approximately 0.2 — a 5% rise in equity market price reduces demand by only 1%, about 100 times smaller than rational or behavioral models would predict [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- The high sensitivity of prices to flows has large consequences: flows in the market and demand shocks affect prices and expected returns in a quantitatively important way [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- Three a priori reasons to expect inelasticity: tight institutional mandates (a fund that invests entirely in equities cannot exchange them for bonds); small transfer of equity risk across investor sectors (about 0.6% of the aggregate value of the equity market per quarter for the average pair of investor sectors); and micro-level demand elasticities for individual stocks of approximately 1, with macro elasticity arguably lower since the equity index and a bond are weaker substitutes than two individual stocks [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- Hedge funds — a natural macro arbitrageur — hold less than 5% of the equity market and tend to reduce their equity allocations in bad times due to outflows and binding risk constraints (citing Ben-David et al. (2012)) [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- A new literature exploring elasticities for U.S. factors such as size and value finds elasticities of around 0.2, consistent with the low macro elasticity estimate [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- The price impact of flows is consistent with a quite long-lasting price impact — perfectly long-lasting in the simplest version of the model — not because flows release information but because a permanent shift in equity demand creates a permanent shift in equilibrium price [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
- Robustness checks across data sets (Flow of Funds, 13F filings) yield price-impact multipliers around 5 [[sources/pdf-b8cec9d1ec0c]].
