# Cash-Efficient Implementation

## Summary

The structural mechanism by which a multi-manager fund deploys the combined positions of multiple strategies inside a single capital base — making the fund twice as cash-efficient as a hedge fund portfolio that splits the same capital between two single-strategy funds, and driving most of the multi-manager structure's return advantage [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].

## Key claims

- Allocating $100 to one Equity Fund and $100 to one Macro Fund (each requiring $100 of capital separately) within a single $100 multi-manager fund makes the multi-manager fund twice as cash-efficient as a 50/50 hedge fund portfolio holding $50 in each [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
- In ClearAlpha's hypothetical, the cash-efficient construction drives the multi-manager fund's 15% total return versus 9% for the hedge fund portfolio that splits capital between the two single-strategy funds [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
- A $100 multi-manager fund holding combined $100 Equity Fund + $100 Macro Fund positions has $360 long and $240 short exposures (gross $600), versus $300 gross for the 50/50 hedge fund portfolio [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
- The cash efficiency draws the same analogy private equity has used for decades: identify viable strategies, then adjust the capital structure to increase return on equity capital — multi-manager funds enable various investment teams to pursue strategies within a capital-efficient framework [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
- Investors are properly cautious about the leverage required to do so; the structure requires significant experience in markets — particularly in liquidity risk, risk modeling, and hedging [[sources/pdf-7663c24d3159]].
