# Trimmed Mean Inflation Measures

## Summary

Trimmed mean measures of CPI and PCE — produced by the Cleveland and Dallas regional Federal Reserve banks — exclude outlier categories to estimate broad-based underlying inflation; the July 13, 2022 Q3-2022 IACFM presentation uses the rapid rise in these trimmed measures to argue inflation has spread beyond a few volatile categories [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].

## Key claims

- Trimmed mean measures of CPI and PCE exclude outlier categories to provide a measure of broad-based underlying inflation [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- The Cleveland Fed publishes the 16% Trimmed Mean CPI; the Dallas Fed publishes a 24% lower / 31% upper tail Trimmed Mean PCE [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- As of mid-2022, year-over-year readings cited in the presentation: CPI 8.6%, PCE 6.3%, Cleveland Fed 16% Trimmed Mean CPI 6.5%, Dallas Fed Trimmed Mean PCE 4.0% [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Month-over-month change in the Cleveland Fed 16% Trimmed Mean CPI (Dec 2021–May 2022): 0.5%, 0.6%, 0.5%, 0.5%, 0.4%, 0.8% — versus headline CPI: 0.6%, 0.6%, 0.8%, 1.2%, 0.3%, 1.0% [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- Month-over-month change in the Dallas Fed Trimmed Mean PCE (Dec 2021–May 2022): 0.4%, 0.5%, 0.3%, 0.2%, 0.2%, 0.4% — versus headline PCE: 0.5%, 0.5%, 0.5%, 0.9%, 0.2%, 0.6% [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
- The IACFM presentation argues the rise in trimmed-mean measures reflects "the increasingly broad-based nature of underlying inflation" — when outliers are excluded and the trimmed mean still rises, inflation has spread beyond a few volatile categories [[sources/pdf-853a7755a470]].
