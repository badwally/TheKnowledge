# Fed Impossible Trinity

## Summary

Zoltan Pozsar's framing of the Fed's three statutory or de facto goals — price stability, full employment, and financial stability — as not simultaneously achievable, requiring the Fed to choose; Pozsar argues that as of May 2022 the Fed has chosen price stability as the priority, accepting slower growth, higher unemployment, and some financial instability as the cost [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].

## Key claims

- Central banking with a multitude of mandates is "a bit like the life of a working parent: it is impossible to deliver 110% at work, 110% at home... and 110% at the tennis court as well" — an impossible trinity [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Price stability, full employment, and financial stability are not all achievable at the same time — something has to give [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- The Fed appears to have chosen price stability as the priority: it wants slower growth and higher unemployment [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
- Tighter financial conditions mean some financial instability by definition — nothing systemic, but turmoil still, with tech stocks and the crypto sell-off cited as evidence [[sources/pdf-3d3d42a0717a]].
