# Cycling Injury Prevention

## Summary

For cyclists, injury prevention is a primary goal of [[concepts/strength-and-conditioning-for-cyclists|strength and conditioning]] — not a side benefit — because repetitive pedalling creates muscular imbalances that off-bike strength and stability work directly counteracts [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].

## Key claims

- Cycling involves repetitive overuse that creates muscular imbalances; structured strength and stability work matches and corrects those imbalances to lower injury risk [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Improved strength and stability are the explicit causal mechanisms cited for risk reduction — i.e., the target qualities are not endurance or aerobic capacity [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- Specific exercises framed as injury-prevention work include the [[concepts/glute-bridge|glute bridge]] (hip mobility + posterior chain) and lying hip abduction (glutes, hamstrings, TFL, hip abductors trained for stability) [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- The [[concepts/cycling-periodization-phases|Prep phase]] of a training cycle is dedicated to neuromuscular efficiency, stability, and functional strength — i.e., movement-quality work that doubles as injury insurance before higher-load phases [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
- During higher-intensity phases, form is the gating constraint: reduce intensity rather than break form, which is itself an injury-prevention rule baked into the prescription [[sources/pdf-d2f73a919c86]].
