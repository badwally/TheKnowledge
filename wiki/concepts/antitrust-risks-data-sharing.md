# Antitrust Risks in Competitor Data Sharing

## Summary

Sharing data between competitors — whether via blockchain or other infrastructures — creates a recurring set of antitrust risks: tacit and explicit collusion, exclusion of competitors from critical infrastructure, and erosion of regulator enforcement capability [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].

## Key claims

- Distributed ledgers that expose sensitive data such as price, cost, or output to competitors can facilitate horizontal collusion including price-fixing and bid-rigging [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- Distributed transaction data give cartelists a powerful monitoring tool for detecting deviations from agreed prices and a transparent reference for reaching tacit consensus on price or market share [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- In oligopolistic markets, firms may achieve cooperation tacitly when transaction-level information becomes universally observable [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- If a permissioned network becomes critical to competing in a market, administrators who hold access credentials may exclude new entrants and bar them from competing [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- Decentralized networks without entry points may prevent governments from enforcing regulations, and anonymous blockchains can make it difficult to identify perpetrators [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
- Antitrust analysis of blockchain consortia should focus on network design and regulatory oversight rather than treat blockchain as inherently anticompetitive; blockchain is primarily a data management tool whose competitive effects depend on implementation [[sources/web-2021-03-07-5c3]].
