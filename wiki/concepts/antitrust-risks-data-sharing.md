# Antitrust Risks in Competitor Data Sharing

## Summary

Sharing data between competitors — whether via blockchain, federated learning, data clean rooms, or other infrastructures — creates a recurring set of antitrust risks, including tacit and explicit coordination on price, output, or market allocation, and information-exchange arrangements that facilitate cartel behaviour [[sources/web-2024-06-27-57a]]. Canadian competition law addresses these risks through both a criminal track (section 45 conspiracy) and a civil track (section 90.1 reviewable agreements), with information sharing called out as a discrete worked example in the Bureau's Competitor Collaboration Guidelines [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].

## Key claims

- The Competition Bureau of Canada's draft Competitor Collaboration Guidelines (2020 consultation) treat "information sharing" as Example 7 in its twelve hypothetical illustrations — i.e., as a distinct fact pattern requiring its own application of the section 45 / section 90.1 framework rather than collapsing into price-fixing or market-allocation analysis [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Whether a competitor data-sharing arrangement attracts criminal exposure under section 45 turns on whether the information exchange operates as a naked restraint (e.g., a vehicle for tacit price-fixing or market allocation) or is integrated with a legitimate collaboration, strategic alliance, or joint venture [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Even where a data-sharing arrangement avoids the section 45 criminal track, it can still be reviewable under section 90.1 if it is likely to prevent or lessen competition substantially [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Where the participating firms in a data-sharing arrangement are jointly dominant, the arrangement may additionally be examined under section 79 (abuse of dominance) for exclusionary, predatory, or disciplinary effects on non-participating competitors [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
- Firms designing a competitor data-sharing arrangement can de-risk by requesting a binding written opinion from the Commissioner under section 124.1 of the Competition Act on the applicability of the conspiracy and civil agreements provisions to the proposed conduct [[sources/web-2022-01-19-c5a]].
