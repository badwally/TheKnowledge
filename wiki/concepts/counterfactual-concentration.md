# Counterfactual Concentration

## Summary

Thought experiment from Mauboussin and Callahan (2024): take the market capitalizations of the top 1, 3, and 10 U.S. companies as of year-end 2023 and discount them back five and ten years at the realized market return to derive what concentration "should" have been if the market had efficiently anticipated the leaders' subsequent total shareholder returns. The exercise inverts the usual question — "is concentration too high now?" — into "was concentration too low before?" [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].

## Key claims

- Methodology: discount end-of-2023 top-1, top-3, and top-10 market caps back at the total shareholder return of the Russell 3000 Index to obtain the "theoretical" historical market caps under efficient pricing [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Five-year result: actual top-10 weighting was 17.8% in 2018, but would have been 22.1% had the market priced efficiently for the subsequent 2023 outcome [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Five-year result, finer slices: top-1 actual 2.7% vs theoretical 4.8%; top-3 actual 7.9% vs theoretical 12.0% [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- Ten-year result: actual top-10 weighting was 13.6% in 2013, but would have been 18.1% under the same counterfactual; top-1 was 2.2% actual vs 3.9% theoretical, top-3 was 5.4% actual vs 9.8% theoretical [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- The implication used by Mauboussin and Callahan: the possibility that large-capitalization stocks were mispriced a decade ago — allowing them to deliver high subsequent total shareholder returns — suggests concentration may have been too low historically, weakening the "concentration is too high now" complaint [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
- The theoretical adjustment is applied only to the top 1, 3, and 10 companies — the rest of the universe is left at its actual historical caps [[sources/pdf-7acc9c92267c]].
