# Manager-diffusion instrumental variable

## Summary

An identification strategy used by Acemoglu, He, and le Maire (2022) to bolster the causal interpretation of the business-manager effect: defining peer groups as region-industry-size class cells and using prior business-manager appointments within a peer group as an instrument for a focal firm's likelihood of subsequently appointing a business manager [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].

## Key claims

- Peer groups are defined as region-industry-size class cells; after a first business manager is appointed within a cell, its peers become significantly more likely to appoint business managers as well, in both the US and Denmark [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
- The strategy controls for differential trends by industry, region, and size class to ensure that it exploits within-group variation [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
- IV effects are robust to controlling for correlation in output and wages across peers [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
- The strategy is conceptually attractive in part because it exploits a completely different source of variation than the non-IV event-study estimates [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
- Estimates from this approach are similar in sign and statistical significance to the baseline but about 50% larger in magnitude in both Denmark and the US [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
- The paper interprets this magnitude difference as systemic effects of practices associated with business education: practices diffuse more broadly within cells, so firms without business managers may also adopt the same managerial practices and firms with business managers may use them more intensively [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
- This reasoning suggests the baseline event-study quantitative effects should be considered lower bounds [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
- The IV strategy is similar to the one used in Acemoglu et al. (2019) to isolate potentially exogenous variation in country-level democracy based on the idea that there are regional democratization waves [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
- The paper acknowledges this IV strategy is not a panacea against all endogeneity concerns and may be confounded with cross-cell trends [[sources/pdf-4dbe002c1c7a]].
