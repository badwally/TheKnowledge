# Algorithmic Price Delivery

## Summary

Algorithmic price delivery is the ICT-style premise that price action in developed markets is driven primarily by algorithms whose role is to move price from one liquidity reference point to another over time according to preset factors; "Lesson 1: Price and Liquidity" claims that algos are responsible for more than 75% of the volume in developed markets and that the trader's job is to understand what the algos are trying to do and follow suit [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].

## Key claims

- Algorithms ("Algo's") are responsible for more than 75% of the volume in developed markets [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].
- The role of an algorithm is to deliver price from one point to another over a period of time based on some preset factors [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].
- The trader's job is framed as simply understanding what the algos are trying to do and following suit, rather than fighting them [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].
- Price action without time is described as useless; coupling price action with time is what allows the trader to understand market structure and anticipate where price will be delivered next [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].
- Once buyside liquidity is purged, the lesson asserts that there will be a lack of buyers interested at higher prices and algos will begin to deliver price back down toward sellside liquidity — an explicit claim that algorithms drive the BSL→SSL round-trip [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].
- Price action on any timeframe "will repeat the same process over and over again," framed by the lesson with the four states consolidation, expansion, retracement, and reversal — algorithmic delivery is treated as predictable rather than exciting and unknown [[sources/pdf-704c439723d0]].
