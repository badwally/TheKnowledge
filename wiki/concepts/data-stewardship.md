# Data Stewardship

## Summary

Data stewardship is the practice of deciding who should have access to data, for what purposes, and to whose benefit — a framing the Open Data Institute uses to position itself in the data-governance space [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]]. The Ada Lovelace Institute and AI Council characterise it as the trustworthy and responsible use and management of data, requiring new governance models for collectives, governments and organisations [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].

## Key claims

- The Ada Lovelace Institute / AI Council 2021 report identifies three legal mechanisms for facilitating responsible data stewardship: data trusts, data cooperatives, and corporate and contractual mechanisms [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
- There is no one-size-fits-all stewardship solution; choosing the governance mechanism depends on the purpose of the data-sharing initiative, the benefits sought, and the nature of the relationship between participating actors (individuals, organisations and governments) [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
- The report focuses on personal data as the most common type of data stewarded under alternative governance models, while flagging non-personal data as an area requiring future exploration [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
- Stewardship is presented as a response to structural power imbalances created by concentrated data holdings, repeated breach scandals, and 'tenuous' public trust in public- and private-sector data sharing [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
- The limitations of consent-based data governance — lengthy and opaque terms, asymmetric information, cumulative effects of pervasive data use, and pressure to opt in to participate in society — are part of the motivation for stewardship-based alternatives [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
- The GDPR's foundational data-protection-by-design principles and Data Protection Impact Assessments (DPIAs) are legal obligations that precede consent and are part of the broader compliance frame within which stewardship operates [[sources/web-2021-03-04-e0f]].
