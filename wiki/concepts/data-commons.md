# Data Commons

## Summary

A data commons is a collective data-governance mechanism in which a community holds, administers and pools rights over a shared data resource as an institutional alternative to individualistic data-protection regimes and market-based data exchange [[sources/web-2025-06-02-56c]]. In the European data-law and political-philosophy literature, data commons are increasingly framed not by their technical infrastructure alone but by the kind of community that governs them [[sources/web-2025-06-02-56c]].

## Key claims

- Data commons are a collective data-governance mechanism that has recently gained traction in theoretical and policy-oriented discussions as a promising alternative to the shortcomings of individualistic data protection and data markets [[sources/web-2025-06-02-56c]].
- Most data-commons approaches in the literature centre on the idea of community as the key social institution capable of overcoming the limitations of individualism in data governance [[sources/web-2025-06-02-56c]].
- The meaning, features and implications of the language of community used by data-commons advocates have received far less attention than the institutional and technical features of commons themselves [[sources/web-2025-06-02-56c]].
- Analysing the key characteristics and moral-political affordances of the community embedded in a data commons has direct implications for devising and implementing policies on collective data governance [[sources/web-2025-06-02-56c]].
