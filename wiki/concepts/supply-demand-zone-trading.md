# Supply and Demand Zone Trading

## Summary

Price-action framework in which "zones" — areas on the chart where a previous imbalance between buying and selling pressure produced a large directional move — are used as reversal-trade triggers, distinguished from supports and resistances by being general areas rather than exact price levels [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].

## Key claims

- A supply or demand zone represents a general area where a previous imbalance led to a large move in a certain direction, in contrast to supports and resistances which are exact numbers [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
- Demand zones are used to enter long (calls) and supply zones are used to enter short (puts) for reversal trades; the same zone structure is used to mark the level at which the trade would be cut for a loss if it goes against the entry [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
- Zones can be found on any time frame: swing traders are directed to daily or weekly charts for the best indication of where a stock's overall trend can change, while daytraders use smaller time frames where the volume and strength of the zone may be lower but still work for the daytrading style [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
- Reversals taken in these zones are framed as having a higher winrate but a lower risk-to-reward profile than breakout trades [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
