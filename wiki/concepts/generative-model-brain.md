# Generative Model (Brain)

## Summary

In the predictive-processing literature, a generative model is a hierarchical, top-down structure that aims to capture the statistical structure of observed inputs by tracking — or schematically recapitulating — the causal matrix responsible for that structure [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]]. In a good generative model for vision, top-down connections within a multilevel bidirectional system come to encode a probabilistic model of the activities of units and groups of units within lower levels, thus tracking interacting causes in the signal source (the body or the external world) [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].

## Key claims

- The brain does not build its current model of distal causes by accumulating low-level cues bottom-up; instead, it tries to predict the current suite of cues from its best models of the possible causes (Hohwy 2007) [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].
- The lineage of generative-model approaches in computational neuroscience runs through Kawato et al. (1993), Hinton and Zemel (1994), Mumford (1994), Hinton et al. (1995), Dayan et al. (1995), Olshausen and Field (1996), Dayan (1997), and Hinton and Ghahramani (1997) [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].
- Top-down generative connections try to produce a "virtual version" of the sensory data via a deep multilevel cascade — the move that lies at the heart of hierarchical predictive coding [[sources/pdf-5f41a1d2e45f]].
