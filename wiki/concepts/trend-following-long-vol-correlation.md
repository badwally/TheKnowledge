# Trend-following / long-vol correlation

## Summary

Claim from Lempérière et al. (2014) that the excess return of trend-following cannot be classified as a risk premium and that, on the contrary, trend-following is correlated with "long-vol" strategies — i.e., it tends to perform best precisely when realized volatility rises, behaving more like an option-buying strategy than a risk-bearing one [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].

## Key claims

- The excess return of trend cannot be classified as a risk-premium — that is, the strategy's gains are not compensation for bearing a recurring downside [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- Trend-following is correlated with "long-vol" strategies [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
- This is offered as a partial explanation for why the anomaly persists: it is not an arbitrage that can be straightforwardly competed away by undiversified risk-seekers [[sources/pdf-be69dd282a8c]].
