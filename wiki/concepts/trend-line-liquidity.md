# Trend Line Liquidity

## Summary

Trend line liquidity, in daytradingrauf's DOL framework, is the pool of stop and entry orders that retail traders attach to drawn trend lines; the contrarian DOL trader treats the trend line as a liquidity container to fade — but only when HTF order flow supports the fade [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].

## Key claims

- Trend line liquidity is enumerated as one of three structural forms of market-structure liquidity, alongside bull/bear flags and failure swings (double tops and bottoms) [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
- Like the other market-structure forms, it is interesting precisely because many retail traders spool orders into it without market context [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
- It is one of the author's "favorite to look at and do the opposite to," provided HTF order flow supports the fade [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
- Fading trend line liquidity is explicitly subject to the HTF order flow confluence rule: "You must have HTF order flow to back up why you're 'fading' these patterns" [[sources/pdf-cce4b539ce1c]].
