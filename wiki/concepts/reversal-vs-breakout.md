# Reversal vs Breakout Tradeoff

## Summary

Trade-classification framework that distinguishes a reversal — a trend change typically anchored in a prior supply/demand imbalance — from a breakout — a move past a support or resistance on big volume — and assigns each a characteristic risk/reward and winrate profile that informs setup choice [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].

## Key claims

- A reversal is when the trend of a stock changes direction, meaning the price is likely to continue in that reversal direction for an extended period; reversals tend to happen in areas where there was previously an imbalance in supply/demand [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
- Reversal mechanism: when a stock reaches an area where there was heavy demand previously, it is likely to reverse there as buyers get their orders filled — and vice versa in a supply zone where sellers are waiting [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
- A breakout is when a stock breaks past a support or resistance with big volume — the result of buyers beating sellers or vice versa; it can be traded by going long (buying calls) when the stock breaks up over a resistance, or going short (buying puts) when it breaks under a support [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
- The risk-to-reward on a reversal is less than a breakout, but a reversal has a higher winrate [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
- The risk-to-reward on a breakout is better than a reversal, but a breakout has a lower winrate due to false breakouts [[sources/pdf-9613f301a3b5]].
